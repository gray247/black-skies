* `Who Owns the Text? Design Patterns for Preserving Authorship in AI-Assisted Writing`
  * Authors: Bohan Zhang; Chengke Bu; Paramveer S. Dhillon
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2601.10236`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; controlled user study; not fiction-specific
  * Supported conclusion: AI assistance may reduce psychological ownership even when it lowers effort. On-demand initiation, micro-suggestions, voice anchoring, and point-of-decision provenance are promising authorship-preserving patterns.
  * Limitation: The evaluated tasks were professional writing tasks rather than long-form fiction.
* `From Planning to Revision: How AI Writing Support at Different Stages Alters Ownership`
  * Authors: Katy Ilonka Gero; Tao Long; Carly Schnitzler; Paramveer Dhillon
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2604.11009`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; controlled study; not fiction-specific
  * Supported conclusion: AI assistance affects ownership differently by writing stage. Planning support caused a smaller ownership reduction than drafting support. Greater AI contribution of text and ideas correlated with weaker ownership.
  * Limitation: The study used short essay writing rather than novels or long-form fiction.
* `"It was 80% me, 20% AI": Seeking Authenticity in Co-Writing with Large Language Models`
  * Authors: Angel Hsing-Chi Hwang; Q. Vera Liao; Su Lin Blodgett; Alexandra Olteanu; Adam Trischler
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2411.13032`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; interviews with professional writers plus reader study
  * Supported conclusion: Writers understand authenticity as involving the process of constructing and expressing creative identity, not merely surface-level voice similarity. Personalization should support writer growth rather than merely imitate previous text.
  * Limitation: The study concerns human-AI co-writing broadly and does not establish one required fiction-tool design.
* `Help Me Write a Story: Evaluating LLMs' Ability to Generate Writing Feedback`
  * Authors: Hannah Rashkin; Elizabeth Clark; Fantine Huot; Mirella Lapata
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2507.16007`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; creative-writing feedback evaluation
  * Supported conclusion: Current models can provide specific and often accurate feedback. They may fail to identify the largest writing problem. They may judge poorly when critical versus positive feedback is appropriate.
  * Limitation: The controlled corrupted-story dataset does not represent every professional editing situation.
* `LLMs Exhibit Significantly Lower Uncertainty in Creative Writing Than Professional Writers`
  * Author: Peiqi Sui
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2602.16162`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; comparative computational study of creative writing
  * Supported conclusion: Model-generated continuations showed lower uncertainty than professional human writing. Constructive ambiguity and indeterminacy may be features of literary richness rather than defects.
  * Limitation: The study supports caution against over-correction but does not prove every ambiguous passage is intentional or effective.
* `Visual Writing: Writing by Manipulating Visual Representations of Stories`
  * Authors: Damien Masson; Zixin Zhao; Fanny Chevalier
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2410.07486`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; prototype evaluation with eight creative writers
  * Supported conclusion: Editable visual representations may help writers find passages, track story elements, specify edits, and explore alternatives.
  * Limitation: The sample was small. The study does not prove that visual systems should be mandatory, persistent, or first-release centerpieces.
* `Generative AI and Creativity: A Systematic Literature Review and Meta-Analysis`
  * Authors: Niklas Holzner; Sebastian Maier; Stefan Feuerriegel
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2505.17241`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; systematic review and meta-analysis across creative tasks
  * Supported conclusion: Human-AI collaboration showed a modest average creative-performance improvement. AI assistance was associated with substantially reduced diversity of ideas across the included studies.
  * Limitation: The included studies covered multiple creative-task types and populations, not exclusively fiction writers.
* `Linguistic Bias in ChatGPT: Language Models Reinforce Dialect Discrimination`
  * Authors: Eve Fleisig; Genevieve Smith; Madeline Bossi; Ishita Rustagi; Xavier Yin; Dan Klein
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2406.08818`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; large-scale dialect-bias evaluation
  * Supported conclusion: Evaluated models responded less appropriately to several non-standard English varieties. Critique and assistance systems require dialect-aware evaluation and must not silently treat difference from standardized English as error.
  * Limitation: The evaluated models and versions do not establish permanent behavior for every future model.
* `Deactivating Refusal Triggers: Understanding and Mitigating Overrefusal in Safety Alignment`
  * Authors: Zhiyu Xue; Zimo Qi; Guangliang Liu; Bocheng Chen; Ramtin Pedarsani
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2603.11388`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; safety-alignment and over-refusal study; not fiction-specific
  * Supported conclusion: Safety-aligned models may reject benign requests because non-harmful linguistic cues can become associated with refusal behavior. Provider refusal must remain distinct from application failure or manuscript-quality judgment.
  * Limitation: The study does not specifically evaluate horror, erotic fiction, violent fiction, or Black Skies workflows.
* `Long-context LLMs Struggle with Long In-context Learning`
  * Authors: Tianle Li; Ge Zhang; Quy Duc Do; Xiang Yue; Wenhu Chen
  * Identifier: `arXiv:2404.02060`
  * Evidence class: Academic preprint; long-context benchmark; not novel-specific
  * Supported conclusion: Nominal context capacity does not guarantee reliable reasoning across complex, context-rich inputs. Long-form model qualification must be task-specific rather than based only on advertised context size.
  * Limitation: The benchmark used extreme-label classification rather than novel analysis.
