## 2. Route And Provider Model

Writer-facing route modes are:

1. `Local Only`
2. `Privacy Preferred`
3. `Free Only`
4. `Balanced`
5. `Best Within Budget`

`Ask Before Paid` remains a separate approval policy.

Default posture:

`Privacy Preferred` with `Ask Before Paid` enabled.

Route mode expresses author-facing intent.

Provider policy remains the advanced execution layer for:

- provider allow and deny rules
- model capability eligibility
- availability
- privacy suitability
- context limits
- cost eligibility
- provider pinning
- fallback restrictions

Ordinary writing does not require provider selection.
No fallback may silently become less private or newly paid.

## 3. Ownership Model

- `Model Routing And Budget Architecture` owns route preferences,
  request-specific route selection, provider eligibility and execution
  policy, estimated cost, budget modes, routing refusal and fallback,
  route/run records, and route, provider, paid-use, spend, override,
  pinning, and fallback approval records.
- `LLM Package Construction Architecture` owns selected package
  contents, context assembly, provider-rendered payloads, package
  summaries, the composed final preview artifact, and the
  package-build-time snapshot of linked approval status.
- `Explicit Content Architecture` owns outbound eligibility,
  exclusions, masks, explicit-content posture, local-only and
  never-send restrictions, protected-package approval records, mask and
  protected-view approvals, and approved-summary records.
- `AI Lifecycle And Approval Matrix` owns approval classes, approval
  vocabulary, state definitions and transitions,
  persistent-versus-request-specific doctrine, expiry, revocation, and
  revalidation doctrine, advisory-until-accepted behavior, and the
  linked approval-lifecycle view.
- `Authorship Provenance AI Visibility` owns source provenance,
  transformation provenance, and provider-response provenance.

`AI Lifecycle And Approval Matrix` does not own a universal
approval-history store.
Package Construction and Provenance may reference approval results
without owning the decisions.
`Command Center Surface` may provide a non-owning unified request
timeline.

## 4. Summary Lifecycle

Three summary states remain distinct:

1. `Summary Candidate`
   - awaiting author review
   - cannot be transmitted externally
2. `Author-Approved Summary`
   - approved wording
   - defined source coverage
   - defined purpose and scope
   - one-time or reusable posture
   - stale, expired, superseded, or revoked state where applicable
3. `Approved Package Use`
   - a specific package uses an approved summary
   - inclusion is visible in the composed preview

Package approval must not silently approve another `Summary Candidate`.
Generated summaries may never be generated and transmitted
automatically without author approval.

## 5. Protected-Package Fallback

Fallback order remains:

1. use an existing `Author-Approved Summary` when permitted and
   previewed
2. offer manual mask or summary preparation
3. refuse outbound execution when no valid package can be produced

A genuinely local eligible route may still be offered.
Direct local writing remains available even when all AI and outbound
routes are blocked.

## 6. Approval Doctrine

These approval classes remain distinct:

- provider permission
- paid-use permission
- spend-threshold approval
- protected-package approval
- destination truth acceptance

Persistent preferences may include:

- default route
- provider allow or deny rules
- paid-use policy
- ordinary spend ceiling

Request-specific approval remains required for:

- over-ceiling spending
- newly included protected or summarized material
- provider or privacy posture changes
- stale approved-summary use
- destination truth mutation

Destination owners retain destination acceptance.
`Import / Export / Google Docs` retains transfer and export approval.

## 7. Writer-Facing And Degraded Posture

Ordinary writing remains quiet.

Only decision-relevant conditions should surface by default:

- local route selected
- AI unavailable
- paid approval required
- spend limit exceeded
- protected material excluded
- approved summary substituted
- request refused
- fallback available

The settled degraded posture preserves:

- no silent spend
- no silent send
- no silent provider downgrade
- no silent truth mutation
- background revalidation
- refusal naming the blocking rule
- honest partial-success reporting
- source preservation after package or route failure
- author-controlled fallback
- direct local writing availability
