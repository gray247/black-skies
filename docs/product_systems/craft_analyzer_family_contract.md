# Craft Analyzer Family Contract

## 1. Status Header

- Artifact name: `Craft Analyzer Family Contract`
- Status: `drafted`
- Artifact type: `product contract`
- Owner / review lane: `Phase 32 product-definition lane`
- Last reviewed: `2026-06-19`
- Scope: `shared craft-analyzer behavior for bounded prose-craft systems`
- Out of scope: `truth ownership`, `GUI design`, `runtime schema`,
  `scoring formulas`, `implementation`

## 2. Purpose

Define the shared product contract for current and future bounded craft
analyzers while keeping `Senses Usage`, `Overused Words`, and
`Cliche Detection` as separate visible writer-facing systems.

This contract is not a new truth owner, workspace, analyzer, or hidden
super-system.

## 3. Family Structure

`Craft Analysis` or `Craft Analyzer Family` is a shared family label and
behavior contract.

Current family members:

- `Senses Usage`
- `Overused Words`
- `Cliche Detection`

Each analyzer retains:

- distinct purpose,
- analyzer-specific settings,
- findings and explanations,
- source evidence,
- writer-facing filters,
- analyzer-specific intentional-use interpretation.

Future analyzers must conform to this contract or enter through
`Plugin / Rubric System` review.

## 4. Authority

Craft analyzers are advisory.

They do not own:

- manuscript truth,
- accepted prose changes,
- `Feedback Notes`,
- `Signals`,
- author intent,
- continuity truth,
- critique conclusions,
- durable revision closure.

Direct writing remains sovereign and non-gated.
Analyzer findings begin temporary.

## 5. Shared Inputs And Outputs

Shared allowed inputs:

- manuscript text and accepted manuscript context,
- accepted narrative assertions,
- selected passage or scoped review context,
- author-approved analyzer preferences,
- author-approved project intent or planning context when relevant,
- protected-content, routing, and visibility boundaries from the
  governing contracts.

Shared outputs may include:

- observations,
- findings,
- insights,
- recommendations,
- warnings,
- candidates,
- source-linked examples,
- bounded comparison views,
- note candidates,
- signal candidates.

These outputs remain advisory unless explicitly converted through the
owning system.

## 6. Shared Finding Lifecycle

Shared craft-analyzer lifecycle:

1. analyzer run
2. temporary finding
3. source inspection
4. author review
5. dismiss, ignore, suppress, or mark intentional
6. optionally save as `Feedback Note`
7. optionally elevate to `Signal`
8. manual revision or separately approved rewrite
9. rerun or re-evaluation
10. result may appear resolved, persist, change form, become stale, or
    recur

Analyzers may recommend that an issue appears resolved.
They may not silently close Notes or Signals.
Later recurrence creates a linked candidate rather than automatically
reopening durable work.
Unchanged dismissed findings should not repeatedly nag the author.

## 7. Durable Analyzer Support State

Craft analyzers may own project-specific durable support state such as:

- enabled or disabled posture,
- analysis-scope defaults,
- sensitivity or threshold preferences,
- display and filter preferences,
- custom terms or exclusions,
- intentional-use markers,
- suppression rules,
- bounded run history,
- comparison state,
- evidence references and source anchors.

This is configuration and support state, not editorial truth.

`Feedback Notes` remain owned by
`Feedback Notes / Revision Resolution`.
`Signals` remain owned by `Signal Architecture`.
References to Notes or Signals do not imply analyzer ownership.

## 8. Exclusion Scopes

Bounded project-specific exclusions or intentional-use scope may exist
at:

- exact occurrence,
- selected passage or `Narrative Assertion`,
- character dialogue,
- phrase or term within the project,
- analyzer-specific project rule.

Application-wide defaults may remain a future option, but project
settings override them.
Every exclusion must preserve visible scope and remain reviewable and
removable.

## 9. Intentional-Use Markers

Intentional-use markers are durable analyzer support state.

Examples:

- deliberate refrain,
- motif,
- character speech habit,
- ritual phrase,
- incantation,
- intentional sensory restraint,
- genre convention,
- parody,
- deliberate pulp register.

Markers must:

- preserve analyzer and scope,
- retain source anchors where applicable,
- stop repetitive warnings within approved scope,
- remain inspectable and removable,
- become stale if source text changes materially.

They do not alter manuscript truth or automatically create Notes.

## 10. History

Craft analyzers use the established bounded editorial-history posture:

- roughly `30` recent completed runs per analyzer per project,
- roughly `180 days` for unpinned history,
- oldest unpinned history trimmed first,
- pinned or converted items preserved,
- trimming or expiry disclosed honestly.

Partial, interrupted, blocked, or protection-limited runs must remain
visibly incomplete.
This contract does not create a competing retention model.

## 11. Analyzer-Specific Boundaries

### Senses Usage

- evaluates sensory presence, balance, grounding, and range
- must not enforce mechanical sensory quotas
- must allow intentional restraint, POV limits, sparse style, genre
  restraint, and deliberate coldness

### Overused Words

- evaluates distracting repetition and repeated word patterns
- must distinguish accidental overuse from voice, refrain, motif,
  rhythm, dialogue habit, ritual language, and incantation

### Cliche Detection

- evaluates potentially stale phrasing or familiar patterns
- must distinguish weak default language from genre convention, voice,
  homage, parody, pulp register, and deliberate cliche

Detection never equals defect automatically.

## 12. Critique And Editorial Workflow

`Critique / Evaluation` remains an aggregator and interpreter.

It may:

- request or consume analyzer findings,
- combine them into broader evaluation,
- identify cross-analyzer patterns,
- explain contextual significance.

It must preserve:

- source analyzer,
- source evidence,
- uncertainty,
- temporary versus durable posture.

It may not:

- own analyzer configuration,
- silently retain findings,
- create Notes or Signals automatically,
- close analyzer-derived durable work,
- rewrite manuscript truth,
- hide which analyzer produced a result.

`Editorial Workflow` defines the journey but owns no durable analyzer
state.

## 13. Cross-Analyzer Patterns

Cross-analyzer patterns are calculated on request, normally during full
craft review.
They remain temporary by default.

Examples:

- several analyzers identifying the same passage,
- cliche findings clustered in one character's dialogue,
- motif candidates appearing as overuse findings,
- repetition concentrated in low-sensory sections.

A cross-analyzer pattern may be converted explicitly into a
`Feedback Note` or `Signal`.
There is no durable cross-analyzer truth class.

## 14. Surface Behavior

### Writing Surface

- quiet findings indicator,
- requested highlights,
- current-passage analysis when invoked,
- direct source explanation,
- easy dismissal and mark-intentional actions,
- no constant policing or manuscript gating.

### Command Center

- full Craft Analysis review,
- analyzer selection and filtering,
- grouped findings,
- source examples,
- history and comparison,
- rerun controls,
- exclusions and intentional-use review,
- cross-analyzer patterns,
- conversion to Notes or Signals.

Surfaces display and request actions.
They do not own analyzer state.

## 15. Companion

`Companion` may explain findings, show sources, and route to the
correct analyzer, Note, or Signal.
It may not silently revise, dismiss, suppress, or convert.

## 16. Execution And Protection

Silent lightweight local analysis is allowed only after explicit
project-level enablement and only when it is:

- local-only,
- free,
- non-destructive,
- advisory,
- within approved scope,
- compliant with protection and exclusion rules,
- not mutating manuscript, truth, Notes, or Signals.

Heavy full-project scans remain author-invoked.

AI-assisted analysis must obey:

- routing rules,
- package preview and outbound approval,
- spend controls,
- masking and exclusion rules,
- refusal and fallback behavior.

Local execution does not override protected, masked, or
excluded-content rules.
Unavailable routes, budget refusal, or analyzer failure must not block
writing.

## 17. Future Analyzer Boundary

This contract governs current and future bounded craft analyzers.

Future analyzers must either:

- conform to this family contract, or
- enter through explicit `Plugin / Rubric System` review.

This pass does not create new analyzers or a future analyzer catalog.

## 18. Remaining Jason Decisions

- whether the family should stay mostly invisible or gain a clearer
  writer-facing umbrella label
- what settings persist by default
- whether exclusions are only per-project or may later inherit from a
  broader preference layer
- how much run history should remain visible
- how visible default craft findings should be in `Writing Surface`
- when cross-analyzer patterns should appear
- whether intentional-use markers should be broadly reusable across
  analyzers or remain analyzer-local
- whether silent lightweight local analysis should default on or stay
  opt-in
- how aggressively recurrence should be surfaced

## 19. Acceptance Criteria

This contract is acceptable only if:

- it keeps current craft analyzers separate and visible,
- it does not create a new truth owner, workspace, or hidden monolith,
- analyzer findings remain temporary by default,
- analyzer-owned durable state stays configuration or support-only,
- note and signal ownership remain unchanged,
- protection and routing doctrine remain inherited rather than bypassed,
- future analyzers are gated by this contract or by `Plugin / Rubric`
  review.
