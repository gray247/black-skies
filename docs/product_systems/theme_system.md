## 2. Purpose

Define Theme System as an optional thematic planning and inspection
support tool that helps the author organize themes, motifs, thematic
development, and observed evidence without turning inferred meaning into
accepted project truth automatically.

This dossier inherits output vocabulary from
`shared_output_vocabulary_contract.md`, truth ownership limits from
`truth_and_state_ownership_matrix.md`, memory and provenance boundaries
from `memory_lab.md` and `provenance_state_model.md`, protection rules
from `protected_content_permission_matrix.md`, and AI conversion limits
from `ai_lifecycle_and_approval_matrix.md`.

## 3. User Problem Solved

The writer may want to track and inspect theme intentionally without
relying on vague memory or allowing AI inference to overclaim meaning.

## 4. What The System Does

- organize theme support around explicit project-level thematic intent
  that is owned elsewhere,
- store thematic threads, motifs, symbols, and planned thematic
  development as durable support state,
- link observed manuscript evidence, Story Units, Outline nodes,
  assertions, character material, and lore material,
- surface candidate and competing interpretations when clearly labeled,
- produce theme findings, observations, insights, recommendations, and
  candidates.

## 5. What The System Does Not Do

- own accepted project truth,
- own manuscript truth,
- own character or lore truth,
- grade the story automatically,
- canonize inferred theme claims silently,
- treat reader interpretation or AI interpretation as fact,
- own Memory Lab, Signals, Notes, or export.

## 6. User-Facing Behavior

Visible behavior should emphasize explicit thematic intent and saved
theme support first and advisory inference second.

## 7. Hidden/Background Behavior

Background thematic hints may exist later, but they remain advisory.
Inferred theme is not confirmed author intent.
Observed pattern is not the same thing as declared theme intent.

## 8. What Appears First

- project-level thematic intent references,
- active thematic threads and motifs,
- linked accepted material,
- small advisory indicators when useful.

## 9. What Is Summonable

- theme evidence,
- thematic thread detail,
- motif detail,
- candidate theme notes,
- competing interpretations,
- advisory analysis,
- structure links.

## 10. What Is Hidden Until Needed

- dense thematic critique,
- provenance-heavy history,
- deep AI explanation.

## 11. Inputs

- accepted project-level thematic intent from
  `Author Intent / Story Setup`,
- accepted assertions,
- manuscript evidence,
- author notes,
- planning links from `Story Unit` and `Outline`,
- advisory analysis.

## 12. Outputs

- theme views,
- theme-linked references,
- motif records and thematic-thread records,
- advisory theme suggestions,
- theme findings,
- observations,
- insights,
- recommendations,
- candidates.

Theme outputs are advisory unless explicitly saved as support state or
explicitly promoted through the correct owner path.

## 13. Which Other Systems Consume Those Outputs

- `Writing Surface`
- `Command Center Surface`
- `Outline`

## 14. What Gets Stored

- thematic threads,
- motifs,
- symbols,
- planned thematic development,
- observed manuscript evidence references,
- candidate interpretations,
- competing interpretations,
- author notes,
- links to `Story Unit`, `Outline`,
  `Narrative Insertion / Narrative Assertion`, `Character Cards`, and
  `Lore Cards`,
- comparison, filter, and view state,
- active, retired, accidental, stale, or superseded support posture.

Stored theme material remains support state unless explicitly promoted
into the accepted project-truth owner.

## 14A. Layer 1 - Author-Owned Thematic Intent

Theme System may reference accepted Story Theme intent owned by
`Author Intent / Story Setup`.

Possible concepts include:

- intended thematic question
- intended thematic claim
- creative thematic boundary
- intended tension or contradiction
- author-approved motif purpose

Theme System may display and organize this intent but does not own it.

## 14B. Layer 2 - Manuscript Evidence

Theme evidence comes from `Narrative Insertion / Narrative Assertion`.

Evidence may include:

- passages
- repeated situations
- character choices
- imagery
- motifs
- symbols
- contrasts
- consequences
- setup and payoff evidence

Evidence remains manuscript-owned.

## 14C. Layer 3 - Advisory Detection And Interpretation

Theme System may represent:

- detected themes
- candidate themes
- inferred thematic claims
- motif recurrence
- symbol interpretation
- thematic drift
- competing readings
- weak or contradictory evidence
- confidence and uncertainty

Detected themes remain advisory.

## 14D. Layer 4 - Display And Trend State

Theme System may store noncanonical presentation state such as:

- recurrence counts
- evidence clusters
- progression views
- strength trends
- filters
- visual grouping
- selected theme comparisons
- workspace-local display state

Display state does not establish accepted thematic intent.

## 14E. Identity And Lifecycle

Theme lifecycle states include:

- candidate
- accepted intent reference
- detected
- reviewed
- revised
- dismissed
- expired
- stale
- superseded
- archived
- restored
- detached
- converted through Author Intent

Accepting a detected theme routes through `Author Intent / Story Setup`.
Manuscript evidence remains linked rather than copied into a second
truth store.
Changes to Author Intent do not silently rewrite manuscript evidence.
Changes to prose may stale evidence links or interpretations.

## 14F. Contradiction And Uncertainty

Theme System distinguishes:

- change over time
- competing interpretations
- insufficient evidence
- stale evidence
- source-owner contradiction
- planning-versus-manuscript divergence
- intended-versus-detected effect
- deliberate ambiguity
- superseded support state

Contradiction detection remains advisory.
No automatic owner mutation is allowed.

## 14G. Notes, Signals, And Review Collections

Findings do not automatically become Notes or Signals.
Explicit conversion is required.
Review collections remain non-owning views.
Dismissal in a review view does not delete source truth.
Recurring concerns create linked candidates rather than automatically
reopening durable work.
Acceptance routes through `Author Intent`, `Outline`, or
`Narrative Insertion / Narrative Assertion` as appropriate.

## 14H. Protection And Visibility

Theme System must respect:

- hidden and protected content
- excluded content
- local-only material
- never-send restrictions
- masks and approved summaries
- author-known secrets
- character-known beliefs
- reader-visible information
- source-specific visibility restrictions

Graphs, summaries, previews, exports, Companion answers, and AI
packages must not leak restricted source information.

## 15. What Remains Temporary

- inferred theme suggestions,
- AI- or analyzer-inferred themes,
- transient analyses,
- unsaved notes.

## 16. Relationship To Author Intent / Story Setup

Accepted project-level thematic intent belongs to
`Author Intent / Story Setup`.
Theme System may display, organize, compare, and link that intent, but
it must not create a second accepted copy or silently revise it.

## 17. Relationship To Narrative Insertion / Assertion

Theme support may read accepted narrative truth as evidence, but it does
not replace it or create a second accepted truth copy.

## 18. Relationship To Story Units

Story Units may hold narrative-purpose planning that Theme System can
link and compare without taking ownership of Story Unit planning state.

## 19. Relationship To Prose / Scene Projection

Projection may show theme-linked material, prototype context, or
observed sequence without becoming theme authority.

## 20. Relationship To Writing Surface

Theme support may be summonable from writing, but inferred commentary
must stay quiet and must not crowd drafting by default.

## 21. Relationship To Command Center Surface

The Command Center may host broader theme evidence review, motif
comparison, competing-interpretation review, and prototype-aware
inspection.

## 22. GUI Placement Principles

Keep theme support optional and bounded.

## 23. Local LLM Role

Local AI may later assist with optional thematic analysis only.

## 24. Paid API Role

Paid theme analysis remains optional and approval-governed.

## 25. Model Routing Notes And Cost / Budget Impact

Any AI theme analysis must respect routing and spend rules.

## 26. Explicit-Content / Send-Package Handling, If Applicable

Theme support must respect masking and send-package rules.

## 27. Privacy / Safety / Censor Behavior, If Applicable

Protected material must not leak through theme summaries, motif labels,
or examples.
Hidden, deleted, masked, local-only, unrevealed, or AI-excluded
material must not leak through theme summaries, candidate views, or
prototype projections.

## 28. Testing Requirements

Prove advisory theme analysis does not become accepted truth silently
and that saved theme support does not silently mutate
`Author Intent / Story Setup`.

## 29. Governance Rules And Risks

- no hidden thematic canon,
- no fake certainty,
- no automatic grading,
- inferred theme is not confirmed author intent,
- repeated language, imagery, or symbols do not become declared theme
  intent without review,
- motifs are first-class support records, not automatic theme truth,
- emergent themes may remain useful support material without becoming
  accepted project-level intent,
- theme findings are advisory unless explicitly saved as support state
  or explicitly promoted through the accepted project-truth owner.

## 30. Failure Modes

If theme support fails, the writer still writes and organizes theme
manually.

## 31. v1 Boundary

Basic thematic support organization with bounded advisory cues,
first-class motifs, and explicit separation from accepted project-level
thematic intent.

## 32. v2 Boundary

Richer linkage, prototype-aware comparison, and optional thematic
analysis.

## 33. Future-Only Boundary

Deep automated theme scoring or prescriptive guidance.
