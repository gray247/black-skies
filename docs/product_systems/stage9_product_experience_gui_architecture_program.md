## Program Constitution

- The author is final authority.
- AI remains advisory until explicitly accepted.
- Analysis may be automatic; truth mutation is manual.
- Narrative Assertion is the foundational manuscript-truth object.
- Scenes and chapters are projections or containers, not truth owners.
- Writing Surface remains sovereign and quiet by default.
- Command Center remains non-sovereign and is not a universal inbox.
- Companion remains optional, contextual, and non-owning.
- Projections do not inherit source ownership.
- Models perform tasks; systems own workflows.
- Route approval, package approval, transmission approval, execution, result receipt, and acceptance remain distinct.
- Save, snapshot, history, backup, archive, export, import, inspection, restore, and verification remain distinct.
- No connector is admitted.
- No silent fallback, substitution, transmission, retry, or truth mutation is allowed.

## Core Stage 9 Principles

### Quiet primary action

Direct writing stays the primary action.
Support information may be available, but it must not make ordinary writing feel gated or dashboard-first.

### Progressive disclosure

Information should appear when it helps the author act, compare, recover, or approve.
Low-risk support can stay quiet or hidden by default.
Higher-risk actions and irreversible changes deserve stronger visibility.

### Owner-preserving projection

A projection may show, summarize, compare, or preview owner state.
It does not own the thing it shows.
Display never transfers ownership.

### Explicit decision boundaries

Stage 9 must make the following boundaries legible:

- information versus action request
- advisory result versus accepted truth
- warning versus failure versus degraded operation
- route approval versus package approval versus destination acceptance
- queue completion versus destination acceptance
- current versus stale versus historical state
- temporary candidate versus durable owner object
- system status versus manuscript status

### Reversible-before-destructive

Where a reversible path exists, Stage 9 should make it easier to inspect, compare, or confirm before destructive action.
That principle does not weaken owner approval rules.

### No-false-completion

A completed job, queued run, copied artifact, or restored preview is not automatically accepted truth or successful ownership transfer.

### Accessibility as architecture

Accessibility is not a cosmetic layer.
It is part of the program constitution: readable terminology, safe focus behavior, keyboard-first action paths, and dismissal that does not hide durable owner state.

## Approved Design Batches

### Batch 1 - Surface hierarchy and direct-writing posture

- Purpose: define the responsibility split between Writing Surface, Command Center, and Companion.
- Systems covered: Writing Surface, Command Center Surface, Companion, workflow spine, support surfaces.
- Required inputs: surface dossiers, current authority, completed Stage 8 burden findings.
- Expected outputs: bounded surface responsibilities, visible support roles, quiet-first writing posture, entry and re-entry principles.
- Decisions permitted: conceptual responsibility split, visibility posture, non-owner posture, direct-writing precedence.
- Decisions forbidden: layout, pane arrangement, component inventory, navigation model, implementation mechanics.
- Closure test: the three surfaces can be described without collapsing ownership or making Command Center mandatory.
- Authority sync after approval: yes, if later Stage 9 findings need the same surface roles.

### Batch 2 - Advisory review, approval paths, and AI routing burden

- Closure test: advisory review can be described without making AI or Command Center authoritative.
- Authority sync after approval: yes, if experience wording needs to track the burden register.

## Genuine Author-Decision Register

These are Stage 9 decisions because they concern presentation burden rather than ownership:

- how dense provenance and currentness disclosure should be by default
- how much stale and partial-result detail should appear before the author asks for more
- how often repeated warnings should be grouped
- how visible Companion should be in ordinary writing versus re-entry
- how much comparison detail should appear for partial acceptance and recovery
- which terminology should be plain language versus support terminology
- how much route, cost, and approval detail should appear before an explicit action
- how the author re-enters writing after review or recovery without losing context

## Stage 10 Boundary

Stage 10 owns operational behavior, not Stage 9 burden framing:

- reliability and retry behavior
- recovery verification
- stale and partial behavior under stress
- provider and queue robustness
- offline behavior
- security, privacy, performance, and conflict testing
- exact package and route implementation
- model qualification, retirement, and migration reliability
