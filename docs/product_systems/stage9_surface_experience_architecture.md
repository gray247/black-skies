# Stage 9 Surface Experience Architecture

Status: active, read-only planning pass complete, implementation blocked.

## Scope

This file defines conceptual responsibilities for the primary Stage 9 surfaces.
It does not choose geometry, component layout, interaction mechanics, or implementation details.

## Shared Cross-Surface Principles

- Display does not transfer ownership.
- Projections stay owner-preserving.
- The author remains final authority.
- AI remains advisory until explicitly accepted.
- Truth mutation remains manual.
- Narrative Assertion remains foundational manuscript truth.
- Writing Surface and Command Center remain separate.
- Companion remains optional and non-owning.
- No connector is admitted.

## Writing Surface

### Responsibilities

- Support sovereign manuscript work.
- Stay quiet by default.
- Allow direct writing without AI.
- Preserve current location so the author can return to the same writing context after review or recovery.
- Keep save-confidence visible enough to avoid false certainty.
- Show support material only when it helps the author continue writing or safely inspect context.
- Support a safe return from review, recovery, or bounded support work.

### Non-responsibilities

- It does not become a dashboard.
- It does not require Command Center before writing starts.
- It does not own Notes, Signals, critique, continuity, recovery, or interchange.
- It does not turn projections or advisory text into truth by display alone.

### Experience Obligations

- direct writing without AI or dashboard use
- quiet default posture
- optional support visibility
- current-location awareness
- save-confidence awareness
- safe return to writing after review or recovery

## Command Center

### Responsibilities

- Surface review, routing, blockers, provenance, cost, and approval state.
- Help the author inspect prepared findings and support state.
- Remain non-sovereign.
- Preserve owner context when routing work to another system.

### Non-responsibilities

- It is not a universal inbox.
- It is not a mandatory gateway to writing.
- It does not own manuscript truth or accepted planning truth.
- It does not silently resolve or mutate routed objects.

### Experience Obligations

- blocker visibility
- provenance visibility
- cost and approval visibility
- routing support
- owner-preserving projections

## Companion

### Responsibilities

- Offer optional and contextual assistance.
- Stay quiet when unnecessary.
- Provide advisory summaries and route suggestions.
- Show owner and source visibility.
- Keep uncertainty and currentness visible.
- Remain dismissible.

### Non-responsibilities

- It does not choose the author's task.
- It does not own workflow, truth, memory, Note, Signal, job, or history.
- It does not become the product narrator.
- It does not silently resolve conflicts.

### Experience Obligations

- bounded re-entry help
- advisory summaries
- source and owner visibility
- uncertainty and currentness visibility
- dismissibility
- quiet default behavior

## Entry And Re-entry

Stage 9 must preserve a clean transition between writing and review.

- Entry into support work should not erase the prior writing context.
- Re-entry to writing should restore the author's orientation, not replace it with a new owner.
- Surface movement must show where the author is going and what state is being projected.
- Support navigation should not force the author through a universal inbox or a hidden gate.

## Mutation Boundaries

- Advisory output is not accepted truth.
- Destination acceptance remains owner-controlled.
- Queue completion is not acceptance.
- Recovered or imported material does not become truth by display alone.
- A preview remains a preview until the owning system accepts it.

## Focus And Keyboard Principles

- Keyboard-first action paths should remain possible.
- Focus should preserve the author's current task and return path.
- Dismissal should not destroy durable owner state.
- Important warnings and blocking decisions should remain operable without mouse-only interaction.

## Multi-Screen Principles

- Multi-screen use must preserve ownership clarity.
- A second surface may expose support state without becoming the new owner.
- Cross-screen movement must preserve the current writing position and the route back to it.
- Additional displays may summarize or compare, but they do not redefine truth.

## Cross-Surface Requirements

- entry and re-entry must preserve the author's place in the work
- movement between writing and review must preserve the current writing context
- projection boundaries must remain visible
- source-owner visibility must remain available
- mutation-boundary visibility must remain available
- focus preservation must survive support-surface handoffs
- keyboard-first access must remain possible
- multi-screen use must not create a second truth owner
- exact layout decisions remain out of scope

## Surface-Boundary Summary

### Writing Surface

Manuscript work, quiet direct entry, save confidence, and safe return path.

### Command Center

Review, routing, blockers, provenance, cost, and approval visibility.

### Companion

Optional advisory summaries, contextual help, source visibility, and dismissible re-entry support.

## Deferred Detail

This file intentionally does not define:

- exact layouts
- exact panel arrangements
- exact terminology lock
- exact visibility thresholds
- exact warning timing
- exact keyboard sequence
- exact multi-screen composition

Those details remain for later Stage 9 batches and, where operational behavior is involved, Stage 10.

## Boundary Reminder

Stage 9 experience architecture is about conceptual responsibility, not implementation.
It must keep the author oriented without turning the Command Center into a universal inbox or the Companion into the product narrator.
