## 1. The Finding In Plain Language

The current screen works like a list of separate files beside a separate
planning form. A writer needs one readable story with a lightweight way to
navigate and shape it.

The author observed all of the following in the exact package-qualified
candidate:

- selecting a manuscript unit replaces the visible page, making it feel as
  though the next portion of the story may not exist;
- manuscript units and outline items look and behave as unrelated systems;
- a `+` has unclear competing meanings, while ordinary unit creation appears
  as an input field and button;
- visible rename, update, move, and delete forms make the story rail feel
  like a database editor;
- ordinary writer actions are buried among advanced structural choices;
- the open left rail can cover content instead of creating a cleanly bounded
  working area;
- Focus mode removes rails but leaves too much product chrome and empty space;
- the current blue/purple treatment reads as generic generated-dashboard
  styling instead of the restrained literary product direction; and
- the Companion correctly opens Command Center but the result experience is
  not yet a useful, self-explanatory conversation.

These are product-composition findings. They are not permission to weaken
manuscript ownership, accepted-order protection, project isolation, explicit
AI consent, or the Command Center's optional role.

## 2. Repair Objective

Build toward this simple rule:

> **One story stream. One unified story rail. One true Focus mode. One quiet
> Companion doorway.**

The normal writer path must be understandable without knowing the terms
`unit`, `fragment`, `gap`, `container`, `planned`, `inferred`, or
`manuscriptUnitId`.

The implementation must make a full story read like a continuous manuscript.
The story rail is a navigation and structure lens over that manuscript, not a
second collection of files or a required planning ritual.

## 4. Interaction Contract

### 4.1 Continuous Manuscript

- The center canvas shows one story stream. A reader can scroll from the end
  of one chapter or scene into the next without selecting another file.
- A story segment is a navigable position in that stream, not evidence that
  the text lives in a separate document.
- Clicking an item in the story rail scrolls to and briefly highlights its
  linked section. Selecting a linked section updates the rail context.
- The product may keep safe internal segmentation during migration, but the
  writer must not be asked to manage it as separate files.
- A continuous manuscript with no accepted divisions remains valid.

#### Pre-closure navigation correction

The manuscript stream and the Writing Studio shell must not share one
unbounded page scroll. The writing canvas owns the long-document vertical
scroll; the opened Story, Review, Project, and Writing Session rails remain
available within the application viewport. Selecting a Unit scrolls the
writing canvas to that Unit while leaving the rail position and the rest of
the shell stable. This is a usability requirement for substantial manuscripts,
not an optional polish item.

### 4.2 Unified Story Rail

- The left rail is one lightweight `Story` rail. It contains chapters, scenes,
  unplaced ideas, and later ghost proposals in one coherent hierarchy.
- The normal `+` means **Add to story here**. It creates one direct, plainly
  named item at the current cursor/selection when available, otherwise an
  unplaced story idea. It does not compete with a separate permanent
  `Create unit` form.
- A newly added item is named inline. Double-clicking an existing name edits
  it inline.
- Dragging changes planned story placement. The normal rail has no `Move up`
  or `Move down` buttons.
- A right-click or compact `More` menu owns uncommon actions: delete, change
  placement, convert structural meaning, inspect source/provenance, split, or
  merge. Destructive actions require their existing appropriate confirmation.
- The rail uses writer language. The primary spine item is a **Unit**. A
  **Note** is a lightweight planning thought that hangs from a Unit or may be
  explicitly left unlinked. Notes are not peer chapters, scenes, or manuscript
  files. Internal kinds and source states appear only when an advanced
  decision actually needs them.
- Units remain visually dominant and form one continuous stacked spine. Notes
  appear as small markers or quiet subordinate rows beneath their Unit (or in
  a separate unlinked-note group); they must not expand the rail into a second
  stack of equally weighted cards.
- A Note has a title and an author-entered body. Creating a Note on a selected
  Unit links it there by default; the author may choose `Unlinked note` in
  advanced options. A cancelled Note draft creates no durable item.
- A `+` used for a note, a story item, or another action must carry a visible
  nearby label or precise tooltip. The same glyph must never silently mean
  several different things in the same surface.
- The `+` chooser has an explicit Cancel action, closes on Escape or an
  outside click, and never requires creating then deleting an item merely to
  dismiss it.

### 4.3 Rail Geometry

- A rail opens as a real layout region that resizes the canvas; it never
  overlays the controls or text beneath it.
- One rail family is open at a time by default. Its close control is in the
  rail header, and its collapsed edge remains a small, non-obstructive target.
- The rail is resizable within accessible limits. Its last safe width may be
  remembered per local workspace only after a separate persistence decision.
- The story rail cannot horizontally scroll merely because a title or advanced
  panel is long. Labels wrap or truncate with accessible full text.
- Rail content is independently scrollable when its own contents exceed the
  viewport. Opening a rail must not move the writer into a separate page
  position or hide the manuscript behind an overlay.
- The Writing Session rail is a compact utility surface for active-unit
  identity and save state. It is not a second manuscript editor and must stay
  reachable without scrolling to the end of the story. The Companion bar must
  reserve space rather than cover it.
- The top Project rail and right Review rail follow the same viewport rule:
  their access and close controls remain usable while the manuscript canvas
  scrolls.
- The shell must not place toast notices, edge controls, or rail headers on top
  of the manuscript title or first readable lines. Layering and reserved
  spacing are part of the acceptance contract.

### 4.3.1 Plain-language help and comparison

- Unfamiliar rail vocabulary and the purpose of `Compare the story plan with
  the manuscript` are explained through one summonable `?` help affordance or
  an equivalent bottom help entry, not repeated permanent instructional
  paragraphs.
- The comparison is a preview-only alignment view: it shows the current
  planning order beside manuscript order and does not move, rewrite, or
  accept prose. Its label, explanatory text, open state, and controls must
  meet the shared light/dark contrast contract.

### 4.4 Real Focus Mode

- Focus mode leaves only the prose, a quiet project/location marker, a subtle
  save state, and one obvious exit affordance.
- It removes top-level surface switching, unit administration, empty gutters,
  and all supporting rails. It must not retain a dashboard-shaped header.
- Escape behavior and keyboard access remain explicit and accessible.

### 4.5 Visual Direction And Theme Choice

- The default is restrained near-black: warm or neutral black surfaces,
  quiet off-white manuscript text, and a very limited accent reserved for
  current position, focus, warning, and explicit selection.
- Do not use saturated blue/purple panels, gradients, glowing borders,
  decorative metrics, or generic dashboard-card treatment as the default
  visual language.
- Add one visible, user-controlled **Dark / Light** theme switch. `Dark` is
  the initial default; `Light` is a real reading and writing alternative, not
  an inverted debug palette.
- Both themes must use the same semantic tokens and meet contrast,
  focus-visible, reduced-motion, high-zoom, and color-independent state
  requirements. Theme preference is local UI preference, never project truth.

### 4.6 Companion Bar And Command Handoff

- The Companion is a quiet one-line bar at the bottom by default. It clearly
  says what information it can use before submission.
- For currently supported local orientation questions, submitting a request
  opens Command Center **with the answer already visible**, its source scope,
  limitation, and a return-to-writing action.
- Unsupported questions receive an immediate, plain explanation of what the
  Companion can do today and what it cannot do. Command Center must never
  appear blank or leave the writer guessing whether anything happened.
- The Companion entry is not a permanent chat column and does not receive
  durable conversation history by default.

## 5. Local LLM Boundary

The present Companion is deliberately deterministic local orientation. It can
truthfully report project and current-writing facts, but it cannot yet answer
open-ended writing questions. That limitation is a product boundary, not a
temporary copy problem.

The local-LLM capability must be planned and authorized separately before it
is represented as complete. Its later plan must cover at least:

- local model installation/discovery and explicit availability states;
- device requirements, offline/degraded behavior, cancellation, and bounded
  foreground or later job lifecycle;
- model and prompt routing; author-controlled context selection; no silent
  whole-project transmission or hidden memory;
- source/provenance, advisory labeling, non-mutation, and return anchors;
- packaging, update, privacy, diagnostics, deterministic fixtures, and
  meaningful local-model evaluation; and
- the relationship between local-only and any future paid/provider route.

Human Gate 2 may accept the Companion **doorway and local-facts workflow**.
It may not claim that the full conversational Companion is complete. A later
local-intelligence human gate must judge actual usefulness once the local model
exists.
