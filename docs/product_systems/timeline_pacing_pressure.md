## 2. Purpose

Define timeline, pacing, and pressure support as advisory analysis of chronology, narrative tempo, and structural pressure without turning analysis into accepted truth automatically.

## 3. User Problem Solved

The writer needs help seeing time, pacing, and pressure problems without letting analytical projections own the story.

## 4. What The System Does

- inspect chronology and telling order,
- surface pacing or pressure cues,
- provide advisory timeline support.

## 5. What The System Does Not Do

- force chronological order,
- rewrite structure silently,
- canonize inferred timing facts automatically.

## 6. User-Facing Behavior

Visible behavior should emphasize cues, comparisons, and optional analysis.

## 7. Hidden/Background Behavior

Background analysis may generate signals or suggestions, but remains advisory.

## 8. What Appears First

- small pacing or timing cues,
- current pressure signals,
- structural comparisons when requested.

## 9. What Is Summonable

- deeper timeline detail,
- chronology versus telling-order views,
- evidence links.

## 10. What Is Hidden Until Needed

- dense analytics,
- full evidence trails,
- speculative timing inferences.

## 11. Inputs

- accepted assertions,
- accepted continuity facts,
- outline order,
- manuscript evidence.

## 12. Outputs

- advisory timeline views,
- pacing signals,
- pressure summaries.

## 13. Which Other Systems Consume Those Outputs

- `Outline`

## 14. What Gets Stored

- accepted timeline references when author-owned,

- signal references.

## 15. What Remains Temporary

- inferred pacing summaries,
- transient comparisons,
- current-session analyses.

## 16. Relationship To Narrative Insertion / Assertion

## 17. Relationship To Story Units
