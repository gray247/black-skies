## 2. Purpose

Define Timeline / Pacing / Pressure as one support system with three
explicit modules that help the writer inspect chronology, narrative
tempo, and bounded pressure development without turning analysis into
accepted truth automatically.

## 3. User Problem Solved

The writer needs help seeing time, pacing, and pressure patterns
without letting analytical projections own the story.

## 4. Overall Authority

Timeline / Pacing / Pressure is a support system, not a truth owner.

It must not own:

- manuscript content,
- authoritative manuscript order,
- accepted chronology truth,
- Character or Lore truth,
- project-level creative intent,
- Outline planning structure,
- Story Unit purpose,
- Continuity truth,
- emotional truth.

Direct writing remains valid without timeline, pacing, or pressure
records.

## 5. Module Split

### Timeline

Timeline compares chronology, manuscript order, reveal order, and
prototype order while displaying accepted time facts from their true
owners.

### Pacing

Pacing compares planned pacing targets and manuscript-observed pacing
without turning pacing estimates into project intent or manuscript
truth.

### Pressure

Pressure projects urgency, consequence, constraint, and conflict
pressure as bounded support analysis without collapsing all tension into
one authoritative score.

## 6. What The System Does

- compare manuscript order, occurrence order, reveal order, and
  prototype order,
- project chronology views, simultaneity, duration displays, and
  uncertainty overlays,
- compare planned pacing targets against manuscript-observed pacing,
- project bounded pressure development from accepted facts and planning
  context,
- surface temporary findings when timing, pacing, or pressure review is
  relevant,
- retain durable support state only where the author chooses to save
  comparisons, references, filters, or presentation context.

## 7. What The System Does Not Do

- force chronological order,
- create duplicate accepted chronology records,
- rewrite manuscript structure silently,
- rewrite pacing intent silently,
- turn analyzer curves or scores into durable state by default,
- replace `Continuity`, `Emotion Graph`, `Outline`, `Story Unit`,
  `Character Cards`, or `Lore Cards` as their owner,
- canonize inferred dates, durations, or pressure claims automatically.

## 8. User-Facing Behavior

Visible behavior should emphasize:

- manuscript-observed pacing first,
- clear order-basis comparisons,
- bounded support overlays,
- optional deeper inspection,
- review opportunities instead of automatic defects.

## 9. Hidden/Background Behavior

Background analysis may generate comparisons, stale-state cues, and
temporary findings, but it remains advisory.

Generated pacing and pressure curves, estimates, or scores remain
temporary by default.

## 17A. Event Model

Timeline represents events as support records, not as a second truth
store.

An event may store:

- event identity
- event subject
- story-world chronology
- manuscript order
- planning chronology
- projected scene or chapter placement
- absolute or relative time
- uncertain dates
- duration
- overlap
- simultaneity
- causal order
- historical status
- future planned status
- source anchors
- accepted-fact versus proposed-event state
- contradiction and uncertainty posture
- hidden or secret chronology
- character-known versus author-known timing
- stale state after manuscript or card changes
- archive, supersession, and deletion posture

Accepted event facts route to the owning truth system for the domain:

- character event facts -> `Character Cards`
- lore or world event facts -> `Lore Cards`
- manuscript occurrence or timing facts -> `Narrative Insertion / Narrative Assertion`
- project-level structural intent -> `Author Intent / Story Setup` or
  `Outline`, depending on whether the claim is intent or structure
- planning-only future events -> `Outline`

Timeline may reference and compare those facts, but it does not become
the universal owner.

## 17B. Chronology Distinctions

Timeline must distinguish:

- story-world chronology
- authoritative manuscript order
- Outline planning order
- projected scene or chapter organization
- event sequence
- reader revelation order

These are related but not identical.
Chronology must not be collapsed into manuscript order, Outline order,
or projection order.

## 17C. Pacing Model

Pacing is interpretive analysis over manuscript and structural evidence.

Pacing may represent:

- unit of analysis
- source range
- manuscript position
- scene or chapter projection
- Story Unit or Outline context
- pace estimate
- transition or change
- density
- delay
- repetition
- compression
- expansion
- reader-experience interpretation
- author-intended pacing
- detected pacing
- uncertainty and evidence
- historical analysis runs
- stale findings
- comparison between manuscript versions

Pacing analysis remains advisory.
Accepted pacing intent, where durable, routes to `Author Intent /
Story Setup` for project-level intent or `Outline` for planning
structure.
Detected pacing does not become canonical pacing by being stored,
compared, or shown.

## 17D. Pressure Model

Pressure is distinct from pacing and emotion.

Pressure may represent:

- narrative pressure
- tension
- urgency
- stakes
- unresolved demand
- threat
- deadline
- emotional pressure
- reader pressure versus character pressure
- intended versus detected pressure
- source evidence
- intensity
- timeframe
- trajectory
- uncertainty
- stale findings
- relationship to `Emotion Graph`, `Theme System`, `Outline`, and
  `Author Intent / Story Setup`

Pressure remains bounded support analysis. It does not own emotional
truth or project truth.
Accepted emotional facts remain with `Character Cards` or the
appropriate existing owner. Accepted project-level pressure intent
routes to `Author Intent / Story Setup`.

Timeline / Pacing / Pressure may represent:

- current chronological state
- prior chronological state
- proposed future state
- bounded-period state
- historical events
- hidden or secret chronology
- character-known versus author-known timing
- historical snapshots
- candidate support state
- planned support state
- detected support state
- reviewed support state
- accepted-through-owner reference state
- dismissed support state
- expired support state
- stale support state
- superseded support state
- partially fulfilled support state
- abandoned support state
- archived support state
- restored support state
- detached support state
- deleted support state

A changed chronology or timing state is not automatically a contradiction.
Deleting support state must not delete source truth.
Stale state must be exposed rather than silently reconstructed as fresh

## 17F. Shared Support State

Timeline may store:

- chronology views
- pacing comparisons
- pressure comparisons
- order-basis comparisons
- evidence references
- selected story periods
- simultaneity groupings
- duration displays
- uncertainty and dispute overlays
- prototype-specific projection state
- saved filters and presentation state

These are support records, not accepted truth.
Historical pacing or pressure comparison views are bounded, labeled,

Findings do not automatically become Notes or Signals. Review

## 18. Timeline Module

Timeline owns durable support state only.

It may represent:

- story chronology,
- manuscript order,
- planned order,
- reveal order,
- relative time,
- fixed dates,
- durations,
- ages by story period,
- flashback placement,
- parallel or simultaneous events,
- gaps between events,
- event dependencies,
- uncertainty and disputed chronology.

Timeline references and projects accepted facts from their owners.
It does not create duplicate accepted chronology records.

## 19. Structured Dates And Durations

Fixed dates and relative durations may be first-class structured facts
when explicitly accepted by the author.

Examples:

- `October 4`
- `three days later`
- `before sunrise`
- `seventeen years earlier`
- `simultaneous with another event`

The accepted fact belongs to the appropriate truth owner:

- manuscript event or timing facts: `Narrative Assertions`
- character age, condition, location, role, or membership by story
  period: `Character Cards`
- world history, rule timing, location history, or institutional timing:
  `Lore Cards`

An inferred date or duration remains temporary until explicitly
accepted through the owning system.

## 20. Pacing Module

Pacing intent belongs to existing owners by scope:

- project-level pacing intent: `Author Intent / Story Setup`
- structural pacing intent: `Outline`
- Story Unit-level pacing purpose or target: `Story Unit`
- manuscript-observed pacing: derived from assertions, manuscript order,
  and projection context

Keep distinct:

- planned pacing target,
- manuscript-observed pacing,
- reader-experience estimate,
- advisory AI or analyzer interpretation.

Observed manuscript pacing should be the primary default.
Planned pacing may appear as a labeled overlay.
Differences are review opportunities, not automatic defects.

## 21. Pressure Module

Pressure remains a distinct support module limited to:

- urgency pressure,
- consequence pressure,
- constraint pressure,
- conflict pressure.

Pressure must not own or replace:

- emotional truth,
- character emotional state,
- project-level stakes intent,
- Continuity findings,
- reader anticipation from setup/payoff,
- a universal tension score.

Pressure projects how accepted facts and planning combine to narrow
choices, increase consequences, create opposition, or force action.

## 22. Uncertain And Disputed Chronology

Support intentional chronology states such as:

- uncertain,
- approximate,
- disputed,
- remembered differently,
- unknown to characters,
- unrevealed to readers,
- deliberately contradictory testimony.

Accepted uncertainty belongs with the appropriate truth owner.
Timeline displays the uncertainty, source, and evidence posture.
Accepted uncertainty or competing accounts must not be treated as an
automatic error merely because exact dates are unavailable.

## 23. Inference Persistence

Analyzer-generated pacing and pressure results remain temporary by
default.

They may participate in bounded analysis history under existing
editorial-history rules.

They become durable only through explicit author action, such as:

- save a planning target,
- save a comparison,
- create a `Feedback Note`,
- elevate to `Signal`,
- mark intentional,
- dismiss,
- revise prose,
- revise planning.

Do not automatically preserve every generated curve, estimate, or score.

## 24. Relationship To Narrative Insertion / Assertion

Accepted time facts and manuscript-observed pacing evidence come from
accepted assertions and accepted manuscript order.

Timeline / Pacing / Pressure may compare and display those facts, but
must not replace `Narrative Insertion / Narrative Assertion` as the
authority for them.

## 25. Relationship To Story Units

Story Units may hold pacing purpose or target and grouped work context.

Timeline / Pacing / Pressure may reference Story Unit planning context,
but it does not own Story Unit purpose.

## 26. Relationship To Outline

Outline owns planning structure, planned order, flashback placement, and
prototype arrangements.

Timeline / Pacing / Pressure may project, compare, and validate against
that planning state without replacing it.

## 27. Relationship To Prose / Scene Projection

Projection may supply display order, prototype comparison context, and
navigation context without becoming authority for chronology or pacing
truth.

## 28. Relationship To Character And Lore Cards

Time-bounded character facts and world-history facts remain with their
existing card owners.

Timeline may compare those facts against assertions or projections, but
it does not own accepted character or lore timing truth.

## 29. Relationship To Emotion Graph

Pressure and emotion remain distinct.

`Emotion Graph` handles emotional movement and accepted emotional
references through its existing owners.
Timeline / Pacing / Pressure handles chronology, pacing comparison, and
bounded urgency/consequence/constraint/conflict pressure.

Disagreement between pressure projections and emotional movement is a
review opportunity, not an automatic truth conflict.

## 30. Relationship To Continuity

Continuity may identify factual timing conflicts such as impossible
travel, contradiction, or unsupported chronology.

Timeline / Pacing / Pressure provides narrower evidence, projections,
and support comparisons for those reviews.

Neither system may silently repair truth.

## 31. Relationship To Writing Surface

The Writing Surface should use quiet indicators and summonable context.
It must not become a full timeline, pacing, or pressure workspace.

## 32. Relationship To Command Center Surface

The Command Center may host full comparison, filtering, history,
prototype comparison, stale-state review, and broader analysis
workflows.

- no timeline shadow canon,

## 41. Prototype Arrangements

Prototype arrangements receive separate labeled Timeline, Pacing, and
Pressure projections.

A prototype may alter:

- reveal order,
- chronology-versus-telling-order comparison,
- event spacing,
- flashback placement,
- observed pacing,
- projected pressure development,
- support evidence order.

Prototype projections do not change:

- accepted time facts,
- manuscript truth,
- authoritative manuscript order,
- Outline truth outside the prototype,
- Character or Lore truth,
- accepted pacing intent.

Discarding a prototype removes only its projected support context.

## 42. Staleness And Recalculation

Changes to manuscript, planning, or accepted facts may make timeline,
pacing, or pressure support state stale.

Surface stale state honestly.

Recalculation may update derived analysis but must not silently:

- repair chronology truth,
- alter manuscript order,
- rewrite pacing intent,
- change Character or Lore facts,
- create or close Notes or Signals.

## 43. Findings And Editorial Flow

Relevant analysis may create temporary findings such as:

- impossible chronology,
- unsupported date,
- duration conflict,
- location conflict by time,
- stale timeline reference,
- planned-versus-observed pacing mismatch,
- pressure collapse,
- prototype reveal-order problem.

Findings begin temporary.

The author may:

- dismiss,
- mark intentional,
- save as `Feedback Note`,
- elevate to `Signal`,
- revise prose,
- revise planning,
- update an accepted time fact.

Do not automatically create durable Notes or Signals.

## 44. Failure Modes

If analysis fails, the writer still works from prose, assertions, and
planning manually.

## 45. v1 Boundary

Basic advisory chronology, pacing, and pressure comparisons with saved
support state, prototype-aware projections, and bounded findings.

First release includes:

- event references,
- chronology comparison,
- chronology conflict identification,
- observed pacing movement,
- bounded pacing observations,
- bounded pressure flags,
- planned-versus-observed distinction,
- supporting source references,
- uncertainty or incomplete-evidence visibility.

Timeline remains support-only and must not become the accepted
event-truth owner. Accepted event truth remains with `Narrative
Insertion / Narrative Assertion` and relevant domain owners. Timeline may
reference accepted events but does not own them.

Chronology comparison, pacing analysis, and pressure analysis are
advisory. A slow section is not automatically defective. Pressure is not
one universal score. Absence of pressure is not automatically a flaw.
Chronology uncertainty must not be hidden.

First release should not require:

- universal event taxonomy,
- universal pacing score,
- universal pressure scale,
- automatic story restructuring,
- automatic canonization of inferred events,
- exhaustive historical simulation,
- a mandatory timeline for every project.

## 46. v2 Boundary

Richer chronology uncertainty handling, deeper pacing comparison, and
stronger pressure support.

Mature ceiling may later include:

- deeper chronology uncertainty handling,
- richer pacing comparison,
- richer pressure analysis,
- planned-versus-observed history,
- relationship or emotion overlays,
- alternative-order comparison,
- source disagreement analysis.

Later expansion requires repeated author demand, Stage 5 external
challenge findings, Stage 6 workflow proof, or demonstrated need that
cannot be met through existing bounded chronology, pacing, or pressure
support.

## 47. Future-Only Boundary

Deep automated chronology reasoning, repair suggestions, or universal
tension scoring.

Explicit exclusions:

- no accepted event-truth ownership,
- no automatic manuscript reorder,
- no universal quality score,
- no requirement that every narrative use conventional pacing,
- no replacement for `Outline`, `Continuity`, `Emotion Graph`, or
  `Narrative Insertion / Narrative Assertion`,
- mature-ceiling features do not automatically enter first release.

Stage 6 workflow-proof needs remain open for reorder preview and
application, chronology conflict handling, planned-versus-observed
comparison, and owner-routed acceptance of changes derived from Timeline
analysis. These workflows are not proven here.
