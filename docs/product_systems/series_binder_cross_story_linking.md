## 2. Purpose

Define a multi-story organization and linkage system that helps the author manage cross-story relationships without creating a shadow canon above author-owned truth.

## 3. User Problem Solved

The writer needs a way to relate books, episodes, or story projects without scattering continuity or lore across disconnected places.

## 4. What The System Does

- organize multi-story collections,
- expose cross-story links,
- support navigation and comparison.

## 5. What The System Does Not Do

- auto-canonize cross-story links,
- replace accepted lore or assertions,
- force series structure on standalone work.

## 6. User-Facing Behavior

Visible behavior should emphasize optional organization and clear source boundaries.

## 7. Hidden/Background Behavior

Background link suggestions may exist later, but remain advisory.

## 8. What Appears First

- project-to-project links,
- accepted high-value series facts,
- current project context.

## 9. What Is Summonable

- deeper series history,
- candidate links,
- continuity evidence,
- support detail.

## 10. What Is Hidden Until Needed

- advisory cross-story inferences,
- archive-heavy comparisons,
- dense evidence trails.

## 11. Inputs

- project metadata,
- accepted assertions,
- accepted lore,
- accepted character facts,
- continuity support.

## 12. Outputs

- series organization views,
- cross-story link views,
- reference paths into supporting systems.

## 13. Which Other Systems Consume Those Outputs

- `Writing Surface`
- `Command Center Surface`
- `Outline`

## 14. What Gets Stored

- collection metadata,
- accepted cross-story references,
- candidate link references,
- visibility state.

## 15. What Remains Temporary

- transient suggestions,
- temporary comparison views,
- unsaved linkage candidates.

## 16. Relationship To Narrative Insertion / Assertion

Cross-story links may reference accepted narrative truth but do not replace it.

## 17. Relationship To Story Units

Story Units may be grouped across projects only as optional support context.

## 18. Relationship To Prose / Scene Projection

Projection may support comparison without becoming authority.

## 19. Relationship To Writing Surface

Cross-story support may help writing, but direct writing remains primary.

## 20. Relationship To Command Center Surface

Heavier cross-story review and cleanup workflows may live in the Command Center.

## 21. GUI Placement Principles

Keep series support optional and avoid burdening single-project writing.

## 22. Local LLM Role

Local AI may later assist with optional linkage suggestions only.

## 23. Paid API Role

Paid help remains optional and approval-governed.

## 24. Model Routing Notes And Cost / Budget Impact

Any AI-assisted cross-story analysis must respect routing, privacy, and spend rules.

## 25. Explicit-Content / Send-Package Handling, If Applicable

Cross-story support must respect package and masking rules.

## 26. Privacy / Safety / Censor Behavior, If Applicable

Protected material must not leak across story boundaries without permission.

## 27. Testing Requirements

Prove cross-story links remain source-bound and non-authoritative.

## 28. Governance Rules And Risks

- no series-level shadow canon,
- no hidden truth promotion,
- no protected-material leakage.

## 29. Failure Modes

If cross-story support fails, individual projects still work independently.

## 30. v1 Boundary

Basic series grouping and accepted-link support.

## 31. v2 Boundary

Richer optional comparisons and multi-project support.

## 32. Future-Only Boundary

Deep automated franchise-scale analysis.
