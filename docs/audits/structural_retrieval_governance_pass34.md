## Structural Retrieval Governance Model

This model is provisional only. It is not retrieval authorization, not topology authorization, not graph authorization, not persistence authorization, not structural canon, and not command/search implementation.

Structural retrieval pressure means a surface, query, result, grouping, traversal, relationship, hierarchy, recomputed structure, stale artifact, advisory result, or continuity-bearing object appears retrievable in a way that implies legitimacy, identity, source-of-truth authority, persistence, or commandability.

Core distinctions:
- retrieval is not persistence authorization
- grouped retrieval is not object identity
- traversal is not durable relationship authority
- contextual retrieval is not canonical retrieval
- advisory retrieval is not authoritative retrieval
- stale retrieval is not current authority
- recomputed retrieval is not durable identity
- retrieval visibility is not command/search implementation

Containment goal:
- preserve retrieval pressure as an analysis target while preventing it from becoming structural retrieval systems, Story Unit persistence, graph identity, topology architecture, or structural command/search authority.

## Executive Findings

- Retrieval visibility creates legitimacy pressure even when retrieval is read-only or hypothetical.
- Repeated retrieval can make recomputed, advisory, stale, or continuity-bearing structures feel stable and authoritative.
- Grouped retrieval is the strongest retrieval-to-identity risk because it can make retrieved sets feel like object families.
- Traversal and retrieval must remain separated: moving through relationships is not the same as retrieving authoritative structural objects.
- Structural retrieval cannot stabilize while source-of-truth vocabulary remains unstable.
- Retrieval over hierarchy-bearing or relationship-bearing structures can imply graph identity and topology architecture.
- Command/search structural inheritance remains deferred because searchable or retrievable structures can quickly become commandable.

## Retrieval Legitimacy Findings

### Repeated Retrieval

- Repeated retrieval creates familiarity and apparent stability.
- A recomputed item retrieved repeatedly can look durable even if no identity is stored.
- Repeated retrieval across sessions can imply persistence.
- Repetition is especially risky for scene-like, outline-like, hierarchy-like, and relationship-bearing results.

### Grouped Retrieval

- Grouped retrieval can imply object-set identity.
- Grouped results can look like approved batch targets.
- Grouped retrieval may blend context grouping, target grouping, evidence grouping, and mutation-scope grouping.
- Grouped retrieval must not imply grouped authority, batch mutation, or Story Unit identity.

### Contextual Retrieval

- Contextual retrieval may surface material relevant to drafting, advisory, generation, verification, or analysis.
- Contextual retrieval is not canonical retrieval.
- Retrieved context is not mutation scope.
- Contextual retrieval must avoid implying persistent structure when it is only situational support.

### Advisory Retrieval

- Advisory retrieval surfaces critique, recommendations, relationship suggestions, or analysis results.
- Advisory retrieval is not authoritative retrieval.
- Retrieved advice may be stale, context-bound, or non-actionable.
- Advisory retrieval must not become structural canon, command routing, or mutation authority.

### Stale Retrieval

- Stale retrieval may surface prior draft, prior structure, prior advisory result, recovery evidence, or old recomputed grouping.
- Stale retrieval is not current authority.
- Stale retrieval can be useful for comparison or support but must not govern current state.
- Stale retrieval is high risk when labels imply latest, current, restored, clean, valid, or recovered.

### Recomputed Retrieval

- Recomputed retrieval surfaces derived structures from current or changed material.
- Recomputed retrieval is not durable identity.
- Similar labels or repeated results do not prove persistent identity.
- Recomputed retrieval should not become structural search authorization.

### Continuity-Bearing Retrieval

- Continuity-bearing retrieval surfaces items because they appear narratively or structurally connected.
- Continuity-bearing retrieval is not persistence authorization.
- Continuity retrieval can imply source-of-truth authority if not bounded.
- Retrieval must distinguish continuity relation from durable structural relationship.

### Hierarchy-Bearing Retrieval

- Hierarchy-bearing retrieval surfaces nested, ordered, parent/child, or outline-like results.
- Hierarchy-bearing retrieval can imply topology canon.
- Retrieved hierarchy may be view-only, advisory, recomputed, stale, or contextual.
- Hierarchy-bearing retrieval remains unauthorized as structural retrieval.

## Traversal Versus Retrieval Findings

- Traversal means moving through visible or implied relationships.
- Retrieval means surfacing items by query, context, relationship, structure, or evidence.
- Traversal can occur without retrieval authority.
- Retrieval can occur without traversal authority.
- Traversal pressure can imply durable relationship paths.
- Retrieval pressure can imply durable object identity.
- Neither traversal nor retrieval authorizes graph identity, topology architecture, persistence, or command/search execution.

## Retrieval Versus Identity Findings

- Retrieved item does not equal stable identity.
- Retrieved group does not equal object family.
- Retrieved hierarchy does not equal canonical structure.
- Retrieved relationship does not equal graph edge.
- Retrieved recomputed structure does not equal durable Story Unit.
- Retrieved stale state does not equal current source of truth.
- Identity pressure rises when retrieved items have names, counts, positions, histories, pins, statuses, or repeated presentation.

## Retrieval Visibility / Legitimacy Drift Findings

- Retrieval result visibility implies legitimacy.
- Ranking implies recommendation.
- Grouping implies object-set coherence.
- Repeated results imply stability.
- Searchable results imply persistence.
- Navigable results imply traversal authority.
- Commandable results imply executable authority and remain unauthorized.
- Transitional GUI placement must not become retrieval design.

## Retrieval Versus Source-Of-Truth Findings

- Retrieval does not establish current authority.
- Retrieved stale, restored, reopened, generated, rewritten, advisory, or recomputed state may be useful without governing work.
- Source-of-truth vocabulary remains unstable, so retrieval labels must not overclaim currentness.
- Current authority, recovered authority, advisory pressure, and recomputed pressure must remain separate.
- Structural retrieval governance cannot stabilize before source-of-truth vocabulary and boundaries are clearer.

## Retrieval Versus Persistence Findings

- Retrieval is not persistence authorization.
- Retrieval across sessions can imply persistence even if data is recomputed.
- Persistent-looking retrieval metadata intensifies Story Unit pressure.
- Retrieved structure must not become durable identity by repeated access.
- Story Unit persistence remains unauthorized.

## Retrieval Versus Structural Mutation Findings

- Retrieval is not mutation authority.
- Retrieved targets are not mutation scope.
- Retrieved groups are not batch mutation sets.
- Retrieved hierarchy is not hierarchy mutation authority.
- Retrieved relationships are not graph mutation authority.
- Structural mutation remains pressure-only and cannot be inferred from retrieval.
