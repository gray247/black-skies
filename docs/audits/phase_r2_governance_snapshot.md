## Distilled Governance Truth

- Authority contamination is the primary governance problem.
- Workflow state governs authority more reliably than surface location alone.
- Visibility does not equal authority.
- Inspection does not equal mutation.
- Suggestion does not equal application.
- Focused Drafting requires explicit protection from competing systems.
- Support and recovery are exceptional paths, not ambient workflow peers.
- Dev/test is not advanced-user mode.
- Command/search is governance-sensitive because it can compress many authority classes into one entry seam.
- Intelligence authority is high-risk when interpretation starts to impersonate judgment or mutation.
- Story Unit pressure remains unresolved, but it is currently contained as governance pressure rather than promoted architecture.

## Stable Findings

- Workflow-state authority separation is more durable than surface-by-surface placement arguments.
- Mutation boundaries matter more than visual convenience.
- Contextual systems and persistent systems must remain distinct.
- Support and dev surfaces require explicit containment rather than soft labeling.
- Topology implications are real, but topology pressure does not authorize architecture by itself.
- Docking, detached panes, and layout controls can contaminate workflow authority if shell mechanics compete with writing flow.
- Any future `apply all` or bulk-rewrite path would be high-risk because it compresses review and mutation.
- A command palette or search surface is not neutral; it can silently widen authority unless its boundaries are classified first.

## Unresolved Questions

### Workflow-state unresolveds

- Which workflow states are canonically recognized for authority decisions?
- What must stay visible in Focused Drafting versus become contextual or hidden?
- Where do structure-review and command/search transitions begin and end?

### Intelligence unresolveds

- Which intelligence surfaces may remain contextual versus advanced-only?
- What evidence is sufficient before intelligence gets wider visible authority?
- How should uncertainty, routing, and provenance stay legible without becoming ambient clutter?

### Mutation unresolveds

- Where is the hard boundary between inspection, suggestion, review, and application?
- Which structural actions count as organization versus consequential mutation?
- How should bulk or multi-target mutation be constrained, if allowed at all?

### Support/dev unresolveds

- Which recovery and support actions are product-visible exception paths versus support-only tools?
- Which status signals are legitimate operator support versus diagnostics leakage?
- What dev/test seams must be hidden, removed, or explicitly fenced?

### Story Unit unresolveds

- Whether Story Units are only workflow objects or require a later persistence spine.
- How Story Units relate to scenes, drafts, outline nodes, and structural movement authority.
- Whether Story Unit pressure eventually forces a separate planning phase before GUI rebuild work.

## Roadmap Implications

- Workflow governance should precede implementation planning in this area.
- The current GUI problems are workflow-authority problems first, not cosmetic-only problems.
- Topology pressure is real, but it remains contained until governance stabilizes.
- Roadmap reconstruction is likely required so future work reflects the corrected governance sequence.
- Support/dev separation may need earlier priority because leakage distorts ordinary product authority.
- Safe and blocked implementation lanes should not reopen until a later Phase R2 closure snapshot records agreement across the governance snapshot, survivability ledger, and tracker.
