## Topology Containment Model

This model is provisional only. It is not topology authorization, not graph authorization, not persistence authorization, not structural canon, and not workflow-state canon.

Topology pressure means relationships, hierarchy, traversal, grouping, continuity links, advisory associations, or recomputed structural relationships begin to imply a durable graph, network, dependency map, or relationship architecture.

Core distinctions:
- relationships are not durable graph identity
- traversal is not persistence authorization
- hierarchy is not topology canon
- recomputed relationships are not durable structural identity
- continuity relationship is not persistence relationship
- advisory relationship is not authoritative relationship
- traversal pressure is not traversal authorization

Containment goal:
- preserve the analytical value of relationship pressure while preventing it from becoming topology architecture, Story Unit persistence, structural retrieval authority, or commandable graph identity.

## Executive Findings

- Topology remains pressure-only and cannot stabilize before Story Unit persistence, source-of-truth authority, structural retrieval governance, and structural mutation boundaries are settled.
- Relationship visibility is the primary topology-risk carrier because visible links can imply graph identity even when they are advisory or recomputed.
- Hierarchy is the most familiar topology pressure, but hierarchy must not become topology canon by presentation alone.
- Traversal pressure is especially risky because moving through relationships can make relationships feel durable, queryable, and commandable.
- Recomputed relationships can look authoritative when they repeat, but repeated recomputation is not durable graph identity.
- Structural retrieval governance must remain deferred because retrieving relationships can imply stable topology objects.
- Command/search structural inheritance remains deferred because commandable relationships would create graph-identity authority.

## Relationship Pressure Findings

### Scene-Like Relationships

- Scene-like relationships can imply sequence, dependency, causality, contrast, or continuity.
- Scene-like links feel natural but are not graph architecture authorization.
- Scene adjacency, order, or repeated comparison can make scenes feel like nodes.
- Scene-like relationships remain pressure only until source-of-truth and persistence rules stabilize.

### Outline Relationships

- Outline relationships imply parent/child, section/subsection, order, and priority.
- Outline relationships may be recomputed views rather than durable structural relationships.
- Outline hierarchy must not become topology canon.
- Outline relationship mutation remains blocked as structural mutation pressure.

### Continuity Relationships

- Continuity relationships connect narrative meaning across drafting, rewrite/apply, generation, restore/reopen, and recomputed groupings.
- Continuity relationships can survive without persistence.
- Continuity relationship is not persistence relationship.
- Continuity links should not authorize traversal, retrieval, mutation, or topology architecture.

### Hierarchy Relationships

- Hierarchy relationships create topology pressure through nesting, order, dependency, and inheritance.
- Hierarchy containment must distinguish view hierarchy, advisory hierarchy, recomputed hierarchy, and persistent hierarchy pressure.
- Persistent hierarchy remains unauthorized.
- Hierarchy relationships should not imply graph or tree architecture.

### Grouping Relationships

- Grouping relationships connect selected or clustered material by theme, continuity, structure, evidence, or advisory logic.
- Grouping relationship is not grouped authority.
- Batch grouping can imply object sets and graph clusters.
- Grouping relationships remain advisory/contextual unless later governance authorizes durable identity.

### Advisory Relationships

- Advisory relationships come from critique, analysis, recommendation, or interpretation.
- Advisory relationship is not authoritative relationship.
- Advisory confidence can make relationships feel canonical.
- Advisory relationships must not become command/search traversal or mutation routes.

### Recomputed Relationships

- Recomputed relationships may appear stable when generated repeatedly from similar material.
- Recomputed relationships are not durable structural identity.
- Similarity across recomputation is not proof of graph continuity.
- Recomputed relationship visibility must remain bounded to avoid source-of-truth overread.

## Traversal Pressure Findings

- Traversal pressure appears when users can move from one structural item to another through relationship, hierarchy, sequence, dependency, or search result.
- Traversal can make relationships feel durable and navigable.
- Traversal is not persistence authorization.
- Traversal is not structural retrieval authorization.
- Traversal is not command/search route authorization.
- Traversal over recovery, stale, advisory, or recomputed relationships risks making uncertain authority feel current.

## Hierarchy Containment Findings

- Hierarchy is a view or pressure family until structural canon is authorized.
- Parent/child and order semantics should remain provisional.
- Hierarchy must not imply topology canon, Story Unit persistence, or source-of-truth authority.
- Hierarchy mutation remains structural mutation pressure.
- Hierarchy retrieval remains deferred.

## Graph-Identity Pressure Findings

- Graph-identity pressure appears when relationship-bearing items feel like nodes and links.
- Node-like cards, connected labels, edge vocabulary, dependency markers, and traversal affordances can create graph identity.
- Graph identity requires object identity, relationship identity, source-of-truth authority, persistence behavior, and mutation boundaries; none are authorized.
- Graph architecture remains unauthorized.
- Graph-like visualization would be high risk unless explicitly bounded as non-authoritative analysis.

## Structural Relationship Versus Persistence Findings

- Structural relationships can be visible without being persistent.
- Relationship persistence would imply durable objects and durable links.
- Retrieval across sessions can imply relationship persistence even if relationships are recomputed.
- Relationship labels, IDs, histories, pins, statuses, or saved layouts intensify persistence pressure.
- Structural relationships must remain pressure until persistence authorization exists.

## Structural Relationship Versus Source-Of-Truth Findings

- A relationship can reflect current analysis without governing current source of truth.
- A stale relationship can remain meaningful while no longer current.
- A recovered or reopened relationship can preserve prior continuity while not governing current state.
- A generated or advisory relationship can be plausible without being authoritative.
- Source-of-truth vocabulary remains provisional; relationships must not overclaim current authority.
