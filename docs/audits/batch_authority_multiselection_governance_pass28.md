## Batch Authority Governance Model

This model is provisional only. It is not implementation authorization, not workflow-state canon, and not final product language.

Batch authority means authority to treat multiple selected, grouped, visible, searched, or related items as one governed action set.

Core rules:
- multi-selection is not batch permission
- grouped visibility is not grouped authority
- grouped context is not grouped blast radius
- grouped evidence is not grouped repair permission
- grouped navigation is not grouped execution
- grouped search results are not command targets
- batch mutation authority must not be normalized

Provisional grouped semantics:
- `Context Grouping`: multiple items inform reading, analysis, advisory, or generation context.
- `Target Grouping`: multiple items are the focus of inspection, review, or possible action consideration.
- `Destination Grouping`: multiple locations may receive generated, rewritten, restored, or inserted material.
- `Evidence Grouping`: multiple evidence items support inspection or support truth.
- `Mutation-Scope Grouping`: multiple items may be changed only after Mutation-Boundary Authority is active.
- `Structural Grouping`: multiple items imply organization, relationship, order, or Story Unit pressure without authorizing persistence.

## Executive Findings

- Batch authority is not a natural extension of selection; it is a higher-risk authority class.
- Multi-selection amplifies every unresolved selection ambiguity: context, target, destination, evidence, mutation scope, and structural hint.
- Grouped visibility can create the illusion of grouped permission even when every item is read-only.
- Batch mutation requires stronger Mutation-Boundary Authority because blast radius can multiply, fan out, or become structurally ambiguous.
- Batch recovery is especially risky because grouped recovery artifacts can imply bulk restore/reopen authority.
- Future command/search must not convert grouped search results or multi-selection into executable targets.
- Structural batch semantics remain blocked pending Story Unit pressure reconstruction.

## Multi-Selection Semantics Findings

### Context Grouping

- Multiple selected items may serve as reference context for drafting, advisory, analysis, or generation.
- Context grouping must not imply every item will be changed.
- Context grouping can still broaden blast radius if hidden context influences generation or rewrite.
- Context grouping should remain non-mutating unless later Mutation-Boundary Authority is entered.

### Target Grouping

- Multiple selected items may become inspection or review targets.
- Target grouping is not grouped approval.
- Target grouping should distinguish inspected targets from affected targets.
- Grouped targets create pressure toward batch commands, even when current use is read-only.

### Destination Grouping

- Multiple destinations are higher risk than multiple sources because they can spread mutation across draft, project, recovery, or structure state.
- Destination grouping must not be inferred from visible grouping alone.
- A grouped destination requires separate boundary questions for each destination and for the set as a whole.
- Destination grouping remains especially unsafe for recovery and structural operations.

### Evidence Grouping

- Multiple evidence items can support verification, inspection, or support truth.
- Grouped evidence does not authorize grouped repair, restore, reopen, rewrite, or generation.
- Evidence grouping can pressure users toward bulk resolution semantics.
- Verification language must remain bounded to the claims each evidence item actually proves.

### Mutation-Scope Grouping

- Mutation-scope grouping begins only after Mutation-Boundary Authority is active.
- The boundary must identify which items are sources, destinations, affected ranges, supporting context, and excluded items.
- The batch set requires its own blast-radius statement beyond per-item scope.
- Mutation-scope grouping must not be inherited silently from selection, search results, or advisory recommendations.

### Structural Grouping

- Structural grouping is deferred and unstable because it can imply Story Unit persistence, topology, or relationship architecture.
- Grouped scenes, beats, nodes, or conceptual units may be useful as analysis context, but they must not become persisted structural authority.
- Structural grouping should not become command/search route authority before Story Unit pressure reconstruction.

## Batch Mutation-Boundary Findings

Batch mutation requires a stronger boundary than single-target mutation.

Required boundary questions:
- what items are included
- what items are excluded
- which items are context, target, source, destination, or evidence
- what changes per item
- what changes at the set level
- whether order, dependency, or structure matters
- whether partial success is possible
- how failure, rollback, or recovery is represented
- what blast radius exists across the whole group

Findings:
- a valid single-item boundary does not imply a valid batch boundary
- batch generation and batch rewrite/apply share the overarching mutation-boundary model but require grouped blast-radius treatment
- batch recovery mutation should remain blocked pending deeper restore/reopen governance
- batch structural mutation remains blocked pending Story Unit pressure reconstruction

## Batch Selection Blast-Radius Findings

- Batch blast radius is not the sum of selected items alone.
- Grouped actions can create cross-item continuity, ordering, dependency, persistence, and recovery effects.
- A small multi-selection can carry broad blast radius if it controls a scene cluster, project span, recovery set, or structural group.
- A broad grouping can remain read-only if it is evidence or inspection only.
- Batch blast radius must distinguish per-item effects from set-level effects.

## Batch Verification / Inspection Findings

- Batch verification can inspect multiple items but does not authorize grouped repair.
- Grouped inspection results may create pressure for bulk fix, bulk restore, or bulk apply.
- Verification summaries can hide item-level uncertainty.
- A clean group result should not imply every future mutation is safe.
- Failed or partial verification should not automatically enter recovery mutation.

## Batch Recovery Governance Findings

- Batch recovery is exceptional and should remain blocked.
- Grouped restore/reopen could replace or fork multiple authoritative states.
- Multi-selection across backups, snapshots, stale sessions, or recovery artifacts can imply bulk recovery authority before governance exists.
- Recovery evidence grouping may be allowed later as read-only support context, but this pass does not authorize it.
- Restore-as-copy versus restore-as-replacement remains deferred and must not be bypassed by batch framing.

## Batch Command/Search Inheritance Findings

- Search grouping is not command grouping.
- Multiple search results are not executable batch targets.
- Command/search must not convert multi-selection into batch mutation permission.
- Read-only search may later retrieve grouped evidence or context, but it must preserve workflow-state, selection, support/recovery, diagnostics, and visibility inheritance.
- Batch command execution remains unauthorized.
- Hidden support, recovery, diagnostics, developer/test, structural, or mutation routes must not become discoverable through grouped search results.

## Structural Batch Risk Findings

- Structural batch risk is blocked by Story Unit pressure.
- Grouped structural items can imply persistent narrative objects even when no persistence is authorized.
- Batch organization can become topology architecture by another name.
- Structural grouping may be useful for advisory or analysis, but it must stay non-persistent and non-mutating until Story Unit pressure reconstruction.
- Structural search should remain deferred because grouped results could imply object identity, relationship authority, or commandable structure.

- Search grouping can make bulk routes appear discoverable even when unauthorized.
- Verification grouping can make repair feel expected.
- Recovery grouping can make bulk restore/reopen feel like routine maintenance.
- Structural grouping can make Story Unit persistence feel already approved.
