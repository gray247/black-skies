## Mutation-Boundary Authority Model

Mutation-Boundary Authority is provisional only. It is not final workflow law, not implementation authorization, not product navigation, and not a normal workflow state.

For this pass, Mutation-Boundary Authority means the governing transition layer that must answer:
- what is the mutation target
- what is the mutation scope
- what will change
- what evidence supports the transition
- what confirmation or review is required
- what blast radius exists
- what happens if the boundary is cancelled or rejected

Mutation-Boundary Authority is distinct from mutation execution:
- boundary authority defines whether a mutation route is governed enough to proceed
- execution authority performs or applies the change
- selection scope can inform boundary authority but is not mutation permission
- advisory suggestion can motivate boundary review but is not rewrite/apply authority
- generation scope can define blast radius but is not the generation action itself

## Executive Findings

- Mutation-Boundary Authority should remain a transition governor across mutation-capable families rather than become a normal workflow state.
- The highest-risk mutation transitions are Focused Drafting to Generation, Advisory / Review to Rewrite / Apply, Selection / Capture to mutation scope, and Support / Recovery Exception to restore/recovery mutation.
- Selection is the most subtle boundary input because it can define target and scope without feeling like permission.
- Advisory output is the most legitimacy-sensitive boundary input because critique, recommendations, and analysis can feel like approval to apply.
- Verification / Inspection can provide evidence for mutation but must not become permission to mutate.
- Future command/search must inherit mutation-boundary restrictions before any mutation-capable route is exposed.
- Structure / Organization remains paused because structural mutation boundaries depend on unresolved Story Unit pressure.

## Mutation-Boundary Transition Map

### Focused Drafting -> Generation

- source authority: protected authoring context
- destination authority: draft-changing generation
- boundary trigger: user requests generated text, replacement material, continuation, expansion, or other content-producing action
- required boundary questions: target, scope, replacement versus addition, continuity scale, confirmation, and result handling
- prohibited shortcut: service availability, visible generation control, or command/search route must not imply permission
- safe exit: cancel boundary and return to Focused Drafting
- risk: generation borrows authoring legitimacy and appears to be ordinary writing rather than mutation

### Focused Drafting -> Rewrite / Apply

- source authority: protected authoring context
- destination authority: transformation of existing material
- boundary trigger: user requests rewrite, apply, accept, sync, replace, or transform existing draft material
- required boundary questions: source material, destination material, diff/review evidence, overwrite behavior, and confirmation
- prohibited shortcut: wording that makes apply sound like closing, syncing, or harmless continuation
- safe exit: discard candidate change and return to Focused Drafting
- risk: rewrite/apply mutates current writing while hiding behind drafting continuity

### Selection / Capture -> Generation

- source authority: target identification without permission
- destination authority: generation with selected scope
- boundary trigger: selected text, scene, range, or project area is used as generation target
- required boundary questions: whether selection is prompt context, replacement target, insertion target, or broad scope marker
- prohibited shortcut: selected equals approved
- safe exit: clear or revise selection before generation
- risk: selection silently defines blast radius

### Selection / Capture -> Rewrite / Apply

- source authority: selected target or captured material
- destination authority: applied transformation
- boundary trigger: selected material is used for rewrite, apply, or replacement
- required boundary questions: selected source, selected destination, full affected range, preview/diff, and rejection path
- prohibited shortcut: selection as implicit apply consent
- safe exit: return to selection or advisory without mutation
- risk: local selection may hide broader project or scene mutation effects

### Advisory / Review -> Rewrite / Apply

- source authority: suggestion, critique, interpretation, or recommendation
- destination authority: mutation execution through rewrite/apply
- boundary trigger: advice becomes candidate change, saved rewrite, apply action, or accepted recommendation
- required boundary questions: what advice is being converted, what material changes, what evidence supports the change, and what confirmation is needed
- prohibited shortcut: advisory confidence as apply permission
- safe exit: keep advisory output without mutation
- risk: advisory legitimacy transfers into mutation authority

### Verification / Inspection -> Mutation-related Action

- source authority: bounded evidence or proof
- destination authority: any mutation informed by inspection
- boundary trigger: report, verification result, snapshot evidence, or inspection finding motivates repair, restore, rewrite, generation, or export side effect
- required boundary questions: what the evidence proves, what it does not prove, and whether mutation remains separately authorized
- prohibited shortcut: verified, clean, valid, or inspected as permission
- safe exit: retain evidence and do not mutate
- risk: proof becomes permission or repair authority

### Support / Recovery Exception -> Restore / Recovery Mutation

- source authority: exceptional support/recovery condition
- destination authority: high-impact restore, reopen, repair, or recovery mutation
- boundary trigger: user or system attempts restore, recovery copy, repair, fallback, or state replacement
- required boundary questions: recovery target, source of truth, overwritten state, created copy, irreversibility, and post-recovery evidence
- prohibited shortcut: exception urgency as bypass for scope or confirmation
- safe exit: cancel recovery mutation and remain in support truth
- risk: recovery mutation normalizes into routine maintenance workflow

### Future Command/Search Overlay -> Mutation-capable Command Route

- source authority: deferred overlay candidate with no current mutation authorization
- destination authority: generation, rewrite/apply, recovery mutation, structure mutation, or export side effect
- boundary trigger: future command/search proposes, routes to, or executes a mutation-capable action
- required boundary questions: inherited workflow state, target, scope, execution versus routing, confirmation, and whether command/search may expose the route at all
- prohibited shortcut: discoverability as approval or execution authority
- safe exit: search remains read-only retrieval or routes to the appropriate state without mutation
- risk: command/search becomes a hidden second app that bypasses workflow-state authority

## Blast-Radius Findings

- Blast radius is not only size. It includes target identity, persistence, overwrite behavior, continuity impact, recovery cost, downstream output effects, and reversibility.
- Generation blast radius can range from local continuation to scene replacement to broader continuity changes.
- Rewrite / Apply blast radius includes source text, destination text, hidden context, saved rewrite artifacts, and current draft state.
- Selection can understate blast radius when the selected region is small but the action uses scene, project, or structural context.
- Recovery mutation has the highest blast-radius risk because restore/reopen/repair actions can replace or fork authoritative state.
- Command/search would magnify blast-radius risk if it exposes mutation routes without the current workflow state's visible boundaries.

## Selection Scope Findings

- Selection scope is input to boundary authority, not permission to mutate.
- Selection needs later reconstruction into at least four meanings: prompt context, inspection target, advisory context, and mutation target.
- Captured material can become mutation scope only after the boundary distinguishes source, destination, affected range, and result handling.
- Selection vocabulary should avoid implying approval, readiness, acceptance, or apply intent.
- Broad selection or project-level capture should be treated as higher blast radius even if the UI presents it compactly.

## Generation Scope Findings

- Generation scope is a boundary decision before it is a generation action.
- Scope must distinguish continuation, insertion, replacement, expansion, scene-level generation, and project-context generation.
- Runtime truth may explain generation availability, but it does not approve generation scope.
- Model/provider/service status may inform support truth but must not become scope recommendation.
- Generation should not inherit Focused Drafting legitimacy without explicit boundary transition.

## Rewrite / Apply Boundary Findings

- Rewrite / Apply is the clearest advisory-to-mutation crossing.
- A saved rewrite or candidate change is not automatically permission to apply.
- Apply language must not hide overwrite, replacement, synchronization, or persistence consequences.
- Boundary evidence should distinguish draft source, candidate result, affected target, and post-apply state.
- Failure handling after apply should enter support truth only when an actual exception condition exists, not developer diagnostics by default.

## Advisory-To-Mutation Transition Findings

- Advisory / Review can suggest, critique, rank, or interpret; it cannot itself grant mutation permission.
- Recommendations require a separate boundary before becoming rewrite/apply or generation.
- Advisory confidence, tone, or specificity can create legitimacy pressure even when technically non-mutating.
- Advanced Analysis inherits the same risk when it recommends structural, rewrite, or generation changes.
- The transition should be governed by the proposed mutation, not by the apparent quality of the advice.

## Verification-To-Mutation Boundary Findings

- Verification / Inspection can support mutation decisions by providing bounded evidence.
- Evidence must remain separate from repair, restore, rewrite, generation, or apply authority.
- `Verified`, `clean`, `valid`, and similar language risks becoming permission unless bounded to the inspected claim.
- Snapshot/report evidence may be necessary before recovery mutation, but it cannot replace recovery boundary questions.
- Inspection that discovers a problem may enter Support / Recovery Exception, but still should not execute recovery mutation directly.

## Recovery Mutation Boundary Findings

- Recovery mutation needs stronger boundary treatment than ordinary generation or rewrite because it can replace, restore, fork, or repair authoritative state.
- Support / Recovery Exception provides context, not automatic mutation permission.
- Recovery Diagnostics can inform recovery evidence but must not become recovery mutation authority.
- Restore/reopen/repair actions need explicit distinction between original state, backup/source state, resulting state, and reversibility.
- Recovery wording must remain exceptional and must not convert recovery mutation into ordinary maintenance workflow.

## Future Command/Search Inheritance Implications

- Command/search cannot define mutation authority for itself.
- Any future mutation-capable command route must inherit the active workflow state plus Mutation-Boundary Authority.
- Search should remain read-only retrieval unless a later pass explicitly reconstructs routing authority.
- Command must distinguish route, prepare, preview, and execute.
- Mutation-capable commands should remain unauthorized until target, scope, confirmation, blast radius, and state inheritance are settled.
- Hidden support, recovery, diagnostics, developer/test, and structure mutation routes should not be discoverable through ordinary command/search.
- Command/search expansion before mutation-boundary inheritance would create bypass risk and likely implementation waste.

## Highest-Risk Mutation-Boundary Ambiguities

- Whether a selection is context, target, destination, or permission.
- Whether generation is continuation, insertion, replacement, or broad continuity mutation.
- Whether advisory/review output is suggestion, candidate change, or apply-ready mutation.
- Whether verification evidence is proof only or permission to repair.
- Whether recovery context justifies restore/recovery mutation without separate boundary review.
- Whether command/search can route to mutation without becoming execution authority.
- Whether compact scope controls hide project-level blast radius.
- Whether Structure / Organization mutation can be modeled before Story Unit pressure reconstruction.

## Contradictions Found

- Generation is a primary product capability but requires mutation-boundary protection before being treated as ordinary action.
- Selection feels lightweight but can define consequential mutation scope.
- Advisory / Review is intentionally non-mutating, yet its output often creates the strongest pressure toward rewrite/apply mutation.
- Verification / Inspection is needed for safer decisions, yet proof language can become permission language.
- Recovery mutation must be available in exceptions, yet exception urgency can pressure boundary bypass.
- Command/search is useful for access, yet access itself can become implied authorization.
- Mutation-Boundary Authority is stable as a concept, but its future UI or workflow placement remains deliberately unstable.
