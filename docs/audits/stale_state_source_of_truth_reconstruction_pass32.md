## Source-Of-Truth Authority Model

This model is provisional only. It is not persistence authorization, not topology authorization, not structural canon, and not workflow-state canon.

Source-of-truth authority means the authority to say which state, artifact, interpretation, generated result, rewrite result, restored copy, reopened project, recomputed structure, or advisory conclusion currently governs the user's work.

Core distinctions:
- restored state is not automatic source-of-truth authority
- reopened state is not canonical continuity authority
- recomputed structure is not durable identity
- continuity survival is not persistence authorization
- advisory continuity is not authoritative continuity
- generated continuity is not accepted authority
- reconstruction pressure is not canonical authority

Provisional authority families:
- `Current Authority`: the active working state currently governing user action.
- `Stale Authority Pressure`: previously valid state that may no longer govern.
- `Restored Authority Pressure`: recovered or restored state that may replace, fork, or compare against current state.
- `Reopened Authority Pressure`: loaded or resumed state that may change current authority.
- `Generated Authority Pressure`: generated result that may propose continuity without being accepted.
- `Rewritten Authority Pressure`: saved rewrite or candidate change that may not be applied authority.
- `Recomputed Authority Pressure`: derived or recomputed structure that appears authoritative without durable identity.
- `Advisory Authority Pressure`: guidance or interpretation that may influence but not govern state.

## Executive Findings

- Source-of-truth authority is the next blocker for structural mutation, continuity survival, restore/reopen governance, structural retrieval, and topology containment.
- Stale state can preserve recognizable continuity while losing current authority.
- Restore and reopen must be split by source-of-truth effect: compare, resume, fork, replace, recover, or rehydrate.
- Generated and rewritten material can carry continuity pressure but does not become accepted authority without explicit mutation governance.
- Recomputed structural authority is especially risky because it can appear current, stable, and object-like without durable identity.
- Advisory authority can describe continuity or structure but cannot become authoritative reconstruction.
- Structural retrieval and command/search inheritance must remain deferred until source-of-truth authority is bounded.

## Stale-State Authority Findings

### Stale Drafting State

- A stale draft may still be meaningful, readable, or continuous with prior work.
- Stale drafting state does not automatically govern current writing.
- Stale draft visibility can create confusion between reference, candidate, recovery evidence, and current authority.
- Stale drafting state requires later source-of-truth rules before reopen, restore, apply, or comparison can stabilize.

### Stale Structural State

- Stale structural state may include outdated outline-like, scene-like, grouped, or hierarchy-like interpretations.
- Stale structure can look more authoritative than current text if it retains labels, order, or grouping.
- Stale structural state must not become Story Unit persistence by survival alone.
- Structural retrieval over stale structures remains deferred.

### Stale Recovery State

- Stale recovery state may reflect earlier backup, snapshot, crash, reopen, or restore evidence.
- It may be useful support evidence without governing current state.
- Stale recovery evidence should not authorize recovery mutation.
- Recovery diagnostics may explain stale state but not convert it into source-of-truth authority.

### Stale Continuity Interpretation

- A prior continuity interpretation may remain plausible while no longer matching current draft, generated result, rewrite result, or restored state.
- Continuity survival does not prove authority survival.
- Stale continuity labels, summaries, or analysis can mislead if shown as current.
- Advisory continuity must be timestamped or bounded later if exposed, but this pass does not authorize implementation.

### Stale Recomputed Grouping

- Recomputed grouping can become stale immediately after text, selection, generation, rewrite, restore, or reopen changes.
- A stale recomputed grouping is not durable structural identity.
- Similar regenerated labels do not prove identity continuity.
- Stale recomputed grouping must not become structural search authority.

### Stale Advisory State

- Advisory state can become stale when draft, selection, context, model result, or recovery source changes.
- Advisory state may remain useful history but cannot govern current mutation.
- Advisory suggestions should not be treated as authoritative reconstruction after source state changes.
- Command/search should not retrieve stale advisory state as actionable authority.

## Source-Of-Truth Findings

### Current Authority

- Current authority is the state that governs ordinary user action in the moment.
- It may be draft text, loaded project state, selected context, or a bounded exception state.
- Current authority is not automatically structural authority.
- Current authority must be distinguished from visible stale, candidate, advisory, recovered, generated, or recomputed state.

### Restored Authority

- Restored state may become reference, comparison, copy, fork, or replacement.
- Restored authority is not automatic on restore visibility or restore availability.
- Restore-as-copy versus restore-as-replacement remains a key split inside source-of-truth pressure.
- Verification evidence can support restored state but does not authorize it as governing truth.

### Reopened Authority

- Reopened state may resume a project, recover a session, rehydrate state, or load a different source.
- Reopened state is not canonical continuity authority by default.
- Reopen can be navigation, recovery, comparison, or source replacement depending on effect.
- Reopen must not be treated as harmless navigation when it changes active authority.

### Generated Authority

- Generated output can propose content and continuity but does not govern current state until accepted through mutation governance.
- Generated continuity is not accepted authority.
- Generated structure is not structural canon.
- Generation scope and source-of-truth effect must stay separate.

### Rewritten Authority

- A saved rewrite or candidate rewrite is not applied authority.
- Rewritten material may preserve, change, or break continuity without governing current draft.
- Rewrite/apply must distinguish candidate authority from accepted mutation authority.
- Rewritten structural effects remain pressure only.

### Recomputed Authority

- Recomputed structure can look current because it is derived from current or changed material.
- Recomputed authority is still derived interpretation, not durable identity.
- Recomputed grouping must not imply Story Unit persistence.
- Recomputed structure needs source-of-truth boundaries before structural retrieval or mutation.

### Advisory Authority

- Advisory authority interprets, suggests, or critiques.
- Advisory authority does not govern draft, structure, recovery, or source of truth.
- Advisory continuity is not authoritative continuity.
- Advisory structural reconstruction is pressure, not canon.

### Structural Authority Pressure

- Structural authority pressure appears when structural views, groupings, labels, or search results seem to govern navigation, mutation, or continuity.
- Structural authority remains unauthorized.
- Structural authority cannot stabilize before source-of-truth, persistence, topology, and retrieval governance.

## Restore/Reopen Source-Of-Truth Findings

- Restore and reopen must be split by source-of-truth effect, not by surface label.
- Restore-as-copy may create a parallel authority candidate without replacing current state.
- Restore-as-replacement may attempt to supersede current authority and requires stronger boundary analysis later.
- Reopen-as-navigation may simply return to known current state.
- Reopen-as-recovery may rehydrate or recover state after failure.
- Reopen-as-replacement may change source authority and continuity interpretation.
- Neither restore nor reopen should be treated as source-of-truth authority until effect, current state, source state, and continuity impact are explicit.

## Continuity Survival Findings

- Continuity can survive even when source-of-truth authority changes.
- Source-of-truth authority can survive even when continuity interpretation changes.
- Recomputed continuity can appear to survive through labels or grouping, but that is not durable identity.
- Generated and rewritten continuity can be meaningful before becoming applied authority.
- Recovery continuity can preserve prior narrative while replacing current authority.
- Continuity survival must not be treated as persistence authorization.

## Authoritative Reconstruction Pressure Findings

- Authoritative reconstruction pressure appears when the system seems to rebuild what the story "really is" from draft, structure, advisory output, recovery evidence, generated material, or recomputed grouping.
- Reconstruction may be useful as analysis, but it is not canon.
- Structural reconstruction pressure is highest when recomputed groupings are visible, searchable, or commandable.
- Recovery reconstruction pressure is highest when restored or reopened state appears to resolve current authority automatically.
- Advisory reconstruction pressure is highest when analysis language sounds definitive.

## Source-Of-Truth Versus Persistence Findings

- Source-of-truth authority says what governs current work.
- Persistence says what durable identity or state is stored and addressable.
- Current authority can exist without Story Unit persistence.
- Persistence-looking structure can imply source-of-truth authority before authorization.
- Source-of-truth reconstruction should contain persistence pressure, not approve it.
- Story Unit persistence remains unauthorized.

## Source-Of-Truth Versus Structural Identity Findings

- Structural identity is not source-of-truth authority by itself.
- A structural grouping can be visible without governing current state.
- A recomputed structure can reflect current text without becoming durable identity.
- A restored structure can be recognizable without becoming current authority.
- Structural identity remains pressure until source-of-truth, persistence, topology, and continuity rules are stable.

## Source-Of-Truth Visibility / Legitimacy Drift Findings

- Visible stale, restored, reopened, generated, rewritten, advisory, or recomputed state can look authoritative through placement and repetition.
- Labels such as current, restored, recovered, generated, saved, rewritten, clean, valid, or latest can create authority overread.
- Searchable state implies stronger authority than visible state.
- Commandable state implies strongest authority and remains unauthorized.
- Transitional GUI placement must not become source-of-truth design.
