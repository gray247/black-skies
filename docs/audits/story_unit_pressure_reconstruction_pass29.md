## Story Unit Pressure Model

This model is provisional only. It is not implementation authorization, not persistence authorization, not topology authorization, and not workflow-state canon.

Story Unit pressure means a surface, vocabulary, selection, grouping, search result, analysis result, or workflow need begins to imply stable narrative object identity.

Pressure is not authorization:
- structural pressure is not implementation need
- narrative grouping is not persistent object identity
- retrieval is not persistence authorization
- organizational hierarchy is not canonical architecture
- topology pressure is not topology authorization
- structural search pressure is not structural search authorization

Provisional pressure families:
- `Structural Identity Pressure`: material begins to read as stable object identity.
- `Persistence Pressure`: a grouped or named structure appears saveable, addressable, or durable.
- `Workflow Pressure`: a structural view appears necessary for ordinary workflow.
- `Search Pressure`: retrieval implies stable structural objects or commandable results.
- `Topology Pressure`: relationships imply graph, dependency, or architecture authority.
- `Mutation Pressure`: structural grouping implies move, merge, split, reorder, or batch transformation authority.

## Executive Findings

- Story Unit pressure is now the central blocker for Structure / Organization, structural grouping, structural search, and topology pressure.
- The most dangerous drift is not explicit Story Unit implementation; it is repeated structural visibility that makes grouped narrative material feel like stable object identity.
- Scene-like labels, outline objects, grouped narrative clusters, and organizational hierarchy all create persistence pressure even when they are only advisory or contextual.
- Structural search cannot stabilize until retrieval stops implying persistent, commandable Story Unit objects.
- Topology remains pressure-only because relationship visibility can imply graph architecture before object identity is authorized.
- Structural mutation remains blocked because move, split, merge, reorder, and batch operations all require unresolved object identity and continuity authority.
- The safest near-term classification is structural hinting as advisory/contextual pressure, not product direction.

## Structural Identity Pressure Findings

### Scene-Like Identity Pressure

- Scenes already feel like natural units of work and can easily become treated as durable Story Unit candidates.
- Scene-like identity pressure increases when surfaces group, label, summarize, search, or batch-select scene material.
- Scene identity may be useful as context, but it must not become persistence authorization.
- Scene-like retrieval should not imply commandable structural objects.

### Outline/Object Identity Pressure

- Outline rows, cards, nodes, beats, or chunks can read as stable objects even when they are only display groupings.
- Object-like affordances create pressure for rename, move, merge, split, reorder, and attach actions.
- The more a structural element has a label, count, status, or persistent-looking visual container, the more it implies object identity.
- Outline/object identity remains pressure only until persistence and mutation boundaries are reconstructed.

### Grouped Narrative Object Pressure

- Grouped narrative material can look like an approved unit for analysis, generation, rewrite, verification, export, or command/search.
- Multi-selection and batch grouping intensify the pressure by making a set feel intentionally addressable.
- Grouped objects can become de facto Story Units even if no data model exists.
- Grouping should remain contextual or advisory unless later governance explicitly authorizes stable identity.

### Structural Continuity Pressure

- Structural continuity pressure appears when an object-like grouping seems to persist across sessions, revisions, recovery, generation, or rewrite/apply.
- Continuity pressure raises source-of-truth questions: what happens if underlying text changes, splits, merges, or is restored.
- Structural continuity is tied to later stale-state/source-of-truth reconstruction.
- Without continuity authority, Story Unit-like objects should not be treated as durable workflow entities.

### Organizational Hierarchy Pressure

- Hierarchy implies parent/child, order, dependency, priority, and possibly topology.
- Organizational hierarchy can become architecture by presentation alone.
- Hierarchy should remain a view or analysis pressure until its source of truth, persistence, mutation authority, and topology containment are reconstructed.
- Hierarchical vocabulary should avoid implying canonical narrative architecture.

## Story Unit Persistence Pressure Findings

- Persistence pressure appears whenever structural units look saveable, addressable, searchable, reorderable, or commandable.
- Persistent-looking labels, IDs, counts, cards, pinned states, histories, and status indicators all imply stronger Story Unit reality.
- Retrieval across sessions can imply persistence even when the system only recomputes structure.
- Export, reports, or diagnostics that name structural units can make temporary groupings feel durable.
- Story Unit persistence remains unauthorized; all current structural pressure must be recorded as pressure only.

## Structural Workflow Pressure Findings

- Structure / Organization cannot stabilize as a workflow family until Story Unit pressure is bounded.
- Structural workflow pressure appears when users are expected to navigate, organize, analyze, or mutate story material through structural units.
- Structural workflow can compete with Focused Drafting if it becomes a second primary workflow.
- Structural workflow can compete with Advisory / Review if analysis output becomes object authority.
- Structural workflow can compete with Generation / Rewrite / Apply if structural groupings become mutation scope.

## Structural Search Pressure Findings

- Grouped structural search can imply batch authority over Story Unit-like objects.

## Topology Pressure Findings

- Topology remains pressure-only.

- Topology pressure depends on stable object identity; without Story Unit authority, relationship authority has no authorized object base.
- Topology vocabulary should not imply graph persistence, relationship mutation, or structural truth.
- Topology containment will need a later pass before any relationship-heavy structural workflow stabilizes.

## Structural Mutation Pressure Findings

- Structural mutation pressure appears around move, split, merge, reorder, group, ungroup, promote, demote, link, unlink, and batch rewrite/generation over structural groupings.
- Structural mutation cannot stabilize without persistent identity, continuity authority, blast-radius governance, and Story Unit pressure resolution.
- Mutation-Boundary Authority would need to define structural target, source, destination, affected range, continuity impact, and reversibility.
- Batch structural mutation remains blocked.
- Structural mutation should not be inferred from structural visibility.

## Structural Selection Pressure Findings

- Structural selection is not Story Unit selection.
- Selection may identify scene-like, outline-like, or group-like material as context, target, evidence, or structural hint.
- Structural selection can imply object identity if it persists, transfers, or appears commandable.
- Structural selection should not become mutation scope before Story Unit pressure and Mutation-Boundary Authority are reconstructed.
- Multi-selection intensifies structural identity pressure by making grouped narrative material feel like an approved object set.
