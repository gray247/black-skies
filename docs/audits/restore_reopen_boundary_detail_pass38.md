## Restore / Reopen Governance Model

This model is provisional only. It is not restore authorization, not reopen authorization, not recovery implementation authorization, not topology authorization, not structural canon, and not workflow-state canon.

Restore and reopen are different authority families:
- `Restore`: attempts to introduce prior, copied, backed-up, recovered, or replacement state into relation with current authority.
- `Reopen`: attempts to return to, load, resume, or rehydrate a state path, project, session, or authority context.

Core distinctions:
- reopen is not harmless navigation by default
- restore is not rollback
- recovery continuity is not current authority
- stale restoration is not canonical restoration
- rehydration is not authority resumption
- restore-as-copy is not restore-as-replacement
- resume is not replacement
- stale continuity is not resumed authority
- recovery visibility is not recovery legitimacy

Containment goal:
- preserve restore/reopen as exceptional source-of-truth pressure families without authorizing execution, command routes, structural persistence, topology, graph identity, or ordinary workflow recovery authority.

## Executive Findings

- Restore and reopen remain compressed authority families and must be separated before recovery-linked routing can stabilize.
- Restore-as-copy creates parallel authority pressure; restore-as-replacement creates current-authority replacement pressure.
- Reopen-as-navigation may be benign only when it does not change source-of-truth authority; reopen-as-authority-resumption is higher risk.
- Rehydration may reconstruct working state, but it is not automatically resumed authority.
- Recovery continuity can preserve meaningful narrative or structural continuity while still failing to establish current source-of-truth authority.
- Stale restoration is a major legitimacy risk because old state can look complete, coherent, and safer than current state.
- Retrieval-linked execution and structural command/search inheritance remain blocked until restore/reopen authority effects are bounded.

## Restore-As-Copy Findings

- Restore-as-copy creates or exposes a parallel state candidate rather than directly replacing current authority.
- Copy semantics can reduce overwrite risk but increase ambiguity between current, copied, recovered, and comparison state.
- A restored copy is not current source-of-truth authority by visibility alone.
- A restored copy may preserve prior continuity without resuming authority.
- Restore-as-copy can still create persistence, retrieval, and command/search pressure if the copy appears durable, searchable, or action-bearing.

## Restore-As-Replacement Findings

- Restore-as-replacement attempts to supersede current authority with prior, backed-up, recovered, or external state.
- Replacement is high-impact recovery mutation.
- Replacement must not inherit permission from verification, recovery visibility, invalid retrieval, or user urgency.
- Replacement can overwrite, abandon, or reclassify current work.
- Restore-as-replacement remains unauthorized and requires later source-of-truth, rollback/failure, continuity, and recovery diagnostics governance.

## Reopen-As-Navigation Findings

- Reopen-as-navigation means returning to an already-authorized current or known state without changing authority.
- Reopen-as-navigation is only low risk if it does not rehydrate, replace, recover, fork, or reinterpret state.
- Reopen must not be treated as harmless navigation by default.
- Reopen-as-navigation can still create legitimacy pressure when labels imply latest, restored, recovered, valid, or canonical state.
- Command/search must not expose reopen as generic navigation until authority effects are classified.

## Reopen-As-Authority-Resumption Findings

- Reopen-as-authority-resumption means loaded state appears to resume governing authority after interruption, failure, stale state, or recovery.
- Authority resumption is not automatic continuity survival.
- Resumed authority must be distinguished from restored authority, recovered authority, rehydrated state, and stale continuity.
- Reopen-as-authority-resumption can hide source-of-truth shifts by feeling like ordinary return.
- Authority resumption remains provisional until source-of-truth vocabulary stabilizes.

## Recovery Continuity Pressure Findings

- Recovery continuity can make restored or reopened state feel legitimate because it appears narratively or structurally continuous.
- Continuity survival is not current authority.
- Recovery continuity can preserve prior work while replacing or conflicting with current work.
- Recovery continuity can also be stale, partial, or recomputed.
- Continuity pressure must not authorize restore, reopen, rollback, retry, or recovery-linked command routes.

## Restoration Legitimacy Drift Findings

- Restoration legitimacy drift appears when restore/reopen surfaces become familiar, visible, or framed as safer than current work.
- Drift increases when restored, reopened, recovered, valid, clean, latest, or copy labels imply authority over current state.
- Drift increases when restore/reopen sits near retrieval, diagnostics, verification, or command/search affordances.
- Repeated recovery visibility can normalize restore/reopen as ordinary workflow.
- Restoration visibility must not become restoration legitimacy.

## Stale Restoration Findings

- Stale restoration pressure appears when old backup, snapshot, copy, recovered state, or reopened state looks complete and trustworthy while no longer matching current authority.
- Stale restoration is not canonical restoration.
- Stale restoration can be useful comparison or support evidence.
- Stale restoration must not authorize replacement, recovery diagnostics, or command/search routes.
- Stale restoration is especially risky when continuity appears preserved.

## Recovery Versus Rollback Findings

- Recovery is an exceptional state family responding to failure, degraded state, stale state, invalidity, or support condition.
- Rollback is pressure to reverse or undo an action.
- Restore may be one recovery mechanism but is not rollback by default.
- Reopen may follow recovery but is not rollback by default.
- Rollback visibility is not rollback authority, and restore visibility is not restore authority.

## Recovery Versus Retry Findings

- Retry attempts an operation again.
- Retry is not rollback.
- Retry is not restore.
- Retry can create new effects and must not be assumed harmless.
- Retry may be support truth when operation availability is the issue, but it becomes authority-bearing if it mutates state or changes source-of-truth pressure.
- Retry remains outside restore/reopen authorization.

## Recovery Versus Rehydration Findings

- Rehydration reconstructs usable state from stored, cached, recovered, or loaded material.
- Rehydration is not authority resumption by default.
- Rehydration may produce a working view without resolving current source of truth.
- Rehydrated structure may be recomputed, stale, partial, or invalid.
- Rehydration must not authorize Story Unit persistence, structural canon, retrieval execution, or topology.
