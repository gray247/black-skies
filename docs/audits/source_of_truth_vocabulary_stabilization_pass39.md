## Vocabulary Stabilization Model

This model is provisional only. It reduces semantic compression and accidental authority implication without normalizing provisional language into canon.

Vocabulary is grouped by what it tends to imply:
- `Authority Vocabulary`: current, authoritative, canonical, accepted, active, source of truth.
- `Recovery / Restoration Vocabulary`: restored, recovered, reopened, resumed, rehydrated.
- `Invalidity / Staleness Vocabulary`: stale, invalid, unresolved, orphaned.
- `Derived-State Vocabulary`: recomputed, advisory, visible, retrievable, grouped.
- `Persistence / Identity Vocabulary`: persisted, continuity, canonical, source of truth.

Core distinctions:
- visibility is not authority
- continuity is not persistence
- recomputation is not durable identity
- restoration is not replacement
- navigation is not authority resumption
- stale meaning is not current authority
- advisory structure is not accepted structure
- grouped retrieval is not object identity

## Executive Findings

- The highest vocabulary risk is authority compression: words like `current`, `active`, `accepted`, `authoritative`, `canonical`, and `source of truth` can collapse distinct governance families into one perceived truth state.
- Recovery words create legitimacy drift when `restored`, `recovered`, `resumed`, `reopened`, and `rehydrated` sound like successful authority resumption.
- Retrieval words create identity drift when `visible`, `retrievable`, `grouped`, `orphaned`, and `invalid` imply persistent structural objects.
- Continuity words create persistence drift when narrative or structural coherence sounds like durable identity.
- Recomputed and advisory terms must stay visibly weaker than accepted, current, or source-of-truth terms.
- The safest provisional vocabulary stance is to prefer condition and relationship language over authority language until source-of-truth rules stabilize.

## Highest-Risk Vocabulary Ambiguities

- `current` versus `active`: active can mean visible or selected, while current should imply governing state only when explicitly bounded.
- `authoritative` versus `accepted`: accepted may mean user-approved candidate, while authoritative implies governing source of truth.
- `canonical` versus `source of truth`: canonical implies settled law; source of truth may still be contextual and provisional.
- `resumed` versus `reopened`: reopened may be navigation; resumed implies authority continuity.
- `restored` versus `recovered`: restored can imply replacement; recovered can imply availability after failure without replacement.
- `rehydrated` versus `resumed`: rehydrated means reconstructed usable state, not resumed authority.
- `stale` versus `invalid`: stale may remain meaningful; invalid should not automatically imply deletion or repair need.
- `unresolved` versus `orphaned`: unresolved means classification failure; orphaned implies detached identity pressure.
- `recomputed` versus `persisted`: recomputed may appear stable but is not durable identity.
- `visible` versus `retrievable`: visible pressure is weaker than retrievable pressure, but neither authorizes identity or execution.
- `grouped` versus `object set`: grouped retrieval or structure is not stable object identity.
- `continuity` versus `persistence`: continuity can survive without durable stored identity.

## Vocabulary Families That Appear Equivalent But Are Not

- `current`, `active`, and `visible`:
  - current should remain reserved for governing state pressure
  - active may mean selected, focused, loaded, or in-use
  - visible only means exposed
- `authoritative`, `canonical`, and `accepted`:
  - authoritative implies governing truth
  - canonical implies stabilized law
  - accepted may only mean a candidate was chosen within a bounded flow
- `restored`, `recovered`, and `rehydrated`:
  - restored implies prior state introduced back into relation with current work
  - recovered implies failure-state material is available
  - rehydrated implies usable state reconstructed without authority guarantee
- `reopened`, `resumed`, and `navigated`:
  - reopened means loaded or returned to
  - resumed implies authority or continuity continuation
  - navigated means moved to a location or context
- `stale`, `invalid`, `unresolved`, and `orphaned`:
  - stale can remain meaningful
  - invalid is not current for a bounded claim
  - unresolved cannot yet be classified
  - orphaned suggests identity pressure but does not prove persistence
- `recomputed`, `retrievable`, and `persisted`:
  - recomputed is derived
  - retrievable is discoverable
  - persisted is durable and stored

## Legitimacy-Drift Terminology Risks

- `current` can imply source-of-truth authority when it only means displayed.
- `active` can imply permission when it only means selected or focused.
- `canonical` can prematurely freeze provisional reconstruction findings.
- `accepted` can imply applied mutation when it may only mean chosen candidate.
- `restored` can imply successful replacement even when state is copied, stale, or comparison-only.
- `reopened` can imply harmless navigation when it may resume or replace authority.
- `recovered` can imply safe use when it may only mean material was found.
- `rehydrated` can imply resumed authority when it may only mean usable state was reconstructed.
- `retrievable` can imply persistent identity.
- `grouped` can imply stable object set or batch authority.
- `orphaned` can imply persistent object loss.
- `continuity` can imply durable identity or correctness.

- Bounded support condition language that explains what is unavailable, incomplete, stale, or failed without implying repair authority.

- Candidate language for generated or rewritten outputs before apply/acceptance.
- Advisory language for critique, interpretation, and structural suggestions when it remains non-authoritative.
- Retrieval pressure language in reconstruction docs when clearly labeled provisional and non-implementing.
- Recovery exception language that remains contextual and does not normalize recovery as ordinary workflow.

## Contradictions Found

- The system needs clearer terms, yet stabilizing terms too strongly can make provisional governance sound canonical.
- Users need to know what is current, yet `current` can overclaim source-of-truth authority.
- Recovery language must be understandable, yet plain words like restored and recovered imply success or safety.
- Retrieval language must explain absence and invalidity, yet missing/invalid/orphaned terms imply persistent object identity.
- Continuity language is necessary for narrative work, yet it can imply persistence or correctness.
- Recomputed results may be useful and current enough for analysis, yet they are not durable identity.
- Advisory structure may be helpful, yet structural vocabulary can make advice sound accepted or authoritative.
