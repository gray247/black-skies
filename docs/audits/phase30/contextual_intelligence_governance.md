# Contextual Intelligence Governance

Status: Draft
Date: 2026-05-22
Phase: 30 - GUI / Workflow Realignment Spec
Pass: 1A - Workflow Philosophy Codification

## Purpose

This document governs acceptable intelligence behavior in the workflow.
It defines what intelligence may help with and what it must not pretend to be.

## Acceptable Intelligence Surface Behavior

- reveal narrative structure or imbalance that may be hard to see locally
- highlight pressure, fragility, repetition, escalation, or pacing drift
- surface relationships, gaps, or hidden patterns as interpretive aids
- remain subordinate to the writer's judgment
- appear contextually or on demand rather than by permanent entitlement

## Prohibited Fake-Authority Behavior

- assigning quality grades or story scores
- implying omniscient judgment
- presenting uncertain interpretation as narrative fact
- behaving like a permanent supervisor over the Writing Surface
- escalating from suggestion to mutation without explicit user control

## Interpretation vs Grading

Interpretation:

- points to possible structure
- frames uncertainty honestly
- invites inspection and choice

Grading:

- collapses ambiguity into a number, rank, or verdict
- implies objective quality authority
- invites false confidence

Directionally, grading systems are rejected.

## Local-First Companion Expectations

- companion behavior should remain local-first where possible
- local-first should be legible rather than hidden behind magical framing
- any non-local escalation should preserve trust signaling and not masquerade as local intuition
- the companion remains assistive interpretation, not narrative sovereignty

## Workflow-Safe Analytics

Workflow-safe analytics:

- help the user inspect a live narrative question
- stay bounded in authority
- do not demand constant ambient attention
- do not imply that the system has determined story quality

## Acceptable Narrative Instrumentation

Acceptable instruments may expose:

- pacing drift
- tension buildup or flatness
- structural imbalance
- fragility in sequence or payoff
- pressure concentration
- escalation patterns
- narrative repetition or collapse

These are instruments for inspection, not verdict engines.

## Narrative Fragility Discovery Examples

Examples of acceptable discovery:

- a subplot disappears for too long
- escalation stalls between adjacent units
- a reveal carries no visible setup pressure
- emotional intensity clusters without release
- bridge material is missing between major turns

These examples describe possible structural questions, not quality grades.

## Pacing / Tension / Structure Interpretation Guidance

- phrase interpretation as possibility, tension, pressure, or mismatch
- avoid language that implies final literary judgment
- prefer "this pattern may indicate" over "this scene is bad"

## Explainability Expectations

- users should be able to see why an instrument surfaced a concern
- provenance and reasoning signals should clarify scope, not create theater
- explainability is required for trust, but explainability alone is not qualitative validation

## Visibility Escalation Rules

- contextual first
- pinned only by intentional user promotion
- advanced-only when density or ambiguity is too high for routine visibility
- hidden/background when a signal is useful but not attention-worthy

## User Trust Preservation Principles

- be explicit about uncertainty
- never pretend interpretation is judgment
- never let polish outrun evidence
- never let routing or provenance signals impersonate wisdom

## Summary Rule

Intelligence systems may help writers inspect story structure.
They may not pretend to grade story quality, assign certainty, or replace author judgment.
