# Pass 58 - Retrieval Grouped-Legitimacy Boundary

## 1. Scope Declaration

Pass 58 is planning/governance/docs-only.

No implementation is authorized by this pass.

This pass does not create final retrieval architecture.

## 2. Purpose

Grouped retrieval legitimacy affects source-of-truth pressure, persistence assumptions, recovery legitimacy, workflow-state canon, topology pressure, and future implementation review because retrieved material can borrow structure, continuity, and relationship confidence that was never explicitly authorized.

Without narrower legitimacy rules, grouped retrieval can make convenience structure look like persistent identity, make continuity summaries look like authoritative history, and make clustered relationships look canonical.

Pass 58 reduces one ambiguity cluster so future planning can distinguish retrieval evidence, convenience groupings, provisional groupings, navigational aids, inferred relationships, and true authority-bearing structure without pretending that final retrieval architecture or persistence behavior is solved.

## 3. Provisional Retrieval Definitions

Pass 58 defines these provisional governance meanings:

- `retrieval`: surfacing material for inspection, orientation, comparison, or bounded use; retrieval does not itself confer persistence, identity, or authority
- `grouped retrieval`: retrieved material assembled into a shared view for convenience, scanning, or bounded orientation; grouping does not itself confer legitimacy
- `clustered retrieval`: retrieval organized by similarity, proximity, inferred commonality, or repeated association; clustering does not itself confer canonical relationship
- `continuity summary`: synthesized description of how retrieved material may relate over time or sequence; continuity summary does not itself settle authoritative history
- `inferred relationship`: provisional linkage suggested by adjacency, similarity, sequence, or repeated co-occurrence; inference does not itself create canonical relationship
- `retrieval evidence`: the surfaced material or comparison basis itself; evidence does not authorize mutation, persistence, or workflow canon
- `retrieval authority`: explicit permission to treat retrieved structure or grouping as decision-bearing within named scope; authority must be granted, not inferred
- `provisional grouping`: temporary grouping retained for analysis or review while legitimacy remains unresolved
- `navigational grouping`: grouping used only to help locate or scan material; it does not imply persistence, identity, or workflow ownership

These are governance semantics, not UI labels or product copy.

## 4. Boundary Rules

Pass 58 preserves these boundary rules:

- grouped retrieval does not mean persistence
- inferred relationship does not mean canonical relationship
- continuity summary does not mean authoritative history
- retrieval visibility does not create authority
- grouped structure does not mean object identity
- retrieval evidence does not authorize mutation
- clustered material requires review before authority use

Additional boundaries:

- navigational grouping does not mean workflow legitimacy
- repeated retrieval does not mean durable object existence
- provisional grouping does not mean stable topology

## 5. Retrieval Classification

Retrieval-related surfaces should be classified as follows:

- retrieved structures: surfaced evidence or orientation material; not automatically persisted, authoritative, or identity-bearing
- grouped narratives: continuity-shaped provisional summaries; not authoritative history or workflow canon
- relationship clusters: inferred or convenience link sets; not canonical topology or stable object relationship
- continuity summaries: synthesized retrieval interpretation; not source-of-truth history by default
- search-result groupings: navigational grouping for scanning and location; not relationship proof
- retrieved historical material: historical evidence that may remain relevant while still not current or authoritative
- stale-but-relevant retrievals: non-current retrieval evidence that may support comparison or audit while remaining unsafe for direct authority use

Classification remains scoped. A retrieval grouping can be current as orientation evidence while remaining invalid as authority-bearing structure.

## 6. False Authority Risks

Pass 58 preserves these false-authority risks:

- grouped retrieval implies persistence
- continuity summaries appear canonical
- relationship clusters imply object identity
- retrieval grouping implies workflow legitimacy
- stale retrieval appears current
- navigational grouping becomes source-of-truth

Additional risks:

- repeated grouped retrieval can look like settled topology
- a polished continuity summary can outrank rougher direct evidence
- clustered retrieval can hide the difference between inferred linkage and explicit relationship authority

## 7. Authorization Interaction

Retrieval-related implementation remains blocked.

Grouped retrieval cannot silently become persistence authority, topology authority, workflow legitimacy, or mutation permission.

If grouping legitimacy, continuity status, or relationship status is ambiguous, the work must escalate rather than assume that retrieval structure is safe to treat as stable.

Authorization records that rely on retrieval surfaces must declare whether those surfaces are evidence-only, navigational, provisional grouping, continuity summary, or separately authority-bearing.

## 8. Reconstruction Control Map Interaction

Pass 58 supports sequencing by narrowing one retrieval ambiguity cluster that currently blocks source-of-truth, persistence-adjacent planning, workflow-state canon, topology, recovery interpretation, and later implementation-candidate review.

This pass does not authorize implementation, reopen blocked domains, or settle final retrieval architecture.

It exists so later planning can distinguish retrieval convenience from retrieval legitimacy.
