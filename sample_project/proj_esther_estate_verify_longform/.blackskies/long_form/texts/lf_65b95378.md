The air in the workshop was thick with the scent of cedar shavings, each breath heavy with the musky aroma of freshly cut wood. Sunlight streamed through the dusty windowpanes, illuminating motes that danced lazily in the warm afternoon glow. Ethan stood at the workbench, the cool steel of his chisel biting into his palm, a reassuring weight that grounded him in the moment. He turned the small block of wood over, examining its grain, the knots like tiny, weathered scars telling stories of storms weathered and seasons passed.

“Not bad for a day’s work,” he murmured to himself, though the words felt hollow, echoing off the bare walls. He wiped his brow with the back of his hand, smudging sawdust across his forehead. The rhythmic rasp of the rasp against the wood was almost meditative, each stroke a reminder of the silence that enveloped him. It was a solitude he both craved and feared, a sanctuary that could quickly morph into a prison if he lingered too long.

The door creaked open, and a chill gust swept through, carrying the distant laughter of children playing outside. Ethan glanced up, heart quickening at the sight of Sarah stepping into the light, her auburn hair catching the sun like fire. She hesitated at the threshold, her brow furrowed slightly, a mixture of concern and curiosity etched in her features. “You still at it?” she asked, her voice warm, yet laced with an unspoken question.

“Just finishing up,” he replied, forcing a smile as he set the chisel down. “Thought I could use the distraction.” The admission hung between them, an invisible thread woven from his unvoiced worries.
