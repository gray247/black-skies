Rain drummed against the cobblestones, each drop a tiny percussionist in the orchestra of the evening. Elara pulled her cloak tighter around her shoulders, the damp fabric clinging to her skin as she navigated the narrow, winding alleyways of the old market district. The air was thick with the scent of wet earth and spices, mingling with the smoky remnants of the day’s meals from nearby taverns. She could hear laughter spilling from an open door, punctuated by the occasional clink of tankards and the warm, inviting glow of lantern light spilling into the street.

As she turned a corner, she caught sight of a figure leaning against the weathered stone of a building, shadows pooling around him like ink. He looked up, his eyes glinting with a mixture of mischief and something more serious, and she felt her heart quicken. “You’re late,” he said, a lopsided grin breaking across his face. The light caught the edges of his jaw, accentuating the hint of stubble that spoke of a long day and a restless night ahead.

“Traffic,” Elara replied, forcing a lightness into her voice that she didn’t quite feel. She stepped closer, her boots splashing through a puddle, sending droplets skittering away like startled insects. The chill of the evening air wrapped around her, but the heat of his gaze held her steady. She could see the tension in his shoulders, the way he shifted his weight, as if poised to spring into action.

“Is it all set?” she asked, letting the urgency of the question hang between them. The distant sound of a bell tolled, echoing through the alley, marking the hour with a solemnity that felt too heavy for their light-hearted banter.
