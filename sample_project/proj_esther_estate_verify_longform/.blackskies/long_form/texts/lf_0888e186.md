“Could’ve been a different world,” she murmured, half to herself, half to the echoes that lingered in the air. The words hung heavy, a wish deflating into the evening chill. Jenna’s fingers brushed against the car’s door, the cold metal sending a shiver up her spine. She imagined the laughter of a family spilling from the backseat, the clamor of siblings bickering over who got to sit where. It felt like a dream she barely remembered, yet the ache of longing pulled at her heart.

“Just a memory,” she whispered, though she wished it could be more. The words felt hollow, swallowed by the silence surrounding her. She stepped back, scanning the expanse of the lot, the overgrown weeds creeping through the cracks as if reclaiming what had been lost. The weight of the world pressed down on her, heavy with the history of neglect and abandonment.

The wind shifted, rustling through the branches of nearby trees, a sound that felt almost conspiratorial. Jenna turned, her breath catching as she spotted a figure silhouetted against the dimming sky. A boy, perhaps no older than twelve, stood near the edge of the lot, watching her with eyes that glinted like shards of glass. He was thin, wearing a faded hoodie that hung loosely from his frame, his hands shoved deep into his pockets. For a moment, time seemed to still between them, a charged silence pulsing in the air.

“What are you doing here?” His voice was rough, like gravel underfoot, breaking the fragile stillness.

Jenna hesitated, her mind racing. Was he a threat? A remnant of this broken world? Or simply another lost soul wandering through the ruins? “Just… looking,” she replied, her voice steadier than she felt. “This place used to be alive.”

“I know,” he said, his gaze flickering away for a moment, the tension in his shoulders easing slightly. “Not anymore. Just ghosts around here.”

“Yeah.” She swallowed against the lump in her throat, feeling an unexpected kinship with this stranger. “Ghosts.”

He stepped closer, the fading light casting long shadows across his features. “You can’t stay here. They don’t like it when people do.”

“Who doesn’t?” Jenna asked, curiosity creeping into her voice despite the warning bells ringing in her head.

He shrugged, a flicker of something—fear? Defiance?—crossing his face. “The ones who used to live here. They get angry when you disturb their memories.”
