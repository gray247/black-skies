Lena stepped through the doorway, the coldness of the ceramic tiles biting at her skin as she navigated the cluttered kitchen. A few mismatched chairs were huddled around a scarred table, remnants of family dinners now replaced by an unsettling quiet. The wind picked up outside, howling through the gaps in the old window frames, causing the frayed edges of the curtains to flutter anxiously, like flags waving in a storm. She brushed her fingers across the table's surface, feeling the roughness of the wood grain, each groove a testament to meals shared, arguments had, and silences that spoke louder than words.

Outside, the sky churned in ominous shades of gray, and heavy droplets of rain began to drum against the window, a soft but insistent percussion that built into a steady rhythm. Lena felt a tightening in her chest as she watched the clouds swirl, a heavy dread settling over her like a thick fog. She turned back to the kitchen, where the warmth of the wood stove offered a stark contrast to the chill that clung to her skin. With a flick of her wrist, she stoked the dying embers, coaxing them back to life. The flames crackled and spat, casting flickering shadows along the walls, momentarily dispelling the shadows lurking in the corners.

“Are you coming back in here or what?” a voice called from the living room, slicing through her solitude. It was Mark, his tone teasing but edged with impatience. She imagined him sprawled on the couch, drumming his fingers against the armrest, waiting for her to join him.

“Just checking the weather,” she replied, her voice light as she turned away from the window and walked toward the living room. The plush rug swallowed her footsteps, and the scent of old books and faint tobacco smoke hung in the air, wrapping around her like a familiar embrace. “Looks like we’re in for a storm.”

“Great. Just what we need,” he said, sarcasm threading through his words. His eyes flicked to the window and back to her, and in that brief moment, she caught a glimpse of concern in his gaze, quickly masked by a smile. “You know, I had plans for us tonight that didn’t involve being held hostage by a thunderstorm.”

Outside, the wind howled louder, a low growl that seeped through the cracks of the old window. Lena glanced toward the glass, where the world beyond was a blur of gray and brown, the trees swaying like dancers caught in a tempest. She pulled open the cupboard, the hinges protesting with a shriek, revealing a collection of chipped mugs and plates, remnants of a time when the kitchen had been a sanctuary rather than a mausoleum. She reached for a mug, its ceramic smooth and cool against her palm, and filled it with water from the faucet, the metal handle clanking as she turned it.

The water splashed into the mug, the sound a sharp contrast against the quiet, a reminder of the life that once thrived here. She watched the ripples settle, her reflection wavering in the surface—eyes wide, hair tousled, a stranger staring back. She took a sip, the water cold and refreshing, but it did little to quench the dryness in her throat, a lingering ache that seemed to grow with every passing second.

“Lena?” The voice came from the hallway, hesitant yet familiar, breaking the spell of her thoughts. It was Oliver, his tone heavy with an unspoken question. She set the mug down abruptly, the sound of ceramic against wood jarring in the stillness.

“Yeah?” She turned, forcing a smile that felt brittle on her lips.
