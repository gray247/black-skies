Sunlight streamed through the tall windows, casting a golden hue on the worn wooden tables, each scar and scratch telling stories of countless meetings and whispered secrets. She paused, letting the comfort of the place sink in, her breath hitching slightly as she took in the scene—baristas moving in a choreographed rhythm, steam curling up from the espresso machine in lazy spirals, and the soft murmur of conversation blending seamlessly with the clinking of cups.

She spotted a corner booth, the one they always occupied, and her heart twisted with an ache that felt both foreign and achingly familiar. The table was set for two, but today it felt like a stage with a missing performer. She sank into the leather seat, its surface cool against her skin, and wrapped her fingers around the ceramic mug that had been placed in front of her. The heat radiated into her palms, grounding her, yet her thoughts fluttered like moths drawn to a flame, restless and agitated.

The barista was a young man with tousled hair and an easy smile, but Lila found it hard to return the gesture. “What can I get you?” he asked, his voice cheerful, a stark contrast to the knot tightening in her chest.

“Just a black coffee, please,” she replied, her voice steadier than she felt. As he moved away, she caught a glimpse of herself in the reflection of the window—a woman with tired eyes and a heart weighed down by unsaid words.
