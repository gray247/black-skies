The air was thick with the scent of damp earth and decaying leaves, a musty perfume released by the recent rain. As Lena stepped carefully across the moss-covered stones of the old footbridge, her heart drummed a frantic rhythm in her chest, each thud echoing in the stillness of the forest. The canopy above whispered secrets, branches swaying gently as if conspiring, the shadows deepening with the fading light. She paused, the cool breeze ruffling her hair, bringing with it the faintest hint of something sweet, a fleeting memory that left her feeling both comforted and unsettled.

“Lena!” The voice cut through the quiet like a knife. It was Jake, his tone a mixture of urgency and worry, drawing her back from the edge of her thoughts. He emerged from the underbrush, mud smeared across his cheeks, his shirt clinging to him as though he had wrestled with the very elements. “You can’t just wander off like that.”

She turned to face him, her brows knitting together. “I needed a moment. It’s so... heavy here.” Her voice trembled slightly, the weight of the woods pressing in around them, and she found herself wishing for the sharp, clean lines of the city, where the air felt less dense and the sky more open.

“Yeah, I get it. But we’re not alone.” Jake scanned the tree line, his eyes darting as if danger lurked just beyond the shadows. “We should stick together.”

Lena swallowed, feeling the chill settle in her bones. “Do you really think...?” She hesitated, the unspoken fear lingering between them, a palpable tension. “That they’re looking for us?”

“They’ve been looking for us since we got here,” he replied, his voice low, almost conspiratorial. “We can’t let our guard down. Not now.”
