It paused, regarding her with an air of disdain, before slinking away into the maze of crates. But the reassurance was fleeting. The air around her felt thick, clinging to her skin, and the scent of damp earth mixed with something sharper—a hint of decay, perhaps. She glanced at the crates, their edges splintered and rough, remnants of long-forgotten days. Were they merely discarded refuse, or were they holding secrets? Her stomach twisted at the thought, a reminder of how vulnerable she felt in this hidden world.

“Get a grip,” she muttered again, more fiercely this time. She needed to keep moving, to reclaim her purpose amidst the shadows. Steeling herself, Elara pushed off from the wall, her fingers brushing against the coarse surface as she straightened. Each step felt like a challenge against the weight of uncertainty pressing down on her. The alley stretched before her, a winding path of hidden corners and whispered dangers.

The flickering light from the market grew dimmer, replaced by the oppressive stillness of the alley. She could hear her own heartbeat, a steady drum echoing in her ears, each thud a reminder of her fragility.
