The wind whipped through the narrow alley, carrying with it the scent of rain-soaked asphalt and the distant hum of life beyond the brick walls. Elena pulled her jacket tighter around her shoulders, the fabric stiff against her skin, as she sidestepped a puddle that gleamed like a mirror under the overcast sky. The muted daylight cast a gray pallor over everything, dulling the vibrancy of the graffiti that splashed color across the walls. Each step echoed softly, a reminder of her solitude in a world teeming with people.

Her heart beat in time with the distant thrumming of music, muffled yet enticing, as if urging her to abandon the shadows for the warmth of the nearby café. She hesitated, her gaze flickering to the door framed with fairy lights that twinkled dimly, promising warmth and laughter. Just then, a burst of laughter erupted from within, followed by the sound of clinking glasses—a reminder of the life she often felt on the periphery of.

“Hey, you!” a voice called out, breaking her reverie. She turned to find Marco leaning against a lamppost, his dark hair tousled as if he had just rolled out of bed. There was a playful glint in his eyes, the kind that always made her stomach flutter. “You look like you’re about to steal the crown jewels or something.”

“Maybe I am,” she shot back, a smile tugging at her lips despite herself. “You never know what a girl might be up to in a place like this.”

“Right, right,” he said, pushing himself off the lamppost and stepping closer, the warmth radiating from his body contrasting sharply with the chill in the air. “But if you’re stealing jewels, I hope you’re sharing the loot. I could use a new watch.”
