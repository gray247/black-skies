He had a way of standing that made him seem both relaxed and ready to spring into action, like a coiled spring waiting for the right moment.

“Just trying not to trip over my own tools,” Calla replied, forcing a grin despite the knot of frustration tightening in her chest. She knew she wasn’t going to finish the project today, not with the way the day had slipped through her fingers like the oil she often cursed.

Calla rolled her eyes, even as warmth spread through her. “A graveyard? More like a museum of my failures.”

“Or a gallery of your genius,” he countered, a hint of mischief dancing in his eyes. He stepped closer, picking up a rusted gear from the floor and turning it over in his hands. “You keep this up, and I’m going to start charging admission.”

“Then you try again.” His voice was steady, grounding, as he set the gear down and met her gaze. “You’ve got more guts than anyone I know. You don’t give up.”
