Rowan moved deeper into the café, her footsteps muffled by the thick carpet, each step a small rebellion against the chill still lingering on her skin. She turned her face towards the warmth of the sun-dappled window, where the fading light flickered like the last notes of a favorite song. A small table near the window beckoned, its surface speckled with crumbs and the remnants of someone else’s pastry. She dragged a chair out with a soft scrape against the floor, the sound swallowed by the ambient noise around her.

As she settled in and pulled her coat tighter, she caught sight of the elderly man in the corner, his brows furrowed in concentration as he scribbled furiously in his notebook. The way his pen danced across the page stirred something in her—a memory of late nights spent lost in her own thoughts, words spilling onto paper like secrets meant only for her. She wondered if he was writing about love or loss, perhaps a moment that had long since faded but still lingered in the corners of his mind.

A barista with bright blue hair approached, her smile wide and inviting. “What can I get you?” she asked, her voice bright against the backdrop of clinking cups.

“Just a coffee, please.” Rowan’s own voice felt strange in the warmth of the café, as if it belonged to someone else entirely. “Black.”

“Coming right up!” The barista turned and moved toward the espresso machine, her movements quick and practiced, a ballet of caffeine and milk that was mesmerizing to watch. Rowan leaned back in her chair, allowing herself a moment to breathe, to let the aroma of rich coffee and baked goods wash over her like a comforting blanket.

The laughter from the group of students rose and fell like waves, filled with the kind of unrestrained joy she had almost forgotten. She could hear snippets of their conversation—plans for the weekend, inside jokes that sparked bursts of laughter. It felt both enchanting and stifling, the weight of her solitude pressing against her chest. She should be part of that laughter, she thought, but instead, she was an observer, an outsider peering through the glass of her own life.

When the barista returned with her coffee, the steam curled up in delicate tendrils, warming her face. “Here you go. Enjoy!” The barista’s smile was genuine, and for a moment, Rowan felt a flicker of connection, a reminder that she was not entirely invisible. “Let me know if you need anything else.”

“Thanks,” Rowan replied, and as the barista moved on, she took a deep sip, the bitter warmth flooding her senses. It was the kind of coffee that could wake the dead, and for a brief moment, the world outside the café faded, leaving only her and the rich, dark brew.

She pulled out her notebook, its leather cover soft and familiar beneath her fingers. The blank pages felt daunting, yet they also held endless possibilities. She flipped through the previous entries, catching glimpses of her scattered thoughts, half-formed ideas, and dreams that had yet to take flight. The ink stains, some smudged from a hasty pen, told stories of their own—a map of her journey thus far.

As she stared at the blank page, her mind drifted to the elderly man, still immersed in his writing. What was he conjuring? She could almost see the words taking shape in the air between them, a shared understanding that connected their solitary pursuits. Rowan felt a spark of inspiration, a soft push in her chest urging her to write something, anything.
