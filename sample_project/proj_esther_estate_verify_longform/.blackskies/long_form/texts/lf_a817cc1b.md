A sharp clang reverberated through the workshop as Calla dropped the wrench, its cold metal clattering against the concrete floor before settling among an array of scattered tools. The dim light cast shadows across the room, highlighting the chaotic symphony of half-finished projects and crumpled sketches that covered every surface. She paused, drawing in a deep breath, the potent scent of oil mingling with the rusted tang of old metal, a fragrance as familiar as the comfort of an old friend.

Outside, the city pulsed with life; the distant hum of traffic merged with the lively calls of street vendors peddling their wares. But within these four walls, time seemed to slow, the vibrant chaos of the outside world reduced to a faint murmur. Leaning against the cluttered workbench, Calla's fingers traced the worn grooves of a rusted gear. Its rough surface echoed the pressure she felt, a physical manifestation of her silent struggle against the weight of her father's expectations.

She glanced at the clock, its hands a reminder of the time slipping away, yet she lingered, tethered by the promise she had made to herself. Her father's voice, stern and unyielding, lingered in her mind—a constant reminder of the legacy she was meant to uphold, the family name that demanded honor and excellence.

"Calla?" The voice cut through her thoughts, warm and familiar. Finn entered the workshop, his presence like a breeze that stirred the still air. He dusted off his worn leather jacket, his dark hair disheveled, eyes twinkling with mischief. "Still at it, huh?"

"Just trying to finish this before the sun sets," she replied, attempting a smile that felt more like a mask than a true reflection of her feelings. She turned back to the workbench, gripping the wrench once more, though her mind wandered. "What about you? Shouldn't you be heading to that meeting?"

Finn shrugged, a playful defiance lighting up his eyes. "They can wait. I'd much rather be here, watching you tackle these mechanical beasts." He leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed, a lazy grin spreading across his face. "Besides, they don't know what they're missing."

Calla chuckled, the sound surprising her with its lightness. For a moment, it eased the tension coiling in her stomach. "You really think I'm tackling them?" She raised an eyebrow, the corners of her lips twitching upwards. "Feels more like a stubborn mule refusing to budge."

"Nah, more like a fierce warrior," Finn replied, stepping closer, the warmth of his presence wrapping around her like a comforting embrace. "You've got this, Calla. I've seen you work wonders with less."

Meeting his gaze, she found sincerity in his green eyes, sparking a flicker of hope within her. It was easy to forget her doubts when he was near, his unwavering belief in her serving as a shield against the world's skepticism. "Wonders? I’m just trying to make something that doesn't fall apart after one use."

"Even the smallest inventions can make a big difference," he said earnestly, retreating slightly to lean against the workbench, his hand resting on a half-assembled contraption. "You know that better than anyone. Just look at what your mom did with that old generator."

The mention of her mother sent a pang through Calla's chest, memories swirling like leaves caught in a breeze. Her mother's laughter, bright and infectious, echoed in her mind—a poignant reminder of the dreams they had once shared.
