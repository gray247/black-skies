The bell above the door jingled softly as Lena stepped into the café, the scent of freshly brewed coffee wrapping around her like a warm embrace. The air was thick with the rich aroma of dark roasts and sweet pastries, mingling with the faint undertone of cinnamon from the muffins cooling on the counter. She took a deep breath, letting the familiarity wash over her. This place, with its mismatched furniture and walls adorned with local art, felt like a refuge from the world outside, where the sharp edges of reality dulled into something softer, more bearable.

As she made her way to the counter, her sneakers scuffed against the worn wooden floor, each step echoing in the quiet morning. A few regulars occupied their usual spots, bent over newspapers and steaming mugs, their faces etched with the kind of contentment that came from routine. Lena’s heart raced slightly, a flicker of anxiety threading through her thoughts. She was here for a reason, and the weight of it settled heavily in her chest.

“Hey there, Lena,” called out Sam, the barista, his voice warm and inviting as he wiped his hands on a stained apron. “The usual?”

“Yeah, please,” she replied, forcing a smile, though her mind was elsewhere, tangled in the knots of what she had to confront. Sam’s easy demeanor was a balm, but it couldn’t completely dull the apprehension curling in her stomach. She watched as he expertly poured the dark liquid, steam rising in spirals, and felt a momentary pang of longing for something so simple yet so elusive: clarity.

“Everything okay?” he asked, his brow furrowing slightly as he slid her cup across the counter. “You seem a bit... off.”
