Olivia leaned against the cold, weathered stone railing of the old bridge, her fingers brushing over the rough texture, each groove telling stories of time. The river surged beneath her, a relentless cascade of water that crashed against the rocks, the sound filling her ears and amplifying the turmoil in her heart. She closed her eyes, inhaling deeply the mingled scents of damp earth and blooming wildflowers, trying to ground herself in the moment.

“Hey, you alright?” A voice cut through the roar of the river, startling Olivia. She turned to see Marcus, his tall silhouette framed against the fading light, concern etched across his brow. He stood a few feet away, hands shoved deep into the pockets of his well-worn jeans, shifting slightly as he awaited her answer.

“Yeah, I’m fine,” she replied too quickly, forcing a smile that felt fragile, like it might shatter at any moment. She turned her body slightly to face him, her gaze drifting reluctantly from the river's turbulent flow. “Just… thinking.”

“Thinking or overthinking?” He stepped closer, the crunch of gravel underfoot breaking the silence that lingered between them. The soft glow of dusk highlighted the angles of his face, but the worry in his expression cast a shadow over the warmth.

“Probably overthinking,” she admitted, a hollow laugh escaping her lips. She couldn’t resist glancing back at the river, its ceaseless motion a stark reminder of her own stagnant worries. “It’s just… everything. You know?”

Marcus leaned against the railing, mirroring her earlier stance, the stone cool against his palms. “Everything can be a lot sometimes,” he said, his voice low and intimate, as if they were sharing a secret only they understood. “But you can’t let it pull you under.”

As he spoke, a sudden gust of wind swept through the bridge, rustling the leaves overhead and sending a shiver down her spine. She shivered again, not entirely from the chill. “I don’t know how to stay afloat,” she whispered, her voice barely rising above the roar of the river, which felt like the very currents of her thoughts threatening to drag her down.

“You’re stronger than you realize.” His words were steady, filled with a certainty that both comforted and unsettled her. She wished she could embrace that belief, to see it as a lifeline rather than a fragile promise, but doubt gnawed at her from within.

“What if I’m not?” she shot back, sharper than she intended. She turned to him, searching his eyes for any sign of uncertainty, any hint that might validate her fears. “What if I drown?”

“Then I’ll jump in after you,” he said, a half-smile breaking through the weight of the moment. “But let’s not test that theory just yet.” The warmth of his words wrapped around her like a protective blanket, yet the tension in her chest remained.

“Thanks, Marcus. I really appreciate it.” She tried to infuse her tone with sincerity, but deep down, a part of her felt like an imposter, wearing a mask that was too tight to breathe behind. The light continued to fade, shadows creeping in, and she couldn’t shake the fear of what might lurk in the dark.
