Max lingered on the threshold, heart thrumming against his ribcage like a caged bird, the familiar scent of aged wood and lingering dust wrapping around him like a shroud. He squinted into the murky depths of the room, where shadows clung to the corners like secrets waiting to be unveiled. A flicker of movement caught his eye—a feathered silhouette darting from one end of the room to the other, only to vanish behind a half-opened window.

“Max?” A voice broke through the silence, laced with an edge of impatience. It was Claire, standing just behind him, her brow furrowed as she peered into the gloom. “Are you coming in or just planning to stand there all day?”

He took a breath, the air thick and musty, tasting faintly of mildew. “Just... taking it all in,” he replied, his voice steadier than he felt. It was a lie, but one that felt comfortable in the moment. He stepped inside, the floorboards sighing beneath his weight, as if they too were reluctant to bear the memories that hung in the air.

Claire followed him, her footsteps light yet purposeful, and he could hear the soft rustle of her clothes, the faint scent of lavender lingering in her wake. “This place gives me the creeps,” she said, her voice barely above a whisper as she glanced around. “How can you stand it?”
