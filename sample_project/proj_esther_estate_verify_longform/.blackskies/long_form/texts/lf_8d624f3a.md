The bell above the café door jingled as Rowan stepped inside, shaking off the late afternoon chill that clung to her like a damp cloak. The warm air enveloped her, heavy with the scent of roasted coffee beans and something sweet, perhaps cinnamon. She paused, taking a moment to absorb the lively chatter and the clinking of cups, the sounds blending into a comforting hum that felt like home.

At the far end, a group of college students huddled over textbooks, their laughter punctuating the air with bursts of energy, while at a corner table, an elderly man scribbled in a notebook, pausing occasionally to sip from a chipped mug. Rowan found it all mesmerizing, the way life unfolded in layers around her, each person a story in motion.

She stepped further in, her boots scuffing softly against the worn wooden floor, and made her way to the counter. The barista, a young woman with turquoise hair and a nose ring, flashed a welcoming smile. “What can I get you?” Her voice was bright, almost musical, cutting through the noise.

“Just a small black coffee, please,” Rowan replied, her voice barely rising above the murmur. She could feel the warmth of the café seeping into her bones, loosening the tension that had settled there since she left work.

As the barista prepared her drink, Rowan's gaze drifted to the window, where the world outside seemed to darken, the sky a bruised purple as the sun retreated. She could almost taste the coming rain—sharp and electric against the backdrop of a long day. The thought sent a shiver through her, not of fear, but of readiness, as if the storm would wash away the weight she carried.

“Here you go,” the barista said, sliding the steaming mug across the counter. Rowan wrapped her fingers around it, reveling in the heat that radiated from the ceramic. “Need a pastry to go with that?” the barista added, tilting her head slightly, the light catching the silver studs on her earlobes.

Rowan hesitated, her stomach fluttering with a mix of hunger and the lingering remnants of anxiety. “Maybe just a scone,” she said, her voice steadier now. “The blueberry one, if you have it.”

“Coming right up!” The barista’s enthusiasm was infectious, her movements quick and precise as she rummaged through a glass display case.

Rowan turned away, scanning the café. Her heart beat a little faster as she noticed a familiar figure seated at a table near the back. Alex. He was hunched over a laptop, the glow of the screen illuminating his features, making the shadows beneath his eyes more pronounced. She felt an instinctive urge to retreat, to slip back out into the evening, but something held her in place—curiosity, hope, a pang of something else that she didn’t dare name.

Just then, Alex looked up, his gaze catching hers. A flicker of surprise crossed his face, quickly masked by a smile that was both warm and tentative. “Rowan! Hey, come join me.”

She hesitated, her mind racing with unspoken words, the weight of their last conversation still heavy on her chest. But the softness in his eyes drew her in, a lifeline in the swirl of uncertainty. She took a deep breath, the aroma of coffee filling her lungs, and made her way over, the mug cradled in her hands.

“Hey,” she said, sliding into the chair opposite him. “Working hard?”

“Always,” he replied, his smile widening as he gestured to the screen. “Just trying to finish this paper on time. It’s like fighting a losing battle.”
