The door creaked open, revealing a narrow hallway shrouded in shadows, the air thick with the scent of damp wood and something faintly metallic. A flickering overhead bulb cast jittery light across peeling floral wallpaper, the faded petals curling like forgotten memories. Clara hesitated, her heart thudding a staccato rhythm against her ribs as she stepped inside, the floorboards groaning beneath her weight like old men protesting unwanted visitors.

“Is anyone here?” Her voice, a tentative thread in the oppressive silence, echoed back at her, swallowed whole by the dimness. She strained to listen, the only response a whisper of wind rustling through a cracked window at the far end of the hall, carrying the distant call of crows from outside. They sounded almost like laughter, dark and mocking, and she shivered.

As she moved deeper into the hallway, her fingers brushed against the wall, tracing the damp spots where the wallpaper had begun to peel away. A chill settled in her bones, a gnawing anxiety curling around her thoughts. What was she even doing here? Memories of the last phone call with her aunt, the urgency in her voice, flickered through her mind like shadows flitting just out of reach. “You have to come. It’s important.”

She swallowed hard, her throat dry. “Important,” she murmured to herself, the word tasting bitter. The air felt thicker with each step, as though the house itself was watching her, waiting. The floor creaked again, this time beneath the weight of her apprehension. She rounded the corner into the living room, where the remnants of a life once lived lay scattered like fallen leaves. An old sofa sagged in the center, its upholstery threadbare and faded, a testament to years of neglect.

“Clara?” The voice, soft yet trembling, broke through the stillness like glass shattering. Her breath caught in her throat
