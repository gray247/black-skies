Rain drummed steadily against the cobblestones, each drop creating a chorus of sound that transformed the streets into a glistening puzzle of reflections. The puddles pooled like forgotten relics, capturing the dim glow of streetlamps that flickered like tired fireflies. A sharp wind sliced through the night, biting at Maris’s exposed skin as she hurried down the uneven path, her boots splashing through the shallow waters, each splash echoing in the stillness. The scent of damp earth mingled with the acrid smoke from distant bonfires, wrapping around her like a shroud of unease. She tugged her collar up tighter, the coarse fabric scratching against her neck, and glanced up at the sky, a swirling mass of charcoal and ink, pregnant with the promise of rain.

“Hey! Maris!” A voice broke through the rhythmic patter of the rain, pulling her focus. She turned, her heart racing, to see Elias emerging from the shadows of a narrow alley, his silhouette stark against the buzzing neon sign above, casting an erratic glow that flickered like a heartbeat. He was soaked to the skin, strands of dark hair plastered to his forehead, yet the fire in his eyes cut through the dreariness like a beacon.

“Elias!” she called back, a wave of relief washing over her as she quickened her pace, the tension in her chest loosening just a fraction. He was here, alive and unbroken, despite the storm that loomed over them. “What are you doing out here? It’s madness.”

“I could say the same about you,” he replied, a crooked smile piercing through the gloom, illuminating his features in a way that made her momentarily forget the chill. He stepped closer, the warmth of his body a stark contrast to the cold that enveloped her. “You shouldn’t have come alone.”

Heat crept into her cheeks—not from the cold, but from the intensity of his gaze, as if he could see the tangled knots of fear and uncertainty coiling in her gut. “I had to. We need to figure out what they’re planning. If they’re moving tonight—”

“Then we need to hurry.” His glance flicked over his shoulder, wary, as if the shadows themselves were listening. “I caught wind of something. They’re gathering at the old docks. Time isn’t on our side.”

The urgency in his voice sent a shiver coursing through her, a thrilling mix of fear and adrenaline. She inhaled deeply, forcing her racing thoughts to align. The docks loomed in her mind like a specter, a place where secrets festered and uncertainty twisted her stomach into knots. “What if they’re waiting for us?”

“Then we’ll be ready.” His conviction ignited something inside her, a flicker of hope amidst the encroaching doubt. “We’ve faced worse.”

As they navigated the rain-slicked streets, each step felt loaded with unspoken fears. Maris’s mind raced, the echoes of their last encounter with the Syndicate ringing in her ears—the chaos, the gunfire, how everything had spiraled out of control in an instant. She could still taste the metallic tang of adrenaline as it surged through her veins. They had escaped, but the scars remained, etched deep in her memory like a map of potential disasters.

“Maris?” Elias’s voice pulled her back from the edge of her spiraling thoughts. He reached out, his fingers brushing against her arm, grounding her in the present. “You’re with me, right?”
