The wind howled through the narrow alley, whipping trash against the damp brick walls as Lena pulled her collar tighter around her neck. The sky hung heavy with clouds, a slate-gray blanket threatening rain. She stepped gingerly over a puddle that mirrored the ashen sky, the chilly water splashing against her ankle. Each step felt like a countdown, the echo of her boots muted by the soft thud of her racing heart.

“Hey! You lost?” The voice broke through the gusts, startling her. A figure leaned against a lamppost, its flickering light casting a wavering halo around him. He was too close yet too far, shadows dancing across his features. She narrowed her eyes, trying to make sense of the smirk tugging at his lips.

“Not lost,” she replied, her voice steadier than she felt. “Just taking the scenic route.” She forced a laugh that fell flat, the tension in her stomach knotting tighter. The world felt too small, the alley too confining, and the air too thick with unspoken words.

He pushed himself off the post, taking a step closer, the light illuminating the scruff on his jaw and the glint of curiosity in his eyes. “Scenic, huh? Might want to steer clear of these parts then. Not much to see but trouble.” His tone was teasing, but there was an edge, a warning that slipped beneath the surface.

Lena shifted her weight, feeling the cool metal of her keys jab into her palm. “Trouble seems to find everyone these days,” she muttered, glancing down the alley as if the shadows might give her a clue to her next move. Her pulse quickened. She felt exposed, the weight of his gaze heavy on her skin.
