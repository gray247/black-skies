Clara knelt beside a cluster of lavender, the soft hum of bees dancing lazily from flower to flower. She watched them, mesmerized by their methodical movements, as if each tiny creature held a secret to the universe. The sun dipped low on the horizon, casting a warm, golden hue over the field, illuminating the lavender in a radiant glow that seemed to pulse with life. She inhaled deeply, letting the floral sweetness wash over her, but beneath the surface, something stirred—an echo of the chaos she had sought to escape.

“Do you ever wonder if they know?” a voice broke her reverie, startling her. It was Tom, his silhouette framed by the fading light, his hands shoved deep into the pockets of his worn jeans. He stepped closer, his presence both comforting and disconcerting, as if he were an anchor tethering her to this moment while threatening to pull her back into the depths of her thoughts.

“Know what?” she replied, her voice barely loud enough to carry above the gentle rustle of the wind through the grass. Clara turned to face him, trying to read his expression, the way the corners of his mouth curled slightly, as if he were wrestling with a thought left unspoken.

“If they know what we’re feeling,” Tom said, gesturing toward the bees, which now buzzed busily around them. “All this chaos in our heads. Do they sense it? Or are they just happy to be alive?”

Clara considered his question, her fingers absentmindedly brushing against the soft petals. “Maybe they’re just... being. Maybe that’s enough.” She found herself wishing she could capture that simplicity, that untroubled existence, like the lavender swaying in the breeze, unaware of the weight of the world pressing down on her shoulders.

Tom knelt beside her, his presence warm and solid, and for a fleeting moment, she felt the urge to confide in him, to share the storm that raged within. Instead, she picked a sprig of lavender and held it up, letting the evening light filter through the delicate flowers. “I think they’re better at it than we are,” she added, a smile breaking through her contemplative frown. “They don’t worry about tomorrow.”

“Or yesterday,” he said, a hint of laughter in his voice. “It’s all just now.” He reached out, brushing his fingers against hers as he took the sprig from her hand, his touch igniting a spark that sent a shiver down her spine. “But we’re not bees, are we? We have to think, we have to plan.”

“Sometimes I wish I didn’t,” Clara murmured, her gaze dropping to the ground, where the grass was interspersed with wildflowers, vivid bursts of color that seemed to defy the order of things. “It feels like a trap, all this thinking.”

“Then let’s not think,” Tom said, his voice suddenly earnest, almost playful. “Let’s just... be.”

She glanced at him, surprised by the intensity in his eyes. The weight of his gaze held the promise of freedom, a momentary reprieve from the noise in her head. “Okay,” she whispered, and the word hung between them like a fragile thread.

He leaned back on his elbows, the grass cushioning his weight, and Clara mirrored his posture, both of them staring up at the sky, where the last remnants of daylight were being swallowed by approaching twilight. The soft chirp of crickets began to fill the air, a gentle symphony that blended with the rustling leaves, creating a melody that soothed her restless spirit.
