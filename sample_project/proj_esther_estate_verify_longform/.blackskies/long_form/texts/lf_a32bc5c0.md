The sun hung low in the sky, casting long shadows that danced across the cobblestone street as Elara stepped outside the cramped confines of the tavern, the heavy door creaking in protest behind her. The smell of roasted meat and stale ale clung to her clothes, mingling with the crisp autumn air that bit at her cheeks. She drew in a deep breath, the coolness invigorating, a stark contrast to the warmth she had left behind. The distant sound of laughter echoed from within the tavern, but outside, the world felt achingly quiet, save for the rustle of leaves caught in the gentle breeze.

Elara pulled her cloak tighter around her shoulders, the fabric scratchy against her skin, and turned her gaze towards the market square. Vendors were closing their stalls, their voices rising in a symphony of haggling and goodbyes. The vibrant colors of fruits and vegetables still lingered in her mind, but she had little coin left to spare. She rubbed the smooth, worn coin in her pocket, half wishing for a miracle to stretch it further than it could go.

A shout broke her reverie, and she turned her head just in time to see a group of children racing past, their laughter ringing like silver bells. One of them, a girl with wild, dark curls, stumbled and fell, skinning her knee. Elara winced, the memory of her own childhood tumbles surfacing—a fleeting moment of innocence now swallowed by the weight of her reality. She hesitated, the instinct to rush in and help battling against the fear that still clung to her like a shadow.
