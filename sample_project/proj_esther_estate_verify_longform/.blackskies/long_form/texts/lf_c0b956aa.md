“Ethan, wait!” she called out, her voice barely breaking the rhythm of the natural world around them. She grasped a low-hanging branch to steady herself, her fingers trembling slightly as she felt the rough bark beneath her touch. “What did you see?”

He turned, the intensity in his eyes making her heart race even faster. “It’s just up ahead,” he said, gesturing animatedly with a long arm, his excitement spilling over into nervous energy. The flicker of movement in his restless posture spoke volumes—he was both eager and terrified.

Clara’s pulse quickened as she followed him, dodging gnarled roots and thick ferns that reached out like curious fingers. The smell of damp earth intensified, mingling with the sweet scent of wildflowers that fought for sunlight. Each step felt heavier as the weight of his words settled on her shoulders.

They emerged into the clearing, and the creek sparkled in front of them, its waters glistening like shards of glass. Clara’s breath caught as she took in the sight—a cluster of stones, unusually arranged, and among them, something dark and glimmering caught the light. She felt a pull deep in her gut, a mix of dread and exhilaration.

“What is it?” she whispered, stepping closer, her heart thudding in her chest, the world around them fading to a dull roar. The creek babbled, its sound now sharp and demanding, drawing her in.

“I don’t know,” Ethan replied, his voice low, almost reverent as he knelt beside the water’s edge. He reached out, fingers brushing the surface lightly, as if not to disturb whatever lay beneath. “But it feels important.”
