It was a small place, no larger than a living room, with mismatched chairs and tables that leaned slightly to one side, a testament to years of wear and countless conversations. The hum of chatter filled the air, punctuated by the occasional hiss of the espresso machine, a sound that always reminded her of her grandmother’s kitchen.

She stepped inside, her boots squeaking against the polished wooden floor, and paused to let her eyes adjust to the dim light. A couple sat at a corner table, their heads bent close together, laughter spilling forth as they shared a secret. An elderly man read a newspaper, the pages crinkling softly as he turned them, his brow furrowed in concentration. The barista, a young woman with bright blue hair and a nose ring, flashed a smile that seemed to brighten the entire room as she poured a latte, its surface swirling with frothy art.

Lena approached the counter, her heart racing slightly, and placed her order. “Just a regular coffee, please.” Her voice felt small amidst the bustle. The barista raised an eyebrow, a playful challenge in her gaze. “Regular? You sure you don’t want a little something special?”
