The rain drummed a steady rhythm on the roof, a symphony of pattering that filled the air with a sweet, earthy scent. Clara leaned against the kitchen counter, her fingertips grazing the cool surface, tracing the edge of a chipped ceramic mug. Steam curled upward, twisting and curling like a wisp of memory, as she poured the last of the coffee into it. The dark liquid swirled, almost as murky as her thoughts, thick with the weight of the past few days.

Outside, the world blurred into a watercolor painting, the trees swaying under the weight of the rain, their leaves glistening like emeralds. She watched them, entranced, the way they danced and bowed, as if in conversation with the sky. Every droplet that hit the window sent a shiver through her, a reminder of the storm brewing not just outside, but within her as well. She took a sip, the warmth spreading through her, a fleeting comfort against the chill that had wrapped itself around her heart.

“Clara?” The voice broke through the silence, soft but edged with urgency. It was Emma, her sister, standing in the doorway, water beading on her shoulders, her hair damp and wild. “Did you hear what I said?”

Clara blinked, pulling herself from the haze of her thoughts. “Sorry, what?” She set the mug down, the clink echoing in the stillness, and met Emma’s gaze, a mixture of concern and determination etched across her features.

“I said we need to talk about Dad,” Emma pressed, stepping further into the kitchen, shaking droplets from her hair. Clara felt the air shift, the tension coiling between them, thick and palpable. “I can’t keep pretending everything’s fine. It’s not.”
