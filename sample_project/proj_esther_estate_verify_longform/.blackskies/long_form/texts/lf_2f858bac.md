The air was thick with the smell of damp earth and decaying leaves, a rich loam that clung to the back of her throat as Elara knelt beside the gnarled roots of an ancient oak. She brushed aside a tangle of ivy, its leaves slick and unyielding under her fingers. The forest was alive, each rustle of the underbrush or distant call of a bird echoing like whispers of secrets shared among the trees. Sunlight filtered through the canopy above, dappling the ground in pools of gold and shadow, illuminating the small clearing where she had sought refuge.

A sharp crack echoed to her left, and her heart quickened, the pulse thrumming in her ears. She glanced up, instinctively drawing her knees closer to her chest. A moment later, a young deer stepped hesitantly into view, its dark eyes wide and alert, muscles rippling beneath its tawny coat. Elara held her breath, captivated by its fragile grace. For a heartbeat, the world condensed to that singular moment, the two of them suspended in an unspoken understanding of caution and curiosity.

“Hey there,” she whispered, though she knew it could hear the tremor in her voice. The deer twitched its ears, the tension palpable as it weighed the risk of approaching. Elara’s fingers itched to reach out, to bridge the gap between wildness and the tenderness she felt stirring inside her. But she stilled herself, allowing the silence to deepen, to settle like the mist that clung to the forest floor.

Then, as if deciding she was not a threat, the deer took a tentative step forward, its hooves making soft impressions in the damp soil. Elara watched, her heart swelling with a bittersweet ache that pulsed beneath the surface of her thoughts.
