Clara leaned closer, her fingers brushing against the text, tracing the faded ink as if it might whisper secrets long forgotten. The scent of damp earth wafted in from the garden, a reminder of the rain that had soaked the ground just hours before. She could almost taste the petrichor, a bittersweet tang that mingled with the faint aroma of the chamomile tea cooling beside her.

Outside, shadows danced under the flickering lamplight, the trees swaying in a rhythm that seemed both chaotic and choreographed. Clara’s heart quickened, matching the pulse of the world beyond the glass, an unspoken connection that tugged at her thoughts. She leaned back, letting the chair creak softly beneath her weight, its familiar protest a comfort in the silence that enveloped the room.

“Are you going to just sit there all night?” a voice broke through, thick with the warmth of familiarity. It was Lena, her sister, standing in the doorway, arms crossed defiantly. The dim light caught the edges of her tousled hair, creating a halo that momentarily softened the sharpness of her expression.

Clara glanced up, a half-smile flickering on her lips. “Just lost in thought,” she replied, trying to mask the heaviness that settled in her chest. “You know how it is.”
