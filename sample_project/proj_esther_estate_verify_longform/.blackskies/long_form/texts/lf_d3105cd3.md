The rain drummed a relentless tattoo against the cobblestones, each drop a tiny percussionist in the symphony of the evening. Beneath the awning of the crumbling café, Maris huddled, her coat pulled tight around her as she watched the world blur through the sheets of water cascading from the eaves. The aroma of wet earth mingled with the faint sweetness of pastries from inside, a tantalizing reminder of warmth just beyond her reach. She wrapped her fingers around the paper cup, the heat seeping into her palms, yet the steam that curled up toward her face felt like a fleeting ghost against the chill of the air.

A couple strolled past, their laughter bright and untroubled, a stark contrast to the weight settling in Maris’s chest. She caught fragments of their conversation—a shared joke, the flicker of affection in their glances—each note striking a chord of longing deep within her. She turned her gaze to the ground, tracing the rivulets of rain as they snaked their way down the stones, carrying away the grime of the day. What would it take to feel that lightness again? The question hung heavy in the air, mingling with the mist.

“Hey,” a voice broke through her reverie, warm and familiar. It was Jonah, his hair damp and unruly, a half-smile teasing at the corners of his mouth. “You’re going to catch a cold out here.”

Maris looked up, feeling a rush of something—relief? Disappointment?—as he stepped closer, shaking droplets from his coat like a dog emerging from a bath. “I don’t mind the rain,” she replied, forcing a smile that didn’t quite reach her eyes. “It’s the company I’m avoiding.”

“Right.” He leaned against the wall, the shadows playing over his features, deepening the lines of concern around his eyes
