She could hear the distant hum of cicadas, their incessant buzz rising and falling like a heartbeat, an unsettling reminder of everything she was trying to escape.

“I’m coming!” she finally called back, her voice carrying a touch of defiance that surprised even her. “Did you finish your homework?” her mother asked, her tone clipped, and Clara felt the tension shift in the air, thickening like the steam rising from the pot.

“Almost,” Clara replied, her heart racing. She could feel the lie curling in her throat like an unwanted vine. “I just need a little more time.”

“Time is something we don’t have, Clara. You can’t keep putting things off.” Her mother’s voice was steady, but the tightness around her mouth betrayed a deeper frustration. Clara felt the weight of those words pressing down on her, the unspoken expectations coiling around her like the tendrils of a creeping vine.

“Right.” Clara busied herself grabbing a glass from the cupboard, the clink of glass against glass loud in the stillness. She poured herself water, the cool liquid rushing down her throat, washing away traces of the day’s warmth. But nothing could wash away the feeling of being trapped in her own life, the walls of the kitchen closing in, the air heavy with unvoiced thoughts.

“Your father will be home soon,” her mother continued, her back still turned, the apron tied around her waist swaying slightly as she moved. “He’s had a long day, and I don’t want to hear about any more excuses.”

“Yeah,” she answered, her voice barely above a whisper. “I’m listening.”
