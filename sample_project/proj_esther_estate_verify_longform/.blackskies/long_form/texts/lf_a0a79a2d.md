Mara glanced at the puddles that pooled at her feet, the water reflecting the dim light of the sputtering streetlamp. Each ripple sent shivers through the surface, distorting the ghostly images of the brick buildings lining the alley. She hesitated, her breath hitching slightly as the chill nipped at her exposed skin. The wind howled again, a mournful cry that seemed to echo her own unease. With a reluctant sigh, she stepped forward, the sound of her boots splashing against the wet stones reverberating in the silence around her.

The alley narrowed ahead, the walls closing in like the pressing weight of her thoughts. She could feel the clinging dampness of the air, each inhalation heavy with the scent of earth and decay. It was a smell she had known since childhood, a reminder of forgotten corners and the secrets they held. As she walked, her fingers brushed against the rough surface of the brick, tracing the jagged edges, feeling the history embedded within.

“Where are you going?” a voice called from behind her, startling her from her reverie. It was Sam, his silhouette framed against the flickering light, a half-smile playing at the corners of his lips. She could see the glint of his eyes, bright against the gloom, and for a moment, that glimmer felt like a lighthouse beacon cutting through her fog.

“Just… out for a walk,” she replied, forcing the words past the tightness in her throat. She turned, trying to mask the nervous flutter in her stomach with a casual shrug, but Sam’s gaze was steady, searching.

“Out here? In this weather?” He stepped closer, the warmth of his presence a stark contrast to the chill that clung to the alley. “You know you shouldn’t wander alone. Not tonight.”

Mara’s heart raced, not from fear of the storm or the dark, but from the weight of unspoken things hanging between them. “I’m fine,” she insisted, though her voice wavered, betraying her. She wanted to tell him about the shadows that felt alive, about the memories that crept up on her uninvited, but instead, she waved a hand dismissively, as if to sweep away the heaviness of her thoughts.

“Fine? You look like you’ve seen a ghost,” he chuckled softly, but there was worry etched in the lines of his brow. “Come on, let’s get out of here. There’s nothing good waiting in dark alleys.”
