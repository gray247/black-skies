The sun hung low in the sky, a molten orb spilling gold across the cobblestones of the narrow street. Elara shifted her weight from one foot to the other, the cool stone pressing against her worn sandals, grounding her in the moment as she watched the shadows lengthen. A cart rattled past, the clatter of wooden wheels echoing like a heartbeat, mingling with the distant laughter of children chasing the last of the day’s warmth. She caught a whiff of fresh bread from the bakery down the way, the scent of crust and yeast swirling with the faintest hint of something sweet, perhaps cinnamon.

“Where are you lost in thought?” a voice broke through, its teasing lilt pulling her back. It was Finn, his hair tousled by the breeze, a challenge glinting in his eyes. He leaned against the weathered wall, arms crossed, as if he owned the street. “You know you can’t daydream forever.”

Elara rolled her eyes but couldn’t suppress a smile. “And you can’t stand still for more than five minutes, can you?” She stepped closer, the space between them charged with an unspoken familiarity, the kind that felt both comfortable and electrifying. His presence was a sunbeam cutting through the late afternoon haze; she could feel the warmth radiating off him, inviting yet a little daunting.

“Maybe I’m just waiting for you to make a decision,” he said, his tone shifting, a hint of seriousness threading through the lightness. “We can’t linger here forever. The festival starts soon, and you promised.”

The festival. The thought sent a ripple of excitement through her, but it was quickly tempered by a swell of anxiety.
