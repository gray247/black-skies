Jenna took a hesitant step forward, her sneakers scuffing against the gravel-strewn pavement. The distant sound of rustling leaves seemed to taunt her, reminding her of how the world had once been alive with color and laughter. She could almost picture children darting between the rusted hulks of cars, their carefree shouts echoing back to her in this desolate expanse. But now, the only company she had was the soft creaking of the metal, a mournful symphony of neglect.

As she moved deeper into the lot, the sun hung lower, casting a crimson glow that painted the edges of the cracked asphalt. It felt surreal, like stepping into a fading memory, the warmth of the sun contrasting sharply with the chill that crept into her bones. Jenna wrapped her arms around herself, a futile attempt to shield against the creeping darkness that seemed to settle in her chest. She paused, allowing her gaze to linger on a dilapidated old sedan, its windows shattered like the dreams of those who had once called this place home.

“Why do I keep coming back here?” she whispered to herself, the question hanging in the thick air, heavy with unspoken grief. The answer eluded her, as it always did. She kicked another pebble, watching it tumble away, wishing it could carry her burdens with it.

“Hey,” a voice called from behind her, rough-edged yet familiar. Jenna turned, her heart quickening at the sight of Leo, his silhouette framed by the dying light. He approached with a cautious gait, his hands stuffed deep into the pockets of his worn jeans. “You shouldn’t be out here alone.”

“Neither should you,” she shot back, trying to keep the edge in her voice, but it faltered as she caught the concern in his eyes. They shared a moment—a silent understanding that ran deeper than words.

Leo moved closer, the scent of smoke clinging to him, mingling with the stale air. “I thought maybe we could find something to salvage. I know a guy who might—”

“Salvage?” she interrupted, her voice rising with an urgency that surprised even her. “What’s left to salvage here, Leo? This place is a ghost of what it used to be.” The bitterness seeped through her words, and she hated how easily it came.

He hesitated, his brow furrowing. “I know, but sometimes—”

“Sometimes what?” she snapped, frustration bubbling over. “Sometimes we just cling to the past? Is that it? What good is it going to do us?”

The silence that followed was thick, filled with the weight of unspoken fears. Jenna felt her pulse quicken, the adrenaline surging through her veins like a tempest. She wanted to scream, to rage against the desolation surrounding them, but instead, she found herself staring into the depths of Leo’s eyes, searching for something—understanding, perhaps, or a glimpse of hope.

“Look,” he finally said, his voice low and steady, “I get it. This place... it’s hard to face. But we can’t just give up, Jenna. We can’t let it swallow us whole.”

She swallowed hard, the lump in her throat tightening as she looked away, unable to meet his gaze. The wind picked up, swirling around them, carrying with it the faint echo of laughter she had heard moments before. It was both a comfort and a torment, a reminder that life had once thrived here, that joy had once danced in the shadows.

“Maybe we can’t salvage the past,” she murmured, her voice barely above a whisper, “but what if we could find a way to create something new from it? A different kind of future?”
