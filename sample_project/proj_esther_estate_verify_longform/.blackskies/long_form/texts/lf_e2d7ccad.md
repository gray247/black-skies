The door creaked open, a reluctant whisper against the stillness of the room. Light pooled across the floor, casting long shadows that danced with the flicker of the overhead bulb. Ava stepped inside, her heart thrumming a nervous beat as the familiar scent of dust and aged wood enveloped her. The air felt thick, almost tangible, pressing against her skin like a forgotten memory.

She paused, eyes scanning the cluttered desk, where stacks of yellowed papers leaned precariously, as if waiting for a gust of wind to topple them. The faint scratch of a pen against paper echoed in the silence, a reminder of the words left unspoken. She took a breath, the musty air filling her lungs, and the taste of old ink lingered at the back of her throat.

“Are you going to stand there all day?” came a voice, sharp and unexpected, slicing through the quiet. Marcus sat in the corner, shadowed yet unmistakable, his brow furrowed as he scribbled furiously. He looked up, eyes narrowing, the light catching the edge of his glasses and illuminating the tension in his jaw.

“I wasn’t sure if you wanted company,” Ava replied, her voice steadier than she felt. She stepped further in, the floorboards creaking beneath her weight, as if protesting her intrusion. The warmth of the room clashed with the chill that had followed her in, a reminder of the storm brewing outside.

“Company is the last thing I need,” he muttered, returning his gaze to the page, frustration radiating off him like heat from a flame.

The sting of his words settled in the air between them, heavy and palpable. Ava’s fingers twitched, itching to reach out, to bridge the gap of silence that had stretched too long. “You can’t do this alone, you know. We’re in this together.”
