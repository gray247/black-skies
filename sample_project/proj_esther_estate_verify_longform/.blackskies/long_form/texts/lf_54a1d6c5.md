Nora stepped inside, the floorboards creaking beneath her weight, a low groan that resonated through the stillness. Each step felt like an intrusion into a world frozen in time, and she could almost hear the house exhale, releasing the stale air it had held captive. David leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed, his silhouette framed by the waning light. “Well?” he urged, a slight quirk at the corner of his mouth suggesting both impatience and something deeper—perhaps concern, or curiosity.

Nora’s gaze flitted across the room, absorbing the details as if they were brushstrokes on a canvas painted by ghosts. The musty scent of old wood and mildew tickled her nostrils, mingling with the faint aroma of something sweet and decayed that clung to the corners. She swallowed hard, her throat dry, and took a hesitant step further inside. “It’s…something,” she managed, her voice barely breaking the silence, each word feeling like a pebble tossed into a still pond.

“Something?” David echoed, pushing off the doorframe to approach her, his brow furrowing as he surveyed the space. “It looks like it hasn’t been touched in years. I think it’s more than just ‘something.’” He moved toward the armchair, running his fingers over the faded upholstery, a gentle touch that sent a shiver down Nora’s spine.

“Did you ever imagine it would be like this?” she asked, her voice quieter now, almost reverent. The air felt thick with unspoken stories, and she could almost hear the laughter of children, the echoes of a family that once filled this space with warmth.

“No. I thought it’d be…different.” His gaze fell to the empty fireplace, where the ashes of past fires lay like forgotten dreams. “I didn’t think it’d be so…abandoned.”

Nora’s heart tightened at the word. Abandoned. It hung in the air, heavy and suffocating. She stepped closer to David, drawn to the warmth radiating from him, a stark contrast to the chill that seeped from the walls. “Do you think they left on purpose?” she asked, the question slipping out before she could stop it.

“Who knows?” he replied, his voice low, thoughtful. “Maybe they were running from something, or maybe they just…stopped caring.” He glanced back at her, eyes searching. “What do you think?”

The weight of his gaze pressed against her, and she felt the urge to look away, to dismiss the question swirling in her mind. But the truth was, she had thought about it—about the things left behind, the lives untold. “I think,” she began slowly, “that sometimes people just get tired of fighting.”

David’s expression shifted, a flicker of understanding passing between them. The room seemed to pulse with the weight of their shared thoughts, the shadows deepening as the sun dipped lower. “It’s a hard fight,” he murmured, looking back at the fireplace, where the remnants of warmth were now just cold stone.

Nora inhaled sharply, the air thick with the scent of earth and decay, and her fingers brushed against the back of the armchair, feeling the texture of the fabric beneath her fingertips. “It feels like a graveyard in here,” she whispered, the words escaping like a confession.

“Yeah, but maybe it’s a graveyard for memories,” David replied softly, his voice almost lost in the stillness. “Not everything has to be buried.” He stepped closer, their shoulders nearly touching, and for a moment, the weight of the past felt lighter, like a shared burden they could carry together.
