The door creaked as Mara pushed it open, the hinges groaning in protest, and she stepped into the dimly lit room. Dust motes swirled in the faint beams of sunlight that streamed through the cracked window, casting a golden haze over the cluttered floor. The air was thick with the scent of aged wood and something metallic, a hint of rust clinging to the corners like an unwelcome guest. She hesitated, her heart drumming a nervous cadence against her ribs.

“Is anyone here?” Her voice sounded small, swallowed by the oppressive silence that followed. She took a cautious step forward, her sneakers squeaking against the old floorboards, which shivered underfoot as if they, too, were uncertain. The walls, lined with peeling wallpaper, seemed to lean in, offering their secrets to anyone brave enough to listen.

As she moved deeper into the room, her fingertips brushed against the rough surface of a forgotten table, the wood splintering under her touch. A flicker of memory washed over her—her grandmother’s stories of this very place, filled with laughter and warmth. But now, it felt like a tomb, each shadow a reminder of what had been lost. She swallowed hard, her throat dry, the lump of grief tightening there.

“Hello?” This time, the word felt more like a plea than a question. The silence that answered was suffocating, wrapping around her like a shroud. She stepped closer to the window, peering out through the grime, her breath fogging the glass. Outside, the world continued in its careless rhythm, oblivious to the weight that hung heavy in her chest.

“Mara?” A voice sliced through the stillness, low and cautious. It was Sam, lingering just outside the threshold. She turned, finding him framed by the door, a mixture of concern and something unspoken in his gaze.

“Did you find anything?”
