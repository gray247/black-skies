The sun hung low in the sky, casting a golden hue over the crumbling brick buildings that lined the narrow street. Dust motes danced lazily in the slanting light, and the heat of the day lingered, wrapping around Jessa like a thick, heavy blanket. She wiped the back of her hand across her brow, feeling the dampness cling to her skin. The faint sound of laughter floated from the café across the road, where a group of friends gathered around a weathered table, their animated voices punctuated by the clink of glasses.

With a deep breath, Jessa stepped forward, her sneakers crunching on the gravel beneath her. The air was thick with the scent of warm asphalt and distant spices from a food cart that had set up at the corner. Her stomach rumbled in response, but she forced herself to focus. She needed to find him. The urgency of that thought propelled her forward, past the café and into the shadow of the old oak tree that stood sentinel at the end of the street.

“Jessa!” A voice called out, sharp and familiar, slicing through the ambient noise. It was Marcus, his tall frame silhouetted against the light, arms crossed over his chest. She felt a rush of relief mingled with frustration. “You’re late,” he said, raising an eyebrow, his voice teasing but edged with something more serious.

“I had to finish up at the shop,” she replied, forcing a smile despite the unease swirling in her gut. The crispness of his shirt and the way he leaned against the tree made her acutely aware of the contrast between them—his polished confidence against her disheveled appearance, the remnants of flour still dusting her jeans from an earlier baking mishap.

“Right,” he said, nodding slowly, eyes narrowing as he took in her expression. “And how’s the soufflé coming along? Ready to impress the critics?”

“More like it’s ready to collapse,” she muttered, casting her gaze down the street, avoiding his probing look. The truth was, every time she stepped into the kitchen, she felt the weight of expectation pressing down on her. It wasn’t just about the food; it was about proving herself in a world that didn’t seem to care if she succeeded or failed.

“Hey.” His voice softened, the teasing tone replaced by a gentle seriousness. “You know they’re rooting for you. Everyone in town is. They want to see you win.”

Jessa bit the inside of her cheek, her heart racing in a way that had nothing to do with his words. “Do they?” she asked, a hint of doubt coating her voice. “Or do they just want a spectacle? It’s easier to watch someone fail than to actually try themselves.”

Marcus stepped closer, the warmth of his presence enveloping her like a summer breeze. “You’re not a spectacle,” he said firmly. “You’re talented. If you keep doubting yourself, you’ll never see that.”

His sincerity was like a balm, but it only deepened her internal conflict. She wanted to believe him, to grasp onto that flicker of hope, but the weight of her own insecurities held her down. “What if I fall flat?” she whispered, the words escaping before she could pull them back.

“The only way to fall flat is to never jump at all,” he replied, a hint of challenge in his tone. “You’re stronger than you think, Jessa. You just need to trust yourself.”
