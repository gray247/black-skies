The air in the dimly lit room was thick with the scent of damp wood and something faintly metallic, like the remnants of an old wound. A single flickering bulb hung from the ceiling, casting uneven shadows that danced across the cracked plaster walls, making the corners of the room seem to breathe. Olivia stood near the window, its glass smeared with grime, peering out into the dark street below where rain pattered softly against the pavement. Each drop seemed to echo a question she had no answer for, a rhythm she couldn't quite catch.

“Are you going to stand there all night?” The voice broke through her reverie, and she turned to find Marcus leaning against the doorframe, arms crossed, a sardonic grin pulling at the corners of his mouth. His tousled hair caught the light, making him look like a wayward angel, but there was a devilish gleam in his eyes that kept her grounded. “You know, standing in the shadows won't make the problems disappear.”

“Neither will pacing the floor,” she shot back, her words sharper than she intended. Olivia’s heart raced, not just from his interruption but from the weight of the night pressing against her. “I can’t help but think—”

“Thinking is overrated,” he interrupted, pushing himself off the door and stepping closer. She could feel the warmth radiating from him, a stark contrast to the chill that settled in the corners of her mind. “You need to act, Liv. You can’t keep waiting for something to happen.”

“Act?” She huffed, crossing her arms defensively. “And what, exactly, do you suggest? We storm the castle? Break down the door and demand answers?”

His expression shifted, the playful facade dropping momentarily. “I’m just saying, hiding away won’t get you anywhere. You can’t let fear rule your life.”

“Easy for you to say.” She felt the familiar bite of frustration creeping in, an old friend she had learned to live with. “You’re not the one who has to face... whatever this is.” Her gaze drifted back to the rain-soaked street, where shadows blended into one another, a murky reflection of her own turmoil.

Marcus stepped closer still, his voice dropping to a more serious tone. “I’ve seen how this eats at you. You can’t let it consume you. You’re stronger than this, Olivia. Remember that time in the woods? You didn’t back down when the storm hit.”

The memory flashed in her mind, a fierce moment of clarity amidst chaos. She could almost feel the weight of the rain, the thrill of adrenaline, the way she had fought against the wind, not just for survival, but for the sheer joy of reclaiming her own power. But that was a different time, a different her.

“Those days are long gone,” she murmured, almost to herself.

“Maybe, but the strength you found then is still in you. You just have to dig it up.” His eyes bore into hers, searching, urging. “You’re not alone in this. I’m here.”

The sincerity in his voice began to chip away at her resolve, a softening of the walls she had built around herself. She swallowed hard, the lump in her throat a reminder of the vulnerability she had fought so desperately to hide. “You think I want to feel like this? Like I’m stuck in a loop I can’t escape?”

“No,” he said, stepping back to allow her space, the intensity of his gaze easing. “But you have to choose to step out of it. You deserve more than this.”
