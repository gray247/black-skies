The rain hammered down in relentless sheets, each drop hitting the pavement with a sharp, staccato rhythm. Puddles formed in the uneven cracks of the asphalt, splashing icy droplets against the worn soles of Iris’s boots as she hurried down the narrow street, her coat pulled tight against the chill. Cold water trickled down her neck, contrasting sharply with the warmth of her breath, which clouded the damp air around her. She glanced up; the city lights flickered weakly through a curtain of gray, their glow dimmed like stars struggling to pierce a thick fog.

The flickering neon sign of the diner glowed ahead, a promise of warmth and familiarity amid the storm. But as she approached, a tight knot formed in her stomach, anticipation mingling with dread. The bell above the door chimed softly as she stepped inside, its sound almost swallowed by the comforting buzz of conversation and the rhythmic sizzle from the grill in the back. The aroma of bacon and burnt coffee enveloped her, both enticing and disquieting, as it clashed with the turmoil swirling in her chest.

“Hey, Iris!” called a voice from behind the counter, breaking her out of her thoughts. Sam, the burly cook with flour dusting his apron, waved her over, his grease-stained hand raised in greeting. His jovial grin felt like a lifeline amidst the chaos swirling in her mind. “You look like you’ve been through a storm!”

“Funny you should say that,” she replied, forcing a smile as she slid onto a stool. The vinyl squeaked beneath her, familiar and grounding, echoing memories of late-night coffee runs and whispered secrets over greasy plates. “Just the usual weather out there.”

“Coffee?” Sam asked, already reaching for a steaming pot, the rich aroma filling the air as he poured. She nodded, wrapping her hands around the warm mug he placed in front of her. The heat seeped into her cold fingers, a small comfort against the chill that had settled in her bones.

As she took a sip, the bitterness flooded her senses, pulling her deeper into the moment, if only briefly. It was a reprieve from the thoughts that clawed at her insides since she’d left the apartment that morning—thoughts that felt like restless ghosts circling her mind. She stared into the cup, watching the steam spiral upward, searching for clarity in the swirling haze.

A giggle broke through the murmur of conversation. Iris turned slightly to see a couple huddled in a far booth, their heads bent close together, sharing whispers and laughter like secrets meant only for them. Envy twisted in her gut, sharp and unwelcome, and she quickly looked away, focusing instead on the well-worn menu propped in front of her. Breakfast felt like a luxury she rarely indulged in these days, but the thought of golden pancakes made her mouth water.

“Thinking about something sweet?” Sam asked, leaning against the counter, his eyes glimmering with mischief. “I can whip up a stack in no time. You know you want it.”

“Maybe,” she murmured, her voice barely rising above the din, the weight of her thoughts pressing heavily on her. “Just coffee for now.”

“Suit yourself,” he replied, but the warmth in his voice lingered like the aroma of the brewing coffee. The diner pulsed with life—clinking silverware, laughter, and the soft hum of the jukebox in the corner. Yet, in that moment, it felt worlds away from her own turbulent thoughts.
