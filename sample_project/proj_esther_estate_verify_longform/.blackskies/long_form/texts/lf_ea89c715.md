Clara pulled her scarf tighter around her neck, the fabric scratchy against her skin, a small comfort against the chill that seemed to seep into her bones. She walked quickly, her footsteps echoing against the cobblestones, the rhythm punctuated by the distant clatter of a cart rattling over the uneven path. Each step felt heavy, weighted with the pressing urgency of her thoughts.

She rounded the corner, the flickering light of a streetlamp casting wavering shadows that danced along the walls of the old buildings. The stone faced her with an unyielding stare, its coldness reflecting the turmoil brewing inside her. Clara paused, her breath visible in the air, a cloud of uncertainty that hung for a moment before dissipating. A hard swallow lodged in her throat; she could almost hear the echo of her own heartbeat, a relentless drum urging her forward.

“Clara!” A voice called from behind her, sharp and familiar, slicing through the fog of her contemplation. She turned to see Isaac striding towards her, his hair tousled by the wind, eyes alight with a mix of concern and determination. “You’re late,” he said, but there was no reprimand in his tone, only a weary acceptance.

“I had to think,” she replied, the words tumbling out before she could catch them. She couldn’t meet his gaze, focusing instead on a flock of pigeons pecking at crumbs near her feet, their coos blending into the backdrop of the city’s murmur. “About… everything.”

“Everything?” Isaac stepped closer, the warmth of him almost tangible against the cold air. “That’s a lot to carry on your own. You know I’m here, right?”

She nodded, but the knot in her stomach tightened. The truth was a heavy stone lodged deep within her, one she feared revealing. “It’s just… I don’t know if I can do this,” she admitted, her voice barely above a whisper. “What if we’re wrong? What if we fail?”

Isaac’s expression softened, and he reached out, brushing a stray hair behind her ear. “We won’t fail. Not if we stick together.” There was a conviction in his voice that wavered under the weight of her doubt. “You have to trust me.”

Trust. The word hung between them, heavy and elusive. Clara took a breath, tasting the cool air, laced with the faintest hint of smoke from a nearby chimney. She thought of the plan they had crafted together, the late nights spent poring over maps and whispering secrets in the shadows. But now, the contours of their plan felt fragile, like the glass baubles that hung in shop windows, beautiful yet so easily shattered.

“I want to,” she said finally, her voice steadier now, though doubt still curled at the edges of her resolve. “But every time I think about it, I see the consequences.” Her words were punctuated by the sound of a distant bell tolling, each chime a reminder of the time slipping away from them.
