The rain fell in heavy sheets, each drop splattering against the cracked pavement with a sound like distant applause. Clara huddled beneath the awning of a shuttered café, arms wrapped tightly around herself, the damp chill seeping through her jacket. She peered into the gray, blurred world beyond the glassy surface of the street, where shadows of passersby flickered like ghosts in the haze. A gust of wind swept through, carrying the scent of wet earth and something sharp, like metal. It reminded her of the old playground, the rusted swings, and the laughter that once echoed there before everything changed.

A car rushed by, tires slicing through puddles, sending arcs of water spiraling into the air. Clara flinched, instinctively stepping back, the sudden splash striking her shoes. She glanced down at the darkening fabric, the tiny droplets clinging like unwelcome memories. “Just a stupid car,” she muttered under her breath, a futile effort to convince herself it was nothing.

Her phone buzzed in her pocket, a small electric jolt that made her heart race. She pulled it out, glancing at the screen, the name blinking like a warning light. She hesitated, feeling the weight of unanswered messages, the implications lurking in the silence. “You really should pick up,” she whispered, her voice barely rising above the rain. The thought of hearing his voice again made her stomach twist in an anxious knot.

As she turned her gaze back to the street, a figure emerged from the downpour, a tall man with a dark coat pulled tight around him, his face obscured by the collar. He stopped at the café entrance, looking left and right, scanning the streets. Clara's breath caught, the familiar shape bringing a rush of conflicting emotions. She tucked a strand of wet hair behind her ear, heartbeat racing. What was he doing here?
