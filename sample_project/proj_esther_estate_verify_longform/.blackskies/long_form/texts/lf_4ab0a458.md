Rain drummed against the roof in a relentless symphony, each drop a note falling into the quietude of the small room. Clara sat cross-legged on the worn rug, the damp scent of earth and wood mingling in the air around her, wrapping her in a cocoon of solitude. The teacup rested on the edge of the low table, steam curling upward like a specter, but the tea had long since cooled, forgotten in the swirl of her restless thoughts. She traced the rim of the cup with her fingertip, feeling the uneven grooves in the porcelain, each one a reminder of the countless times she had sought comfort in its embrace.

Outside, the world blurred under a veil of gray, the silhouettes of trees swaying gently, their leaves whispering secrets she could not discern. A sudden gust rattled the windowpanes, and she glanced up, her heart skipping slightly as she imagined someone standing just beyond the glass, watching her, waiting. Dismissing the notion, she turned her attention back to the small, flickering candle at the center of the table. Its flame danced, casting playful shadows that stretched and twisted across the walls, a fleeting performance that seemed to echo her own inner turmoil.

“Clara?” The voice sliced through the stillness, soft yet insistent, laced with a familiar urgency. It was Eliza, her sister, stepping cautiously into the room, her hair damp and wild, plastered against her forehead. “You’ve been in here for hours. You okay?”

“Yeah,” Clara replied, her voice barely above a whisper, a half-hearted smile tugging at her lips as she met Eliza’s concerned gaze. “Just thinking.”

“Thinking or brooding?” Eliza arched an eyebrow, crossing her arms, the light catching the silver bracelet on her wrist, a tiny jangling echo of her presence. Clara couldn’t help but admire how effortlessly her sister commanded attention, even in the dull light of a rainy afternoon.

“Maybe a bit of both,” Clara admitted, letting out a breath she hadn’t realized she was holding. She felt the weight of Eliza’s scrutiny, a familiar pressure that both comforted and irritated her. “I just... I don’t know what to do next.”

Eliza stepped closer, dropping onto the rug beside her with a soft thud, the fabric shifting under them like a living entity. “You know you don’t have to have it all figured out right now, right? We’re just… we’re in this together.”

Clara nodded, but the knot in her stomach tightened. The words were meant to soothe, but they felt like a lifeline tossed into an ocean of uncertainty. She picked at a loose thread on the rug, the fibers fraying under her fingers, mirroring the disarray of her thoughts. “It feels different this time,” she murmured, almost to herself. “Like everything’s shifting, and I’m not sure if I should stand still or jump in.”

Eliza’s brow furrowed, and she leaned forward, her eyes serious. “What’s really bothering you? It’s more than just the job, isn’t it?”

Clara hesitated, the truth lingering on her lips like an unspoken prayer.
