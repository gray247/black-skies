Clara traced the rim of her mug with her fingertip, the porcelain warm and smooth under her touch, a stark contrast to the chill that still clung to her bones. Outside, the rain drummed a steady rhythm against the café’s glass façade, creating a soothing backdrop that mingled with the low hum of conversation and the occasional hiss of the espresso machine. She stole a glance at the droplets racing each other down the pane, momentarily lost in their ceaseless chase, each tiny collision a reminder of the world’s relentless motion, even as she remained still, cocooned in this small sanctuary.

“Hey, you.” A voice broke through her reverie, warm and familiar, pulling her gaze from the window. It was Mia, her hair a wild halo of curls, damp at the edges from the rain. She shrugged off her coat, droplets splattering onto the floor like scattered ink. “I thought I’d find you here.”

“Just me and the rain,” Clara replied, a smile tugging at her lips. The corners of Mia’s mouth curled in response, her presence a welcome distraction from the shadows creeping around Clara’s thoughts. “What’s your excuse for braving the weather?”

Mia plopped down across from her, shaking her head as she peeled off her damp scarf. “I needed caffeine. Plus, I figured you could use some company. You’ve been hiding out too much lately.”

Clara’s smile faltered slightly, and she glanced back out at the rain, the droplets now blurring her reflection. Hiding, yes. More like retreating. “I guess I just needed a break from everything.” Her voice was softer than she intended, a flicker of vulnerability threading through the words.

“Everything?” Mia raised an eyebrow, pushing back the stray curls that had escaped her updo. “You mean that mess with Jacob? Or are we talking about the whole life crisis situation?”

A small laugh escaped Clara, but it felt more like a choke than joy. “Maybe both.” She took a deep breath, inhaling the rich aroma of coffee, grounding herself in the moment. “It’s just… I don’t know. I thought I’d have it all figured out by now.”

Mia leaned forward, her eyes sparkling with understanding. “Life doesn’t come with a manual, Clara. It’s messy, and sometimes you just have to roll with it.” She reached across the table, her hand resting on Clara’s for a heartbeat, a fleeting connection that warmed more than the mug in Clara’s hands.

“I know you’re right,” Clara admitted, biting her lip as she wrestled with the weight of her own unspoken fears. “But it’s hard not to feel like I’m falling behind. Everyone else seems to have their lives together.”

“Look around.” Mia gestured to the café, where laughter mingled with the clinking of cups and the soft jazz playing in the background. “Every single one of these people has their own struggles. You just don’t see them.” Her gaze softened, a hint of empathy shining through. “And you’re not falling behind. You’re just… taking a different path.”

The warmth of Mia’s hand lingered even after she withdrew it, and Clara let the silence settle between them, the sound of rain providing a gentle soundtrack to her thoughts. “I don’t want to be stuck, you know?” The admission slipped out before she could catch it, raw and unfiltered.

“You won’t be.” Mia’s voice was steady, a firm anchor amidst Clara’s swirling doubts. “You’re too strong for that. Just take it one day at a time.”

Clara nodded, her heart swelling with gratitude. She sipped her coffee, the rich bitterness grounding her, infusing her with a sense of purpose. Outside
