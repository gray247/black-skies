Clara set the mug down with a soft clink, the sound jarring against the persistent patter of the rain. She traced a finger over the table’s surface, the coolness of the wood a stark contrast to the warmth still echoing in her palms. The scratches formed a map of her history—each imperfection a reminder of laughter, tears, and the occasional outburst of frustration. She felt the weight of it all, a collective memory that seemed to press down on her as heavily as the storm outside.

With a sigh, she pushed back her chair, its legs scraping against the floor, a sound that startled her for a moment. The house was quiet, save for the rhythmic thrum of the rain. She stood and moved to the window, the chill creeping back into her bones as she gazed outside. The rain blurred the world into a watercolor painting, colors bleeding together in a melancholic haze. She pressed her forehead against the cold glass, the droplets tracing down like tears, and closed her eyes, letting the sound wash over her.

“Clara?” The voice was soft but carried a weight that made her heart skip. She turned, finding Amelia standing at the doorway, her hair a wild halo around her face, droplets clinging to her sweater. “You okay?”

“Just… thinking,” Clara replied, forcing a smile that didn’t quite reach her eyes. Amelia stepped closer, the warmth of her body a welcome contrast to the biting chill. There was concern etched on her features, a furrow deepening between her brows.

“About what?” Amelia asked, her tone gentle as she crossed her arms, the movement drawing Clara’s gaze to the way her fingers fidgeted against her sleeves.

“Just memories,” Clara said, trying to dismiss the heaviness lurking just beneath her words. “You know how it is when the rain hits.”

Amelia nodded, her eyes searching Clara’s. “You mean the kind that stick around when you want them to fade?”

Clara swallowed hard, her gaze dropping to the floor. She remembered the laughter of friends gathered around this very table, the clinking of glasses, the shared secrets, the promises made under the soft glow of the kitchen light. But alongside those memories were shadows, moments that felt too close, too raw. “Something like that,” she admitted, her voice barely above a whisper.

“I get it.” Amelia stepped forward, close enough that Clara could feel the heat radiating from her. “I know how it is to be trapped in your head. Want to talk about it?”

The offer hung in the air, heavy and inviting. Clara hesitated, the weight of her thoughts slipping just out of reach, like the rain pooling at the windowsill, waiting for a chance to spill over. “Not yet,” she finally said, her voice steadier than she felt. “Maybe later.”

Amelia’s expression softened, a flicker of understanding passing between them. “Okay. Just know I’m here.”

Clara nodded, grateful for the reassurance, the warmth of friendship that cut through the damp chill. She turned back to the window, the rain still pounding down, and watched as the world blurred even further. “It’s beautiful out there, isn’t it?” she said, almost to herself, as the trees danced wildly in the wind.

“It is, in a chaotic way.” Amelia leaned against the frame, her presence a comforting anchor. “Like life, I suppose. A little messy and unpredictable.”

“Yeah,” Clara murmured, the truth of it settling in her chest. She wrapped her arms around herself, feeling the vulnerability and strength in that messiness, the way it intertwined with her own stories. “I just wish the storms would pass more quickly sometimes.”

“Me too,” Amelia replied, her voice low.
