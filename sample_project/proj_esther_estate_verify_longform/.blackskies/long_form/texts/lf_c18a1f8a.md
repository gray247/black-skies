The wind whipped through the narrow alley, tugging at the hem of Clara’s coat as she shoved her hands deep into the pockets. The chill nipped at her cheeks, a biting reminder of the early autumn that had settled over the city like a grey blanket. She squinted against the dim light filtering through the overhanging awnings, the smell of damp earth and distant cooking wafting around her, mingling with the sharper scent of wet concrete.

“Hey! You got a minute?” A voice broke through her thoughts, pulling her back from the precipice of her own swirling worries. It was Marco, leaning against a graffiti-tagged wall, his dark hair tousled and his face flushed with the excitement of something unspoken.

Clara hesitated, her heart racing. “What’s up?” she replied, trying to keep her tone casual. The last thing she wanted was to show just how much she both craved and feared this conversation.

He pushed off the wall and stepped closer, the echo of his boots against the pavement loud in the silence that hung between them. “I just heard some news about the old factory on Fifth. You know, the one they’re supposed to demolish?” His voice lowered conspiratorially, and a thrill of electricity shot through her.

“What about it?” she asked, though she had a sinking feeling in her gut. She could almost taste the dust of that place, the ghosts of broken dreams and empty promises rising like the fog that often clung to the mornings.
