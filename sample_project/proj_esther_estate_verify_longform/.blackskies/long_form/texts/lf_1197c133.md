She turned, the creak of the porch beneath her feet echoing the tension in the pit of her stomach. It’s getting chilly.”

The coolness brushed against Clara’s skin, creeping through the thin fabric of her blouse. She could feel the weight of her mother’s gaze, heavy with expectation and something else—an unspoken worry that curled in the air between them, thick as smoke. “Just a minute,” Clara called back, her voice barely carrying over the rustling leaves. She didn’t want to step back into that cramped kitchen, with its bright lights and the smell of burnt garlic lingering like a stubborn ghost. The warmth inside felt suffocating, a stark contrast to the comforting chill of the evening.

With a deep breath, Clara leaned against the banister, the rough wood pressing against her palms, grounding her. The scent of damp earth and decaying leaves wrapped around her like a familiar shawl, evoking memories of childhood—barefoot summers spent chasing fireflies and the laughter that echoed in the dusk. She watched as the last rays of sunlight blinked out, leaving the sky a deep indigo, punctuated by the first stars twinkling hesitantly, as if unsure they should even be there.

“Clara!” her mother’s voice was sharper now, tinged with impatience, and Clara could see the way her brow furrowed, the lines of worry deepening with each passing moment. “I need you to help me with the dishes.”

“Okay,” she replied, but her feet remained rooted to the porch. It was easier to stay out here, where the world felt vast and open, than to venture back into the small, cluttered space that felt increasingly like a cage. The thought of the dishes made her stomach churn. The clatter of plates and the smell of soap felt like a reminder of everything she was trying to escape.

A gust of wind whistled through the trees, rustling the branches, and Clara shivered, not entirely from the cold. She could hear the soft murmur of her mother’s voice inside, a steady stream of words that seemed to flow like water beneath the surface, but Clara couldn’t quite make out the meaning. Was it frustration? Concern? Or just the mundane rhythm of a life that felt so ordinary it was suffocating?

“Clara!” The call was more insistent now, pulling at her resolve. She let out a breath she didn’t know she was holding, feeling the weight of her decision settling in her chest. “I’ll be right there!” she shouted, the words tumbling out before she could take them back.

She pushed herself away from the banister, the wood rough against her palms, and took a reluctant step toward the door. The kitchen was bright, almost blinding after the twilight, and the scent of burnt garlic now mingled with something sweeter—cinnamon, perhaps, from the pie cooling on the counter.
