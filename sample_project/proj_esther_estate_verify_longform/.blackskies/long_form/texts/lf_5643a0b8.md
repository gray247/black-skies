The door creaked open, an old hinge protesting against the weight of time, and Clara stepped into the dimly lit room. Dust motes danced lazily in the narrow beams of light filtering through grimy windows, catching her breath in a moment of stillness before the world outside rushed back. The scent of aged wood and mothballs wrapped around her like a familiar shawl, stirring memories she thought she had buried, and yet here they were, vivid as ever.

“Did you even bother to clean?” she muttered under her breath, her voice barely breaking the silence. It echoed back at her, scolding and soft, as if the room itself were judging her for returning. She ran her fingers along the edge of a peeling counter, the rough texture scraping against her skin, bringing back the feeling of childhood. The thrill of exploration mingled with a subtle dread, a knot tightening in her stomach as she ventured further inside.

Her footsteps were muffled against the worn carpet, and with each step, the thrum of her heart quickened, the anticipation and apprehension swirling within her like a tempest. She stopped before a faded photograph hanging crookedly on the wall, the faces within it smiling obliviously at the passing years. “Mom, why’d you keep this place?” she whispered, the question hanging heavy in the air, unanswered.

Just then, a floorboard creaked behind her, a sound both familiar and foreign, and her breath caught. “Clara?” A voice, soft yet laden with years of distance, broke through the stillness. It was as if a ghost had spoken, and she turned slowly, her heart racing as she met her brother's gaze, his expression a mix of surprise and something deeper—perhaps regret.

“I didn’t think you’d come,” he said, shifting his weight awkwardly from one foot to the other, the tension between them palpable like a thick fog.
