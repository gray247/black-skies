The bell above the café door jingled, a soft chime that cut through the steam and warmth of the room. Claire stepped inside, the scent of roasted coffee beans washing over her like a familiar embrace. She paused, letting the noise and chatter swirl around her before she moved further in, her fingers brushing the cool surface of the wooden counter. The barista, a young man with a mop of dark curls, glanced up and flashed a quick smile, his hands deftly working the espresso machine, the hiss and sputter mingling with the low hum of conversation.

“Hey there, what can I get for you?” he asked, his voice warm with the kind of casual friendliness that felt like a hug on a chilly morning.

“A cappuccino, please,” Claire replied, her voice barely breaking through the clattering of cups and the low murmur of patrons. She leaned closer to the counter, drawn in by the rich aroma of coffee and the promise of warmth. The sunlight streamed through the window, dappling the floor with golden patches, and she found herself momentarily lost in the patterns, a swirl of light and shadow that reminded her of late afternoons spent dreaming on her grandmother’s porch.

As the barista prepared her drink, she caught a glimpse of herself in the reflective surface of a nearby window. Her hair was a wild tangle, and the dark circles under her eyes were more pronounced than she’d like to admit. She turned her attention back to the counter, focusing on the rhythmic clinking of cups and the soft music playing in the background—a melody that felt both nostalgic and bittersweet.

“Here you go,” the barista said, sliding the cup across the counter, a frothy crown of milk foam perched on top. His eyes sparkled with an energy that momentarily pulled her from her thoughts. “Enjoy,” he added, a hint of genuine enthusiasm in his tone.
