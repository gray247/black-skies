The wind whipped through the narrow alley like a restless spirit, tugging at the hem of Clara’s cloak as she hurried past the crumbling stone walls. Each gust carried the scent of rain-soaked earth and distant smoke, mingling with the sharper notes of damp wood and the lingering aroma of fried dough from a nearby vendor. She could hear the faint chime of a bell somewhere in the distance, its sound mingling with the low murmur of voices and the clatter of carts on cobblestones. The city felt alive, thrumming with energy, but Clara’s heart raced for a different reason.

She paused beneath a flickering lantern, its light casting wavering shadows that danced along the cobbles, and pressed a hand against the cool stone of the wall. The summons, delivered with a clipped note and a wax seal, had ignited a fire in her chest that threatened to consume her entirely. The words had been simple, yet they weighed heavily in her mind: “Come to the Square at dusk. There is much to discuss.” The urgency had been palpable, a tugging sensation that urged her forward even as her instincts screamed for caution.

“Clara!” The voice cut through the din, sharp and familiar, pulling her from her reverie. Turning, she saw Thomas weaving through the throng, his blond curls bouncing with each determined step. His eyes were wide, pupils dilated with excitement—or perhaps fear, she couldn’t tell. “You received it too, didn’t you?”

She nodded, the motion almost mechanical. “You’re late.”

“I had to avoid the patrols. They’re everywhere tonight.” He glanced over his shoulder, as if the shadows themselves might leap out and swallow him whole. “We can’t be seen together for too long. Not after…” His voice trailed off, leaving an unsaid weight hanging in the air between them.

“Not after the last meeting,” she finished for him, her voice barely above a whisper. The memory of shouting voices and accusations still echoed in her mind, the way the air had thickened with tension, stifling her breath. The Council’s discontent had been palpable, a storm brewing beneath their civilities. “What do you think they want this time?”

“Something about the borders. Rumors of unrest,” he said, glancing again toward the mouth of the alley. “And us. They suspect we know more than we’re letting on.”

Clara’s fingers curled into her cloak, the fabric rough against her skin. She had never wanted to be at the center of their machinations, but the choices of the past had led her here, tethered to a fate that felt increasingly beyond her grasp. “We don’t know anything,” she insisted, though a flicker of doubt crept into her voice. “They can’t hold us accountable for what we haven’t seen.”

“Tell that to the ones who’ve already been taken.” Thomas’s words were harsh, but there was a tremor beneath the bravado, a fear that rippled through his frame. “If they suspect us…” He swallowed hard, his throat working visibly. “We need to be careful, Clara.”

“I know,” she said softly, the weight of their shared anxiety pressing against her chest. “But we can’t just stand here. We have to go.”

He nodded, though his reluctance was evident in the way his shoulders hunched slightly, as if bracing against an unseen force. They stepped from the alley into the main square, the cobblestones slick with rain and the air buzzing with anticipation. Lanterns flickered like fireflies, illuminating the faces of those gathered, a motley collection of citizens, traders, and cloaked figures whose intentions were masked behind shadows.
