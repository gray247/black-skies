Clara's heart raced, a drum echoing in her ears as she leaned further into the peeling wall, the coolness of the paint seeping through her clothes. She cradled the ceramic fox, its chipped surface cool against her palm, and felt an urge to pull it out, to hold it up like a talisman against the dark corners of the nursery. The room felt alive with whispers, secrets hidden beneath layers of dust and time, as if the very walls were leaning in to listen.

“Are you sure we should be here?” Jun’s voice cut through her thoughts, low and urgent, as he adjusted his grip on the lantern. The sputtering light wavered, casting erratic shadows that seemed to shift and scurry away whenever she looked directly at them. His soaked coat clung tightly to his frame, the fabric heavy with rain, and Clara caught a whiff of damp earth and lingering chlorophyll from the greenhouse.

“I need to know,” she whispered, her gaze darting to the sealed window, the paint thick and glistening like old blood. “We can’t just leave it like this.” The thought of what might be hidden behind that locked door gnawed at her, a relentless itch beneath her skin. It was a risk, and she could feel the weight of the decision settling heavily on her shoulders, entwined with a sense of dread and anticipation.

Jun shifted beside her, his eyes scanning the room, flitting from corner to corner as if expecting something to emerge from the shadows. “What if we’re not alone?” he murmured, the hint of fear threading through his voice. His gaze fell on the door, the heavy chain that rattled occasionally, a sound that seemed to echo from somewhere just beyond their reach. “Someone’s been testing that lock.”

“Maybe it’s just the house,” Clara replied, though her voice felt weak against the rising tide of uncertainty. She took a slow breath, trying to ground herself in the moment. But another cough echoed from above—a dry, rasping sound that sent a shiver up her spine. She looked at Jun, her heart pounding with a mix of confusion and fear. “Did you hear that?”

He hesitated, his brow furrowing as he mulled over her question. “I thought… I thought I heard something, but it felt like it was just in my head.” His words hung between them, heavy and fraught, as if admitting it would give life to the shadows lurking in every corner.

“Right.” Clara nodded, though doubt twisted in her chest. “Let’s just focus on the task at hand.” She glanced down at the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon a stark contrast against her pale skin. It felt tighter than before, digging into her flesh as she tugged at it absentmindedly. Was it her imagination, or had the ribbon grown shorter, binding her to the very secrets she sought to uncover?

The lantern flickered again, casting a final sputter before dimming to a near-extinguished glow. “We should decide now,” Clara said, her voice trembling with determination as the light waned. “Do we open the nursery door, or do we leave it be?”

Jun’s gaze darkened, shadows pooling in his eyes. “We can’t just ignore it, Clara. If there’s something—or someone—up there… We have to know what we’re dealing with.”

The weight of his words wrapped around her like a vise, tightening until she could barely breathe. She stepped away from the wall, the cool paint now a chilly reminder of the isolation they were stepping into. “Then we go. Together.”
