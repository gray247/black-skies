The lantern sputtered again, the flickering flame swallowing in and out of its brass confines, as Clara and Jun stood before the nursery door, the air thick with the scent of damp wood and something sharper, like burnt matches. Clara's heart raced, a rabbit caught in the underbrush, while she tried to steady her breath, the cold air biting at her lungs. She could feel the weight of the ceramic fox pressing against her thigh, its chipped surface a reminder of both innocence and something lost—like a promise that had faded into the silence of the house.

“Do you hear it?” Jun’s voice was low, urgent, and it sent a shiver through her. He squinted into the dark, his soaked coat clinging to his skin, the lantern casting long shadows that danced along the walls like specters. She wanted to say yes, to claim the sound of a child's cough echoing faintly from within the nursery, but all she felt was the oppressive quiet, the absence of any life behind the painted window.

Clara turned her wrist, the parlor key tied to the red ribbon glimmering dimly in the lantern’s erratic light. She could still feel the dampness of the greenhouse on her skin, the way the rain had clung to her hair, the memory of Jun’s laughter breaking through the tension, but it was all slipping away now, replaced by the echoes in her mind. “No one’s been here for years,” she murmured, more to herself than to him. She could hear Nora’s voice as if it were a whispering ghost, insisting that the nursery had been empty, forgotten like the dust gathering in the corners of the rooms.

“Clara.” Jun’s voice snapped her back to the moment, his brow furrowed as he pointed towards the chain on the service corridor door. It rattled again, a metallic jingle that seemed to mock their presence. “Someone's testing it.”

Her heart dropped. The chain was supposed to be two floors below, but the sound, insistent and taunting, was unmistakably above them. “We should go,” she said, her voice barely above a whisper, the words catching in her throat. She was drawn to the door, to the lore that surrounded it, but the chill creeping up her spine told her it was safer to leave whatever lay behind it undisturbed.

“What if—” Jun began, but she cut him off, the fierce urgency in her voice surprising even herself. “What if it’s just the wind? Or the house settling?” But even as she spoke, she could feel the tension knotting in her stomach, the way shadows loomed larger than they should, the sense that they were being watched, scrutinized by something unseen.

“No, Clara.” His tone was firm, grounding her in the moment, yet something flickered in his eyes, a glimmer of fear that mirrored her own. “I heard a cough. It wasn’t just the wind.”

The lantern dimmed again, and she felt the darkness thicken around them, pressing against her skin. “You must be imagining it,” she said, but the words felt inadequate, flimsy against the weight of what they were facing.

“Clara—” he started again, but the urgency in his voice was cut short by a soft creak from behind them, a noise that didn’t belong in the stillness, as if the house itself had exhaled. She turned, half-expecting to see someone emerging from the shadows, but there was only the hallway, stretching out behind them in a cocoon of darkness.
