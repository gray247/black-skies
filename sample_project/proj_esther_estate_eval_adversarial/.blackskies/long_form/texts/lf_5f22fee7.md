Clara’s breath misted in the chill of the dim corridor, the air heavy with the scent of damp wood and something else—something faint and stale that tugged at the back of her mind like a forgotten memory. She clutched the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket, its cool surface a comfort against her thigh, though the weight of it felt like a promise she didn’t understand. Jun stood a step behind her, the cracked brass lantern flickering weakly, its glow barely illuminating the peeling wallpaper adorned with faded floral motifs. The house seemed to breathe around them, the old bones of it creaking and settling, whispering secrets she wished she could decipher.

“What if it’s just the wind?” Jun said, his voice low, an attempt at reason, but even he didn’t sound convinced. The rain still dripped from his coat, pooling at his feet in small puddles that reflected the lantern's dying light. She could see his fingers twitch, wanting to reach for her arm, but he held back, as if afraid of shattering the fragile air between them.

Clara turned toward the nursery door, the rough surface of the wood stark against her palm as she pushed against it, but it wouldn’t budge. The chain rattled softly on the other side, a sound that sent a shiver crawling up her spine. “It’s always been locked,” she murmured, though her heart drummed a different story, one that whispered of secrets kept and doors untried. The door felt like a threshold, not just to a room, but to a past she couldn’t quite grasp.

“Maybe we should go back,” Jun suggested, the lantern's light dimming further, casting shadows that danced and twisted across the walls. His gaze flickered toward the staircase leading down, where the faintest echo of a child’s cough seemed to linger, but Clara shook her head, her resolve hardening.

“No. We have to see.” She didn’t know why she felt such urgency, why the need to push through the fear was stronger than the instinct to flee. Maybe it was the way time had twisted in this house, how moments felt stretched and wrong, like the clock striking thirteen.

“Clara,” he said, his voice a thread of caution. “Nora said—”

“Nora doesn’t know everything,” she interrupted, her heart racing as she felt the warmth of the ribbon against her wrist. The key dangled there, a small weight of possibility and dread. She had tied it tighter, hadn’t she? Yet here it was, a sliver of red against her skin, the only thing that felt certain in the chaos of the night.

Before she could second-guess herself, she pressed her ear against the door. Silence. And then, a breath—a soft, almost imperceptible sound that didn’t belong to either of them. Clara's pulse quickened, the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket felt heavier. She nodded to Jun, who hesitated, his brow furrowed in worry, but he moved to her side nonetheless, lantern held aloft like a fragile shield against the unknown.

“On three?” he suggested, his eyes darting to the door and then back to hers, searching for a signal of agreement. She nodded, her heart thudding, the moment stretching like a taut string ready to snap.

“One… two…”
