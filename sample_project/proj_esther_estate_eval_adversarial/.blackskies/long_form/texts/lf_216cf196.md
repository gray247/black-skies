Jun’s lantern sputtered again, casting erratic beams that illuminated the walls, revealing the peeling wallpaper like a tattered shroud. Shadows stretched and recoiled, teasing her imagination with shapes that seemed to flicker just beyond comprehension. She could feel Jun’s anxious energy radiating beside her, a palpable tension that thrummed in the air between them.

“Clara,” he whispered, his voice low and trembling, “we shouldn’t—”

“Stop it,” she snapped, her tone sharper than intended, the urgency in her voice a brittle shield against her own rising fear. “We need to keep moving.” The words tasted bitter on her tongue, but she was unwilling to linger in the suffocating uncertainty that threatened to swallow them whole. She clutched the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key swinging gently, a small weight of familiarity amidst the chaos.

As they passed under a low beam, the lantern flickered again, dimming to a glow that barely pierced the gloom. Clara felt the chill seep deeper, cold fingers curling around her spine, intertwining with the dampness that clung to her skin like a second layer. A shiver coursed through her, not just from the temperature but from an instinctive awareness that the corridor was alive, breathing with a presence that felt both foreign and ominous.
