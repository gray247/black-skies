The air in the servants' passage was thick and damp, clinging to Clara’s skin like a shroud. She pressed forward, the flickering brass lantern casting trembling shadows that danced along the peeling wallpaper, revealing faded patterns of vines that seemed to writhe under the dim light. Jun’s coat, still soaked from the greenhouse rain, brushed against her arm, a reminder of their hurried escape, the chill of the fabric seeping into her own warmth.

“Clara,” he whispered, his voice strained, barely above the sound of their footsteps. “Do you hear that?”

She paused, the lantern sputtering weakly in his grip, the glow dimming as if the very air was suffocating it. Clara strained to listen, her heart thudding in her chest, the echo of Jun's fear igniting an unease that wrapped around her thoughts like a vine. “What do you mean?” she shot back, her tone sharper than she intended, the words spilling out before she could reign them in.

A faint rustle, the sound of something shifting—a breath, a cough? Jun's eyes were wide, darting toward the far end of the corridor where the chain on the door rattled again, as if something on the other side were testing its strength. Clara felt her stomach drop. “It’s just the wind,” she lied, though the words tasted bitter in her mouth.

“No,” he insisted, fear creeping into his tone. “I swear it sounded like a—”

“Like a child?” she interjected, her heart racing. She gripped the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the key dangling like a forgotten promise, a relic of safety she couldn’t quite recall tying tighter.
