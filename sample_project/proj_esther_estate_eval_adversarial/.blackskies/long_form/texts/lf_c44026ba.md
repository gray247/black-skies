The air in the corridor was thick with dampness, the chill seeping through the walls and settling in the marrow of Clara's bones. She shivered, not from the cold alone but from the unsettling sense that something was lurking just out of sight. The dim light from Jun's cracked brass lantern flickered, casting long shadows that danced along the peeling wallpaper, revealing hints of faded floral patterns that seemed to whisper secrets of the past.

Clara's heart raced as she stepped forward, the sound of her shoes squelching against the damp floor echoing like a warning. The lantern sputtered again, a weak attempt to pierce the darkness, and for a moment, she caught a glimpse of muddy footprints leading ahead, stark against the mildew-stained floorboards. They weren’t hers—she had left no trace but the faintest outline of her presence, and yet these marks seemed to beckon her deeper into the maw of the corridor.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled, barely above a whisper, as he tightened his grip on the lantern, the flickering light casting anxious glances at Clara's face, searching for reassurance. His coat was still drenched from the downpour in the greenhouse, water pooling at his feet, mingling with the grime of the passage. “I thought I heard something earlier… like a cough.”

She swallowed hard, the taste of brine lingering on her tongue, and shook her head, though the sound echoed in her mind—a child’s cough, sharp and desperate. It gnawed at her resolve, a reminder of the sealed upper floor they had avoided, the place where whispers of a forgotten presence lingered like smoke. “It’s just the wind,” she said, a little too quickly, the sharpness in her tone betraying her own uncertainty. “Let’s keep moving.”
