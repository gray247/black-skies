They moved cautiously, their footsteps muffled by the thick, musty carpet that smelled of mildew and age, an odor that curled in her nose and made her stomach churn.

“Do you think we should go back?” Jun's voice was barely a whisper, hoarse with fear as he pointed the dim light ahead, his soaked coat clinging to his skin like an unwelcome shroud. Clara’s heart raced, the rhythm thrumming in her ears, and she could feel the weight of the parlor key against her wrist, almost a reminder of safety that felt increasingly distant.

“No,” she snapped, her voice sharper than intended, a brittle edge that caught the darkness around them. She could see the doubt flicker in his eyes, but she pushed past it. “We need to find out what’s happening.” The words tumbled from her lips, a mantra against the rising tide of anxiety that threatened to pull her under. Muddy footprints trailed ahead, smudging the carpet with a dark stain, their presence both a mystery and a warning.
