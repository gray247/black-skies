Clara pressed against the nursery door, its surface cold beneath her palm, and took a breath that caught in her throat. The air was thick with the scent of damp wood and something more acrid, a whisper of decay that made her stomach twist. She could hear Jun’s quiet movements behind her, the soft rustle of his coat, still soaked from the greenhouse rain, an echo of their hurried escape. The lantern sputtered, flickering like a heartbeat, casting uneven shadows that danced along the cracked plaster walls.

“Are you sure about this?” Jun’s voice was low, almost lost in the thickening silence of the corridor. He shifted his weight, the faintest sound of mud squelching beneath his boots reminding Clara of the prints they had crossed earlier—those muddy footprints that led nowhere and everywhere all at once.

“I have to find out,” she replied, her voice steadier than she felt. She glanced at him over her shoulder, catching the glint of worry in his eyes, the way his brow knitted together, as if he could feel the weight of the house pressing down on them. The lantern’s light caught the edges of his face, highlighting the droplets still clinging to his hair, and for a moment, she was overwhelmed by the urge to reach out, to sweep them away as if she could brush aside the unease settling between them.

The chain on the service corridor door rattled again, a sharp clink that made her heart race in her chest. “Did you hear that?” she asked, a tremor threading through her voice. She turned back to the door, the red ribbon key tied around her wrist suddenly feeling too tight, a reminder of something she couldn’t quite grasp.

“Maybe it’s just the wind,” Jun suggested, though the uncertainty in his tone betrayed him. He stepped closer, the lantern illuminating the cracks in the floorboards, dark lines that seemed to weave a path of their own. “Or… something else.”

Clara swallowed hard, her fingers brushing over the cool surface of the ceramic fox in her pocket. It felt heavier now, a weight of expectation and doubt. “Nora said no one has slept in here for years,” she whispered, half to herself. The words echoed in the stillness, stirring the shadows around them. “But what if…”

“Clara,” Jun interrupted, his voice urgent, “we should—”

But before he could finish, she turned the key in the lock, the metal grinding against the rusted mechanism. It felt as if the house held its breath, time suspended in that moment. The latch clicked, and the door creaked open a fraction, revealing only darkness beyond. Clara hesitated, the threshold suddenly daunting, the scent of mildew wafting out like a warning.

“Let’s just look inside,” she said, more to convince herself than him. She stepped forward, her heart pounding louder than the distant ticking of the clock above. The lantern’s glow flickered again, and the light seemed to pull back, reluctant to reveal what lay beyond.

Jun stood close behind her, his breath mingling with the chill in the air, as if they were both unwilling participants in a game set by the house itself. “Stay close,” he murmured, and she could hear the tremor in his voice, the unspoken fear that something was waiting for them in the dark.

“Always,” she replied, though she wasn’t certain if she meant it. They stepped into the nursery, the door swinging wider with a groan, and Clara’s breath caught again, not from the cold but from the sudden rush of memories that flooded her mind, dissolving the boundary between past and present.
