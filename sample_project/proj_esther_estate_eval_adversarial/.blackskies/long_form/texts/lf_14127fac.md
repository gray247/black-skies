The sound of rain drummed a steady rhythm against the greenhouse glass as Clara and Jun slipped into the service corridor, the air thick with the musty scent of damp wood and something else—something sour, like old potatoes. The cracked brass lantern in Jun’s hand flickered erratically, casting long shadows that danced across the peeling wallpaper, its glow barely illuminating the narrow passage. Clara could feel the weight of the parlor key, tied to her wrist with the red ribbon, a constant reminder of the secrets that waited behind the heavy door.

“Do you think anyone’s down here?” Jun’s voice trembled, barely louder than a whisper, as he tugged his soaked coat tighter around him. Water dripped from the hem, puddling around his feet. Clara glanced back toward the greenhouse, where the rain continued to patter like a frantic heartbeat. Her gut twisted at the thought of what might be lurking in the dark, yet she forced a smile, a brittle facade.

“Just us,” she replied, her tone sharper than intended. She rubbed her thumb over the ribbon, feeling the fibers cling to her skin. It was tighter than she remembered, as if it had a life of its own. The lantern sputtered again, dimming to a mere flicker, and a cold draft snaked through the corridor, carrying a chill that raised goosebumps on her arms.

Ahead, muddy footprints smeared the floor, leading away from the chained door that rattled ominously behind them, the chain clinking as if someone were testing their hold from the other side. Clara’s heart raced, each beat echoing in the silence that enveloped them. She caught Jun’s gaze, his eyes wide and glistening with fear, and she pressed her lips together, fighting the urge to reassure him. Any comfort she offered would only deepen her own sense of dread.
