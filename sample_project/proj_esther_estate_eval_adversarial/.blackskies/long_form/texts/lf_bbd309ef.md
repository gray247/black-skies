Clara’s heart raced as she stepped closer to the nursery door, the dim glow of Jun’s lantern barely pushing back the encroaching shadows. The air was thick with the scent of damp wood and something sweetly rotten, a smell that tangled with the memory of the greenhouse, where life once thrived. She could almost feel the pulse of the house, thumping in time with her own anxiety. The chain rattled softly from behind the door, a muted whisper that sent a chill skittering down her spine.

“Do you hear that?” Jun’s voice was quiet, strained, as though each word were a delicate thread threatening to snap. He shifted his weight, the soaked fabric of his coat clinging to him like a second skin. Clara nodded, her mouth suddenly dry. “It’s nothing,” she breathed, though the words felt heavy and hollow, like a promise she couldn’t keep.

The lantern flickered, its light dimming further, casting their surroundings into a deeper gloom. Clara glanced at Jun, his brow glistening with beads of moisture, and her grip tightened around the red ribbon tied to her wrist. The key dangled there, a talisman that felt both protective and burdensome. It was a reminder of the secrets buried within these walls, of the choices they had already made and those still looming ahead.

“Should we—” Jun began, but the words fell away as a sudden cough echoed from the depths of the nursery. It was a soft, raspy sound, unsettling in its innocence, and Clara’s breath caught in her throat. She exchanged a glance with Jun, who wore an expression of shared dread. He was about to speak again, but the words got lost in the rustle of Clara’s coat pocket, where the ceramic fox lay, its chipped paint a reminder of childhood stories and laughter that had long since faded.

“No one has slept in there for years,” Nora had said, her voice wavering even as she had tried to sound resolute. Clara could still see the flash of something in her eyes, a flicker of fear or recognition. It unsettled her more than the thought of a child hidden away in that room.

“Let’s get this over with,” Jun said, his voice firming up as he reached for the door. His fingers brushed against the latch, the metal cold under his touch, and Clara felt her pulse quicken in anticipation. They were about to breach a threshold into a space that had been sealed off, where echoes of the past might collide with the present. The door creaked as Jun pulled it, but it didn’t budge.

“Wait,” Clara said, her voice a breathless whisper. She stepped back, her eyes darting to the chain that rattled again, a sound too deliberate for her comfort. “What if we shouldn’t?”

Jun turned to her, his expression straddling frustration and concern. “It’s just a door, Clara. We can’t keep avoiding this.” His lantern flickered once more, a dying ember struggling against the night.

“But what’s behind it?” she pressed, her thoughts racing. The cough echoed again, a haunting reminder that they weren’t alone in this labyrinth of memories and shadows. “What if it’s something we can’t—”

“We won’t know until we try,” he cut in, determination hardening his features. She saw the resolve in his eyes, but beneath it, a flash of something else—fear, perhaps, or doubt. Clara swallowed hard, the knot in her stomach tightening as she felt the weight of the decision drawing closer, a precipice they were teetering on.
