Clara's fingers curled around the smooth, chipped ceramic fox nestled in her coat pocket, a weighty presence that felt both comforting and eerie. She could almost hear the faint echo of its painted eyes watching her, as if it held the nursery's secrets within its silent form. Jun shifted beside her, the damp fabric of his coat clinging to him like a second skin, the smell of wet earth and rain mingling with the musty scent of the corridor. The lantern flickered once more, casting a wan light that danced across the walls, illuminating patches of peeling wallpaper that whispered of faded memories and forgotten laughter.

“Clara,” Jun breathed, his voice low, almost a murmur against the creaking house. “We shouldn’t be here. Not with…” He hesitated, searching her face for something—an understanding, perhaps?—but she felt only the chill settling deeper into her bones. “Not with the door chained, and the prints…”

“It’s just a house,” Clara replied, more sharply than she intended, the sting of her own words catching in the air. The shadows seemed to deepen, thickening around them, wrapping tighter. She exhaled slowly, the fog of her breath mingling with the frost that clung to the air. “It’s not like it’s alive.”

Yet, as soon as she spoke, the quiet seemed to shift, as if the house itself sighed, responding to her denial with a creak of wood and a distant thud that reverberated through the floorboards. Clara’s heart quickened, her pulse a steady drumbeat in her ears, drowning out Jun's next words. “You don’t think it’s possible that someone…?” he began, but the words trailed off, leaving a silence that felt heavy enough to crush them both.

A sudden rattle echoed from above, the chain on the corridor door trembling as if someone were testing it from the far side. Clara’s breath caught in her throat. “Did you hear that?” she whispered, her voice trembling, a thread woven with threads of fear and disbelief. Her gaze darted to Jun, who had gone pale, the lantern’s light reflecting in his wide eyes.

“I thought it was just… the house settling,” he said, but the tremor in his voice betrayed him. He stepped closer, the lantern casting shifting shadows that flickered along the walls like specters. “We should check the nursery. It’s the only place left.”

The thought of crossing the threshold into that sealed room—where the cough had echoed in her mind like a distant memory—sent a coil of dread winding tightly in her stomach. “But, Nora said…” Clara started, but stopped short, remembering the way Nora’s eyes had flinched when she saw the fox. What secrets were tied to that nursery? What had been kept hidden all these years?

“Forget what Nora said,” Jun urged, his voice dropping to a whisper as if the walls themselves might overhear. “We need to know what’s in there. We can’t just leave it.”

Clara glanced back at the chained door, the metallic clink echoing in her ears, a taunting reminder of what lay beyond. She could feel the weight of the key on her wrist, the red ribbon a stark contrast against her pale skin. Had she really tied it tighter, or had it simply been a promise to herself that she was losing faith in?

With a reluctant nod, she stepped forward, her heart pounding in time with the soft thud of Jun's footsteps behind her.
