She could almost hear the whispers of the house, secrets long buried beneath layers of dust and time.

She glanced back at him, catching the worry etched in the lines of his face, the soaked fabric of his coat clinging to him like a second skin.

“Stay close,” she whispered, stepping into the corridor. The air was thick with a musty smell, a blend of aged wood and something else—something sweet and decaying. She wrapped her fingers tighter around the key, feeling the red ribbon scratch against her wrist, a reminder of her grip on the moment.

She tried to push it away, but it lingered, a ghostly presence that clung to her thoughts.

“Do you hear that?” Jun’s voice cut through her reverie, a cautious urgency lacing his words. Clara paused, straining to catch any sound over the creaking of the old house. For a moment, silence enveloped them, thick and oppressive, and then it came again—a soft, rasping cough, echoing from somewhere above.

“Is it…?” he began, but Clara shook her head, cutting him off. The memory of Nora’s flinch at the sight of the ceramic fox in her pocket surged back, a warning that something was not right. “It’s nothing,” she insisted, though her own voice felt brittle, as if it might shatter under the weight of the truth.

Jun looked unconvinced, his brow furrowed, and Clara felt her resolve waver. “Let’s just check the nursery,” she urged, forcing herself to sound more certain than she felt. The lantern flickered again, sputtering weakly, and she noticed Jun’s fingers tightening around it, as if he could will it to burn brighter.

They approached the end of the corridor where the door to the nursery stood, solid and unyielding. Clara reached for the doorknob, her pulse racing in her throat. “What if it’s wrong?” Jun asked, his voice low and uncertain. The anxiety in his tone mirrored her own, a shared vulnerability that hung in the air between them.

“Then we try again,” Clara replied, but even as she spoke, she felt the truth of it slipping away. The key trembled in her grasp, and she couldn’t shake the feeling that the house was watching, listening, waiting.
