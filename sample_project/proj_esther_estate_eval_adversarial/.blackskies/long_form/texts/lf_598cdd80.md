Clara pressed her palm harder against the nursery door, the grain of the wood pressing back against her skin like a heartbeat. A faint tremor ran through her fingers, not from fear but from an unsettling mix of longing and dread. The room beyond seemed to breathe in sync with her, the stale air thick with the scent of mothballs and something else—something sweet and decayed, like forgotten candy. She could almost hear the echoes of childhood laughter trapped within these walls, a hollow mockery of joy that sent a shiver down her spine.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice broke through the stillness, low and urgent, but Clara barely registered it. She was caught in the moment, transfixed by the thought of what lay behind the door. The lantern’s flicker cast long shadows, stretching and curling like fingers around them. It sputtered again, the dim light revealing the chipped ceramic fox nestled in the folds of her coat pocket, a small anchor to a past she could barely grasp.

“What?” She turned, her eyes searching Jun’s face for confirmation, but his brow was knitted in concentration, the lines etched deeper by the weight of the moment. He glanced at the door, then at the chain rattling ominously on the opposite side, each sound a reminder of their precarious situation.

“Clara, the cough—” he started, but she cut him off, shaking her head. "No, it’s nothing. Just the house settling." She didn’t want to admit it, didn’t want the shadows to coalesce into the form of a child, a specter of innocence that had long since vanished.

The lantern flickered again, and she felt a surge of panic. “We should go back,” she suggested, though the words tasted bitter on her tongue. The air felt heavier, laden with the weight of unspoken fears. Every heartbeat pulsed through the silence, a reminder that they were more than just intruders in this space—they were trespassers in a memory.

“No, we need to see what’s in there,” Jun insisted, his voice steady, but Clara could see the flicker of doubt in his eyes. He shifted his weight, the soaked fabric of his coat clinging to his frame, the dampness a stark reminder of their earlier escapade in the greenhouse. “We can’t just leave it like this.”

“Like what?” she snapped, the edge of her voice surprising even her. “There’s nothing here for us.” But the truth was, the nursery had always drawn her in, even as a child. It was a place filled with secrets, a sanctuary where dreams and nightmares intertwined. She could almost hear the whispers of the past, the ghostly echoes of a lullaby, beckoning her to cross the threshold.

With a deep breath, Clara stepped back, her heart racing as she wrestled with the instinct to flee. “If we’re going in, we need to be ready,” she finally said, and the words hung in the air, heavy with implications. Jun nodded, his determination flaring like the last gasps of the lantern’s light.

“On three,” he said, his voice firm despite the tremor in his hands. One. The echoes of their past flooded her mind—the laughter, the warmth, the chaos of childhood. Two. She could feel the weight of the key tied to her wrist, the ribbon a tether to memories she couldn’t fully recall. Three.

Together, they pushed against the door, the wood groaning in protest as if it were a living thing fighting to keep its secrets. Clara’s heart pounded in her chest, the sound a drumbeat of dread and anticipation.
