The lantern flickered, casting jittery shadows against the damp stone walls of the corridor. Jun squeezed the cracked brass handle, his soaked coat clinging to his skin like a second layer of dread. Water dripped from his hair, pooling at his feet, mingling with the muddy prints that seemed to snake ahead of them, leading deeper into the dark. Clara, her heart racing, tugged at the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key still a heavy weight against her skin. She felt it tugging at her, a reminder of the heavy air and the secrets they had unearthed in the greenhouse.

“Do you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled, barely a whisper in the thick silence. His eyes darted from shadow to shadow, as if expecting something to leap out at him from the dark.

Clara shook her head, although the faintest echo of a child’s cough lingered at the edges of her mind, elusive and disconcerting. “It’s just the wind,” she replied too quickly, her voice sharper than she intended. The words hung in the air, heavier than the tension that pressed around them, and she couldn’t shake the feeling that something was watching.

The chain on the door rattled again, sending a shiver down her spine. A soft clinking that seemed to beckon them, testing the strength of its binding. “It’s probably just—” she started, but Jun interrupted her, his face pale under the dim light.

“Clara, it sounds like it’s coming from the other side.”

She took a step closer to him, instinctively drawing the chipped ceramic fox from her pocket, its rough edges grounding her in the moment.
