The air in the nursery felt thick, as if the very walls were holding their breath. Clara stood before the door, the chipped ceramic fox pressing against her side, the cool glaze a stark contrast to the warmth of her palm. She could almost hear its painted eyes watching her, a silent witness to the uncertainty that thrummed in her chest. Beside her, Jun fidgeted with the cracked brass lantern, its weak light flickering, a feeble heartbeat against the encroaching darkness. He had tried to wipe the rain from his coat, but the damp fabric clung to him like a second skin, a reminder of the storm that had swept through the greenhouse.

“Do you think it’s locked?” Jun asked, his voice low, almost swallowed by the shadows that pooled in the corners of the room. Clara could see the shadows shifting, the way they danced along the walls as if they were alive, pulsing with some unseen energy. She didn’t answer right away, her fingers brushing against the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key resting against her skin like a promise. She had felt the weight of it all day, a tangible link to something—what, she couldn't quite say.

The house creaked, a low groan that echoed through the silence, and Clara’s heart raced in response. Was it just the house settling, or something more? Something waiting just beyond the door? “I don’t know,” she finally murmured. “But we should try.” She stepped closer, the lantern’s glow illuminating the ornate doorknob, tarnished and worn. She hesitated, glancing at Jun, whose brow was furrowed with concern. “What if it’s not just locked?” she added, the words slipping out before she could stop them.

Jun swallowed hard, his gaze darting to the hallway, then back to her. “You heard it too, right?” There was an urgency in his voice, a shared understanding that didn’t need to be voiced explicitly. Clara felt a chill race up her spine, the memory of the cough still echoing in her mind, though she had insisted it was just a trick of the wind. But now, the thought began to gnaw at her, a persistent itch beneath her skin.

“Let’s just see,” she said, more to convince herself than him. She reached for the doorknob, its cold metal sending a shiver through her fingers. The lantern flickered again, the light dimming further, casting long shadows that twisted and curled like fingers reaching out. She took a deep breath, steadying herself as she turned the knob. It clicked, but the door didn’t budge.

“Chain.” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, but the word hung heavy in the air. Clara felt her heart sink. She could hear the faint rattling from the other side, the sound of metal scraping against metal, as if someone—or something—was testing the lock, echoing her own growing dread.

“Should we knock?” Jun suggested, his eyes darting nervously to the ceiling, where the sound seemed to vibrate and crawl above them. Clara shook her head, a lump forming in her throat. “What if it’s not…?” The thought hung between them, unspoken but palpable. The thought of a child trapped within those walls, the nursery that Nora had insisted was long abandoned, sent a surge of fear coursing through her.

“Do you think it could be—” Jun began, but Clara cut him off, the urgency of the moment propelling her forward. “We should just try the key,” she said, her voice firmer than she felt. She withdrew the ribbon from her wrist, the key dangling precariously as she fought against the flutter of doubt in her stomach.
