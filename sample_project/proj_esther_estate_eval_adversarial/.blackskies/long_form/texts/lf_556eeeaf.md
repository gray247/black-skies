The lantern flickered again, its dying light casting long, spindly shadows that clawed at the walls, whispering of memories best left undisturbed.

“Do you think we should knock?” Jun asked, his voice low and cautious, as if raising the question might summon something from the dark. He shifted his weight, the soaked fabric of his coat rustling like dry leaves. The rhythmic drip of water from his collar was a reminder of where they had just been, the greenhouse heavy with rain and the taste of damp earth still clinging to her senses.

Clara frowned, her fingers brushing the smooth surface of the ceramic fox tucked in her pocket. It felt strangely warm against her skin, as if it were trying to impart a comfort she desperately needed. “I don’t know,” she replied, her voice barely above a whisper. The cough she had heard earlier still echoed in her mind, a child’s sound, haunting and fragile. But Jun hadn’t heard it. He’d looked at her with those dark eyes, full of concern and something else—a flicker of disbelief that stung more than she expected.

“Maybe it’s just a cat,” he offered, though the quiver in his tone betrayed his own uncertainty. Clara could feel the weight of the house pressing down on them, the very air charged with a tension that made her skin crawl. It was as if the walls were collectively holding their breath, waiting for her to make a choice.

The lantern sputtered again, and for a moment, she thought it might finally go out, plunging them into utter darkness. But it remained, the weak glow illuminating the door’s intricate carvings, the once-vibrant colors now muted and peeling. She traced a finger along the edge of a flower, imagining the nursery alive with laughter and play, the kind of innocent joy that felt impossibly far away now.

“Clara,” Jun’s voice broke through her reverie, softer, more insistent. “What if there’s someone in there?” The chain rattled behind them, a soft clinking sound that forced her attention away from the door. It was almost rhythmic, as if someone were testing it, trying to find a way in. Clara’s stomach dropped, the once familiar corridors of the house morphing into a labyrinth of dread.

“No one’s supposed to be here,” she murmured, her gaze flicking to the stairs leading to the upper floors. The chain shouldn’t have been moving at all; it was anchored two floors down, a relic of a past she was losing her grip on. Yet, it rattled again, as if mocking her certainty.

“Then we need to get out of here,” Jun urged, his voice tightening. The lantern’s light dimmed further, the sputtering flame fighting against the encroaching shadows. “Let’s just go back. We can tell someone—”
