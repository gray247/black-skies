Clara pressed her ear against the sealed nursery door, the coarse wood biting into her skin, but she barely noticed. The faint echoes of the house settling whispered secrets, and she could almost convince herself that a child’s breath stirred within the walls. “We can’t just leave it," she murmured again, the words caught in the tension between her heart and throat. The lantern sputtered, casting shadows that flickered like fleeting memories, and she felt a chill run down her spine, a reminder of the rain-soaked greenhouse and the muddy trail they had followed.

Jun shifted beside her, the weight of the cracked brass lantern heavy in his hands. His coat was still soaked through, a testament to the relentless downpour that had not only drenched his clothes but also seemed to wash away the last vestiges of their childhood innocence. “If we leave now, we might not get another chance,” he replied, his voice low and urgent, but tinged with an uncharacteristic tremor. Clara could see the way his fingers clenched around the lantern, the light flickering in response to his agitation.

“Do you hear it?” she pressed, the question spilling from her lips like a desperate plea. “The cough?”

Jun hesitated, his gaze darting to the door, as if he could will the sound to return. “I didn’t hear anything,” he said, the words coming out clipped, as if he were trying to convince himself more than her. She caught the flicker of doubt in his eyes and it gnawed at her. Were they both losing their minds, or was there truly something lurking in this house?

The lantern dimmed further, a flicker of life before it surrendered to the encroaching darkness. Clara felt the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket, its chipped surface a reminder of the nursery’s long-abandoned past. Nora Barrett’s voice echoed in her mind, stern and dismissive: “No child has slept in that nursery for years.” But Clara could feel the truth simmering beneath the surface, a pulse of something urgent and alive that called to her.

“Maybe we should—” Jun began, but the rattling of the chain on the corridor door above them cut him off, a sharp jolt that sent Clara’s heart racing. She could almost hear the heartbeat of the house, thumping in rhythm with her rising anxiety. The sound was not one of resignation, but of a challenge—something was testing their resolve, and it was becoming harder to distinguish between the familiar and the foreign.

“Do you think someone is up there?” Clara asked, her voice barely a whisper, as if the very act of speaking might summon whatever lay beyond the door. Jun’s eyes widened, and for a moment, the unspoken fear hung between them like a thick fog.

“We should go,” he said finally, his voice strained, but Clara felt rooted to the spot, her fingers still brushing the rough grain of the door. “What if it’s just the wind?” he added, the words sounding hollow even to his own ears. It was a feeble attempt at rationality, but they both knew that the wind had a way of carrying more than just air; it could carry memories, echoes of laughter, and perhaps the softest of whispers that danced just out of reach.

Clara turned her gaze back to the door, willing it to yield to her touch, to open and reveal the truth hidden behind the threshold. “What if it’s not?” she countered, her voice steadying with each word. The thought of leaving without knowing felt like a splinter lodged in her mind, a painful reminder of the past they had tried to forget.
