The air in the corridor was thick with an unsettling stillness, broken only by the soft creaking of the old wooden floor beneath their feet. Clara's heart pounded in her chest, each beat a reminder of the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon fraying slightly at the edges. She could feel the weight of it, the smooth metal cool against her skin, a tether to the safety of the parlor they had just left. Jun walked beside her, his coat still damp from the greenhouse rain, the scent of wet earth and foliage clinging to him like a second skin. He held the cracked brass lantern aloft, its flickering light casting erratic shadows that danced across the peeling wallpaper.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, his eyes darting toward the heavy door at the far end of the corridor, the chain rattling softly as if something—someone—was testing its strength from the other side.

Clara strained to listen, the silence pressing down on her like a weight. “What did you hear?” she shot back, too quickly, her tone sharper than she intended. A part of her wanted to dismiss his fear, to push it away like she had done with the thoughts that clawed at her mind.

“I thought—” He hesitated, the lantern sputtering, its light dimming even further, as if reluctant to illuminate the darkness beyond. “I thought I heard a child cough.”

She frowned, the image of a small figure lingering in the shadows sending a chill through her veins. “There’s no one here but us,” Clara replied, though her voice trembled just enough to betray her confidence. She reached into her coat pocket, fingers brushing against the chipped ceramic fox she had pocketed from the nursery, its cold surface grounding her in the moment.
