Clara stepped into the nursery, the air thick with a musty odor that clawed at her throat. Dust motes danced in the feeble light of the sputtering lantern, each flicker revealing glimpses of forgotten toys and shadows that loomed like specters from another time. The floor creaked beneath her, a low, mournful groan that echoed through the silence. She hesitated, the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket pressing against her thigh, its cool surface a reminder of the weight of the past, of memories tangled with the present.

Jun followed closely, his coat still damp from the greenhouse, the scent of wet earth clinging to him. He glanced around, his brow furrowing as he surveyed the room. “This feels wrong,” he murmured, his voice barely above a whisper, as if the walls would hear him and respond. Clara nodded, her heart racing as she moved deeper into the room, drawn by an invisible thread. The lantern flickered again, casting eerie shadows that seemed to reach out, grasping at her like hands longing for something lost.

“Do you think it’s safe?” Jun's eyes darted to the window, painted shut, its heavy drapes drawn tight, as if the light itself were barred from entering. Clara could feel the weight of the stillness, pressing down on her, a blanket of unease that wrapped around them both. She wanted to reassure him, to say that they would be okay, but the words were lodged somewhere deep inside, tangled with her own uncertainty.

“Just… just a quick look,” she whispered, though even she didn’t believe it. The lantern dimmed again, and she felt a chill sweep over her, a whisper of something just beyond her comprehension. The cough—a child’s cough—echoed in her mind, sharp and insistent. But when she glanced at Jun, his expression was strained, as if he’d heard it too, though he wouldn’t say so. “You heard that, right?” she pressed, searching his face for confirmation.

“Maybe it’s just the house settling,” he replied, but there was a tremor in his voice that betrayed him. Clara could sense the tension coiling between them, a palpable energy that crackled in the air. She took a step further into the room, her gaze drawn to the corner where the old chest stood, its surface dust-covered and untouched, as though it had been waiting for someone to open it for decades.

“Do you think it’s locked?” she asked, her fingers twitching with the urge to pry it open, to unearth whatever secrets lay hidden inside. Jun moved beside her, and together they examined the chest. The latch was rusted, but it appeared to hold firm, a stubborn guardian of its contents.

“Maybe it’s better if we don’t,” he suggested, his voice low, but Clara could see the flicker of curiosity in his eyes. She bit her lip, torn between the thrill of discovery and the gnawing fear that whatever lay inside could be more than they bargained for.

As if in response to her thoughts, the sound of the chain rattled from somewhere above them, a sharp clank that made her pulse quicken. Clara’s breath caught in her throat; the sound was too deliberate, too alive. “Did you hear that?” she blurted, her eyes wide as they locked onto Jun’s.

“Yeah,” he said, glancing toward the door, the chain now seeming to pulse with a life of its own. “What if… what if someone’s trying to get in?”

The idea sent a shiver down Clara’s spine, and she felt the weight of the key against her wrist, the red ribbon biting into her skin as if trying to anchor her to the moment.
