Clara leaned closer to the sealed nursery door, the wood grain rough against her fingertips. The cracked brass lantern Jun held sputtered like a dying firefly, casting a feeble glow that danced across the door’s surface, illuminating the dust motes swirling in the stagnant air. She could almost taste the dampness, thick and heavy, like old memories rising from the depths of the house. “We can’t just leave it,” she whispered, the words slipping from her lips before she could catch them. The thought of turning back felt like a betrayal, a surrender to whatever lingered in the shadows.

Jun’s breath hitched, a sharp intake that drew her gaze. “Clara, what if—” He paused, the tremor in his voice revealing more than the words left unsaid. “What if we’re not supposed to be here?”

The lantern flickered again, and for a moment, the light caught the worn edges of the door, highlighting where the paint had chipped away like the house was shedding its skin. Clara felt a pull, an unexplainable urgency that gnawed at her insides. “We have to see,” she insisted, her fingers brushing against the red ribbon that bound the parlor key to her wrist, a tether to a truth she desperately sought but couldn’t quite grasp. The key was warm against her skin, pulsing softly like a heartbeat, echoing her own rising anxiety.

In the silence that followed, the house settled around them, creaking in a language of its own, and Clara fought the sensation that it was listening, waiting. The faintest sound—like a child’s cough—echoed from somewhere within the nursery, a memory clawing its way back into her consciousness. Jun stiffened beside her, his eyes widening in the dim light. “Did you hear that?” His voice trembled, and Clara felt a chill skitter down her spine, but she shook her head, unwilling to acknowledge what she couldn’t see.

“It could be nothing,” she said, though her heart raced. She reached for the door handle, the cold metal sending a jolt through her fingertips. “We need to know what’s behind here.” The chain rattled softly, a ghostly whisper from the other side, and she hesitated, the weight of uncertainty pressing against her chest.

“What if it is something?” Jun’s voice lowered, barely above a whisper, as if speaking too loudly might awaken whatever lay in wait. “Nora said—”

“—Nora flinched at the fox,” Clara interrupted, her voice sharp, laced with a tension that crackled in the air between them. She pulled the chipped ceramic fox from her coat pocket, its surface cool and oddly comforting in her palm. “She knows more than she’s letting on. We both do.”

Jun’s gaze darted to the floorboards, where muddy prints seemed to lead away from the door, indistinct shapes that twisted into the darkness. “But where did they come from?” he murmured, almost to himself, the question hanging in the air like fog.

“Maybe they never left,” Clara replied, her mind racing with images of the unseen. She felt the weight of the house pressing down on them, a labyrinth of whispers and secrets, and the thought of retreating sent a wave of nausea through her. She couldn’t shake the feeling that if they turned back now, they might lose their chance to uncover something vital, something buried deep within the walls.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice strained, “we have to decide.” The lantern flickered again, casting shadows that twisted and turned like living things, and Clara could feel the pulse of the moment thrum in her veins.
