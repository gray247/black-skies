Clara stood in front of the nursery door, the chipped ceramic fox heavy in her pocket, its cool surface grounding her against the disquiet that swirled in her mind. The lantern in Jun’s hand sputtered, casting a dim light that flickered like a heartbeat, illuminating the dust motes dancing in the stagnant air. It felt as if the house itself held its breath, the oppressive silence pressing against her like a weight. Above them, a clock began to chime, each deep toll reverberating through the wooden beams, echoing a sense of urgency that gnawed at her gut.

“Do you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice barely above a murmur, eyes darting to the shadows that clung to the corners of the hallway. Clara shook her head, her heart racing. She didn’t want to acknowledge what he might mean. It was just the house settling, wasn’t it? Just the house. But her fingers tightened around the red ribbon, the key brushing against her wrist, as if it had grown heavier since they found it tangled in the greenhouse vines. The sound of the clock faded, replaced by an unsettling quiet, punctuated only by the distant creaking of old wood.

Jun stepped closer, lowering his voice further. “What if… what if we’re not alone?” There was a tremor in his tone that Clara recognized, a mix of fear and disbelief. She met his gaze, searching for reassurance but finding only the reflection of her own unease. The muddy prints they had followed, the way they seemed to lead into nothingness, gnawed at her thoughts. “We have to open the door,” she said, her voice steadier than she felt, pushing aside the gnawing anxiety that whispered of threats lurking just beyond their sight.

With a deep breath, she reached for the doorknob, the cold metal biting into her palm, and turned it. The door didn’t budge. A chain rattled from the inside, a sharp sound that reverberated through the corridor, and something primal shifted in her chest. Jun’s lantern flickered again, casting long, wavering shadows that seemed to stretch toward her, as if the house itself were reaching out, urging them to reconsider. “It’s locked,” she said, her heart sinking.

“Maybe we should go back,” he suggested, glancing over his shoulder as though expecting someone to materialize from the darkened passage behind them. Clara’s pulse quickened. “And leave the prints? The key? We can’t.” She turned back to the door, the chipped paint peeling like dead skin. “We need to know what’s in there.”

“Clara—”

“No!” The word burst out, sharper than she intended, the echo of her voice melding with the oppressive silence. She pressed her palm against the door, feeling the coolness seep into her skin, and closed her eyes for a moment. She wanted to believe that the house was just a house, but the weight of history pressed heavily on her, wrapping around her like a shroud. She thought of the nursery, of the forgotten child that no one spoke of, and the way Nora’s smile had faltered at the mere mention of it.

“Please,” she added, softer now, feeling a flicker of desperation. “Just help me.” Jun hesitated but nodded, the lantern dimming further as if in response to their uncertainty. He moved to her side, shoulder brushing against hers, and together they pushed against the door. The chain rattled again, a taunting reminder of what lay within.
