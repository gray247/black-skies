The nursery door loomed before them, its surface splintered and dull, the paint peeling back like the skin of a forgotten fruit. She could feel the weight of the cracked brass lantern in Jun’s grip, its sputtering glow casting a feeble light that flickered against the darkness pooling around them. The damp air thickened, smelling of decay and something unnamable that clung to the back of her throat.

“Do we really have to?” she breathed, her fingers tightening around the key’s red ribbon. It felt like a lifeline, tethering her to a reality she half-wished to escape. The tang of fear laced through her words, and she glanced at Jun, searching his face for reassurance. Instead, she found his brow furrowed, lines of worry etched deep into his skin, each shadow a silent testament to the choices they had made leading up to this moment.

“Yes,” he replied, though his voice trembled slightly, betraying a crack in his own bravado. The lantern flickered again, and for a heartbeat, the shadows twisted into shapes that danced along the door, flitting away as quickly as they had come. Jun stepped closer, the damp fabric of his coat brushing against her arm, the cold seeping through to her skin. “I mean it, Clara. We can’t just stand here.”

The chain rattled behind them, a metallic clatter that sent a jolt of panic through Clara. She turned, half-expecting to see the door to the service corridor burst open, but nothing followed the sound, just the quiet sigh of the house settling into itself. “What if someone’s trying to get in?” she whispered, her voice barely more than a breath, heavy with dread.

“Or trying to get out,” Jun countered, his eyes narrowing as he focused on the door. “I heard it too, right before we left the dining room.” The admission hung in the air like a storm cloud, heavy with unspoken fears. Clara felt a chill skitter down her spine, and she quickly glanced at the chipped ceramic fox nestled in her pocket, its smooth surface oddly comforting against the turmoil roiling within her.

“Maybe it’s just the wind,” she offered weakly, yet her mind flickered back to Nora’s flinch when Clara had mentioned the nursery. They both knew better than to dismiss the strange events that had been unfolding around them, the inexplicable muddy prints that began nowhere and led to nowhere. The house was no longer a refuge; it had become a labyrinth of secrets and shadows.

“Clara,” Jun’s voice softened, pulling her gaze back to him. “Whatever’s in there, we’ll face it together. We have to know.” He gestured toward the door, his resolve hardening in the dim light. Clara nodded slowly, her heart heavy with resignation. She had to trust him, even if the air around them felt charged, as if something unseen was waiting to pounce.

With trembling fingers, she fit the key into the lock. The metal felt cool against her skin, an anchor to the present moment even as her mind swirled with echoes of the past. She hesitated, the key poised at the threshold, the air thickening with anticipation. “What if it’s just memories?” she murmured, her voice barely above a whisper. “What if we unlock something we can’t put back?”

Jun’s hand brushed against hers, steadying, a silent promise in that fleeting touch. “Then we deal with it. Together.”
