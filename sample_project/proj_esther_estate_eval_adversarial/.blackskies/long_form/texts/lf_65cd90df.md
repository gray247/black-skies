“I’m not sure we have a choice,” he replied, his voice barely above a whisper, the weariness of his soaked coat evident in the way he shifted his weight from one foot to the other. She cut him off, the memory of what he had heard clawing at her resolve. “It could be anything. The house has its own sounds, you know that.” But even as she said it, her heart thudded in her chest, a drumbeat of dread echoing the childish wheeze that had echoed through her mind.

Jun glanced at the door, the wood warped and painted shut, a heavy silence settling like dust in the air. “But what if it’s not? A gust of wind howled outside, rattling the windowpanes, and Clara could almost see the shadows shift, as if the walls themselves were leaning in to overhear. “We need to decide,” she said, her voice trembling slightly as she fought against the growing urge to flee. “We can’t just stand here.” She fumbled with the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon feeling slick against her skin, a tether to her courage and yet a reminder of her doubts.

“What if we’re wrong?” Jun’s gaze was steady, but Clara could see the flicker of fear in his eyes, the unspoken question of whether they were inviting danger into the world they thought they knew. The lantern sputtered once more, the light dimming to a mere wisp, and Clara felt the weight of the moment pressing down on her, as suffocating as the stillness surrounding them.

“Then we’ll deal with it,” she said, more fiercely than she felt. “Together.” But in her mind, the images of the muddy prints trailing into the nursery lingered, a chilling reminder of how easily the past could seep into the present, how quickly the familiar could turn foreign.

With a shuddering breath, she stepped closer to the door, the ceramic fox brushing against her leg, a grounding presence amid the rising tide of uncertainty. She raised her hand, feeling the cool metal of the key in her palm. The lock seemed to glimmer, a promise of answers or perhaps just more questions. “Ready?” she asked, glancing over her shoulder at Jun, who stood a step behind, as if the threshold itself was a threshold to his own fears.

“Do it,” he urged, the word barely escaping his lips, but it held a weight that pressed against her resolve. Clara inserted the key, her heartbeat quickening as she turned it, feeling the resistance of the lock beneath her fingers. The sound of the mechanism shifting was both a relief and a harbinger, echoing in the stillness like the tolling of a distant bell.
