She could still feel the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket, its chipped glaze cool against her palm—an unbidden relic from a time she wished she could forget.

“Do you think we should?” Jun asked, his voice a low murmur, the hesitance threading through his words like an unseen current. He shifted his weight, the sodden fabric of his coat scraping against his skin, a reminder of the storm that had swept through the greenhouse. The rain had soaked through to his bones, but it was the chill of dread that gripped him now.

Clara glanced at him, their eyes briefly locking. She could see the flicker of uncertainty in Jun’s gaze, the way his brow furrowed as if he were trying to solve a puzzle whose pieces had been carelessly scattered. “We have to try,” she replied, her voice steadier than she felt. She reached up instinctively to tug at the red ribbon binding the parlor key to her wrist, a talisman of sorts, though it felt more like a noose tightening with every passing moment.

A sudden noise echoed from within the nursery—soft but unmistakable, like the rustle of fabric against the floor. Clara's heart seized, and she turned to Jun, whose eyes widened in alarm. “Did you hear that?” she whispered, fear coiling in her stomach.

His nod was almost imperceptible, but the lantern flickered in response, the light dimming further as if the very air around them was sucking it away. “Maybe it’s just the house settling,” he said, though his voice lacked conviction. “Or the wind.”

Clara pressed her lips together, resisting the urge to voice her thoughts. The house always settled, but it felt different tonight—like something was shifting, restless beneath the surface. The shadows seemed to thicken, wrapping around them like an unwelcome embrace. She took a breath, the air heavy with the scent of damp wood and something else, something faintly sweet and decayed.

“Clara,” Jun said, breaking the silence, “what if there’s… someone in there?”

“Then we’ll deal with it,” she replied, though her pulse quickened at the thought. The idea of an intruder sent shivers down her spine, and yet, a part of her wondered if it was more than that. She could almost hear the echoes of laughter that had once filled this house, a distant memory that felt tainted now, clouded by years of neglect and secrets.

The soft cough returned, echoing through the heavy air, and this time, Clara felt it resonate deep within her. It was a sound that didn’t belong, a ghostly reminder of childhood innocence lost. “Jun…” she started, but the words caught in her throat, the urgency of the moment pressing against her chest.

“Let’s just—” he began, but the chain on the corridor door rattled, a harsh sound that sliced through the tension between them. Clara whirled around, her breath hitching, but the corridor lay still, the shadowy remains of the landing holding its secrets close.

“What was that?” she asked, her voice barely a whisper.

“I don’t know,” he replied, gripping the lantern tighter, the metal pressing into his palm. “But we can’t stay here.”
