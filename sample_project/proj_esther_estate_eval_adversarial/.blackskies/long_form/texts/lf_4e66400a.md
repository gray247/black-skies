“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, his brow furrowing as he cocked his head toward the shadows at the far end of the corridor. The muddy footprints they had followed seemed to pulse in her mind, a ghostly echo of something that had slipped past them unseen.

“I thought you said it was just the wind,” Clara murmured, her gaze darting back to the door they had just passed. The chain rattled again, a distinct sound that sent a shiver down her spine. Clara followed, the fabric of her coat brushing against the chipped ceramic fox tucked safely in her pocket. It felt warm against her skin, as if it were somehow alive, but she refused to think about it too long.

With each step, the air thickened, charged with something unspoken. Clara’s thoughts swirled, recalling Nora’s tight smile and the way her eyes flickered over the fox. The way she said, “No child has slept in that nursery for years,” but her voice trembled ever so slightly, like the lantern’s light. Clara felt a pang in her chest, a mix of relief and grief, as if the house itself was mourning something long lost.

“Do you think anyone is up there?” Jun asked, breaking the silence, his eyes darting toward the ceiling as if he expected to see a shadow flitting just out of sight. Clara could feel the weight of his gaze, the question hanging between them like a fragile thread. “I mean, besides us?”

“Maybe it’s just the house,” Clara suggested, though doubt gnawed at her. The cough she hadn’t heard echoed back in her mind, a child’s soft rasp piercing through the fog of memory. Jun glanced at her, and for a fleeting moment, she saw the glimmer of something raw in his expression—a fear mirrored by her own.

The door to the nursery loomed ahead, its wooden surface worn and splintered, the paint chipped away to reveal layers of history beneath. Clara’s heart raced as she approached, the lantern sputtering again, casting erratic shadows that danced like specters. She reached for the handle, hesitating just a breath away, the air thick with expectation. “What if it’s locked?”

“Then we’ll find another way in,” Jun replied, though she could hear the uncertainty creeping into his tone. She noticed the way his fingers tightened around the lantern, as if it were a lifeline.
