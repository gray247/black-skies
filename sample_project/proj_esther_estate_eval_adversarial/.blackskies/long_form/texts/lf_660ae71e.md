Clara pressed forward, the lantern’s flickering light casting anxious shadows against the damp stone walls of the corridor. Each step splashed through a growing pool of muddy water, remnants of the relentless rain that had invaded the greenhouse, now seeping into the very bones of the house. Jun walked close behind her, his coat still clinging to him like a second skin, soaked through and heavy. She could hear the soft squelch of his shoes as he shuffled, the sound echoing in her mind, a reminder that they were not alone in this suffocating stillness.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, but the tremor in it was unmistakable, a raw thread of fear weaving through the dim air. Clara’s heart quickened at the thought, but she shook her head, forcing a dismissive laugh that felt brittle on her tongue. “It’s just the house settling,” she replied, quicker than she intended, her pulse pounding loudly in her ears. She tugged at the ribbon around her wrist, the parlor key swinging gently, brushing against her skin like a warning.

As they rounded the corner, the lantern sputtered again, flickering out for a heartbeat before the light returned, casting a weak glow on the walls. Clara squinted, the shadows elongating and twisting, making it hard to tell where the darkness ended and the light began. The air felt thick, almost gelatinous, as if the very atmosphere was pushing against her, urging her to turn back. But there was no turning back, not now.

“Look,” Jun said, his voice strained, pointing ahead. Muddy prints marred the floor, leading away from them, leaving no trace of a return.
