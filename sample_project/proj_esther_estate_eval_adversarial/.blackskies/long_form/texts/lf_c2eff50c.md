Clara pressed her ear against the door, the wood cool and unyielding, its surface rough against her skin. The chill seeped into her bones, a reminder of the storm brewing outside, or perhaps the storm that had long taken residence inside this house. She could feel Jun’s presence beside her, the tension in his shoulders palpable even in the dim light of the sputtering lantern. Shadows danced across the walls, twisting like the thoughts racing through her mind. What if nothing was waiting behind the door? Or worse, what if something was?

“Clara,” Jun’s voice was low, almost lost in the hush that enveloped them. “Maybe we should just—”

“No,” she interrupted, her voice sharper than intended, echoing off the walls. She hesitated, then softened her tone. “We can’t leave it like this. We have to know.”

He glanced down the corridor, his eyes flitting nervously toward the chained door behind them, the sound of rattling metal still echoing in the back of her mind. “But what if it’s just the house settling? What if it’s just our imagination?”

The notion of the house being alive, a breathing entity filled with secrets, sent a shiver up her spine. She felt the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket, the chipped glaze cool and reassuring against the fabric of her coat. A talisman, perhaps, against whatever was lurking within these walls. “You heard the cough, didn’t you?”

He hesitated, the lantern flickering again, throwing erratic beams of light that illuminated the dust motes swirling in the air. “I thought I did,” he murmured, his voice dropping to a near whisper, “but Clara—”

She turned to face him, the urgency of their situation pinching her chest. “But it could be a child. A lost child, Jun.” The thought dug at her, twisting into a knot of worry and regret. She didn't want to believe it, but the idea unfurled like the vines they had forced through in the greenhouse, reaching out for something, someone.

The air thickened with uncertainty, and Clara’s grip tightened around the ribbon tied to her wrist, where the parlor key hung like a silent promise. She could almost feel Nora’s eyes on her, the way they shifted when Clara had produced the key earlier. “No child has slept in the nursery for years,” she had insisted, but the flinch was unmistakable, a crack in her composure that Clara had seen clearly.

Jun shifted nervously, the weight of the lantern seeming heavier in his hand, its light dwindling further. “We should go back. This isn’t safe.”

But Clara shook her head, her heart thudding against her ribs, drowning out the echoes of doubt. “What if it is safe? What if we’re just too afraid to look?”

With a deep breath, she grasped the doorknob, its cold metal biting into her palm. The sound of the chain on the other side rattled again, a low rumble like distant thunder, but she couldn’t step back now. She had come too far. “Help me,” she murmured, her voice barely above a whisper, and Jun took a step closer, his warmth grounding her in the encroaching darkness.

Together, they turned the knob, the door creaking open in a slow, deliberate arc, revealing a nursery steeped in shadow. The faint smell of mildew and aged wood wrapped around them, thick and oppressive. Clara squinted into the gloom, her heart racing as the lantern’s light struggled against the darkness, revealing outlines of forgotten toys and faded wallpaper peeling at the edges.

“See?” she whispered, her voice trembling. “Nothing to be afraid of.”
