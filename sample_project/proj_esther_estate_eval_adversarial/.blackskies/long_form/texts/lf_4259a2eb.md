Clara drew her hand back from the nursery door, a shiver coursing through her fingers as if the wood itself had whispered something too fragile to grasp. The air was thick with the scent of damp earth, mingling with the stale odor of old paint and sun-faded wallpaper. Time felt suspended in this moment, each ticking second echoing in the silence that enveloped them, heavy and oppressive. Jun shifted beside her, the crackling lantern flickering in his hand, casting restless shadows that danced across the peeling surface of the door.

“What if there’s someone in there?” he murmured, his brow furrowed with worry, the weight of his soaked coat pulling at him like a leaden shroud. Clara could see the way his fingers tightened around the lantern as if it were a lifeline. “Or something?”

“Or something,” she echoed, her voice barely rising above the hum of the house settling around them. She glanced at the cracked brass lantern, its glow dwindling to a near nothingness, and felt the pulse of hesitation thrumming between them. They were standing at the threshold not just of a room but of the past—her past, and perhaps more frighteningly, the past of the house itself.

“I still can’t believe you found the key,” Jun said, a hint of admiration threading through his anxiety. Clara’s eyes flicked to the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the key dangling like a secret she couldn’t quite bring herself to unveil. She remembered the moment she’d discovered it tangled in the wet vines, the way it had felt like an invitation and a warning all at once.

“Do you think it’ll fit?” Clara asked, her voice wavering. She tilted her head, examining the ancient lock, its surface worn and grooved from years of neglect. It seemed to mock her, a stubborn guardian of whatever lay beyond. The soft thud of her heartbeat echoed in her ears, and she brushed her thumb over the key, feeling its cold metal warmth against her skin.

“I don’t know. But we can’t just stand here,” Jun pressed, his words urgent and laden with a quiet desperation. He glanced over his shoulder, as if expecting something—or someone—to emerge from the shadows. Clara followed his gaze, the chill creeping up her spine. She couldn’t shake the feeling that they were being watched, the very walls of the house holding their breath, waiting for them to make a move.

With a slow, deliberate motion, she raised her hand, the key poised at the lock. The sound of the chain rattling against the door behind them sent a jolt through her. “Did you hear that?” she whispered, her voice barely above a breath. Jun nodded, his eyes wide, the lantern’s flickering light casting deep shadows on his face.

“It’s probably just the wind,” he offered, though the tremor in his voice betrayed his uncertainty. Clara felt the weight of his gaze, and in that moment, she realized how much they were tethered to each other, two souls adrift in a sea of unknowing. The house felt alive, breathing with them, its very structure creaking under the strain of memories long buried.

Clara turned back to the lock, her resolve hardening. She took a breath, steadying herself against the swirl of emotions—fear, curiosity, guilt. The key slid into the lock with a soft click, and she felt a shiver run through her, as if she had awakened something that had lain dormant for too long. “Here goes nothing,” she said, though the words felt hollow in the face of the moment.
