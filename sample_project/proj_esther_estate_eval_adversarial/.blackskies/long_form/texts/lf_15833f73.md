The lantern flickered, casting a weak glow that danced on the damp walls of the service corridor, shadows collapsing and reforming like anxious thoughts. Clara clutched the cracked brass handle, the bite of cold metal biting into her palm, as she stepped forward, the soles of her shoes squishing against the damp floor. The air was thick, heavy with the scent of wet earth and something else, something sour lingering in the corners of the corridor. She could feel the weight of the soundless house pressing in on her, suffocating in its stillness.

Jun stood close behind her, his coat still soaked from the greenhouse rain, droplets pooling on the ground beneath him. He shivered, not just from the cold but from a dread that clawed at the edges of his voice. “We shouldn’t be here, Clara. This feels wrong.” His breath fogged in the cool air, a ghostly wisp that vanished too quickly.

“Nothing’s wrong,” Clara snapped, the sharpness of her tone surprising even herself. She glanced back at him, taking in the flash of fear in his eyes, the way his fingers trembled against the lantern’s side. “We’re just looking for a way out.” But even as she said it, unease curled in her stomach, a knot tightening with each step deeper into the corridor. She could feel the parlor key, tied to her wrist with that absurd red ribbon, brushing against her skin, a reminder of their hasty escape from the greenhouse. The thought of the key’s weight made her anxious; she couldn’t remember if she had tied it tighter that morning or if it had simply come loose in the chaos.
