She could feel the rough grain of the wood beneath her fingertips, the ancient knots and splinters worn smooth by years of neglect. “What if someone’s in there?” she whispered, her breath ghosting against the door. The idea of a presence lurking just beyond it twisted her stomach into knots.

Jun shifted beside her, the damp fabric of his coat rustling as he leaned closer, the lantern’s flickering light casting erratic shadows that danced on the walls. “We can’t just leave,” he said, his voice low but firm. “Not after finding those prints.” He gestured toward the muddy trail that snaked away from the door, the way it seemed to lead deeper into the shadows rather than retreating. Clara swallowed hard, her throat tight.

“Maybe it’s just the house playing tricks,” she offered, trying to sound more convinced than she felt. But the chill creeping up her spine told a different story. She shivered, imagining the secrets the walls had swallowed over the years, the way they kept watch over the memories that lingered like dust motes in the air. She glanced at the lantern, its glow dimming further, the light sputtering as if it was gasping for breath. “We need a better plan.”

Jun’s brows furrowed, and for a moment, the uncertainty in his eyes mirrored her own. “What if we just—” He hesitated, glancing at the door again. “What if we make some noise? See if anything responds?”

Clara bit her lip, the thought of inviting whatever might be lurking inside sent a fresh wave of dread crashing over her. “And what if it’s not just a memory? What if it’s something that shouldn’t be here?” She felt the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket, its chipped surface pressing against her thigh like a reminder of the nursery’s secrets—the one place they had been told to avoid.
