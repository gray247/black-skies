Clara tugged the damp fabric of her coat closer around her shoulders, the chill of the air settling under her skin like an unwelcome guest. The lantern in Jun's hand flickered weakly, casting a jittery light that danced across the peeling wallpaper, its glow barely reaching the corners of the cluttered parlor. Clara squinted into the gloom, the shadows of furniture looming like specters, remnants of a life once vibrant but now faded and forgotten.

“Do you think it’s safe?” Jun’s voice was a low murmur, edged with a tension that seemed to crackle in the air between them. He shifted his weight, water still dripping from his coat onto the floor, forming small dark puddles that mingled with the dust. She could see the worry etched in the lines of his brow, the way his mouth pressed into a thin line of uncertainty.

“It has to be,” she replied, her tone brisker than she intended. She didn’t want to dwell on the fraying threads of unease coiling in her stomach. They’d come too far to turn back now, but the memory of the greenhouse—its suffocating humidity and the frantic rustle of the vines—haunted her. The key, still tied to her wrist with a red ribbon, felt heavier than its weight suggested, a tether to something both precious and perilous.

Clara glanced toward the service corridor door, its chain rattling softly with a gust of wind that crept through the cracks. A faint echo of her heartbeat thrummed in her ears, urging her forward. “We should check upstairs,” she suggested, though the words tasted bitter, like the remnants of rain on her tongue.

“Upstairs?” Jun’s eyes widened, mirroring the reluctance she felt blooming within her. “What if—”

“What if what?” she interrupted, the sharpness in her voice surprising even herself.
