Water dripped from the hem, pooling at his feet and mingling with the mud that clung to his boots. Clara walked a step ahead, her posture tense, the red ribbon around her wrist stark against the damp fabric of her coat, the parlor key dangling like a promise or a curse.

“Did you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice barely above a murmur, the words hanging heavy with unspoken dread. He could feel the pulse of fear in his throat, a desperate urge to flee this place that felt alive with secrets. Clara stopped, her back to him, and for a moment, the lantern’s light caught the edge of her profile, illuminating the sharpness of her jaw and the set of her lips. She was too still, as if she were listening for something just out of reach.

“I didn’t hear anything,” she replied, but her tone was too quick, a brittle edge creeping in that betrayed her own unease. The tension twisted between them, palpable and thick, a silent storm gathering force. Jun moved closer, the floor creaking beneath him as he stepped into the pool of light, the darkness beyond feeling like an open mouth ready to swallow them whole.

“Clara,” he pressed, his voice shaking, “we shouldn’t be here.
