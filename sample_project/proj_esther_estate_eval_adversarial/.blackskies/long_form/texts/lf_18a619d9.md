The lantern sputtered again, a feeble pulse of light that barely cut through the enveloping gloom. Clara pressed her ear against the nursery door, the wood cold and unyielding beneath her cheek. The muffled sounds of the house settling—creaks and sighs—seemed to echo her own unease. She could feel Jun's presence beside her, a taut line of tension that vibrated in the air. “Clara,” he whispered, his breath warm against the chill, “we should—”

“Wait,” she interrupted, her voice sharper than she intended, slicing through the thick atmosphere. The urgency thrummed in her chest, a drumbeat of instinct that urged her to listen, to focus. The lantern flickered again, shadows stretching and warping, twisting into grotesque shapes that danced along the walls. She could almost see the specters of her own fears flitting just beyond the edge of the light, teasing her with the possibility of the unknown.

She straightened, drawing back from the door, her thoughts swirling like the damp air around them. “Did you hear that?” she asked, a tremor threading through her voice. She was acutely aware of the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key dangling like a weighty promise, or perhaps a curse.

Jun hesitated, his brow furrowed, eyes darting to the door. “What? The cough?” His voice was cautious, each word measured. Clara felt the tug of the ceramic fox nestled in her coat pocket, its chipped surface cool against her fingertips. The way Nora had flinched when she saw it flashed in her mind, a flicker of something unspoken, something buried.

“Yeah,” she said, forcing herself to keep her tone steady. “It sounded like a child.” The thought sent a shiver racing down her spine, igniting a thread of fear in her gut. The nursery was supposed to be empty, a relic of a past that had long since faded.

“I didn’t hear anything,” Jun replied, his eyes narrowing, as if he were trying to pierce through the door. The shadows around them thickened, and Clara felt the weight of his gaze—worried, searching. “But you’re sure it came from in there?”

“Not sure of anything,” Clara admitted, her voice falling to a whisper as she glanced at the closed window, painted shut from the inside, as if even the light feared what it might find within. The chain rattled again, a metallic whisper that sent a bolt of ice through her veins. It should have been two floors down, not here, not now.

“Let’s just get out of here,” Jun urged, a note of desperation creeping into his tone. He shifted, the fabric of his soaked coat rustling, and Clara could smell the damp earth clinging to him, an unshakeable reminder of the greenhouse and the rain. Something about that rain felt like a prelude, a portent of the storm gathering in the distance, dark clouds that loomed behind her eyes, threatening to pour.

The lantern dimmed again, its glow dwindling to a breath of light. Clara felt a surge of frustration, a desperate need to break this stasis, to act before whatever lay beyond the door became too real. “We can’t just leave,” she said, her heart pounding in sync with the ticking clock somewhere above them. “What if there’s something—someone—who needs help?”

Jun shook his head, a quick and sharp movement, but the worry etched into his features softened his resolve. “And what if it’s not? Clara, we don’t know what’s waiting for us in there.”
