She turned to him, the dim light from his lantern casting a flickering glow on his drenched coat, clinging to him like a second skin. The brass lantern sputtered, its weak beam flickering like a heartbeat on the verge of faltering.

“I don’t know,” she replied, too quickly, too sharply. Her words felt jagged, like shards of glass caught in her throat. She glanced back down the dark corridor, where the service door loomed like a sentinel, the heavy chain rattling softly as if something or someone on the other side was testing it, probing for a way through. The sound sent a shiver racing up her spine, and she instinctively tugged at the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key dangling from it like a lifeline.

“What if—” Jun started, but Clara cut him off, her voice rising in defiance. “It’s just the wind.
