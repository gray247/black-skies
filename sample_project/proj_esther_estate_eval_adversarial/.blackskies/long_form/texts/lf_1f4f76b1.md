Jun squinted into the dimness, the sputtering lantern casting flickering shadows that danced like specters against the damp stone walls. The corridor felt alive, a narrow passageway that pulsed with secrets, each hollow sound echoing back to them—a reminder that they were not alone, though they should be. The mud underfoot squished, a viscous reminder of the rain that had flooded the greenhouse, the wet earth seeming to creep closer with every heartbeat. He could feel Clara's presence beside him, her breath quick and shallow, a sharp contrast to the oppressive silence that loomed ahead.

“Do you hear that?” Jun’s voice barely rose above a whisper, his throat dry and scratchy, the remnants of unease clawing at his insides. He thought he caught the echo of a faint cough, a child's cough, perhaps, but Clara shook her head, her dark hair brushing against her shoulder as she focused on the door ahead, the chain rattling softly, a persistent reminder of something on the other side.

“It’s nothing,” she said, too quickly, the words spilling out like a desperate plea. “It’s just the wind.” But even as she spoke, her fingers twitched at the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key nestled against her skin, a tangible weight that offered no comfort. She had tightened the ribbon earlier, hadn’t she? It felt looser now, a fraying lifeline in this oppressive darkness.

Jun swallowed hard, the slickness of his coat clinging uncomfortably as he shifted, the damp fabric serving not only as a reminder of the storm but of his own rising panic. “I don’t think it’s just the wind,” he replied, his voice wavering slightly as he adjusted his grip on the lantern, the light dimming further as if it too shared in their trepidation. “We should—”
