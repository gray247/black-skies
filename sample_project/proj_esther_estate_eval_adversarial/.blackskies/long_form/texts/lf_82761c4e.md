The air hung thickly around Clara as she and Jun stepped into the dim corridor, the faint echo of their footsteps swallowed by the damp walls. The cracks in the plaster seemed to pulse with shadows, the lantern’s sputtering flame casting a flickering glow that barely reached the corners. Clara tightened her grip on the parlor key, the red ribbon digging into her wrist, a constant reminder of the weight of what they had unearthed.

Jun’s breath came in quick gasps, his coat still soaked from the drenching rain in the greenhouse, droplets clinging to the fabric like stubborn memories. “Do you hear that?” he whispered, his voice trembling as he paused, eyes darting toward the chained door at the end of the passage. The chain rattled softly, a sound that sent a shiver racing up Clara's spine. She felt the chill of dread settle in her stomach, but she couldn’t let herself show it. Not now.

“No,” she replied too sharply, pushing past him, each step echoing louder than the last. Muddy prints trailed ahead of them, stark against the worn floorboards, leading deeper into the shadows. Clara’s heart thudded in her chest, the rhythm quickening as she caught sight of the marks. Who had come this way? The question curled in her mind, twisting tighter with every breath.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice low as he caught up to her. “What if—what if it’s someone still in the house?”

Her gaze flickered back to the door, the chain still vibrating as if something on the other side was testing its strength. “Then we need to find out,” she replied, though the words felt hollow even to her. She glanced at the lantern, now dimmer than before, the weak battery threatening to give out entirely. “We can’t just stand here.”
