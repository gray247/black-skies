The lantern flickered, its warm glow barely piercing the thick air of the nursery landing. Clara stood frozen before the door, the heavy wooden panel looming like a sentry. She could feel the damp chill seeping through her coat, a remnant of the greenhouse downpour, and she shifted her weight from one foot to the other, her eyes darting back to Jun, who leaned against the wall, the cracked brass lantern clutched tightly in his trembling hand.

“Do you hear that?” he whispered, his voice barely rising above the creaking house, as if afraid the walls might listen. Clara shook her head, though a tightness coiled in her chest. She didn’t want to admit the truth—that she felt something, a nagging presence just beyond the threshold of her understanding, something that pulled at her with a child’s cough echoing in her mind.

The door to the nursery remained shut, painted over, sealed tight, but Clara could almost feel the weight of its secrets pressing against her. She glanced down at the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the key dangling like a forgotten promise. How had it come to hold such gravity, tying her to a place she wasn’t sure she wanted to enter? She tugged at it, feeling the roughness of the fabric against her skin, as if the ribbon itself were alive, coaxing her toward the mysteries hidden behind the door.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice low and urgent, “we can’t just stand here.”

The air thickened with unspoken fears, the kind that wrapped around her throat and made it hard to breathe. She nodded, forcing herself to take a step closer, the floorboards creaking under her weight as she reached for the handle. It felt cool beneath her palm, a stark contrast to the warmth of her skin. She hesitated, a flash of doubt igniting the back of her mind. What if the key didn’t fit? What if they were wrong about everything? But the thought of returning to the uncertainty of the hallway, with its muffled echoes and the rattling chain that seemed to mock them, was worse.

“Do you think anyone is really up there?” Jun asked, his eyes narrowing as he peered at the door as if he could see through it, through the years and the silence. “Nora said—”

“Nora is wrong,” Clara interrupted, her voice sharper than she intended. She could feel the weight of his gaze on her, searching for reassurance, for a shared belief in the tangible. But she had lost her own anchor in this place, the solidity of what she thought she knew.

With a deep breath, she inserted the key into the lock, the metal cold and unyielding under her fingers. A turn, a click, and the sound resonated loudly in the stillness, echoing in her ears, drowning out the whispers of doubt. She pushed the door open, heart racing as the hinges creaked like the groan of a distant memory coming back to life.

The nursery lay before them, cloaked in shadows, the air thick with the scent of dust and something else—something sweet and rotten at once. The dim light from the lantern illuminated the edges of the room, revealing a faded rocking horse in the corner, its paint chipped and peeling, and a small chest that seemed to have been moved recently, the absence of scrape marks on the floor betraying the care taken to relocate it.

“Where did that come from?” Jun murmured, stepping inside, his voice barely above a whisper.
