The air around them felt heavy, almost sentient, wrapping around her chest as if urging her to retreat into the safety of the unfamiliar shadows. She glanced sideways at Jun, whose damp coat clung to him, the fabric saturated with the scent of the rain-soaked greenhouse. The cracked brass lantern in his grasp sputtered weakly, its flickering flame casting jittery shadows that danced erratically on the walls, teasing the edges of the room into a semblance of life.

“Maybe we should just go,” he murmured, his voice a thread of sound that seemed to dissolve into the creaking of the house. Clara could sense the tremor in his words, the uncertainty that lay beneath. He shifted his weight, and the lantern wobbled dangerously, the light dimming further, as if echoing their collective doubt.

“No,” Clara said, more forcefully than she intended, the word cracking like thunder in the stillness. “We can’t just leave.” The urgency in her own voice surprised her, resonating against the door, challenging the silence that enveloped them. It felt wrong to turn back now, not when they had come this far. The nursery stood as a threshold to something they had both been avoiding, something buried beneath layers of neglect and shadow.

She slipped her fingers around the red ribbon tied to her wrist, the key dangling against her palm, its presence both comforting and unsettling. She couldn’t recall tying it tighter, but the thought of losing it felt more alarming than the idea of what lay behind the door. With a deep breath, she pulled the key from her wrist, the satin fabric gliding against her skin, and turned to the lock, her heart racing in a rhythm that felt both familiar and frightening.

“Clara,” Jun’s voice came again, softer this time, laced with a fragile kind of concern. “What if it’s not what we think?”

She paused, the key hovering just above the lock. His words slithered through her resolve, pulling at the threads of her determination. She remembered the muddy prints, the way they had appeared out of nowhere, shifting the narrative of their search into something darker. “What if it is?” she countered, the challenge in her voice revealing her desperation. “What if it’s exactly what we need to face?”

The lantern flickered once more, struggling against the darkness, and she felt the weight of Jun’s gaze on her, steady and searching. He wanted her to reconsider, to weigh the risks that hung between them like the shadows that licked at the corners of the hallway. But she could see it in his eyes—the same flicker of curiosity that had driven them both to this moment, this very door.

“Okay,” he finally said, exhaling a long breath. “But if something goes wrong—”

“I know.” She locked her gaze on the keyhole, the shape of the key almost blurring against her vision. With a firm turn, she slid the key into the lock. It caught for a heartbeat before giving way, the sound of the mechanism clicking echoing through the corridor, reverberating against the walls like an awakening. Clara’s heart raced, and she pushed against the door, feeling the resistance give way to a heavy creak that felt like the house exhaling.

As the door swung open, the dim light from the lantern spilled into the nursery, illuminating dust motes swirling lazily in the stale air. The room was a time capsule, untouched yet heavy with the weight of memories, the musty scent of aged wood and forgotten toys flooding her senses.
