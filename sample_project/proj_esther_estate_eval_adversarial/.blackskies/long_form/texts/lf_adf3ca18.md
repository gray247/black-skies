Clara pressed her ear against the nursery door, willing the wood to yield secrets. The clock’s thirteenth chime resonated in her bones, a warning or perhaps a plea. Beneath the hollow notes, she could feel Jun’s presence, a warm solidity against the encroaching chill, yet the lantern’s flickering light revealed more shadows than it dispelled. She shifted her weight, the ceramic fox pressing uncomfortably against her thigh, its chipped surface cool and oddly soothing.

“Do you hear that?” Clara whispered, half-hopeful, half-fearful. Her heart raced, thrumming in time with the clock’s relentless rhythm. She strained to catch a sound, anything beyond the muffled thud of her pulse and the distant creaks of the house settling around them. Jun’s expression was obscured in the dim light, but she could feel the tension radiating from him, a wire pulled taut and ready to snap.

He shook his head, his breath visible in the chill air. “It’s just the house,” he murmured, but there was an edge to his voice, a flicker of doubt that made her heart lurch. “Let’s just try to open the door.”

Clara nodded, though her gaze lingered on the worn brass knob, the ornate designs dulled with age. She could feel the weight of the world pressing down on her, the secrets of the nursery mounting in the silence. With a swift movement, she turned the knob, but the door resisted her, a stubborn guardian of whatever lay beyond. The faint scent of dust and something sweeter, almost floral, wafted from the crack, teasing her senses.

“Maybe it’s locked?” Jun suggested, his voice low, almost conspiratorial. He stepped closer, the lantern casting a feeble glow that seemed to shudder with each flicker. Clara glanced at him, her heart heavy with an unspoken question—what if they weren’t alone?

“No,” she breathed, feeling the key on her wrist, the red ribbon almost a tether to her childhood, to memories that felt both distant and hauntingly close. She hesitated, then pulled the key from the ribbon, her fingers trembling. The metal was surprisingly warm against her skin, as though it had absorbed the heat of her body. “I think I can get it open.”

“Wait—Clara,” he cautioned, his voice a low murmur, but the resolve in her chest pressed her forward. She inserted the key into the lock, the metal grinding against rust. It felt wrong, like the key was meant for a different door, a door that wasn’t meant to be opened.

“Just let me try,” she insisted, though uncertainty gnawed at her. She twisted the key, a soft click echoing in the silence that followed, and for a moment, she felt suspended in time, caught between the past and the unknown future that loomed behind the door.

But as the latch released, a gust of stale air rushed out, pulling at her hair and filling her lungs with something sharp, familiar, and yet foreign. Clara recoiled slightly, a chill running down her spine. The lantern flickered violently, casting frantic shadows against the walls, and she felt a connection to something deeper, something buried in the nursery’s history that she couldn’t quite grasp.

“Clara…” Jun’s voice trailed off, laced with warning, but she didn’t want to turn back. She pushed the door open, its creaking hinges sounding like a mournful sigh, and stepped inside, the threshold feeling like a crossing into another realm.
