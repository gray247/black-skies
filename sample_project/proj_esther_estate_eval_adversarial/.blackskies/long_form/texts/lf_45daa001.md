Clara pressed her back against the sealed nursery door, the coolness of the wood seeping through the thin fabric of her dress. The lantern flickered once more, casting a feeble light that barely cut through the encroaching shadows. Jun stood beside her, his soaked coat clinging uncomfortably to his skin, the damp fabric a constant reminder of their reckless venture into the greenhouse. The air felt thick, as if the house itself was holding its breath, waiting for something to happen. She could feel the pulse of the moment, the way her heart raced in sync with the distant, echoing chime of the clock somewhere above, counting down to an unknown reckoning.

“Do you hear that?” Jun whispered, his eyes flickering toward the staircase, where the darkness seemed to thicken. Clara shook her head, though her thoughts were a tangle of fears and memories. The cough. He’d nearly said something about it. The child’s cough echoing in her mind, as real as the humid air swirling around them, yet as intangible as the shadows that slipped across the walls. “It’s just the house,” she said, her voice steadier than she felt. “It’s always making noises.”

But the way Jun’s gaze lingered on the upper landing suggested he wasn’t convinced, as if he could see something just beyond her perception, something lurking in the corners of the house. The lantern sputtered again, and Clara felt the tremor in her hand, the red ribbon key tied tightly around her wrist, a small comfort in this oppressive darkness. She had tied it tighter, hadn’t she? Her fingers brushed against the smooth ceramic of the fox charm tucked deep in her pocket, an anchor to childhood memories that felt like they belonged to someone else entirely.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice low, almost conspiratorial. “What if we’re not alone?” The words hung in the air, heavy with unspoken implications. The shadows seemed to close in around them, the walls of the corridor breathing in and out, whispering secrets she wasn’t ready to hear. The chain rattled again, a sound that sent a chill racing down her spine. It shouldn’t be moving. It couldn’t be moving. They were the only ones here.

“Maybe it’s just the wind,” she offered, but even to herself, her voice sounded hollow, lacking conviction. The house had settled into a silence that was almost conspiratorial, as if it were listening, waiting for them to make a choice. “Or—” she hesitated, the thought unspooling like a thread. “Or maybe it’s just the storm.”

“Yeah, the storm,” he echoed, but the uncertainty in his voice betrayed him. He glanced back at the door, the chain that held it closed taunting them with its very presence. Clara felt the weight of his gaze, the way it anchored her in this moment, in this shared fear. The muddy prints that had led them here felt like a riddle with no answer, twisting around them like the very vines that had tangled the parlor key outside. Where had they begun? Where did they lead?

“We could go back,” she suggested, though the words tasted bitter on her tongue. The thought of retreating felt like surrender, a yielding to the dark corners of the house that had always felt too alive, too aware. But standing here, in front of this sealed door, her resolve wavered. Would they find something inside, or would it simply echo her own fears, the ghosts of a past she thought she'd buried?

Jun shifted beside her, the lantern dimming further, the glow dwindling like their courage. “What if it’s not just the house?” he asked, his voice barely a whisper now. “What if it’s something more?”
