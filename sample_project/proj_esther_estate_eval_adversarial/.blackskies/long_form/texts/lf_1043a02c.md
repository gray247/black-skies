Clara leaned closer to the nursery door, her breath hitching as the cool wood seemed to pulse beneath her palm. It felt almost alive, holding memories she could not name. The flickering light of Jun’s lantern cast long shadows that reached for her, as if the house itself were beckoning her closer, whispering secrets she was unprepared to hear. The soft sputter of the dying battery sent a fresh wave of anxiety through her, the light dimming with each passing moment, mirroring her growing uncertainty.

“Do you hear that?” Jun's voice broke through the thick silence, barely above a whisper. His eyes were wide, reflecting the faint glow of the lantern. Clara felt the weight of his gaze on her, a mix of fear and something she couldn’t quite place.

“Hear what?” she asked, forcing the words out, though a chill prickled at the back of her neck. The house seemed to hold its breath, and she felt as if the very walls were leaning in, eavesdropping on their exchange.

“There’s something in there,” Jun said, tilting his head toward the door. “I thought I heard a—”

She cut him off, the memory of the child’s cough echoing in her mind, a sound she had dismissed as nothing but a trick of the imagination. “There’s nothing in there,” she insisted, her voice firmer than she felt. “Nora said the nursery hasn’t been used in years.”

Jun shifted, his coat still damp from the rain, beads of water glistening in the lantern's waning light. “But the prints… they weren’t ours. They came from here.” He pointed toward the door, his finger trembling slightly. “What if someone else is trying to get in?”

The uncertainty in his voice mirrored her own spiraling thoughts. Clara’s fingers brushed against the ribbon tying the parlor key to her wrist, as if seeking reassurance in the fabric’s familiar texture. Each tug of the ribbon felt like a tether to something solid, yet the more she focused on it, the more it slipped from her grasp, an enigma wrapped in a riddle. “We should just go back,” she suggested, though her heart wasn’t in it. The urge to turn away battled fiercely with the magnetic pull of the door.

Then, just above them, a sound—a soft scraping, like a fingernail tracing the edge of a window. She froze, eyes darting upward, the air thick with tension. Jun’s breath quickened beside her, and she could feel the pulse in his throat, the frantic rhythm matching her own.

“We’re not alone,” he said, his voice barely a breath, and Clara felt a rush of panic as the chain rattled again from the other side of the corridor door. The sound echoed down the length of the passage, a stark reminder of their vulnerability. Something—or someone—was testing the chain, pushing against it with a persistence that sent cold tendrils of dread creeping up her spine.

“Jun,” she breathed, her voice trembling. “What if…” The thought hung between them, unspoken yet heavy, pressing down like the damp, stagnant air of the nursery.

But before she could finish, the lantern flickered violently, casting frantic shadows that seemed to leap in time with her heartbeat. Clara’s hand shot to the door, fingers curling around the handle, but she hesitated, the sudden weight of indecision anchoring her feet to the floor. “What if it’s the cough? What if it’s—”

“Clara!” Jun’s voice was urgent, slicing through the haze of her thoughts. “We need to move. Now.”
