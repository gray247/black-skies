Clara pressed her shoulder against the cool, damp stone of the corridor, her breath shallow as the shadows deepened around them. The lantern sputtered again, its feeble glow barely piercing the oppressive darkness, and she could feel the weight of Jun’s fear pressing against her like the humidity that clung to the air. “What if there’s someone in here with us?” he whispered, his voice trembling, a feather caught in the wind.

“Don’t be ridiculous,” she snapped, too quickly, the harshness of her tone echoing against the walls. She tried to keep her voice steady, but it quavered at the edges, betraying the unease that had begun to knot like a snake in her stomach. The red ribbon around her wrist felt tighter somehow, as if it were constricting with each shaky breath she took. She glanced at Jun, his coat still soaked from the greenhouse rain, the fabric clinging to him like a second skin, accentuating the hollowness in his cheeks.

They moved deeper into the corridor, the air thick with an earthy musk, mingling with the faint scent of something sweetly decayed. Clara's fingers brushed against the chipped ceramic fox nestled in her pocket, its cool surface a reminder of innocence, of childhood stories that felt light years away. “Look,” she said, her voice lowering to a hushed urgency, “those prints…”

Mud smeared the stone floor ahead, fresh tracks leading into the shadows, not the way they had come. Her heart pounded as she traced the path with her eyes, the imprints deep and erratic, as if whoever had made them was in a hurry. “Jun,” she breathed, the word slipping out like a prayer. “We should go back.”
