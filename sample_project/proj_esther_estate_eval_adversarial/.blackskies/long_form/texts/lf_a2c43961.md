She could almost convince herself that the whispers of the house were alive, curling around her ankles like the tendrils of the ivy outside the greenhouse. Jun shifted beside her, his coat still glistening with residual rainwater, droplets falling to the floor in a muted patter that echoed the unease in her heart.

“Do you hear that?” His voice was low, almost a murmur, as if speaking too loudly would shatter the fragile stillness that enveloped them. Clara’s fingers curled tightly around the parlor key, the red ribbon pressing into her wrist like a reminder of the unraveling mystery before them. She shook her head, though a pit of uncertainty opened in her stomach.

“I don’t hear anything,” she replied, her voice a whisper, laden with uncertainty. The thought of a child’s cough lingered in her mind, a ghostly echo of something that shouldn’t exist in a house that had long been empty of laughter. Yet, every fiber of her being wanted to believe Jun, to share in the fear that clung to them like the dampness in the air.

Jun stepped closer, the lantern swaying in his grip as he peered into the shadows pooled around them. “I swear I heard it,” he insisted, his brow furrowing in concentration. Clara caught the flicker of doubt in his eyes, a fleeting moment of vulnerability that made her want to reach out and assure him that they were not alone in this, that whatever lurked in the corners of the nursery wouldn’t swallow them whole.

She glanced at the door, its weathered surface marred with scratches and faded paint, remnants of a time when children’s laughter had echoed through its threshold. The chipped ceramic fox in her pocket felt heavier now, as if it were a talisman weighing her down with memories of innocence lost. “We should just open it,” she said, forcing the words out, though her heart raced at the prospect. “Let’s see what’s inside.”

“Yeah, but…” Jun hesitated, the lantern dimming momentarily, the shadows creeping closer as if they were sentient, hungry for their hesitation. “What if we find something we don’t want to see?”

Clara met his gaze, the question hanging in the air, thick with the weight of their shared history—the secrets that lay buried beneath layers of dust and time. “What if we find the answers we need?” she countered, her voice steadier than she felt. The house creaked and settled around them, the sound a low rumble that felt like an agreement, or perhaps a warning.

Just as she reached for the door handle, a sharp clatter echoed above them, the sound of metal scraping against wood. It was the chain on the service corridor door, shifting as if someone were testing it from the other side. Clara’s heart dropped, the color draining from her face as she locked eyes with Jun, who looked equally pale. “That’s not possible,” he said, his voice barely a breath.

“Someone’s up there,” Clara whispered, the chill of realization wrapping around her like a shroud. The air felt heavy, as if the house itself was holding its breath. “We need to go, Jun. Now.”
