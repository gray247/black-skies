Clara’s heart pounded in rhythm with the thudding of the clock behind her, each strike echoing through the narrow corridor as she pressed her shoulder against the nursery door, the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket cold against her thigh. Jun stood beside her, his cracked brass lantern sputtering a feeble light that cut through the encroaching shadows. The air was thick with the dampness of the house, a heavy blanket that clung to the back of her throat, mingling with the faint scent of mildew. She could feel the weight of the silence, a tangible thing that wrapped around them, and for a moment, she wished they were anywhere but here.

“Are you sure we should be doing this?” Jun asked, his voice low, almost a whisper. The lantern flickered again, casting erratic shadows that danced across the walls, making them look alive, as if the house itself were breathing, waiting.

Clara nodded, though she felt the uncertainty gnawing at her resolve. “We have to see if the window is still sealed. If it is, then…” Her words trailed off, leaving a gap filled only by the ticking of the clock and the distant sound of water dripping—somewhere, there was a leak, she was sure of it, a reminder of the rain that had poured without pause.

With a deep breath, she stepped back, reaching for the key tied to the red ribbon around her wrist, the fabric now damp from the greenhouse rain. Her fingers trembled slightly as she fumbled with the lock. It was an old key, its metal cool and slightly tarnished, but it felt familiar, almost like a talisman against the growing dread that coiled in her stomach. When she inserted it into the lock, a jarring rattle echoed in the stillness, and she froze, glancing at Jun, who was watching her with a mixture of concern and fear.

“Did you hear that?” he asked, his eyes darting to the ceiling, as if expecting something to drop down from the shadows above. Clara shook her head, though the unease settled deeper. She could have sworn she’d heard a faint cough, like the sound of a child trying to clear their throat, but it had melted into the dark silence before she could fully grasp it.

“Just the house settling,” she replied, forcing a casualness into her tone that she didn’t feel. “Let’s just—”

Before she could finish, the chain on the service corridor door rattled again, more insistent this time, as if someone was testing it from the other side. Clara’s breath caught in her throat. “Jun… did you hear that?”

He nodded, eyes wide. “What if someone is trying to get in?”

“Or out.” Clara’s voice was barely a whisper, the implications curling like smoke in her mind. She thought of Nora, her forced cheer, the way she had flinched at the sight of the fox. “She said no child has been in the nursery for years, but what if…?”

The lantern flickered once more, and in that instant, the world outside felt far away, the storm beyond the walls a mere whisper compared to the cacophony of the house around them. Clara could feel her heart racing, the pulse of fear and longing entwined within her. “We need to move,” she urged, voice steadier than she felt.

With a nod, Jun stepped closer, his coat still soaked from the greenhouse, droplets pooling at his feet. The lantern’s light dimmed again, casting long shadows that seemed to stretch toward the door, as if they were alive and reaching for something just out of grasp. Clara steadied herself, the red ribbon brushing against her skin, a reminder that some bonds were not easily broken.
