The room appeared untouched, yet she could feel its history pressing against her, a weight she could not define. The window, painted shut, seemed to hold the pale sun at bay, smothering any light that dared to enter.

The lantern’s glow, though weak, caught the edge of something—a glimmer of porcelain nestled in the folds of her coat pocket. Clara reached for the ceramic fox, fingers brushing against its chipped surface. It felt warm against the coolness of her skin, as if it bore some remnant of the memories it had witnessed. “What do you think it meant?” she murmured, half to herself, half to Jun, who lingered by the door, his eyes darting over the room, wary.

“Maybe it’s a warning,” he replied, his brow furrowing. “Or a reminder of what we’ve lost.” His voice trembled slightly, and Clara could see the way his hands gripped the lantern, knuckles white against the brass.

“Or maybe it’s just a fox,” she said, attempting to lighten the atmosphere, but her laughter felt brittle, echoing hollowly in the stillness, swallowed by the room. The thought of lost children, of laughter that had faded into the walls, wrapped around her like a shroud, tightening with each breath.

As they stepped further into the room, the floor creaked beneath them, an unsettling groan that felt almost sentient, as if the house itself were protesting their presence. Clara's gaze flicked to the corner, where a chest had been moved, its polished wood glinting dully in the lantern’s light. She could have sworn it had been against the wall earlier, but now it sat defiantly, its lid closed, an invitation or a challenge.
