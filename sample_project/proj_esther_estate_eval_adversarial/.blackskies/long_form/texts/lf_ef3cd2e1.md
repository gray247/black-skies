The air hung thick with a damp chill as Clara and Jun crept down the servants' passage, shadows clinging to their heels like forgotten whispers. The weak flicker of the cracked brass lantern cast flickering patterns on the walls, illuminating the peeling paint and crumbling plaster. Clara could feel the cool metal of the lantern’s handle biting into her palm, a stark contrast to the warmth of the red ribbon tied firmly around her wrist. She glanced at it again, the key a silent promise of safety, yet it felt heavier with each cautious step they took.

Jun's coat, still sodden from the relentless rain in the greenhouse, clung to his frame and exuded a musty scent that mingled with the earthy aroma of wet soil. He glanced over at Clara, his eyes wide, shadows deepening under his brow. “Did you hear that?” he whispered, his voice barely a breath against the stillness. Clara felt the tension in the air thicken, a taut wire ready to snap. She shook her head, though a chill crawled down her spine at the memory of the child’s cough echoing from the upper floor—an unsettling sound that had seemed so distant yet now felt ominously close.

As they reached a bend in the corridor, a soft rattling echoed from behind the chained door, a sound that sent Clara’s heart racing. “It’s probably just the wind,” she replied too quickly, her voice sharper than intended, as if she could slice through the fear that wrapped around them. Jun didn’t respond, his eyes darting toward the door as the chain trembled again, a quiet challenge from the other side. Clara clenched her jaw, forcing herself to breathe steadily. She couldn’t let him see how the sound gnawed at her, how it echoed the unease pooling in her stomach.
