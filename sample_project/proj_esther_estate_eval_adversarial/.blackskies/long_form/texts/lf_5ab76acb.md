Jun shifted beside her, the damp fabric of his coat clinging uncomfortably to his skin as he leaned closer, the faint smell of rain and wet earth mingling with the scent of old wood and dust. Clara’s heart raced, each beat a desperate plea for clarity as she pressed her ear harder against the door, her breath catching in her throat. The cool metal of the chain felt like a warning, a boundary that separated her from whatever lay behind the thick wood. She felt the texture of the door—its ridges and knots digging into her palm like the claws of some hidden creature, waiting, watching.

“Clara,” Jun whispered, his voice low and edged with concern. “We can’t just stand here. What if—”

“What if what?” she interrupted, her voice sharper than she intended. “What if there’s someone in there? Or something?” The words hung in the air, heavy and unsettling. She turned to meet his gaze, the lantern’s dim light flickering across his face, illuminating the uncertainty pooling in his eyes.

He swallowed hard, glancing back at the door, as if he could somehow divine the secrets hidden just beyond it. “You heard the cough, didn’t you? Just before we found the—” He hesitated, the memory of the ceramic fox in her pocket lingering between them like a ghost. “Maybe we should just open it.”

“No,” Clara said, shaking her head fiercely. “Nora said no one’s been in the nursery for years. There’s no child—”

“Then what was that sound?” Jun pressed, his voice rising slightly, a mix of fear and frustration spilling over. The lantern sputtered again, the flame wavering like their resolve, and Clara felt a chill creep up her spine as the air thickened with tension.

The door rattled slightly against the chain, and they both froze, Clara’s heart drumming in her ears. “Did you feel that?” she whispered, barely able to keep the tremor out of her voice. The house creaked around them, the sound of wood stretching and settling, as if it were alive, part of a larger, unknowable design.

“I don’t like this, Clara,” he admitted, his breath ghosting against her cheek, warm and real. The shadows grew longer, twisting in the corners of her vision, and she glanced back at the hallway clock, its face watching them, counting down moments that felt like lifetimes. Thirteen sharp chimes echoed, a sinister reminder that time was not their ally.
