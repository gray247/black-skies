The chain rattled again, a persistent reminder of something restless on the other side, and she flinched, heart hammering in her chest. Jun shifted beside her, the weight of his soaked coat brushing against her arm, a reminder of the rain-soaked greenhouse and the secrets that had clung to the vines. The dying light of the lantern flickered, casting erratic shadows that danced across the faded wallpaper, each movement a fleeting echo of the stories that had once filled this room.

“Do you think…?” His voice trailed off, the unspoken words hanging heavy in the air. Clara turned to him, searching his expression for something solid in the chaos—the fear that gnawed at her insides, the grief that shadowed her memories. But all she found was the same uncertainty mirrored in his dark eyes, all too familiar yet alien in the flickering light.

“I don’t know,” she admitted, her voice a thin whisper, barely making it past the knot of worry lodged in her throat. The aroma of damp earth and the lingering sweetness of decay wrapped around her like a shroud, pulling her deeper into the weight of the moment. The nursery was sealed, yet it felt alive, as if it were holding its breath, waiting for her to decide whether to open the door to the past or keep it locked away.

The sound of the chain rattling again jolted her from her thoughts, and she reached for the red ribbon tied around her wrist, fingers brushing the smooth fabric. A jolt of connection surged through her, but with it came a creeping doubt—hadn’t she tied it tighter just moments ago? She glanced down at the key dangling from it, the brass glinting weakly in the lantern’s glow. The wrong key for the wrong lock, yet it felt more significant than she could grasp, a thread connecting her to something that had been lost.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice low, “maybe we should just go back.” His words cut through her like a chill wind, but the thought of retreat ignited something rebellious in her chest. She couldn’t shake the memory of the muddy prints that had led them here, or the way Nora’s laughter had felt like a thin veil over a storm brewing beneath.

“No. We need to know.” Her voice was steadier than she felt, a thin shield against the rising tide of uncertainty. She could almost hear the house’s heartbeat quicken in response, a low thrum reverberating through the floorboards, urging her forward.

As she grasped the key, she felt Jun’s hand on her arm, grounding yet tentative, as if he were holding her back from a precipice. “What if—”

“Just let me try.” She interrupted, her pulse thrumming in her ears as she inserted the key into the lock, the metal cold against her skin. The mechanism clicked, and for a moment, hope flickered, a fragile flame against the dark. But as she turned the key, an unsettling silence enveloped them, the kind that swallowed sound and replaced it with a heavy weight that pressed down on her chest.

“Clara…” Jun’s voice faltered, laden with an unspoken fear.

The door creaked open a sliver, revealing a slant of darkness that seemed to breathe, exhaling a musty exhalation reminiscent of old linens and forgotten whispers.
