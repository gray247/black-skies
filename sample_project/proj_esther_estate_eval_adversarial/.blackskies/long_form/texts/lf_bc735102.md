Clara pressed forward, the cold stone beneath her feet slick with water and age, a reminder of how easily the past could seep into the present. Each step felt heavy, as if the air itself conspired to hold her back. The flickering light from Jun’s lantern cast an unsteady glow, illuminating patches of wall mottled with damp moss and creeping shadows that seemed to stretch and curl, whispering secrets just out of earshot. She could hear the low drip, drip, drip echoing through the corridor, a relentless cadence that quickened her pulse.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice, barely above a whisper, broke the taut silence. The tremor in his words curled into the air between them, thick and tangible. Clara's heart raced, a sudden surge of adrenaline sharpening her senses. She had heard it—an indistinct rustle, like the whispered breath of someone sliding just out of sight. She shifted her weight, the parlor key on her wrist pulling downward, the ribbon biting into her skin with a sharp insistence. “It’s just the wind,” she replied too quickly, the edge of her voice sharper than she intended. She wanted to sound reassuring, but instead, she felt the words slip away, leaving her exposed.

Jun glanced back at her, his soaked coat clinging to him like a second skin, water pooling at his feet. “The wind doesn’t cough.” His eyes were wide, searching the darkness beyond the glow of the lantern, and Clara followed his gaze, her own dread blossoming with each flicker of the light. The sound of a child’s cough echoed in her mind, though she pushed it down, determined to focus on the here and now, on the way the shadows danced with an almost sentient life.
