Jun's breath fogged in the frigid air as he squeezed the cracked brass lantern, its flickering glow barely pushing back the shadows. The dim light cast ghostly shapes on the walls, the corners of the corridor seeming to stretch and curl like tendrils of smoke. Clara could feel the chill seep into her skin, a creeping sensation that made her shiver, but it wasn't just the cold that unsettled her. It was the weight of the house, heavy and watchful, as if it were cradling secrets in its very bones.

“Did you really hear something?” Jun's voice broke through the silence, taut with a tension that mirrored Clara's own. She hesitated, the memory of that cough still raw in her mind, a sound too real to dismiss yet too vague to pin down. A child's cough, perhaps, or the ghost of one—she could almost feel its echo resonating in the hollow spaces of the house.

“Maybe it was just…” she began, but the words faltered, crumbling into the air between them. Clara shook her head, the motion unwittingly tightening the red ribbon around her wrist, the parlor key swaying slightly. She had tied it there, hadn’t she? A small gesture of comfort that now felt like a noose.

The chain rattled against the door at the end of the corridor, a sound that sent a shiver racing down her spine. It was as if someone on the other side was testing it, pulling and pushing, their intentions cloaked in the shadows. “Jun,” she whispered, her voice barely more than a breath. “Did you hear that?”

He nodded, his face pale in the dim light, and for a moment, they stood frozen, the air thick with unspoken fears. The lantern flickered again, its sputtering flame casting a weak halo around them, but Clara felt as if the darkness was closing in, wrapping around her like a wet blanket. She glanced at Jun, and the worry etched in his brow twisted something inside her.

“Let’s just check the nursery,” she said, though the words felt like an incantation, a way to stave off the reality pressing in around them. She turned towards the door, the chipped ceramic fox nestled in her coat pocket pressing against her thigh, a small reassurance in this growing dread. The house seemed to hum, a low vibration that matched the pulse of her heart. It was alive, she thought, but whose life was coursing through its veins?

Behind them, the corridor seemed to breathe, the walls creaking softly as if they were aware of their every move. Clara reached for the door handle, her fingers brushing against the cold metal, and she hesitated, the weight of the world shifting on her shoulders. “Jun,” she said softly, “what if…”

“What if what?” he pressed, stepping closer, his breath a ghostly wisp in the air.

“What if it’s not just the house?” she murmured, the shadows pooling around them, dense and heavy. The flickering lantern cast a brief light upon the door’s surface, and she could see the faint outline of paint peeling away, as if it were shedding its history. It felt wrong, as if the door itself were a barrier not just to the nursery but to the truth of what lay beyond.
