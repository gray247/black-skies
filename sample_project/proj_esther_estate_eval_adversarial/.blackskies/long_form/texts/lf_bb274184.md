Each breath felt laced with foreboding, a reminder that they were far from the safety of the greenhouse.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice, a strained whisper, sliced through the silence. He stood close, his coat still soaked from the rain, droplets dripping like a metronome marking the tension between them. Clara felt her heart race, the thumping echoing in her ears louder than the murmur of the world outside, where the storm still raged. She wanted to reach out, to reassure him, but the knot in her stomach tightened; she could not even tell him that she hadn’t heard anything, that the silence felt heavy and oppressive, as if holding secrets that were meant to stay buried.

“Just the wind,” she said too quickly, the sharpness of her tone slicing through the damp air. “It’s nothing.” But even as she spoke, a chill ran down her spine, and she couldn’t shake the feeling that they were not alone.
