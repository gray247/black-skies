The lantern flickered in Jun's trembling hands, shadows dancing along the damp stone walls of the corridor as they pressed forward. A low, persistent drip echoed in the stillness, each drop a metronome counting down the moments until something broke. Clara felt the weight of the parlor key tugging at her wrist, the red ribbon digging into her skin, a reminder of her recent intrusion into the greenhouse, of the way the vines had coiled around her fingers like the tendrils of some unseen menace.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled, a whisper that seemed to vibrate against the stone. His coat was still soaked from their escape, the fabric clinging to his skin, and the chill it carried seeped into Clara’s bones. She fought the urge to shiver as the dampness wrapped around her like a shroud.

“No,” she lied, though the faint echo of a cough had clawed at the edges of her awareness. It felt wrong to admit she couldn’t shake the feeling that they were being watched, that eyes lurked in the shadows just beyond the reach of the lantern’s dim glow. She glanced back, half-expecting the chained door to rattle open, but it remained steadfastly closed, the chain taut as if someone were testing it from the other side.

“Clara,” he whispered again, his voice barely above the sound of the dripping water. “We should go back.”

“No,” she replied sharply, her pulse quickening. The word felt too loud, too defiant in the suffocating silence. She turned her gaze down the corridor, where muddy footprints marred the stone floor, leading away from them instead of toward. “We can’t go back. Not now.”
