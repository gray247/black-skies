Clara pressed her back against the cool wall of the corridor, the red ribbon taut against her wrist. The key dangled just above her pulse, a small, heavy promise of possibility. Jun’s breath hitched beside her, the sound barely cutting through the oppressive silence that hung around them, thick like the humid air of the greenhouse. He shifted the cracked brass lantern, its flickering flame casting erratic shapes that danced against the once-white plaster. Shadows morphed and twisted, suggesting the presence of something just beyond comprehension, waiting to emerge.

“Clara,” he whispered, his voice trembling like the dying light. “What if we’re not alone?” His wide eyes reflected the lantern's glow, searching hers for an anchor amidst the swell of uncertainty. She felt the weight of his gaze, an unspoken question lingering like the dampness clinging to his coat.

“I don’t know,” she admitted, the words slipping from her lips as if they were heavy stones. Her heart raced, propelled by a mix of fear and curiosity that coiled tightly in her chest. The air, thick and stifling, tugged at her lungs. “But we have to keep going.”

The chain rattled again, a sharp sound that pierced through the stillness. Clara jumped, instinctively clutching the ceramic fox nestled in her pocket, its chipped surface cool against her fingers. The muffled clinks echoed through the corridor, a mocking reminder that they were not just navigating the physical space of the house, but also the tangled history it housed.

Jun took a step forward, the mud on his boots squelching softly against the floor. “What if it’s someone… or something… trying to get in?” His voice dropped lower, almost a whisper, as if the very act of speaking it might summon whatever lurked beyond the chained door. Clara could almost feel the pulse of the house beneath her feet, a heartbeat that seemed to quicken in response to their fear.

“I heard it too,” she said, her voice steadier than she felt. “But it’s just the house settling.” Her eyes darted toward the far end of the corridor, where shadows clung like secrets. “Isn’t it?”

The lantern sputtered again, the light waning further, its flicker casting doubt in the corners of her mind. Clara tightened her grip on the ribbon, a feeble attempt to ground herself in reality. “We need to check the nursery,” she urged, forcing her legs to move, each step heavier than the last, as if the floor itself were trying to hold her back.

Jun hesitated, his brow furrowed, but his resolve solidified as he fell into step beside her. “If we find something…” He trailed off, uncertainty painting his features. “What do we do then?”

“I don’t know,” she said, the truth of it spilling out before she could catch it. A bitter taste lingered in her mouth, the weight of their mission pressing down on her like the humidity outside. “But we can’t let it stop us.”

They reached the sealed nursery door, the faded paint peeling like the memories trapped within. Clara’s heart raced as she pulled the lantern closer, illuminating the cracks and imperfections of a barrier that felt more like a tomb than a room. “This is it,” she breathed, the words barely escaping her lips.

Jun stood beside her, studying the door with a mix of trepidation and resolve. She could feel the tension radiating from him, the way he held his breath, waiting for something—anything—to happen.
