Clara pressed her palm against the nursery door, feeling the cool, rough texture of the wood beneath her fingers. The lantern flickered, its flame sputtering like a timid heartbeat, casting an erratic glow that danced nervously along the walls. Shadows leapt and twisted, as if eager to engulf her and Jun in their depth. She stole a glance at him; his body was tense, shoulders hunched, the cracked brass lantern barely illuminating the apprehension etched across his face. “Are you sure we should?” he murmured, his voice nearly lost in the heavy silence that hung between them.

She felt the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket, its chipped surface cool against her thigh, a tangible reminder of memories she had tried to bury. “We have to know,” she replied, her voice steadier than the quiver in her stomach suggested. A suffocating tension cloaked the air, thick with the ghosts of unspoken fears. The echo of a child's cough lingered in her mind, haunting and insistent, but she kept it to herself, unwilling to reveal how the thought unsettled her—how it made the house feel like a living thing, watching them with a breath held tight.

Jun shifted his weight, the damp fabric of his coat releasing a faint scent of wet earth that mingled with the stale air. “If there's someone in there…” he trailed off, the weight of his unfinished thought hanging heavily between them. Clara nodded, her heart racing, caught in a tug-of-war between curiosity and dread. The chain on the service corridor door rattled again, the sound sending a shiver racing along her spine, as if testing their resolve.

“Let’s just check,” Clara insisted, her fingers tightening around the key tied to the red ribbon on her wrist—a comforting anchor amidst the swirling uncertainty. She slipped the key into the lock, her hands trembling as she turned it, the click echoing with an unsettling finality. The door creaked open, revealing the nursery, its stale air thick with dust and a faint, sickly-sweet scent of decay.

Inside, the twilight hung heavy, the dim glow from the lantern illuminating the nursery furniture—a ghostly assembly draped in dust. The rocking chair stood silent, its paint faded and peeling, while the crib loomed like a forgotten relic, a promise that had long since withered. “No one has slept here for years,” Nora’s voice echoed in her mind, but the reality of the moment drowned out the old woman's reassurances.

Jun stepped in behind her, the light from his lantern casting jittery shadows that flitted across the walls. “What’s that?” he asked, pointing to a chest that had been moved, its surface pristine, as if it had been carefully placed rather than shifted through time. Clara’s heart raced; it shouldn’t have been disturbed, not without effort, not without a reason.

“Maybe it’s just the wind,” she offered, but her voice faltered, uncertainty creeping in. The house settled around them, creaking wood and shifting plaster whispering secrets long kept. The weight of its history pressed down, stories trapped within the walls, and her mind wandered back to the muddy prints they had seen—where they began and where they led.

A soft sound broke through her reverie—a faint cough, rising from the shadows, unmistakably a child’s cough, chilling her to the bone. Clara’s heart raced. “Did you hear that?” she whispered, her voice trembling, woven with threads of fear and disbelief. She glanced at Jun, whose face had drained of color, eyes wide as the lantern’s flicker caught the panic reflected in them. The nursery felt suddenly alive, the air thick with unseen presence, as they stood on the brink of something unknown.
