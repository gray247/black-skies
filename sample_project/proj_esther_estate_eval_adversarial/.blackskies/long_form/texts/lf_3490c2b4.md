The weak light from Jun’s cracked brass lantern flickered, casting uncertain shadows that danced along the damp walls of the corridor. Clara squinted into the murky darkness, her heart racing at the sound of the chain rattling against the door, as if something—or someone—on the other side was testing its hold. The air was thick with the lingering scent of wet earth, an unsettling reminder of the storm that had swept through the greenhouse, leaving everything soaked and heavy with moisture. Jun's coat clung to him like a second skin, the fabric darkened by rain, and she caught the tremor in his voice as he whispered, “Did you hear that?”

Clara felt the pulse of fear in her own throat, but she brushed the question off with a sharpness that surprised her. “It’s just the wind,” she insisted, but even as she spoke, her fingers tightened around the parlor key, its red ribbon cutting into her wrist. She couldn’t remember tying it tighter, and the thought sent a chill down her spine, mingling with the damp air that wrapped around them like a shroud.

They moved deeper into the corridor, the muddy footprints ahead stark against the stone floor, and Clara's breath quickened. “Someone’s been here,” she said, her voice barely a whisper, still too loud in the suffocating silence. Jun’s eyes widened, and his grip on the lantern faltered for a moment, the light sputtering before casting a feeble glow once more.

“Clara,” he said, his voice trembling, “we should go back.”

The idea twisted something inside her. The thought of retreat felt like a betrayal, like abandoning the very mystery they had stumbled upon. “We can’t,” she replied, her tone sharper than intended. “We need to know what’s happening. We can’t just—”
