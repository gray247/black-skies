The wood felt alive beneath her fingers, a creature breathing softly, the knots and splinters familiar yet foreign, a history she could almost touch. “What if someone’s in there?” she murmured again, her voice barely rising above the whisper of her own trepidation. She could almost convince herself that the house had been waiting for this moment, the moment when she would press her ear to the door, seeking answers in the silence.

Jun shifted beside her, the damp fabric of his coat rustling, a reminder of the downpour they’d slipped through. The cracked brass lantern he held flickered again, its feeble light casting erratic shadows that danced along the corridor walls, giving life to the dark corners. “We should—” he started, then hesitated. The words lingered in the air, heavy with the weight of unspoken fears. Clara could see the hesitation in his eyes, the uncertainty that mirrored her own.

“Should we try the door?” she asked, the question tasting bitter on her tongue, as if inviting trouble. The dim glow illuminated the contours of his face, shadowing the worry etched into his brow. She could feel the pulse of shared anxiety between them, a thread that had tightened since they’d left the greenhouse, the murky prints trailing behind them like a question mark left unanswered.

The lantern sputtered, the light dimming further, and Clara’s grip on the door tightened. “What if it’s just a trick of the house?” she whispered, though the thought felt flimsy, like the ribbon of the key that still clung to her wrist—a tether to something she couldn’t quite grasp. “Nora said no one has been in the nursery for years.” But even as she spoke, the memory of Nora’s flinched expression when she caught sight of the ceramic fox in Clara’s pocket nagged at her, gnawing at her certainty.

“No,” Clara cut him off, the word sharper than she intended. The thought of retreating felt like surrendering to the shadows that crept through the corners of her mind.
