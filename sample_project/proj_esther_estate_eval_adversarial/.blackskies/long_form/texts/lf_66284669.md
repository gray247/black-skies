Clara stood before the nursery door, a heavy silence curling around her like the dampness in the air. The lantern sputtered in Jun’s hand, its feeble light casting jittery shadows that danced across the peeling wallpaper. It felt like a living thing, the way it flickered, as if it were trying to warn them away from what lay beyond. She could almost hear the house exhale, a low creak that echoed through the stillness, mingling with the residual scent of wet earth and the faintest hint of lavender left behind by the greenhouse rain.

“Should we?” Jun’s voice, though soft, held a thread of urgency. His coat clung to him, heavy and sodden, as he shifted his weight from one foot to the other, the fabric rustling like the leaves outside. Clara glanced at him, at the way his brow furrowed in uncertainty. She wanted to reach out, to tell him it would be okay, but the words caught in her throat like the dust that settled in corners of the nursery they had been told to forget.

The key, tied tightly to her wrist with that stubborn red ribbon, felt suddenly burdensome, a reminder of choices they hadn’t made yet, of paths they hadn’t walked. She ran her thumb over the smooth metal, feeling the coolness, the assurance of it, but doubt gnawed at her like a hungry thing. “Nora said no one’s been in there for years,” she replied, her voice barely above a whisper, as if saying the words too loudly would wake something from its slumber.

“Doesn’t mean we have to believe her,” Jun shot back, the sharpness in his tone surprising her. He shifted again, casting a glance over his shoulder as if expecting to find something lurking behind them in the corridor, the shadows seeming to stretch and twist closer with each passing second. “Something’s wrong, Clara. The muddy prints—”

“—could belong to anyone,” she interjected, the urgency of her own thoughts spilling out. “Or maybe they’re just old. You said it yourself, the rain could have washed them down from the roof.”

“I heard a cough,” he said, his voice dropping to a gravelly hush, as if sharing a secret that had grown too heavy to keep. “It didn’t sound like a memory.”

The words hung in the air, charged and electric. Clara’s pulse quickened. She had chosen not to hear it, to push the sound to the back of her mind, a figment of her imagination. But now, the weight of his admission settled over her, heavy and insistent, like the thick fog that clung to the ground outside. “We need to go back. We shouldn’t be here.”

But as she spoke, her hand instinctively reached for the doorknob. It felt warm beneath her fingers, a strange contrast to the chill that draped the hallway. The lantern flickered again, light waning as if it, too, sensed the gravity of the moment. The chain on the corridor door rattled faintly, a sinister whisper from the other side, and both Clara and Jun froze, their breaths caught in unison.

“Did you hear that?” Clara’s heart raced, the swell of fear rising like bile in her throat. She felt the tremor in her hands, the way they shook slightly as she tightened her grip on the knob.
