The lantern sputtered again, its flickering light barely cutting through the oppressive shadows. Clara felt the weight of the darkness pressing against her, as if the walls themselves were leaning in, eager to swallow them whole. She pushed onward, the cool stone floor slick beneath her feet, while the faint scent of damp earth mingled with something metallic that curled around her senses like a warning. Her fingers tightened around the cracked brass handle, each wobble of the flame sending a chill racing up her spine.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled in the hush of the corridor, barely above a whisper. He was still behind her, the dampness of his coat radiating a cold that seemed to seep into the very marrow of her bones. She didn’t turn to look at him, afraid that if she did, the shadows would claim him too.

“Hear what?” she snapped, too sharp, the words echoing off the stone walls like a challenge. A child’s cough, he had said. But Clara couldn’t let herself dwell on that. Instead, she focused on the chain rattling against the door at the far end of the passage, each clink a reminder of what lay just out of reach. It felt as if something was testing its strength from the other side, a presence that made her skin crawl.

Jun sighed behind her, a soft sound of fear mixed with exhaustion. “Let’s just go back, Clara. We shouldn’t be here.”

“No,” she said, her voice firmer than she felt. She could feel the ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key dangling there like a promise and a curse. She had found it tangled in the wet vines of the greenhouse, an echo of something she couldn’t quite grasp.
