Clara’s heart thundered in her chest as they crept deeper into the corridor, the shadows swirling around them like anxious ghosts. The dim light from Jun’s lantern sputtered again, its flickering flame barely holding back the darkness that encroached on all sides. She pressed her back against the frigid stone wall, the chill seeping through her clothes, as if the very stones were imbued with the dampness of the storm. A low rumble of thunder echoed outside, punctuating the silence with a sense of foreboding that made her stomach twist.

“Did you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice barely rising above the sound of the storm, eyes darting past her into the blackness. The fear in his tone cut deeper than the cold, a raw edge that made her want to reach out and steady him. But Clara was too busy trying to quell her own rising anxiety, her fingers brushing against the smooth, chipped ceramic of the fox tucked in her coat pocket, a small comfort in this oppressive gloom.

“I didn’t hear anything,” she replied too quickly, her voice sharper than intended. She could feel the weight of his gaze, questioning, probing. “It’s just the storm.” She wished she could believe it herself. Yet, the sound of something—a cough?—still echoed in her mind, lingering like a ghostly whisper from somewhere above. She bit her lip, forcing her focus back on the here and now, on Jun’s pale, rain-soaked face illuminated by the flickering light.

The air thickened with tension as they moved further into the passage, each step squelching softly against the damp floor. The faint smell of mildew mixed with the earthy scent of the rain-soaked walls, each breath a reminder of the hidden dangers that lurked beyond the lantern’s glow.
