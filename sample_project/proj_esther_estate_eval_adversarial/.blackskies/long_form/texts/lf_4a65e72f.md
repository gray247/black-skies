Clara tightened her grip on the lantern as they stepped deeper into the servants’ passage, the dim light flickering like a heartbeat against the damp stone walls. The air was thick with the musty scent of mildew, mingling with the sharp tang of rust that clung to the metal fixtures. Each breath felt heavy, laden with a sense of foreboding that wrapped around her like the weight of a wet blanket. She could feel the red ribbon, the parlor key still tied snugly around her wrist, a faint pulse of warmth reminding her of the outside world, of the rain-soaked greenhouse where they had found it tangled in the vines.

Jun walked beside her, his coat dripping with the remnants of the storm, the fabric clinging to him like a second skin. The lantern sputtered again, casting long shadows that stretched and twisted across the corridor, momentarily illuminating the chipped tiles underfoot, their patterns obscured by mud. “Clara,” he said, his voice low and strained, “do you hear that?”

At first, she didn’t, her ears still ringing from the echoes of the greenhouse, the frantic rustling of leaves, the sound of the latch snapping open. But then, a faint cough echoed from above, a child’s cough that curled around her heart like a vine, squeezing tighter. She hesitated, her pulse quickening. “What? I don’t hear anything.” The words tumbled out too sharply, betraying the flutter of anxiety beneath her bravado.

Jun frowned, his brows knitting together as he glanced toward the far end of the passage where the door stood ominously chained from the inside. “It sounded like it came from the upper floor. We should—”
