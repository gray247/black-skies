Clara pressed her back against the nursery door, feeling the jagged edges of the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket digging into her thigh, a constant reminder of the weight of their predicament. The walls seemed to lean in closer, as if the house itself were eavesdropping on their whispered fears. Jun stood beside her, his cracked brass lantern sputtering with a weak battery, sending flickering beams of light that danced across the faded floral wallpaper, revealing the echoes of laughter and the hushed cries of children long gone. The air felt heavy, saturated by the scent of damp earth and a sharper tang of mildew that lingered in the corners like a memory refusing to be forgotten.

“What if she’s right?” Clara whispered, her voice trembling as it cut through the low hum of the wind rattling the windowpanes. “What if no child has been here?”

Jun turned to her, his damp hair clinging to his forehead, casting shadows over his furrowed brow. “But we heard something, Clara. A cough. It was real.” He lowered his voice, as if the walls themselves could hear him. He shifted slightly, the wet fabric of his coat brushing against her arm, a reminder of the storm that had driven them from the greenhouse, leaving muddy tracks behind them that now felt like a trail leading into the unknown.

“Maybe it was just…” Her words faltered, the truth curling in her throat like a coiled snake. “Maybe it was just the house settling.”

“Or someone else,” he replied, the lantern flickering again, the flame straining to stay alive. Silence enveloped them, thick and oppressive, and Clara could almost feel the house’s heartbeat, a slow, rhythmic pulse echoing the frantic thud of her own heart. She glanced at the door, the chains rattling softly, as if someone on the other side were testing their strength, a constant reminder that they were not alone.

“Do you think it’s locked?” Jun’s voice was barely a whisper, but the question hung heavily in the air. Clara shivered, tightening her grip on the key tied to her wrist with a red ribbon, its familiar touch grounding her in a reality that felt increasingly fragile. “What if we can’t get out?”

“We have to try.” Clara stepped closer, her fingers brushing against the cool brass of the doorknob, but as soon as she made contact, a chill swept through the air, forcing her to recoil. The floor creaked beneath them, as if the house were protesting their intrusion. “It’s just a door,” she said, forcing a steadiness into her voice, though the tremor betrayed her fear.

“This isn’t just any door.” Jun’s eyes flickered with urgency, the lantern casting wavering shadows across his face, illuminating the concern etched into his features. “It’s the nursery. Whoever or whatever is in there… it’s been waiting.”

The weight of his words settled heavily between them, suffocating and palpable. Clara felt it pressing against her chest, a heavy burden that made each breath feel labored. She turned to the dark hallway beyond, a void that seemed to swallow the light, breathing in shadows. “What if we’re not supposed to be here?”

“Then we’ll find out.” Jun’s determination radiated warmth in the cold, damp air. “You’re not afraid of the dark, are you?”

She met his gaze, the fire in his eyes pulling her back from the brink of panic. “I’m afraid of what the dark hides,” she admitted, her voice quaking with a mix of fear and resolve. “But we can’t leave this place without knowing.”
