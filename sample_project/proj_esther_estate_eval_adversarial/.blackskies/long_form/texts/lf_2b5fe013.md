The air in the service corridor was thick with the scent of damp wood and something sweeter, almost decayed, lingering at the edges of Clara’s senses. She pressed her shoulder against the cool stone wall, breathing in the musty remnants of old varnish, her heart racing to the rhythm of their footsteps. Jun’s lantern barely held its flickering light, casting long, wavering shadows that danced like specters across the narrow space. Each flicker sent a shiver down her spine, a reminder that they were not alone, even if the corridor felt abandoned.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely more than a whisper, his breath hitching as he strained to listen. His coat clung to his skin, soaked from the sudden downpour in the greenhouse, droplets still forming and trailing down the fabric like tears.

Clara shook her head, though unease coiled tightly in her stomach. “It’s just the wind,” she snapped, too quickly, too sharply, as if her words could slice through the tension that surrounded them. She could feel the weight of her own lie, the raw edge of fear gnawing at her resolve. The chain on the door behind them rattled again, an insistent reminder that something was testing its hold from the other side.

“Are you sure?” Jun pressed, his eyes wide and glistening in the lantern’s dim light. “It sounded like—”

“Stop it,” she interjected, her voice sharper than intended, echoing off the stone walls in the stillness. She lifted her wrist, the red ribbon binding the parlor key glinting like a warning in the lantern’s glow. It felt tighter than before, though she couldn’t remember tying it. The image of the key, tangled in the wet vines, flashed in her mind; how had it come to be there?
