The flickering glow cast a wavering light on the damp stone walls, illuminating patches of moss that thrived in the shadows, their earthy scent mingling with the stale air. She glanced at Jun, his coat still soaked from their hurried escape through the greenhouse, droplets clinging to the edges like tiny tears. His eyes darted nervously, catching every flicker of movement in the dark corners of the corridor.

“Clara, we shouldn’t be here,” he whispered, his voice a low tremor that threatened to shatter the fragile tension that hung between them. She could hear the tremors in his breath, the way they hitched at the end of each word, and it twisted something deep inside her—something that dared her to defy the fear creeping into her own throat.

“They can’t know we found it. Not yet,” she replied too quickly, the sharpness of her tone surprising even herself. She felt the ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key resting against her skin, an anchor to a place that felt both familiar and foreign. The dim light danced across her face, and she could almost convince herself that Jun's worry was an overreaction, a product of their wild imaginations.
