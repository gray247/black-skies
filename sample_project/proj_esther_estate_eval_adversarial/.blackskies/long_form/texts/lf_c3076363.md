Clara’s breath quickened as they stepped deeper into the corridor, the pungent scent of mildew wrapping around her like a shroud. The weak beam from the lantern cast a trembling light, revealing the muddy prints underfoot that seemed to lead from the walls themselves, as if the house were exhaling phantoms of its past. The air felt heavy, pregnant with unspoken words, and Clara’s fingers instinctively tightened around the parlor key, the red ribbon brushing against her skin like a warning.

“Where do you think they lead?” Jun’s voice quavered, breaking the oppressive silence. His coat, still soaked from the greenhouse rain, clung to him like a second skin, and Clara could see the shiver that ran through him, whether from cold or fear, she couldn’t tell.

“I don’t know,” she replied too sharply, her voice echoing against the damp walls, a sound that felt foreign and unwelcome. She forced herself to step forward, the lantern’s light flickering ominously as if it too sensed the weight of their uncertainty. Mud squelched beneath her boots, the sound eerily loud in the stillness, and she glanced back at Jun, his face pale and drawn, eyes wide with something that bordered on terror.

“Clara,” he whispered, almost as if saying her name would tether them to some semblance of safety. “What if—”

“Don’t,” she cut him off, the urgency of the moment swelling in her chest. “We need to keep moving.” The chain on the service corridor door rattled behind them, a sound that sent a chill spiraling down her spine. Someone was testing it from the other side, a quiet insistence that felt like a knotted threat. Clara’s heart raced, thrumming against her ribs, echoing the panic that bubbled just beneath her skin.
