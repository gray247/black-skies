Clara’s breath fogged the air, mingling with the dampness that clung to the walls of the narrow corridor. The faint hum of the cracked brass lantern, carried by Jun with a grip that betrayed his unease, cast jittery shadows that danced along the peeling wallpaper. Each flicker seemed to fill the silence with a restless energy, punctuated by the sound of the rain outside, relentless against the greenhouse’s glass.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice cracked, a whisper threading through the low light. He shifted, his soaked coat clinging to him like a second skin, the fabric heavy with moisture, the scent of earth and greenery still clinging to it.

Clara pressed her lips together, instinctively reaching for the red ribbon tied around her wrist, her fingers brushing against the cool metal of the key. It felt heavier than it should, and she couldn’t shake the strange sensation that it was somehow both a lifeline and a burden. “It’s just the wind,” she replied too quickly, her voice sharper than she intended, echoing against the corridor's damp walls. “We need to keep moving.”

The chain rattled on the door ahead, a hollow sound that resonated deep within her chest. She hesitated, her heart quickening as she tried to peer through the gloom, but the lantern’s flickering light barely pierced the darkness. “It’s locked,” she murmured, more to herself than to Jun, whose wide eyes spoke of the fear spiraling in his mind. Muddy footprints marred the corridor floor, leading them deeper into the shadows, the prints fresh and indistinct, as if someone—or something—was just ahead.

“Clara…” Jun’s voice trembled, and she could see his fingers tightening around the lantern’s handle. “What if—”
