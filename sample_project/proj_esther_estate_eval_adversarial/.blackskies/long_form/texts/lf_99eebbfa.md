She tightened the ribbon around her wrist, the parlor key jabbing into her skin, a reminder of their reckless search for answers buried beneath the surface of the house.

“Yeah, but…” He hesitated, his gaze drifting to the floor where the muddy prints had appeared, leading away from them, deeper into the shadows of the house. “We heard something earlier. What if—”

Things settle. They creak.”

“But the prints—” he started again, but she could see the weight of her words settling over him like a thick fog. He swallowed, the muscles in his jaw tightening, and Clara could almost feel the tension radiating off him, a coil ready to snap.

“Maybe we should just knock,” she said, forcing a lightness into her tone, almost as if she could will the dread away. But her heart thudded painfully in her chest, the thought of what lay beyond the door sending a shiver down her spine. She could almost see the dust motes swirling in the air, illuminated by the weak lantern light, swirling like lost memories.

Jun reached out, his fingers brushing against the door handle, pausing as if he could sense the pulse of the nursery beyond. “If there’s someone in there…” his voice trailed off, but the implication hung in the air, thick and suffocating.

“Then we’ll figure it out,” Clara said, more to herself than to him, the words ringing hollow in her ears. She could feel the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket, its chipped surface cool against her thigh, a reminder of the childhood that had slipped away like sand through her fingers. “We have to know what’s going on.”

With a deep breath, she stepped back, allowing Jun to take the lead. The lantern dimmed further, casting an eerie glow over the door, and she felt a chill creep up her spine as she noticed the chain rattling again, the sound echoing from above as if someone were testing it from the other side. Her stomach twisted, the knot of dread tightening.

“Clara?” Jun’s voice was low, edged with uncertainty. “What do we do now?”
