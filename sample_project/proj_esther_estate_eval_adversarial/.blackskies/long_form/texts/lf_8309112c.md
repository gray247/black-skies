Clara pressed her ear against the nursery door, straining to hear beneath the echo of her own heartbeat. The wood felt cold and unyielding, a barrier that held its secrets tight. She could still feel the rough grain beneath her palm, but now it seemed to pulse with a different rhythm, a heartbeat that thudded in sync with her anxiety. The lantern flickered again, casting a tremulous light that danced across the doorframe, illuminating the chipped paint and the heavy shadows that clung to the corners like whispered fears.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, a thread of tension woven through the air. His breath fogged the space between them, mingling with the damp chill that seeped through the walls. Clara’s pulse quickened, and she shook her head, feeling the weight of the ceramic fox in her coat pocket press against her side like a warning. The last thing she wanted was to admit that she felt it too—the undeniable presence lurking behind the door, watching, waiting.

“Maybe we should just go,” she said, her voice trembling despite her efforts to sound resolute. The words felt heavy, like she was dragging them up from the depths of a well. “There’s nothing in there, right?”

Jun shifted beside her, his coat still soaked from the rain that had fallen in the greenhouse, beads of water glistening like stars on the fabric. He hesitated, glancing at the door as if it might crack open at any moment to reveal the secrets it held. “Nora said no one’s been in there for years…” His voice trailed off, a thin veil of doubt hanging between them.

“Exactly,” Clara said, trying to summon the steadiness she once had. The room felt charged, the air thick with an unspoken dread. She could almost feel the history of the place pressing in around her, the weight of all the memories trapped behind the nursery door. “There’s no reason to think—”

A faint sound echoed from within the nursery, a soft cough that curled through the air like smoke. Clara’s breath caught in her throat, an ice-cold grip tightening around her chest. She glanced at Jun, who stood frozen, his eyes wide and searching. “What was that?” he breathed, the color draining from his face.

“I—” Clara started, but the words faltered. It couldn’t be. It had to be a trick of the mind, a remnant of the stories they’d heard about the house, tales spun from fear and imagination. Yet the sound lingered, a spectral echo that sent a shiver racing down her spine. “There’s no child here,” she insisted, though the certainty in her voice began to waver.

Jun took a step back, as if he could physically distance himself from the door. “But there’s something—someone—inside,” he said, his voice barely a whisper. The lantern sputtered again, the light dimming further, and for a moment, they were enveloped in darkness, a thick cocoon of uncertainty.

Clara could feel the weight of the key on her wrist, the red ribbon tangling with her skin, a tether to something she couldn’t quite grasp. “We need to go,” she said, her voice stronger now, forged from a desperate need to escape the suffocating atmosphere that threatened to pull them under. “If we stay here—”

But before she could finish, the sound of metal against wood echoed in the corridor, a rattle that made her heart race. Clara turned abruptly, staring at the chained door leading back to the service passage. The chain shifted, as if someone were testing it from the other side. Panic surged through her, a tide of dread that threatened to drown her resolve.
