Clara pressed her back against the door, the cool wood biting into her skin, and inhaled sharply as the muffled clatter of the chain echoed above her like a warning bell. The lantern in Jun’s hand flickered, its weak glow casting elongated shadows that danced across the narrow corridor. She could feel the dampness of his coat seeping through the fabric of her sleeve, the scent of wet earth mingling with the faint, musty odor of the house, an unsettling reminder of the rain-soaked greenhouse they had just escaped.

"Did you hear that?" Jun's voice was barely above a whisper, his eyes darting toward the staircase that spiraled up into darkness. Clara frowned, the unease curling in her gut. She shook her head, but the conviction faltered under the weight of her own doubt. The last time she had heard something, she had dismissed it, and now the memory felt like a specter haunting her thoughts.

“I didn’t hear anything,” she said, her voice steadier than she felt. “Maybe it was just the house settling.”

“Settling,” he echoed, but his skepticism lingered in the air like the smell of damp wood. He squeezed the lantern’s handle, and for a heartbeat, the light brightened, illuminating the path ahead, revealing the muddy prints that led deeper into the shadows. They were unmistakably fresh, the earth still clinging to the grooves of the floorboards, as if someone or something had hurried past.

“Clara,” Jun said, a note of urgency creeping into his tone. “These prints—they didn’t come from us. They’re leading toward the nursery.”

Her breath hitched as she instinctively reached for the ceramic fox tucked away in her pocket, its chipped ear a reminder of the nursery that felt like a trap, a place where echoes of laughter had long since been buried beneath layers of dust and silence. “But no one’s been in there for years, right? Not since—”

“Not since the last child left,” he finished, his gaze piercing her, searching for the certainty she couldn’t muster. “But what if someone has come back?”

The idea churned in her mind, swirling with the weight of every whispered rumor that had passed through the house over the years, tales of things that slithered through the dark, of children who never truly left. “We can’t just stand here,” she said, forcing her voice to remain firm, though the tremor in her heart betrayed her. “We should check it out. Together.”

Jun hesitated, glancing back at the rattling chain that seemed to mock them from above. “What if—”

“Jun,” she interrupted, the urgency in her tone shattering the fragile thread of doubt. “If we don’t go, we’ll never know. If there’s really someone in the nursery… we can’t leave it like this.”

His jaw tightened, and for a moment, she could see the conflict in his eyes, the battle between his instinct for caution and the reckless pull of loyalty. Finally, he nodded, resolve hardening his features. “Okay. But we stay close together.”

They moved cautiously, the lantern’s flicker a meager shield against encroaching shadows as they approached the nursery door. Clara fumbled with the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon slick against her skin. The fabric felt oddly comforting despite the chill of the air, a tether to her resolve. She could almost hear the clock striking, the deep toll echoing through her mind, counting down the moments until they would either find nothing or confront whatever lingered beyond that door.
