Clara pressed her ear against the nursery door, the wood cool and smooth against her cheek, as if it were a living thing harboring whispered secrets. Thunder rumbled in the distance, a low growl that sent a shiver racing down her spine. She half-expected the door to crack open, to spill forth whatever lay behind it, but instead, silence pooled in the air around them, thick and suffocating.

Jun shifted again, his damp coat clinging uncomfortably to him, the fabric reeking of earth and rain. “What if there’s someone in there?” His voice was low, barely above a whisper, yet it carried the weight of his unease. Clara could see the muscles in his jaw tighten, the faint tremor in his hand as he gripped the lantern, its flickering flame casting erratic shadows that danced along the walls, momentarily illuminating their anxious faces.

“I don’t know,” she replied, her voice barely escaping her throat. With a steadying breath, she pulled her palm away from the door, the pulse of the house beneath her fingers momentarily receding. The air felt charged with unspoken truths, and she could almost hear the soft echo of a child’s cough again, distant yet urgent. It was maddening, the way the sound clung to her thoughts, a cruel reminder of something lost.

“Do you think it’s…?” Jun hesitated, his gaze darting toward the window, where rain lashed against the glass like frantic fingers. “Do you think it’s a child?”

Clara shook her head, the ribbon around her wrist brushing against her skin like a ghostly touch. “Nora said no child has been here in years.” But as she spoke, she could feel the weight of the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket, the smooth surface cool against her palm, a talisman of sorts, a remnant of a past she struggled to understand.

“Yet here we are,” Jun murmured, his eyes narrowing as he studied the door. “What if someone moved the chest up here? It was down on the landing last time I checked.” He angled the lantern, its light catching the faint outline of the doorframe as if seeking a way in. “The chain sounds like it’s shifting again.”

Clara’s heart raced. “It can’t be two floors down. I heard it sliding above us.” The uncertainty twisted in her gut, nudging at the edges of her resolve. She glanced back at the corridor, where shadows seemed to pool, where the chain rattled in a rhythm that felt almost deliberate.

“Maybe we should just go,” Jun suggested, his voice tight. “Get Nora. She’ll know what to do.”

“Or she’ll just tell us we’re imagining things,” Clara replied, frustration seeping into her tone. “We need to figure this out ourselves.” The stubbornness in her voice surprised her, but something deep inside urged her forward, a flicker of defiance against the weight of the house, the weight of their shared apprehension.

She reached for the key tied to her wrist, the smooth metal cool beneath her fingers, and hesitated, the memory of fumbling with the wrong lock flooding her mind. The clock had chimed thirteen, and every echo of that tolling sounded like a warning. “If we can just open this door…”

"Clara," Jun interrupted, his eyes wide, a mixture of fear and determination. "What if it’s not just a door? What if it’s a trap?"

“What else can we do?” she shot back, her voice sharper than intended. “There’s something in there, Jun. I can feel it.”
