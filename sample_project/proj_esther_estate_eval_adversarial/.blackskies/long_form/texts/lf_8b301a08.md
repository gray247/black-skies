The dim corridor swallowed the flickering light from Jun's cracked brass lantern, casting elongated shadows that danced against the peeling wallpaper. Clara's heart raced, the rhythmic thump echoing in her ears as she pulled the lantern closer, its sputtering flame a fragile beacon in the encroaching darkness. The air was thick, damp with the lingering scent of rain-soaked earth, and something else, something stale that hung like a shroud.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled, barely above a whisper, his eyes wide as he scanned the corridor ahead. The lantern’s light wavered, revealing only the jagged edges of the wooden floorboards, each creak a reminder of the house’s age—its secrets rotting beneath layers of dust and neglect. Clara felt the weight of his fear pressing against her, a palpable force that threatened to unravel her own resolve.

“Don’t be ridiculous,” she snapped, too harshly, her voice cracking like the lantern’s flickering flame. She bit her lip, regretting the sharpness. “It’s just the wind.” The words felt hollow even as they left her mouth, a feeble attempt to mask the unease gnawing at her insides. She glanced down at the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the key dangling from it like a fragile promise. Why did it feel so loose? Hadn’t she tied it tight enough before leaving the greenhouse?
