Clara swallowed hard, the taste of damp air mingling with the acrid scent of wet wood. The flickering light from the cracked brass lantern cast long shadows that danced along the walls of the corridor, their movements unsettling, as if the very shadows were alive, mocking her unease. She tightened her grip on the lantern, the metal cool and slick against her palm, and hesitated, peering into the darkness ahead. “We have to see where it leads,” she said, more to convince herself than Jun.

Jun shifted again, the fabric of his soaked coat rustling softly, a sound swallowed by the oppressive stillness. “Does it ever end?” he whispered, his voice trembling like the light from the lantern. He glanced at the chain rattling at the door behind them, a sharp, metallic clink that sent a shiver down Clara's spine. It was as if someone were testing the limits of their fear, waiting for them to falter.

“Just a little further,” Clara replied too sharply, the words spilling out before she could catch them. She felt the ribbon around her wrist, the parlor key brushing against her skin, inexplicably tight as if it were binding not just the key, but her very breath. She couldn’t remember tying it tighter, but there it was, a constant reminder of her responsibility, her purpose. The muddy footprints ahead twisted and turned, leading deeper into the gloom, where the corridor seemed to narrow and close in around them.

“Look,” Jun pointed, his voice barely above a breath. Clara followed his gaze, heart racing, as the light revealed a smear of dark brown against the floorboards—a fresh print, unmistakably out of place.
