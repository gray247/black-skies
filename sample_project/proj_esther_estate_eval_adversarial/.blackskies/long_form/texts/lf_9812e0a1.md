The air in the narrow corridor was thick, a damp chill that clung to Clara's skin like a second layer. Jun moved ahead, his soaked coat whispering against the wood-paneled walls, each step a soft squelch as his boots sank into the old floorboards. The dim glow from the cracked brass lantern flickered, casting long shadows that seemed to stretch and shudder with every heartbeat. Clara tightened her grip on the lantern, feeling the weight of it along with the red ribbon tied snugly around her wrist, a constant reminder of the key they had unearthed from the greenhouse's tangled embrace.

“Do you hear that?” Jun’s voice, strained and low, broke through the oppressive silence. His eyes darted toward the far end of the corridor, where the shadows pooled thicker. Clara held her breath, straining to catch any sound, but all she felt was the thudding of her heart, echoing in her ears. "It's like... someone’s testing the chain. From the other side."

She shook her head, pushing aside the growing sense of dread. “It’s just the wind,” she replied too quickly, her voice sharper than she intended. “Or... something.” The words tasted bitter on her tongue, and she knew they were a feeble shield against the unease creeping in. The air felt alive, charged with an energy that sent shivers racing down her spine.

Jun took a cautious step forward, and Clara followed, the lantern’s light sputtering again, reducing their world to a flickering illusion of safety. Muddy prints marred the floor ahead of them—fresh, unmistakable against the worn wood—and her stomach twisted as she took them in. “Someone was here,” she murmured, her voice barely above a whisper, the words curling in the damp air.
