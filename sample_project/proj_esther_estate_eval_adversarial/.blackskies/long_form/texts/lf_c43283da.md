The lantern in Jun's hands flickered again, casting a weak, sputtering light that barely illuminated the shadows swirling around them. A shiver threaded through her, not just from the chill in the air that wrapped around her like a damp shroud, but from the inexplicable sense that something was waiting just beyond the threshold.

“Did you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice low, barely breaking the silence. His breath hung in the air, a fragile mist against the darkness. She shook her head, though the truth was she didn’t know what to listen for—only the echoes of the house settling, the distant groans of wood and plaster, the sound of their own heartbeats thrumming in the stillness.

A soft cough, muffled and distant, drifted through the air, making her heart stutter. It was a child's cough, light and fragile, but a moment later, it vanished as quickly as it had come, leaving an unsettling void. Jun’s gaze searched her face, uncertainty flickering in his dark eyes. “You didn’t hear it, did you?”

“No,” she replied, forcing a steadiness into her voice that she didn’t feel. “It’s just the house.” Her fingers tightened around the red ribbon key, the cool metal pressing into her wrist as if it could anchor her to the present, to reality. She could still feel the weight of the ceramic fox in her coat pocket, a relic of something simpler, and she could almost hear Nora’s voice, insisting no child had slept in the nursery for years. Yet every instinct screamed that something was wrong, that the house was holding its breath, waiting for them to make a move.

Jun shifted, the lantern sputtering once more, shadows shifting like whispers around them. “Clara, we shouldn’t be here. If there’s someone in the house—”

“There’s no one here,” she interrupted, though she could feel the tremor in her own voice. “Nora said—”

“Nora flinched when she saw that fox.” His words hung between them, heavy with unspoken fears. “What if—”

“Stop it.” Her voice was sharper than she intended, a flash of irritation igniting beneath her anxiety. But she couldn’t allow her mind to spiral into those dark corners where doubt lingered, whispering of intruders and ghosts. “We just need to open this door.”

With a deep inhale, Clara stepped back from the door and glanced at the lantern, which had dimmed to a mere flicker, barely more than a whisper of light. She felt the weight of the key on her wrist, the ribbon a tether to a forgotten world. Perhaps it was foolish to think they could unlock the past, but the nursery held remnants of laughter and dreams; a promise of safety buried under layers of dust and time.

“Here,” she said, moving forward to take the lantern from Jun. The moment their hands brushed, a spark of warmth flickered against the coldness surrounding them. She cradled the lantern, adjusting the angle to catch the faintest rays of light against the door. “If we can just see what’s inside…”
