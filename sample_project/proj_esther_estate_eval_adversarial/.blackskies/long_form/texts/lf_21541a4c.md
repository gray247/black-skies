She didn’t remember tying it so tightly, and the thought sent a shiver through her.

A sound echoed from above—a low, rhythmic creaking that resonated in her bones, something not entirely foreign but tinged with a disquieting strangeness. He nodded, eyes wide, and the lantern flickered again, its glow sputtering as if responding to the tension in the air. “It sounded like—”

“Like a cough,” she interjected, her voice sharper than she intended, a reminder of the child’s sound that had slipped through her mind like a wisp of smoke. It was there, and yet it wasn’t, a ghost of a memory that she couldn’t quite grasp. The silence that followed felt thick, heavy with unspoken thoughts, and Jun shifted beside her, his gaze flickering to the door as if it might come alive at any moment.

“What if it’s not just the house?” he said, his voice low, his breath hitching. “What if something… someone is in there?”

Clara bit her lip, glancing at the door again, her fingers tightening around the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket, its smoothness a stark contrast to the rough wood beneath her palm. She could almost feel the pulse of the house as it breathed around them, a living thing with a heartbeat that echoed in her ears, drumming a rhythm of secrets and shadows.

“Then we need to open it,” she said, her resolve hardening, the flicker of determination igniting within her. “We need to know.”

Jun hesitated, his gaze drifting to the lantern, the dim light barely illuminating the contours of the door. “What if it’s something we can’t handle?”

Clara met his eyes, searching for the courage that had begun to wane between them, the air thick with their shared fears. “We can handle it,” she insisted, though her heart fluttered at the thought of the darkness beyond. “We have to try.”
