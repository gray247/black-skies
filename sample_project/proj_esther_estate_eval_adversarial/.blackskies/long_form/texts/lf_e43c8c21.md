Clara pressed her back against the damp stone wall of the corridor, her breath quickening as she glanced at Jun, whose face was pale under the flickering light of the cracked brass lantern. The sputtering glow illuminated the muddy prints trailing down the narrow passage ahead, stark against the weathered floorboards. Each footprint seemed to lead deeper into the dark, an invitation to follow, or perhaps a warning to retreat.

“Do you see that?” Jun whispered, his voice a tremor that echoed in the silence. His soaked coat clung to him, heavy and cold, the fabric darkened by the remnants of the greenhouse rain. Clara nodded, though her heart raced more from the weight of unspoken fears than from anything tangible.

The lantern flickered again, casting shadows that danced along the walls, shifting and morphing like the specters of her own anxiety. She could feel the red ribbon around her wrist, its texture a sharp contrast against her clammy skin. The key hung there, a small weight that felt both reassuring and ominous. Had she tied it tighter? She couldn’t remember, but it seemed to constrict with each pulse of her heart, tightening with her rising dread.

“Clara,” Jun urged, his gaze darting toward the chained door at the end of the corridor, its presence looming like a sentry, guard against whatever lay beyond. “What if someone’s on the other side? What if they—”
