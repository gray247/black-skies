The lantern flickered in Jun's grasp, the weak beam casting long, wavering shadows that danced along the damp stone walls of the corridor. Clara could feel the chill of the air creeping through her clothes, a stark contrast to the humid warmth of the greenhouse they had just escaped. The faint smell of wet earth clung to her, mingling with the metallic tang of the lantern’s cracked brass. She tightened her grip on the ribbon around her wrist, the rough texture a tether to something solid in the growing unease.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled, barely above a whisper, as he squinted into the darkness ahead, the shadows seeming to stretch and swallow any sense of comfort. Clara felt the knot in her stomach twist tighter, the echo of a child’s cough ringing in her ears, though she couldn’t remember where it had come from. The thought of it alone made her skin prickle. She shook her head, forcing her voice to hold steadiness, though it came out sharper than intended.

“It’s just the wind. We’re fine.”

Jun glanced back at her, water still dripping from his coat, pooling at his feet in dark, muddy splotches. “This doesn’t feel right, Clara.” His eyes darted to the chained door at the end of the passage, where the chain rattled softly as if something on the other side tested its hold. “We shouldn’t be here.”

The weight of his fear was palpable, pressing against her ribcage, but Clara refused to let it seep into her own heart. Instead, she stepped forward, the sound of her boots squelching in the muck echoing through the narrow corridor. “We have to keep moving. We can’t stay in one place.”

The lantern sputtered again, the light dimming to a mere glow, and with it, a sense of urgency spread through her.
