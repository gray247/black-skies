The lantern sputtered again, a feeble glow that barely pierced the shadows clinging to the corners of the corridor. Clara's heart raced, each thud echoing in her ears, drowned out only by the persistent drip of water from the ceiling, a ghostly reminder of the storm still raging outside. She glanced at Jun, whose soaked coat hung heavy on him, dark fabric clinging uncomfortably to his skin. The cracked brass lantern looked more like a relic than a source of light, its feeble illumination casting their faces into sharp relief, highlighting the fear etched in the corners of his eyes.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled, barely above a whisper, as he moved a step closer to her. She could see the way his hands trembled around the lantern, the way his fingers flexed as if to hold onto something solid in a world that felt increasingly unstable.

“No,” Clara replied too quickly, her voice sharp. The words tasted bitter on her tongue, but she couldn’t let the panic slip out. Not now. The air grew heavier, pressing against her chest as if the stone walls were closing in, suffocating the light. She tightened her grip on the parlor key, the red ribbon digging into her wrist, a tether to a reality she was desperate to cling to. Why had she tied it so loosely?

Ahead, muddy footprints marred the corridor floor, stark against the faded wood, heading toward the chained door.
