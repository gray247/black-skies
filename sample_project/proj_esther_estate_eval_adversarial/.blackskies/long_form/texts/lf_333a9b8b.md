The lantern flickered, its weak glow casting long shadows that danced along the damp walls of the corridor. Clara clutched the cracked brass handle, feeling its cool metal against her palm, a stark contrast to the warmth of the ribbon tied snugly around her wrist. The parlor key dangled there like a promise, yet its weight felt more like a curse in the oppressive darkness. A cold breath of air snaked through the narrow passage, brushing against her cheek, and she shivered, an involuntary tremor that echoed the growing unease curling in her stomach.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely a whisper, strained and edged with fear. He stood close, the fabric of his coat still heavy with moisture from the greenhouse rain, the scent of earth and dampness clinging to him like a shroud. His eyes darted to the end of the corridor, wide and unblinking, as if expecting something—someone—to emerge from the shadows.

Clara shook her head, though doubt gnawed at her. “No, nothing,” she replied, but the sharpness in her tone betrayed her. She stepped forward, the squelch of her own boots in the mud-slicked floor mingling with the distant sound of water dripping from unseen eaves. Each step felt like an intrusion on a stillness that had settled too thickly around them. The lantern sputtered again, its light dimming, and she felt Jun’s breath hitch beside her.

“I swear I heard a cough,” he insisted, his fingers curling into the fabric of his coat, knuckles white. “From upstairs.”
