Clara’s breath came in shallow bursts, the air thick with the scent of mildew and something else, something sharp that clawed at the back of her throat.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was low, a hushed whisper that skittered in the tight space between them, the fear evident and raw. He shifted his weight, the waterlogged fabric of his coat clinging to him like a second skin, the remnants of the greenhouse rain still pooling at his feet. Clara’s fingers tightened around the parlor key, the ribbon scratching against her wrist, a reminder of the door that had led them here, and the secrets that still lay beyond the chained door.

“No,” she replied too quickly, her voice sharper than intended, bouncing off the walls like a stray bullet. The truth was, she hadn’t heard anything but the hollow echo of her own heartbeat, a relentless drum that drowned out reason. But the muddy footprints ahead of them, fresh and stark against the faded wood, told a story she didn’t want to hear. They were leading away from the door, deeper into the passage where the shadows thickened.

A sound, a faint cough, drifted through the air, barely perceptible yet insistent, like a whisper of a memory. It was coming from above, echoing down from the sealed upper floor, and Clara felt a shiver race down her spine.
