Clara pressed her back against the cool, damp wall, the rough surface digging into her skin as she leaned forward to peer into the shadows. Jun’s lantern flickered again, casting erratic beams of light that seemed to dance mockingly at the edges of the corridor. “I swear I heard something,” he muttered, his voice trembling as if trying to convince himself as much as her. The air was thick with the scent of wet earth and something else—sour and metallic, like old coins left too long in a pocket.

She could feel the pulse of her heart, a rapid tattoo echoing the frantic whispers in her mind. The chained door loomed before them, ominous and resolute, its presence felt more than seen. Clara’s fingers brushed over the smooth surface of the parlor key, the red ribbon tight against her wrist. She couldn’t remember tightening it, yet here it was, a tangible reminder of her connection to the world outside this suffocating gloom. “We should go back,” Jun suggested, his eyes wide and glistening with fear.

“No,” Clara snapped, sharper than she intended. The words hung in the air, thickening the tension. She could almost feel the weight of his disappointment settling between them, but she couldn’t let him see her falter. “We need to find out what’s happening.” The words felt hollow, even to her; a brave face masking uncertainty.

Jun hesitated, the lantern’s light illuminating the worry etched across his brow. “What if something’s in there?” He nodded toward the chained door, his voice dropping to a whisper. “What if it’s not just the wind?”
