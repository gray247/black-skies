The air hung thick with the damp scent of earth, a musty blend of mold and rain-soaked wood that clung to Clara’s skin as she stepped deeper into the dimly lit corridor. Shadows danced along the walls, flickering in the weak glow of Jun’s cracked brass lantern, which sputtered like a tired breath. The light barely managed to push back the encroaching darkness, leaving long stretches of corridor draped in inky blackness. Clara's heart quickened, each thud echoing in her ears as the lantern flickered again, sending a spasm of anxiety through her gut.

“Do you think it’s safe?” Jun whispered, his voice barely audible, thick with fear. The fabric of his coat, still drenched from the rain that had poured down in torrents, clung to him, and Clara could almost see the chill racing down his spine, even in the half-light.

“It has to be,” she replied too quickly, a sharpness in her tone she hadn’t intended. Her fingers brushed the red ribbon tied around her wrist, feeling the weight of the parlor key against her skin, a tether to something familiar amidst the chaos. She couldn't shake the feeling that it was somehow tied to the strange happenings in the house, to the whispers that seemed to curl around her like smoke.
