She could sense the weight of the house around them, the very air thick with the musty scent of old memories and stale dreams. A shiver ran down her spine, threading through her thoughts like a chill wind; she hadn’t thought of the nursery in years.

“Do you think anyone’s really up there?” Jun’s voice broke the stillness, low and hesitant. He shifted his weight, his coat still heavy with the rain from the greenhouse, clinging to him like a second skin. Clara met his gaze, the lantern’s dying glow flickering across his face, revealing the uncertainty etched in his features. She wanted to reassure him, to tell him it was just the house settling, just the ghosts of a forgotten past, but the words caught in her throat.

“I don’t know,” she admitted, her voice barely above a whisper. The old stories swirled in her mind, tales of laughter that once filled this space, now silenced by years of neglect. “Nora said no child has slept here for ages.” But even as she spoke, doubt gnawed at her. The way Nora had flinched at the sight of the ceramic fox, its chipped ear a reminder of days long gone, sent a ripple of unease through her. She could still feel the fox nestled in her pocket, its rough edges a tether to a childhood she couldn't quite grasp.

Was someone testing it from the other side, or was it merely the house shifting, settling into its own disquiet?
