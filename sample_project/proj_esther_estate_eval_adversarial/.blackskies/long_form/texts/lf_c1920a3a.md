Clara’s heart thudded in her chest, a nervous drumbeat that seemed to sync with the sputtering of the lantern as Jun pressed forward, his wet coat clinging to him like a second skin. The air was saturated with the scent of damp earth and rust, a reminder of the storm brewing outside, though the walls around them felt like they were holding their breath. Each step echoed slightly, the sound swallowed by the oppressive silence of the corridor, broken only by the relentless drip of water from the greenhouse. Clara's fingers wrapped tighter around the brass lantern, hoping the flickering light would fend off the shadows creeping at the edges of her vision.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled, breaking the stillness. He paused, glancing back toward the darkened passage they had just traversed, his anxious eyes glinting under the lantern's feeble glow. Clara felt the tension in his shoulders, the way he shifted his weight, as if ready to spring back into the safety of the greenhouse.

“It’s just the wind,” she replied, her tone sharper than intended, the words tumbling from her lips like dry leaves caught in a gust. She felt the ribbon around her wrist, the parlor key now a heavy weight that seemed to draw her back toward the door they had left behind. She couldn’t remember tying it tighter, but the red fabric felt taut against her skin, a tether to something she couldn’t quite grasp.

They moved onward, the dim light revealing the corridor’s secrets: faded portraits with eyes that seemed to follow them, and cracked ceramic tiles that crunched softly underfoot. Clara’s gaze flickered to the floor, where muddy prints trailed ahead, stark against the dust. “Those weren’t here before,” she murmured, her voice barely more than a whisper, the knot in her stomach tightening.
