“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice quavered, each word clipped and sharp, reverberating off the walls like a distant echo of his own fear. Clara strained to listen, her heart thumping in her chest, the pulse echoing in her ears. The air was thick with the earthy scent of wet stone and decaying wood, mingling with the lingering aroma of rain-soaked leaves that had somehow made their way inside. She shook her head, though a part of her—a small, nagging part—wondered if she had truly heard something, a soft cough perhaps, drifting down from the sealed upper floor.

“Jun, we need to keep moving.” Her voice came out too harsh, too quick, like a snap of the fingers that she wished she could take back. She glanced at him, the sheen of rain still glistening on his coat, droplets clinging to the fibers like the remnants of their frantic escape from the greenhouse. “It’s just the wind,” she added, though her own words felt hollow, as if the shadows themselves might devour them the moment they turned away.

He hesitated, his eyes darting toward the heavy wooden door at the end of the corridor, the chain rattling softly as if something on the other side compelled it to move.
