Jun’s silhouette flickered in the lantern’s waning light, his expression taut with fear, the sheen of his wet coat reflecting the briefest spark of illumination. “What if it’s not just the storm?” he whispered, glancing toward the chained door, the chain rattling softly as if something on the other side was testing its strength.

“What else could it be?” The words escaped Clara sharper than intended, and she swallowed hard, the taste of anxiety bitter on her tongue. She didn’t dare mention the cough she hadn’t heard, nor the muddy prints that seemed to trail ahead of them rather than behind. The air was thick with tension, and she felt it pooling in her stomach, heavy and unyielding.

“Just… stay close,” she said, her own voice sounding foreign and brittle, as if the corridor itself would swallow it whole. She moved forward, the lantern’s glow sputtering, casting a flickering halo that danced like anxious spirits along the corridor walls. Each step felt like a pact with the darkness, her heart drumming in time with the sound of Jun’s boots squelching in the muck beneath them.

The end of the corridor loomed, a shadowed maw that seemed to yawn wider as they approached. Clara could feel the pulse of the storm outside, each distant rumble of thunder vibrating through the floorboards, echoing in her bones.
