The air in the corridor was thick with the scent of damp wood and mildew, the lingering evidence of the rain that had drummed relentlessly on the greenhouse roof. Clara clutched the cracked brass lantern, its light flickering like a dying star, casting elongated shadows that crawled across the walls. She could feel the cool metal of the parlor key against her wrist, the ribbon’s texture a reminder of the urgency that pressed down on her like the weight of the storm outside.

Jun’s coat dripped with the last vestiges of the downpour, the fabric heavy and clinging to his skin. He moved beside her, his eyes darting to the chained door at the end of the corridor. The chain rattled softly, as if something on the other side was testing its strength, and each metallic clink reverberated through Clara’s chest, amplifying the flutter of anxiety that took root there. “We should go back,” he whispered, his voice barely rising above the soft patter of water dripping from his coat.

“No, we need to find out what’s happening,” Clara replied too quickly, the edge in her tone betraying the nerves she struggled to mask. The words tasted bitter in her mouth, and she felt the knot in her stomach tighten. She wished she could summon the conviction of her decision, but the uncertainty gnawed at her like a hungry animal.

Ahead, the floor was littered with muddy prints, smudges that trailed toward the door rather than away from it, as if someone had ventured in rather than out. Clara’s heart raced. “Look,” she murmured, pointing with a trembling finger. “Someone's been here.” She stepped closer, the lantern’s light revealing the prints’ chaotic pattern, a contradiction to the stillness that surrounded them.
