The lantern sputtered again, its flickering light catching Clara’s breath in her throat. She pressed forward, the damp chill of the corridor curling around her like an unwelcome shroud. The shadows seemed to stretch and yawn, whispering secrets she was not meant to hear. Each step echoed with an unsettling finality, the old wooden floor creaking beneath her weight, as if protesting their intrusion into this forgotten space. Jun lingered close behind, his presence a taut string pulled tight in the dark, the anxiety radiating from him palpable, almost a living thing.

“Clara,” he whispered, his voice quaking. “We should go back.” The words felt heavy, laden with an unspoken dread that gnawed at her resolve. But she couldn’t bear the thought of retreating now, not when the air was thick with something she couldn’t quite name—curiosity, perhaps, or the urgent need to unravel the mystery that lay ahead.

“I heard something,” she replied, her tone sharper than intended, almost defensive. “We can’t just leave.” The key on her wrist brushed against her skin, the red ribbon a stark reminder of their purpose. It felt tighter somehow, though she could not recall pulling it. She ran her thumb over the knot, that small, familiar comfort anchoring her as they advanced deeper into the corridor.

The lantern flickered once more, dimming as if it, too, sensed the weight of the silence.
