Clara’s breath came in shallow bursts, a tightness coiling in her chest as she clutched the damp brass lantern, its sputtering light casting jittery shadows against the stone walls of the corridor. The air felt thick, as if the very silence were pressing down on her, clinging to her skin like the dampness from the greenhouse rain still soaking through Jun's coat. She could hear the faint echo of their footsteps, muted against the cold, unyielding floor, but it was the sound of something else—an insistent rattling—that made her heart race.

“Did you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice hushed, almost breathless. His eyes, wide and glistening, darted toward the door chained from the inside, the link catching the dim light and glinting ominously. Clara felt the pulse of fear radiate from him, and though she wanted to reassure him, the tremor in her own voice betrayed her.

“It’s probably just the wind,” she said, forcing the words out, but even as she spoke, she felt the weight of her own doubt. The wind didn’t rattle chains. Her gaze shifted to the muddy prints ahead, stark against the worn stone, leading deeper into the shadows. They weren’t hers; she was certain of that. Clara tightened her grip on the lantern, the metal cool and slick in her palm, as if it were trying to slip away.

“Clara,” Jun’s voice quavered, and she turned to him, catching the flicker of light in his eyes, a mixture of fear and something deeper that made her heart ache. “We should go back.”
