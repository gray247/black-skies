Clara glanced at the door, its paint cracked and peeling, as if it had been waiting for years to be disturbed. The lantern sputtered in Jun’s hand, a dying ember of light casting elongated shadows that seemed to stretch toward the darkness beyond. Her heart thudded against her ribs, each beat echoing the uncertainty coiling in her stomach. “What if there’s nothing in there?” she asked, her voice trembling as she traced the chipped edge of the ceramic fox in her pocket, the cool glaze grounding her in the moment.

“Or what if there is?” Jun countered, his brow furrowed, the weight of his soaked coat pulling him down. He stepped closer, eyeing the door with a mix of fear and resolve. “We can’t keep running from this.” The resolve in his tone felt like a thin thread, fraying under the pressure of unspoken fears.

The air, heavy with the scent of damp wood and something faintly sweet, wrapped around them, as if the house was holding its breath. Clara shivered, not entirely from the chill that lingered in the corridor. She felt the weight of Nora’s gaze still lingering in her mind, the way her smile had strained, a mask barely hiding something darker. Clara had seen that flinch when she had pulled the fox from her pocket, and it echoed in her thoughts like the ticking of the old clock above them. “What did you hear, Jun?” she finally asked, the question tumbling from her lips before she could catch it.

Jun hesitated, the lantern flickering again, casting a brief glow that illuminated the uncertainty etched on his face. “I didn’t—”

“Then let’s not talk about it.” She cut him off, the words sharp, desperate to clamp down on the rising tide of dread. “We can’t afford to know more than we already do.”

The lantern’s light guttered, dimming further, and Clara felt the pulse of the house around them, the faint creaking of the walls, the whisper of memories long frozen in time. The door loomed larger with each passing moment, a threshold to the unknown. “Are you ready?” Jun asked, his voice barely a whisper, like he was afraid of waking something inside.

“Ready or not,” she murmured, stepping forward, the red ribbon of the parlor key brushing against her wrist, a reminder of the choices they had made to get here. The door felt cool beneath her fingertips as she reached for the handle, the metal cold and unyielding. It was locked, of course. She could feel the weight of it, the way it held back whatever lay beyond—a secret, a truth, or perhaps just more questions.

With a deep breath, she pulled the key from her wrist, its ribbon trailing like a memory. The key’s teeth gleamed dully in the lantern's flicker, and she hesitated. “What if it’s the wrong one?” she asked, her voice barely above a whisper, fear creeping into her thoughts.

“Then we’ll find another way,” he replied, but his eyes betrayed him, searching the corners of the nursery door for signs of life, or the absence of it. Clara clenched the key tighter, uncertainty gnawing at her resolve. She inserted it into the lock, feeling the cold metal against her palm, and twisted. The latch clicked, sending a shiver through her spine, as if the house itself sighed in response.

The door swung open with a hesitant creak, revealing a room swallowed by shadows, the air thick with dust and the weight of forgotten dreams. Clara stepped inside, the lantern’s light barely penetrating the gloom, and the silence pressed in around them, heavy and suffocating.
