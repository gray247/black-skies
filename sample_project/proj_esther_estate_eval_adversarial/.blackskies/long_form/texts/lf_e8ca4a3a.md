Clara pressed her palm against the nursery door, the wood cold and rough beneath her fingertips. She could feel the thrum of the house beneath her skin, a pulse of anxiety that matched the storm outside, each gust of wind rattling the windows as if the world wanted to intrude. Jun stood beside her, his coat still heavy with the downpour from the greenhouse, droplets pooling at his feet like dark memories. The weak light from the lantern flickered, casting a dance of shadows that played across the walls, making the nursery feel alive, almost sentient.

“What if there’s someone in there?” he muttered, the tremor in his voice betraying his bravado. The lantern sputtered, and the glow waned further, shadows creeping up the door like fingers reaching for secrets. Clara squeezed the red ribbon at her wrist, the parlor key swinging lightly, a tether to something familiar, yet she was anything but certain about what lay behind this door.

“I don’t know,” she finally replied, her voice barely above a whisper. The cough she hadn’t heard echoed in her mind, a ghostly reminder that something, or someone, lurked just beyond the threshold. Her heart quickened, not from fear alone but from a tangled mix of curiosity and dread. She had to know.

Jun shifted beside her, the brass lantern trembling in his grasp, as if it too felt the weight of their decision. “Maybe we should just go,” he suggested, glancing back toward the staircase, where the chain rattled above them, a sound that had no business being there. Clara felt the chain’s movement reverberate through her bones, a chilling reminder that they were not alone in this house.

“No,” she said, firming her resolve, though uncertainty gnawed at her. “We can’t. Not after everything.” She pressed against the door, feeling the grain of the wood, the coolness of it grounding her even as her mind spun with questions. What if the cough belonged to a child? What if it was an echo of a memory? The thought sent a shiver racing up her spine, igniting an unsettling spark of urgency.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice low but insistent, “what if it’s dangerous? What if we’re just—”

“Just what?” She turned to meet his gaze, her breath hitching as the lantern’s feeble light momentarily illuminated the worry etched across his features. “Aren’t you curious? We’ve come this far.” Her words hung in the air, heavy with unspoken fears and the weight of expectations. The nursery had been sealed for years, a forgotten realm of shadows and whispers, and yet it beckoned to her like a siren’s call.

He hesitated, and in that silence, Clara felt the pulse of the house grow louder, a heartbeat thrumming through the floorboards and into her bones. “What if the prints belong to someone who shouldn’t be here?” he asked, glancing nervously at the door.

With a deep breath, Clara tightened her grip on the doorknob, the chill of the metal sending a jolt through her fingers. “Then we’ll find out, won’t we?” The words slipped from her mouth more confidently than she felt, but she resolved to push through the door, to uncover whatever lay hidden behind it.

As she turned the knob, the door creaked open, releasing a stale breath of air that smelled of dust and something else—something sweet and rotten, like flowers wilting in the dark.
