Clara's fingers trembled against the nursery door, the cool wood a stark contrast to the warmth pooling in her chest, an ache that had settled there since they’d first stepped out of the greenhouse. The thirteenth chime echoed hauntingly in the stillness, a somber reminder that time, too, felt displaced within these walls. Jun's breath came in quick puffs beside her, visible in the dim light of the sputtering lantern. Its flickering flame danced wildly, casting shadows that seemed to claw at the corners of the room, as if the house itself were alive, watching.

“What do you think is in there?” he asked, his voice low and edged with a nervous tension that matched the atmosphere. He shifted again, the wet fabric of his coat whispering against his skin, mirroring the unease that wrapped around them both. “You heard the cough, right?”

Clara bit her lip, the taste of uncertainty bitter as she recalled the sound that had flitted through her mind, a shiver of a memory she couldn’t quite grasp. “No,” she finally said, her voice steadier than her heart. “I didn’t hear anything.”

“Clara,” he pressed, but she felt the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket, its chipped surface cool against her fingers, grounding her amidst the chaos. She could almost feel its gaze upon her, urging her to remember what the old nursery held—not just dust and discarded dreams, but something more alive.

“The key,” she murmured, lifting her wrist to catch the lantern's light. The red ribbon tied around it glimmered, a vivid splash of color in the monochrome gloom. “I have it. We could—”

“Could what?” His frustration rippled through the air, thickening the silence like fog. “Open a door to memories best left sealed?”

Her breath caught, a mix of indignation and sorrow. “You don’t understand. It’s not just about the door. It’s about—” she hesitated, searching for the right words. “It’s about knowing what happened. About facing it.”

“Then let’s face it,” he said, taking a step closer, the warmth of his presence pushing against her cold dread. “But not like this. Not here. We should go back, find Nora.”

“Back?” The word felt foreign, a betrayal to the determination that had propelled them this far. “And leave this… whatever this is, behind us?”

Jun fell silent, the tension straining the air between them. The lantern flickered violently, and she felt the urge to reach out, to touch the door, to push past the unknown. But what if it was worse than the fears they harbored? What if this was where the truth lay?

“Clara,” he said finally, his voice softer now, almost pleading. “If there’s something in there, it could be dangerous. We’re not equipped to handle—”

“I’m not afraid,” she snapped, the defiance rising like bile. But even as she said it, a tremor ran through her hands, and she could hear the chain rattling faintly from somewhere above, mocking her bravado.

The sensation of eyes upon her grew heavier, and she turned her gaze back to the door. “We can’t keep running from it. We owe it to whoever was here… whoever is still here,” she whispered, more to herself than to him.

“Okay,” Jun relented, his voice a quiet anchor against the storm brewing in her mind. “But let’s be smart about this.” He moved beside her, the lantern tilting precariously in his grip, illuminating the door’s worn surface, the intricate carvings barely visible beneath layers of grime.
