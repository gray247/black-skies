The air in the narrow corridor felt thick, as if it were pressing in on Clara and Jun from all sides, the dim light of the cracked brass lantern casting wavering shadows that danced along the peeling wallpaper. Clara’s breath quickened, the sharp scent of mildew mixing with the earthy odor of damp wood, reminding her of the greenhouse’s choking humidity. With each step, the floorboards creaked beneath her feet, a nervous symphony echoing her growing unease.

“Do you think it’s safe?” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, his eyes darting to every corner as if expecting something—or someone—to leap out from the gloom. His coat clung to him, heavy and sodden, still carrying the weight of the rain. Clara glanced at him, noting the way his hands trembled slightly around the lantern, the dim light sputtering in protest.

“It has to be,” she replied too quickly, her voice sharper than intended. The words hung between them, heavy with unspoken fears. Clara tightened the red ribbon around her wrist, the parlor key resting against her skin, a reminder of their purpose and the urgency that had driven them into this darkness. She couldn’t quite remember when she had tied it tighter, but the sensation of the fabric was grounding.

They moved deeper into the corridor, following the muddy footprints that snaked ahead, dark smudges against the worn wood.
