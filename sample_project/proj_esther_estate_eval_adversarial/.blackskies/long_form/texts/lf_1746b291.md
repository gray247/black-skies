Jun shifted beside her, the lantern flickering weakly, illuminating the curling wallpaper that seemed to peel away from the walls in protest, as if the house itself wished to shed its past.

“Do you think we should?” he asked again, glancing at her, his breath catching in the dampness that clung to the air. The tremor in his voice felt like a rope tightening around her chest. Clara could hear it then, the faint echo of a cough, almost like a whisper, threading through the silence. It was so soft, so fleeting, that she wondered if she had imagined it. Yet, it lingered like a ghost, teasing the edges of her memory and setting her heart racing.

The lantern sputtered, casting a fitful light that danced against the door, and for a moment, she almost saw the outlines of children’s drawings—smudged figures that played in her mind’s eye but were lost to the shadows. “What if there’s someone in there?” Jun continued, his gaze flickering toward the door as if it might spring open at any moment, revealing whatever secrets had been tucked away. Clara had no answer, her throat felt tight, a knot of grief and suspicion twisting painfully within her.

“Maybe it’s just the house settling,” she ventured, though uncertainty dripped from her words. The clock overhead ticked steadily, a metronome for her racing thoughts, and she felt a shiver crawl up her spine. “Nora said no one’s been in here for years.”

Jun hesitated, his brow furrowing, and she caught a glimpse of the wet fabric clinging to his coat, a muted reminder of their hasty exit from the rain-soaked greenhouse. “What if she’s wrong?” His voice dropped lower, almost conspiratorial, and Clara felt the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket, a relic of a childhood that felt more burden than comfort now.

“Maybe we should just—”

But something rattled against the door behind them, a faint sound that made her heart jump. The chain. It was sliding, testing its bonds with a quiet insistence from beyond the corridor. Clara’s breath quickened, her senses sharpening. Was it a trick of the house, or had someone—something—been waiting for them?

“Did you hear that?” she whispered, her voice barely above the creak of the floorboards. Jun nodded, eyes wide, the lantern dimming further as if sensing their collective dread. “We can’t stay here,” he said, urgency bleeding through the calm he tried to project.
