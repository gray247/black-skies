Clara pressed her back against the cool, peeling wall of the nursery, the weight of the chipped ceramic fox in her coat pocket pressing into her thigh. The lantern sputtered again, its flickering light casting monstrous shadows that danced along the faded wallpaper, where once-rosy blooms had turned a sickly yellow. She glanced at Jun, his soaked coat clinging to him like a second skin, the fabric glimmering with droplets that caught the lantern's weak glow. Despite the chill creeping through the room, a warmth unfurled in her chest at the sight of him, even as the air thickened with an unseen tension, the musty scent of dust and neglect swirling around them.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice, low and cautious, sliced through the heavy silence. His eyes darted to the corner of the room, where shadows pooled like secrets waiting to be unearthed. Clara felt her heart quicken, a silent drum echoing in her ears, but she shook her head, willing the memory of the child’s cough to retreat into the recesses of her mind. It was only her imagination, she told herself, a trick played by the house that had sheltered them and haunted them in equal measure.

“Maybe we should just go back,” he suggested, though Clara could hear the uncertainty in his tone. The chain rattled again from the service corridor, a sound too deliberate to be wind or the settling of an old house. It was as if something—or someone—was testing its restraints, a pulse thrumming beneath the floorboards. She swallowed hard, the taste of fear bitter on her tongue.

“Back where?” she replied, the words sharper than she intended. Her fingers tightened around the ribbon tied to her wrist, the key dangling like a promise she couldn't quite grasp. “Nora won’t let us in. She’s convinced there’s nothing in here but dust and old memories.”

“Maybe that’s all it is.” Jun’s voice dropped, almost a whisper, as he stepped closer, the lantern casting his face into stark relief. “Just memories.” Yet the way he avoided her gaze suggested he wasn’t so sure.

Clara turned her attention to the window, painted shut from the inside, its glass fogged with age. The nursery felt trapped in time, and she couldn’t shake the feeling that something inside was clamoring to escape. As she breathed in the stale air, a shiver ran down her spine. What if the child had always been here, just beyond the reach of their understanding, waiting for her to listen?

“Do you think it’s true?” she asked, her voice barely above a whisper. “What Nora said, about the nursery being empty for years?”

Jun shifted, the lantern flickering again as if to punctuate her question. “I don’t know. But if there’s a chance—”

“—that we could find something, or someone?” She interrupted, her heart racing at the thought. “But what if it’s not just memories? What if it’s something else?”

A sound reverberated through the air, a soft cough that seemed to rise from the very walls, echoing Clara's fears. She felt her stomach drop, the warmth in her chest evaporating into icy dread. “Did you hear that?” she asked, her voice trembling, and this time, she didn’t need to wait for an answer.

Jun’s eyes widened, and he nodded slowly, his face paling in the dim light. The lantern flicked once more, casting their reflections into grotesque shapes on the wall, and Clara’s breath caught in her throat as she considered the implications. They were not alone.
