The air in the corridor was thick with the scent of damp earth and something sweeter, almost cloying, that Clara couldn't quite place. The flickering light of the cracked brass lantern cast long shadows along the worn stone walls, where the echoes of their footsteps faded into the darkness like whispers carrying secrets. Jun’s soaked coat clung to him, the fabric heavy and cold, and Clara could see the tremor in his fingers as he adjusted his grip on the lantern, its weak beam sputtering like a dying flame.

“Do you think it’s safe?” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, trembling against the quiet. His gaze darted around, as if the shadows themselves might spring to life and swallow them whole.

Clara tightened her grip on the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon digging into her skin. She had no memory of tying it tighter, but the pressure was oddly comforting, a lifeline in the darkness. “We have to find out,” she replied, her voice sharper than she intended, echoing with uncertainty. She didn’t want to sound afraid, but the dim corridor felt alive, as if it breathed and pulsed with the weight of their shared dread.

Ahead, muddy prints marred the dust, leading deeper into the shadows. Clara’s heart raced, an insistent drumbeat against her ribs. “Look,” she said, pointing at the trail. “Someone’s been here.”

Jun leaned closer, squinting at the ground. “But how? We were the only ones outside.” His brow furrowed, and Clara felt a flicker of panic spark between them.

“Maybe it’s just… old footprints,” she suggested, though the words felt thin against the thickening tension. She stepped forward, the sound of her boot breaking the silence, and the lantern flickered again, casting the corridor into a deeper shade of uncertainty.
