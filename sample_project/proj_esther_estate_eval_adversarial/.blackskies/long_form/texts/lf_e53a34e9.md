The lantern sputtered in Jun's grip, casting a flickering light that barely pierced the oppressive shadows of the corridor. Clara could feel the dampness seeping through her clothes, a cold reminder of the greenhouse rain that had soaked Jun’s coat. The air was thick with the scent of wet earth and decay, mingling with an undercurrent of something sharper, like burnt wires. She pulled the red ribbon tighter around her wrist, though she couldn’t remember how it had loosened, and the soft fabric brushed against her skin like a whisper of warning.

“Do you hear that?” Jun's voice was barely above a whisper, his eyes darting toward the far end of the passage, where the dim light struggled to reach. Clara strained to listen, but all she could hear was the thudding of her own heart, echoing in her ears like a drum signaling an oncoming storm. She shook her head, forcing a steadiness into her voice. “It’s just the wind.”

But the wind didn’t sound like this, pressing against the walls as if it were trying to push through. A faint rattling came from the door at the end of the corridor, the chain jingling softly, a sound that sent a shiver racing down Clara’s spine. Jun took a step closer, his breath quickening, and she felt the weight of his fear seep into her own resolve. “What if someone is on the other side?” he asked, his voice trembling.

She didn’t want to think about that—the idea of someone waiting, testing the chain, eager to break through. “No one’s there,” she replied too sharply, her fingers curling around the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket, its edges rough against her skin, grounding her in this moment, in this dread. “We should keep moving.”
