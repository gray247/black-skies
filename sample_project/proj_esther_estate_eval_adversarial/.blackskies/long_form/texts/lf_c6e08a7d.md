Jun stood behind her, his presence a quiet anchor amidst the storm of her thoughts, the scent of wet earth and rain-soaked fabric clinging to him like a shroud. “We can’t just stand here,” he murmured, his voice strained, a taut string ready to snap.

“What if we’re wrong?” Clara repeated, her voice barely more than a breath. The air was thick, charged with an unnameable tension, as if the house itself held its breath. A soft creak overhead sent a shiver down her spine. Clara glanced at Jun, who had stiffened, his eyes darting toward the ceiling, where the thud of movement echoed like a heartbeat. “Did you hear that?” he asked, his brow furrowing. She shook her head, though she felt the tremor in her own heart, a rhythm that matched the house’s quiet pulse.

The lantern sputtered, its glow dimming, leaving only a flicker that danced across the door’s surface, revealing more than it concealed. “It’s just the house settling,” Clara said, the words tasting hollow on her tongue. She turned the key in the lock, the mechanism grinding against rust and disuse, but it wouldn’t yield. “We have to go in, Jun. We need to know.”

Jun shook his head, water droplets flying from his hair. “What if there’s something… someone in there?”

“It’s not like we can just leave it,” she replied, her voice rising, catching in the tension that thickened the air. The memory of the cough, that faint echo of a child’s breath, pressed at the back of her mind, though she knew Jun hadn’t heard it. The thought twisted in her gut, a knot of fear and uncertainty.

Behind her, the chain rattled against the doorframe. Clara spun around, her heart racing. “Did you hear that?”

Jun stepped back, his expression a mix of confusion and dread. “What is happening?” he asked, the lantern flickering again, casting shadows that seemed to crawl along the walls. “We should leave. It’s not safe.”

“No,” she insisted, the resolve hardening within her. “We can’t just ignore this.” She turned back to the door, her fingers gripping the key tighter, the red ribbon digging into her wrist. “If we don’t find out what’s happening, we might never know.”

The space was untouched, yet something felt wrong, too quiet, too still.
