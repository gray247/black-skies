Clara pressed herself against the cool stone wall, her breath hitching in her throat as the rattling chain on the door reverberated through the corridor. The dim light from Jun’s lantern sputtered again, its feeble glow flickering like a heartbeat, and she felt an inexplicable chill crawl up her spine. “We should go back,” Jun whispered, his voice barely cutting through the oppressive silence, each word laced with the tremor of fear.

“No,” Clara replied too sharply, her voice echoing unnaturally in the confined space. She gripped the parlor key, tied to the red ribbon around her wrist, feeling its reassuring weight against her skin. It was a strange comfort, yet it felt more like a reminder of the chaos brewing just outside their reach. She glanced at Jun, his soaked coat glistening in the lantern's light, water still pooling around his feet, darkening the already muddy floor. The air was thick with the scent of damp earth, mingling with something she couldn't quite place—a metallic tang that made her stomach churn.

“Clara,” he urged again, his eyes darting toward the door, where the chain rattled once more, a soft, ominous clink that sent her pulse racing. Each sound pulled at her resolve, a silent plea that felt like an echo of something she couldn’t quite grasp. She felt the ceramic fox nestled in her pocket, its chipped surface cool against her fingers, grounding her in the moment. But it was a fragile tether, one that could unravel at any moment.
