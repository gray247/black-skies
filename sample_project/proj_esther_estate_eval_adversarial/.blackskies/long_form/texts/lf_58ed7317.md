“Nothing. I thought I heard… someone.” Jun’s brow furrowed, his wet coat clinging to him, the fabric darkened and heavy like the looming clouds outside. Clara could almost see the shiver that coursed through him, mirrored in the tremor of the lantern’s light. She glanced down at the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon now damp, a cruel reminder of the secrets they had unearthed. What were they supposed to do with a key that felt so heavy with unspoken dread?

“Maybe it was just the wind,” she said, though her words felt flimsy against the weight of uncertainty. The lantern sputtered again, its glow fading further, as if the very shadows were conspiring to swallow them whole. The corridor stretched before them, the narrow passageway lined with peeling wallpaper that bore the stains of time. Clara could almost hear the whispers of those who had walked here before, their laughter long snuffed out, replaced now by the scent of mildew and decay.

“Still…” Jun hesitated, his voice trailing off, eyes darting toward the heavy door they had come through. The chain rattled again, a soft clink like a distant bell tolling, and Clara’s heart leaped. Had they truly been alone?
