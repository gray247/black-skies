She glanced at Jun, whose brow was furrowed in concentration, his lantern sputtering weakly as shadows danced across their faces. “We should move,” he murmured, the flickering light casting an anxious glow on his damp features.

The corridor narrowed ahead, an unyielding passage that seemed to pulse with memories long buried. Clara could hear the faint echoes of their footsteps mixing with the distant drip of water, a reminder of the greenhouse’s relentless rain. The earthy scent of wet soil clung to them, a reminder of their hasty escape and the secrets submerged beneath the surface. She stole another glance at Jun, his coat still soaked, the fabric clinging to him like a second skin. “Do you think it’s just mud?” she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.

Jun paused, his eyes narrowing as he scanned the darkness ahead. “It’s too fresh,” he replied, his tone steady yet edged with unease. “It shouldn’t be here.” The muddy prints that marred the floor seemed to lead deeper into the house, a trail of uncertainty that made Clara’s stomach twist. What had come before them? A shiver danced up her spine, and she felt the weight of the sealed nursery like a stone in her pocket, the ceramic fox pressing against her thigh, a talisman of both comfort and dread.

As they crept forward, the lantern’s light flickered then dimmed, casting them into shadow. Clara felt the chill of the air wrap around her, a stark contrast to the warmth of Jun’s presence beside her. “Do you hear that?” she asked, suddenly aware of the way the house exhaled, the creaks and groans blending into a symphony of unease. She thought she discerned a cough, faint and childlike, echoing from somewhere above, but it was so quick she almost doubted it.

Jun nodded, his expression tightening. “It’s coming from the nursery,” he said, his voice low, like he was afraid to disturb whatever lay within those walls. Clara’s heart raced, torn between a desire to flee and an inexplicable pull to uncover the truth. The ribbon felt heavier on her wrist, a binding tether to the past, and she couldn’t shake the feeling that something was watching them from the shadows.

As they reached a door at the end of the corridor, Clara's breath caught in her throat. The chain rattled from the other side, a mocking taunt that sent a jolt of adrenaline coursing through her veins. “It’s locked,” she said, her voice trembling slightly. “We can’t get through.”

Jun stepped closer, the lantern barely illuminating the chipped wood of the door. “Maybe we can find another way,” he suggested, but Clara felt a pull toward the door, a desperate need to confront whatever lay beyond. She hesitated, her fingers brushing over the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon a stark reminder of the choices laid before her.

“Clara,” Jun said, his tone shifting from caution to urgency. “We need to decide. If there’s someone in there…” He trailed off, the unspoken fear hanging between them like a thick fog.

“I know,” she replied, her voice steadier than she felt. “But we have to try.” With trembling hands, she pulled the key from its ribbon, the metal cold against her palm, and inserted it into the lock. The mechanism resisted at first, but then there was a click, and the door creaked open, revealing darkness beyond.
