Clara pressed forward, the cracked brass lantern casting wobbly shadows on the damp stone walls of the corridor. Each flicker seemed to mimic the quickening thrum of her pulse, a frantic rhythm that drowned out the distant patter of rain still clinging to her coat. The air was thick with the musty scent of old wood, mingling with the sharp tang of mildew. She could feel the dampness seeping into her bones, a chill that had nothing to do with the weather outside.

"Clara," Jun whispered, his voice low and edged with a tremor that worried her more than she wanted to admit. He was still shaking off droplets from his coat, the fabric clinging to his skin, his breath coming in quick bursts of steam that dissipated into the heavy air. "Are you sure we should be down here? This feels wrong."

She turned to him, trying to mask the unease gnawing at her insides. "We have to keep moving. We can’t just stand here waiting for... whatever that was to find us." Her voice came out sharper than intended, a brittle snap that made Jun flinch. She hated how easily fear turned her words into knives.

The lantern flickered again, casting a ghostly glow on a patch of muddy prints leading deeper into the darkness. Clara’s heart raced as she traced the outline of the prints with her eyes. They were fresh, undeniably recent, but there was no one else here—at least, no one she could see. Shadows danced along the walls, and with them came a creeping sensation of being watched, a prickling awareness that settled at the nape of her neck.

“Jun,” she said, lowering her voice. “Do you see those?” She gestured toward the prints. “They lead toward the service door.”
