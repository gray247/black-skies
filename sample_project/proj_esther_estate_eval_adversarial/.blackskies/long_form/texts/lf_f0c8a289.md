The air in the corridor was thick with the scent of damp earth and the lingering bitterness of rain. Clara's heart thudded in her chest, each beat a drumroll against the tense silence. She tightened her grip on the lantern, its cracked brass body cool against her palms, the flickering light casting uneven shadows that danced along the peeling wallpaper. Jun shuffled beside her, his coat clinging to him like a second skin, soaked through and heavy with the weight of the storm that had just passed. She could see his breath misting in the dim glow, and when he glanced at her, his eyes were wide with something deeper than fear.

“Did you hear that?” he whispered, his voice barely above a breath.

Clara shook her head, though a sliver of doubt crept in. The silence was oppressive, thickened by the soft patter of water dripping from the ceiling above. But something stirred within her, a tension that had settled like dust in the corners of her mind, waiting for the right moment to emerge. She glanced back at the door they had just come through, the chain rattling softly as if someone were testing it from the other side.

“It's just the wind,” she said, too quickly, too sharply. Her own voice sounded foreign in the stillness, and she felt the warmth of embarrassment rise in her cheeks. “We should keep moving.”

Jun nodded but lingered, his gaze fixed on the ground ahead. Muddy prints marred the wooden floor, stark against the faded floral pattern, leading deeper into the shadows. Clara felt a prickle at the back of her neck, an instinctive warning that made her hesitate. "We can’t just stand here," she urged, though a part of her wanted to turn back, to feel the safety of the greenhouse behind them—its splintered glass and wet vines.
