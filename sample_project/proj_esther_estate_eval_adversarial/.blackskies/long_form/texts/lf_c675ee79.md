“Only one way to find out,” Clara replied, her pulse quickening as she stepped closer to the door, her heart racing like a moth drawn to the flickering light. The wood felt reassuringly solid beneath her fingers, a barrier that separated her from whatever lay hidden behind it. She glanced back at Jun, his coat darkened by moisture, droplets pooling on the fabric like tiny, anxious memories of the storm. The sight twisted her stomach into knots.

Clara's breath caught in her throat, and she turned to Jun, whose wide eyes reflected a mix of disbelief and dread, as if he could peer straight through the door into the nursery beyond.

“Did you hear it too?” he pressed, urgency tightening his features.

“I didn’t—” she started, but the cough came again, clearer and more insistent this time. Her heart raced as it pierced through the silence, muffled yet unmistakably a child's cough, stirring a buried memory that clawed its way to the surface. She shook her head, trying to dispel the sensation. “No one’s been here in years, Jun. It must be the house.”

Yet even as she spoke, doubt curled in her mind, a whisper that urged her to confront her fears. Nora’s warning that no child had set foot in the nursery for as long as she could remember flashed through her thoughts, along with the way the older woman had flinched at the sight of the fox. Clara turned back to Jun, whose furrowed brow mirrored her own uncertainty, a cocktail of skepticism and fear brewing in his expression.

“Should we go in?” he asked, the lantern’s light wavering, casting flickering shapes that danced between them and the uncertainty ahead.

Clara hesitated, the weight of the past pressing heavily on her shoulders. The nursery had been a sealed-off realm of dust and memories for years, yet the pull of the unknown was almost magnetic. “We can’t just stand here,” she said, her voice steadier than the turmoil inside her. “We need to know.”
