The cracked brass lantern in Jun’s grip flickered erratically, casting long shadows that danced and twisted along the narrow corridor. It threw a wavering light onto the faded wallpaper, revealing patches of peeling floral patterns that seemed to writhe under the gloom. She could hear the distant patter of water seeping through the walls, a reminder of the rain-soaked greenhouse they had just escaped.

“Did you see that?” Jun’s voice came out thin, strained, almost lost amidst the low thrum of the house settling around them. His coat clung to him, soaked through and heavy, a reminder of how quickly the storm had turned. Clara felt the pulse of anxiety quicken in her chest, matching the frantic rhythm of her thoughts. She didn’t turn to him, didn’t let his fear seep into her own. Instead, her fingers brushed against the cool ceramic of the fox in her pocket, the chipped edges a small comfort that grounded her amidst the chaos.
