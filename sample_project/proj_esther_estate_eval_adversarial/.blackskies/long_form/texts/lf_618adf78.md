Clara's grip tightened around the lantern, its sputtering light casting flickering shadows that danced along the damp corridor walls. The air felt heavy, thick with the scent of rotting wood and the pervasive chill of something unseen lurking just beyond the reach of the weak glow. She could feel Jun’s shivering beside her, the weight of his fear palpable. His soaked coat clung to him like a shroud, and she could almost sense the cold seeping into him, an unwelcome guest settling deep in his bones.

“Clara, we have to go,” he urged, his voice a low whisper, trembling with urgency. The fear in his eyes mirrored the indecision gnawing at her stomach. She couldn’t shake the feeling that they were being watched, and each creak of the house felt like an echo of some long-forgotten warning.

“Just a moment,” Clara replied, perhaps too sharp, her tone betraying the anxiety bubbling beneath her skin. She glanced back toward the chained door, a flimsy barrier against whatever prowled outside their fragile sanctuary. Her heart raced as she imagined the chain rattling, not from a breeze, but from a deliberate hand testing its limits.

Ahead of them, muddy footprints marred the pristine dust of the passage, leading deeper into the shadows. Clara’s breath quickened. “Jun, look.” She pointed, her voice dropping to a breathless whisper. The prints were fresh, almost as if they had appeared in the time it took them to cross the threshold. “Someone’s been here.”
