Clara pressed further into the dim corridor, the air thick with the scent of damp wood and something faintly acrid, as if the walls themselves were sweating secrets. The lantern flickered again, a sputtering light that barely illuminated the muddy footprints ahead, each one a silent testament to someone—or something—having passed through just moments before. She felt Jun’s presence beside her, his coat still soaked, the fabric clinging to him like a second skin, and she could almost hear the rapid thrum of his heartbeat mingling with her own.

“There’s something in here,” he whispered, a tremor in his voice that she could not ignore. His eyes darted to the shadowy corners of the corridor, haunted by the fear that had begun to seep into her bones as well. Clara forced herself to breathe, to remain steady even as the parlor key felt heavier against her wrist, the red ribbon tightening with each pulse. “It’s just the wind,” she replied too quickly, the words spilling out sharper than she intended, but she couldn’t afford to show weakness—not now.

The door at the far end rattled, a metallic clank that echoed through the stillness, and they both froze. Clara’s heart raced, the sound rippling through her like a shockwave. “Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely more than a whisper, his eyes wide with a mixture of dread and disbelief. She nodded, though the knot in her stomach twisted tighter. “It’s probably just the chain,” she said, but even as she spoke, she felt the chill of uncertainty creeping up her spine.

They edged closer, the floorboards creaking beneath their feet, the sound swallowed by the oppressive silence.
