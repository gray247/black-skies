Jun’s breath hitched as he peered into the nursery’s heavy door, the cracked brass lantern sputtering between them. Shadows danced, stretching and retreating like timid children caught in a game of hide-and-seek. Clara stood beside him, her heart pounding a frantic rhythm that felt painfully out of sync with the house’s slow, creaking pulse. She could feel the dampness still clinging to her skin, the ghost of the greenhouse rain mingling with the dust in the air, each particle a reminder of the secrets they were trying to unearth.

“It’s just a door,” Jun whispered, though the tremor in his voice betrayed the unease cloaked beneath his bravado. He shifted his weight, the soaked fabric of his coat whispering against his skin like a warning. Clara caught the scent of wet earth and fading flowers lingering in his damp clothes, strange echoes of the greenhouse they had escaped, yet those same fragrances morphed into a lingering dread as she focused on the door.

The lantern flickered again, its light growing weaker, and for a moment, the shadows appeared to swell, coiling around them like unseen hands eager to pull them back into darkness. Clara wrapped her fingers around the red ribbon tied to her wrist, feeling the rough texture against her skin, a tether to a past that seemed both reassuring and ominous. “We should—” she began, but her voice faltered as a faint sound echoed from the other side of the door.

“Did you hear that?” Jun asked, his eyes wide, searching her face for answers she didn’t have. A child’s cough? Or perhaps the creaking of the house settling? Clara wasn’t sure, but it sent an icy shiver racing down her spine, tinged with an unsettling familiarity.

“Maybe it’s nothing,” she replied, her voice a fragile thread. Her thoughts spiraled, bouncing between the image of the sealed nursery and the chilling possibility of what lurked behind it. Nora’s words echoed in her mind—no child had slept in the nursery for years. Yet, she couldn’t suppress the feeling that something was waiting for them, just out of sight.

Jun hesitated, the lantern’s glow illuminating the haphazard dust motes swirling around them, caught in a strange ballet. He raised a hand to knock, but Clara caught his wrist, her grip instinctive, urgent. “Wait.”

“What if we’re too late?” he murmured, his gaze darting back to the door, as if it might suddenly yield to their curiosity.

Clara swallowed hard, the weight of her own uncertainty pressing down on her chest. She could feel the cold air seeping through the cracks around the door, a breath from the room beyond, and it coiled around her heart like a serpent. “What if we’re not?” she replied, her voice steadier than she felt. “What if we’re meant to find out?”

Jun squinted, his brow furrowing. “But… they’re leading away from the door.”

“Or towards something,” Clara countered, her heart racing, a strange thrill igniting her senses. The air thickened with tension as they exchanged glances, unspoken fears passing between them like a current.
