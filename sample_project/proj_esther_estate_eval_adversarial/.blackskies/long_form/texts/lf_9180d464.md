Clara's breath hitched as they stepped deeper into the dim corridor, the air thick with the scent of damp earth and mildew. The cracked brass lantern flickered in Jun's grip, casting uneven shadows that danced along the peeling walls, revealing the outlines of faded wallpaper—a floral pattern long since forgotten. She could feel the chill creeping through her soaked clothes, a reminder of the greenhouse's relentless rain, but it was the cold touch of fear that gripped her more tightly.

"What was that?" Jun's voice trembled, breaking the oppressive silence, his eyes darting to the shadowy corners, searching for something unseen. Clara's heart raced, each thud echoing in her ears as she forced herself to focus. It was just the wind, she told herself, though the unsettling echo of a child’s cough still lingered in her mind, a ghostly whisper that only Jun had heard.

"I don’t know," she replied, her tone sharper than intended, fingers tightening around the red ribbon tied to her wrist. The key dangled there, a small weight that felt oddly reassuring yet suffocating at the same time. Why couldn’t she remember tying it tighter? Each movement of the chain rattling against the door sent a shiver down her spine, as if something—or someone—was testing their resolve from the other side.

Mud squelched beneath their feet, new prints appearing ahead like a breadcrumb trail leading them deeper into uncertainty. Clara glanced at Jun, his brow furrowed, soaked hair clinging to his forehead, and she saw the fear pooling in his dark eyes. She wanted to reach out, to comfort him, but the tension coiled between them, thick and electric, left her feeling more like a stranger than a friend.

“Clara,” he whispered, the lantern flickering again as if in response to his unease. “We shouldn’t be here.”
