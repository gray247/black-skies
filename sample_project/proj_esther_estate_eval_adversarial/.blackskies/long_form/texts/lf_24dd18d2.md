The lantern flickered uncertainly in Jun’s hand, its weak beam casting elongated shadows that danced against the damp stone walls of the corridor. Clara could feel the chill of the air creeping in around them, as if the very walls were exhaling a breath of cold dread. She pulled her coat tighter around her, the fabric slightly clammy against her skin, a reminder of the downpour they had just escaped. The faint scent of wet earth lingered in her nostrils, mingling with the metallic tang of rust and something else—a hint of decay that gnawed at her resolve.

“Did you see that?” Jun's voice trembled, his eyes wide, reflecting more than just fear. There was a desperation in his gaze, and Clara felt the weight of it settle in the pit of her stomach. She followed his line of sight to the door at the end of the passage, its rusty chain rattling softly, an unsettling echo of movement from the other side.

“No,” she replied too quickly, her voice sharper than intended, the words slicing through the heavy silence. The truth was she had felt something—a faint tremor of presence, as if the house itself were listening. She forced her gaze away, focusing instead on the muddy prints that marred the stone floor, trailing ahead of them. They were fresh, too fresh, and Clara’s heart quickened as she imagined the owner of those footsteps lurking just out of view.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice barely above a whisper. “What if someone’s in here with us?”
