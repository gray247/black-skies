Clara pressed her back against the damp wall of the service corridor, the cold seeping through her coat, mingling with the uneven rhythm of her heartbeat. The lantern flickered in Jun’s grip, casting wavering shadows that danced across the peeling wallpaper, each pulse of light revealing the remnants of a forgotten time. Muddy footprints snaked ahead, leading deeper into the corridor, and with every step Clara could almost feel the weight of unseen eyes watching, waiting.

“Do you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice barely more than a breath, the tremor in it betraying his resolve. Clara held her breath, straining against the silence that settled like a thick fog around them, but only the distant drip of water from the greenhouse roof interrupted the stillness. She shook her head, forcing a calmness into her voice that felt as thin as the air between them. “It’s just the wind, Jun. Let’s keep moving.”

The chain rattled again, a sharp metallic clink echoing from the door behind them, and Clara’s stomach dropped. The sound seemed to curl around her, tight and unsettling, as if the corridor itself were alive, testing the strength of its own barriers. She glanced back, her grip tightening around the cracked ceramic fox nestled in her pocket, its jagged edges digging into her palm like an anchor.

Jun took a step forward, his soaked coat clinging to him, the fabric heavy with the memories of rain-soaked leaves and the chaotic dance of yesterday’s storm. “We shouldn’t be here,” he said, urgency creeping into his tone. “What if—”
