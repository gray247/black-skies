Clara pressed her palm against the nursery door, feeling the grain of the wood beneath her fingertips, the coolness radiating through the fragile fabric of her dress. The brass lantern sputtered in Jun's grasp, its feeble light casting trembling shadows across the door’s surface, where the paint was chipped and peeling like old memories refusing to fade. She could hear the faintest echoes of the house settling, a sigh that seemed to resonate with her own trepidation.

“Do you think...” Jun began, his voice low, laced with an uncertainty that mirrored her own. He glanced down the dim corridor behind them, as if expecting to see something lurking in the half-light. “Do you think it’s really locked? Or just... stuck?”

Clara swallowed hard, her throat dry as she recalled the weight of the nursery key around her wrist, the way it felt like a promise and a curse all at once. “It’s locked.” She hoped it was. Hope was all she had left, stitched together with frayed threads of memory and the unmistakable sense that they were being watched. “Nora said it hasn’t been used in years.”

Jun’s brow furrowed, the lantern flickering again, its light dimming as if in response to their doubts. “But what if something—” He hesitated, the unspoken word hanging between them like a specter. “What if something is in there?”

A shiver raced down Clara’s spine, and she tightened her grip on the ceramic fox nestled in her pocket. Its chipped glaze felt warm against her skin, a strange comfort amidst the unsettling atmosphere. “We can’t stay out here forever,” she murmured, glancing at the door, the way it loomed over them, solid and unyielding.

“Maybe we should try the other door,” Jun suggested, shifting on his feet, the wet fabric of his coat slapping against his legs. But Clara shook her head, a sudden resolve pushing her forward.

“Maybe it’s time we stopped running from it.” She stepped closer to the door, the lantern’s light illuminating the dust motes swirling in the air, caught in a dance that felt almost alive. “What if the cough we heard... what if it’s asking for help?”

Jun’s eyes widened slightly, the shadows playing tricks on his face, but Clara could see the flicker of understanding dawn there too. “If that’s true, then we need to see what’s in there, Clara.” His voice was steady, but the tremor in his hands betrayed his fear.

With a deep breath, Clara reached for the key, its smooth surface glinting in the dim light. The ribbon felt taut against her wrist, a reminder of the bond she had forged with this strange place and its secrets. “Just hold the lantern steady,” she instructed, her voice firm despite the unease twisting in her stomach.

As she inserted the key into the lock, the metallic click echoed in the silence, a sound that reverberated through the air like a thunderclap. Clara hesitated, her heart racing as she turned the key. Would the door open to reveal something forgotten, or would it unleash the darkness that had lingered in their minds?

The latch caught, and she pushed against the door, feeling it yield slightly. A rush of stale air poured out, thick with the scent of dust and something faintly sweet, almost like blooming flowers in a forgotten garden. Clara squinted into the dimness beyond, heart pounding, as if the house itself was waiting to see what they would do next.

“Are you ready?” Jun’s voice was barely a whisper, his breath hitching as he leaned closer, the lantern flickering once more, casting a wavering light that danced on the threshold of the unknown.
