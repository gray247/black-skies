The lantern flickered feebly in Jun’s grasp, its light barely managing to push back the darkness that clung to the narrow corridor. Clara could feel the dampness of his coat seeping through the layers of her own clothing, a chill that crept beneath her skin like a whispering ghost. Each breath she took seemed to echo against the peeling walls, a reminder of the oppressive silence that enveloped them, broken only by the distant patter of rain still drumming against the greenhouse’s roof.

“Clara,” Jun murmured, his voice tight and strained. She could see the tremor in his fingers as he adjusted the lantern, the brass surface warm to his touch yet cold in its fading glow. The shadows danced around them, elongating and contracting like restless spirits, and she felt a sudden urge to reach out, to assure him that they were still together in this suffocating gloom.

“Let’s just get to the end,” she said too quickly, her words sharp like the crack of a twig underfoot. The chain rattled against the door, an insistent reminder that they weren’t alone, even if the corridor felt empty. She glanced at the door, half expecting it to burst open, though part of her wished for it—the chaos of uncertainty was preferable to this waiting.

Jun shifted beside her, his breath hitching as he caught sight of the muddy footprints that marred the polished floor ahead. “They’re fresh,” he said, his voice low. “Why would they lead this way?”

“Maybe someone else got locked in,” Clara replied, forcing herself to sound calm, though her heart thrummed like a caged bird. She tightened her grip on the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the key’s weight a constant reminder of her responsibility, and yet, in that moment, it felt more like a shackle. Why couldn’t she remember tying it tighter?
