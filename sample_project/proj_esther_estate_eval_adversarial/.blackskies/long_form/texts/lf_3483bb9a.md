Clara pressed her ear to the door, the wood cool and unyielding beneath her palm. She could still feel the warmth from the greenhouse clinging to her skin, a ghostly reminder of the chaotic excitement that had gripped her moments before. “What do you think is in there?” she whispered, the words barely breaking the thick air between them.

Jun shifted beside her, the damp fabric of his coat rustling softly as he leaned in closer, his breath fogging the air between them. “I don’t know,” he murmured, his voice a blend of curiosity and unease. The lantern flickered again, its weak light casting trembling shadows that danced across the walls, making the old wood grain writhe like serpents. Clara could feel the pulse of the house beneath her, a heartbeat echoing in the silence, as if it were a living thing, holding its breath.

She stole a glance at the cracked brass lantern in Jun’s hands, its glow dimming further, like a candle flickering in a storm. The lantern’s sputtering flame was a poor defense against the encroaching darkness, and her heart raced with the unspoken fear that they were not alone. “We shouldn’t be here,” she thought, but the thought was drowned out by another—a desperate need to know what lay beyond that door.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice broke into her reverie, a slight tremor lacing his words. She felt a shiver crawl down her spine, the chill of his tone settling into her bones. “It sounded like... a cough.”

Clara’s pulse quickened. The memory of a child’s cough echoed in her mind, but she couldn’t recall the sound clearly. She shook her head, forcing a smile that felt brittle on her lips. “You’re imagining things. There’s no one here.” But as the words left her mouth, she felt the weight of her own dishonesty pressing on her chest. The house had been whispering secrets, and she could almost hear them now, swirling around her like the damp air.

Jun's gaze flickered towards the lantern, and Clara caught the way his brow furrowed, the flickering light reflecting in his eyes. “Clara, I think we should go back,” he said, his voice low, as if speaking too loudly might awaken whatever lingered on the other side of the door. She wanted to agree, but the thought of retreating made her insides twist.

The chain on the service corridor door rattled again, a soft clink that seemed to pulse with the rhythm of her heartbeat. Clara’s fingers tightened around the parlor key on her wrist, the red ribbon cutting into her skin, a tangible reminder of her resolve. “No, we can’t just leave. We have to find out what’s in there.”

“Why?” Jun asked, his voice strained. “What if it’s something we can’t face?”

She turned to him, searching his face for the courage she needed to push forward. “What if it’s something we need to remember?” The words hung heavy in the air, laden with all they had lost, all that had been buried in silence.

He opened his mouth to argue, but the thirteenth chime of the clock echoed again, resonating through the walls, and for a moment, the house felt like it was holding its breath. Clara’s resolve wavered. She thought of the muddy prints trailing away from them, of the way the house had shifted while they had been inside, and she felt the weight of her own uncertainty.

“Okay,” Jun said finally, his voice a fragile thread. “But we do this together.”
