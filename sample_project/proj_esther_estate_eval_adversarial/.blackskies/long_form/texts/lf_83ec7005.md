Clara hesitated at the threshold, the shadows pooling inside the nursery beckoning her forward. Clara’s eyes settled on the corner where a painted wooden chest stood, its surface marred and chipped, yet it seemed to hum with memories too brittle to touch. “What do you think is in there?” she asked, trying to steady her voice against the tide of anxiety rising within her.

Clara felt a shiver travel down her spine, her fingers brushing against the ceramic fox nestled in her pocket, its chipped surface warm against her skin. The flinch of memory, a child’s laughter echoing faintly, slipped through her mind like the last rays of sun before dusk. It felt wrong, like a forgotten name whispered on the wind.

With a resolve that surprised her, she moved to the chest, crouching low to inspect the lock. The metal was cool to her touch, and as she fumbled with the ribbon key, a sound broke the stillness—a faint, rasping cough that seemed to rise from the floorboards themselves. Clara’s breath caught, her heart stuttering as she glanced at Jun, who had gone pale, his eyes searching the corners of the room, as if expecting someone to emerge from the shadows. “Did you hear that?” she asked, her voice trembling.

Jun nodded slowly, his brow furrowed. “Yeah. But it’s probably just the house settling, right?” His attempt at reassurance felt flimsy, like a paper bridge over a chasm. Clara swallowed hard, her fingers now trembling as they traced the outlines of the chest, feeling the grooves and dents of history etched into the wood. She wanted to believe him, to dismiss the sound as a trick of the mind, but deep down, something coiled in her stomach, whispering that there was more to this place than the dust and shadows.

“Do you think…” she began, but the words caught in her throat. The chain rattled again, a clink that reverberated through the corridor beyond, drawing her attention like a moth to flame.
