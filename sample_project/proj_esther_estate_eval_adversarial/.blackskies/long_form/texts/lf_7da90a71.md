Jun shifted beside her, his soaked coat clinging to him, the damp fabric marking him a ghost of the storm they had escaped. “Do you hear that?” he whispered again, his breath barely a wisp against the chilling silence.

The clock’s thirteenth chime droned on, each deep echo vibrating through the floor beneath them. Clara could feel it in her bones, a rhythmic reminder of time slipping past. She glanced up at Jun, his brows knitted together in concern, and she caught the flicker of unease in his dark eyes. “What do you think it is?” she asked, her voice barely above a whisper, as if raising it too high might shatter the fragile atmosphere.

“A child,” he replied, just as softly, but the certainty in his voice sent a chill racing down her spine. A child. The word hung between them, heavy with implications. Clara wanted to deny it, to dismiss the thought as a trick of the house, but the way Jun’s gaze flickered to the door—a door that had not been opened in years—seemed to echo her own fears.

She reached for the doorknob, the cool metal reminding her of the weight of the key tied to her wrist. The red ribbon felt reassuring, yet it also made her pulse quicken; its very presence was a reminder of what lay beyond. A nursery that had been sealed off, a childhood long forgotten. Clara’s heart thudded against her ribs, a drum of anxiety. What if the stories were true? What if the house was more alive than it appeared, hiding secrets within its walls?

“Maybe we shouldn’t,” she said, hesitating. “What if we’re wrong?” The words felt like a plea, a desperate wish to turn back, to tether herself to the familiar chaos of the living room instead of stepping into the unknown.

Jun’s lantern flickered again, the weak battery sputtering as if it too shared her trepidation. “We have to know,” he said, a resolve hardening his tone. “If there’s a child… if there’s anything in there, we can’t just leave it.”

The chain rattled suddenly from the service corridor door behind them, a metallic clank that sent Clara’s heart leaping into her throat. She turned, breath caught, her mind racing. Was someone testing it from the other side? The sensation of being watched prickled at her skin, and she instinctively stepped closer to Jun, who remained focused on the nursery door, his brow furrowed.

“Clara…” he started, but she cut him off, the rush of adrenaline sharpening her senses. “Did you hear that?”

He nodded, his expression taut. “It’s just the house settling,” he offered, but the way his voice wavered betrayed his uncertainty. The lantern’s glow dimmed further, casting long shadows that twisted and coiled like whispers around them. The air thickened, and Clara felt the weight of the house’s history pressing down, memories layered like dust on forgotten shelves.

“What if it’s not just the house?” she whispered, the words trembling on her lips. “What if it’s something else?”

The uncertainty hung there, a fragile thread that could snap at any moment. Jun’s eyes flickered to her, searching for something in her gaze, and Clara realized they were both teetering on the edge of a decision that felt monumental.
