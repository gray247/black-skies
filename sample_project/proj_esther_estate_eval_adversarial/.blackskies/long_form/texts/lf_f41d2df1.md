Clara pressed her ear against the nursery door, her heart thudding in sync with the distant echo of the clock, a persistent reminder of time slipping through their fingers. The wood was cold, a dull thrum against her cheek, and she could almost feel the pulse of whatever lay beyond—the breath of secrets stifled by years of neglect. She glanced at Jun, whose eyes were narrowed, scanning the dim hallway as if seeking an escape. The lantern’s flicker cast long shadows, dancing erratically on the walls, and the damp scent of his coat mingled with the stale air, a heaviness that settled in her chest.

“Do you think we should?” she whispered, the words barely escaping her lips. The tremor in her voice betrayed the uncertainty that coiled in her stomach. Jun shifted, the lantern sputtering again as if it shared their anxiety. “We can’t just stand here,” he replied, his voice steady yet laced with an undercurrent of tension. “What if someone is really up there?”

She nodded, though the thought made her stomach churn. “What if it’s just the house?” The words slipped out, a feeble attempt to cloak her fear in rationality. Yet, the memory of the muddy footprints creeping through the servants' passage haunted her, each mark a whisper of something sinister lurking just out of sight. She could feel the coldness of the ceramic fox in her coat pocket, a tangible reminder of the nursery's past. It pressed against her side, a token that no longer felt like a charm but a weight.

With a shaky breath, she reached for the handle, the brass cool beneath her fingers, and she hesitated, the knot in her throat tightening. “Clara,” Jun urged, his voice softer now, almost imploring. “We have to know.”

The lantern flickered again, and she felt the air grow heavier, as if the house itself were holding its breath. “On three?” she suggested, though it felt more like a plea than a plan, her heart racing at the thought of what lay beyond that door. Jun nodded, and they fell into a rhythm—one, two, three—and she turned the handle, pushing the door open just enough to peer inside.

The nursery was cloaked in shadows, a room caught between past and present. Dust motes swirled in the faint light, and Clara could make out the outlines of forgotten toys, a rocking horse with peeling paint, a stuffed bear with one eye missing. It felt like trespassing, stepping into a memory not meant for them. “It’s empty,” she breathed, though the muffled cough from above echoed in her mind, a reminder that they were not alone.

“Are you sure?” Jun asked, his eyes darting around the room, searching for movement. The lantern dimmed further, and she could see the uncertainty etched on his face, the way his hands tightened around the handle, as if it could offer him strength. “We should—”

Suddenly, a clang echoed from the hallway, sharp and jarring, making them both jump. Clara's heart raced as she turned, her eyes wide, scanning the shadows. “What was that?” She felt a wave of dread wash over her, as if the house itself were stirring, awakening from its slumber, ready to reveal its secrets.

“Probably just the wind,” Jun said, though his voice lacked conviction. The sound had come from the chained door, a reminder that they were not the only ones prowling the halls. Clara's gaze flicked back to the nursery, the weight of the decision pressing down on her. They could leave, seal the door and forget—yet something about the room called to her, the echoes of laughter and sorrow intertwining like vines in her mind.
