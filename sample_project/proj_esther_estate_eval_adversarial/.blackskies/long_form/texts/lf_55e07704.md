The lantern’s flickering light cast jittery shadows that darted across the walls, elongating and contracting like the ghosts of memories trapped in the peeling paint. Jun’s breath was a sharp whisper beside her, the fear palpable in the tremor of his voice.

“Do you think we should go back?” he asked, his eyes darting nervously as he clutched the lantern tighter, its brass surface slick and cold against his palm. Clara felt a similar chill, but it was more than the damp air; it was a creeping dread that settled beneath her skin, a heaviness that made her want to turn and run, yet kept her rooted in place.

“No,” she replied, a bit too quickly, her voice biting the air between them. “We can’t. We have to find out what’s happening.” The words tumbled out, more an assertion than a belief, but she didn’t dare let herself falter. The chain rattled against the door behind them, faint but insistent, echoing the unease that skittered along her spine.

The passage narrowed, the walls closing in as if they were being swallowed by the house itself. Clara’s fingers brushed against the fabric of her coat pocket, feeling the chipped ceramic fox nestled there, a strange talisman from a childhood that seemed distant now. She wondered fleetingly why she had brought it, but the question was swallowed by the urgency of the moment.
