The thought of those footprints, so fresh against the floorboards, sent her imagination spiraling into dark corners. Who had come this way? And why?

“No. Not yet. We’ve come this far.” The resolve in her tone surprised her, but there was something anchoring her, a sense of purpose that felt both right and wrong. She touched the ceramic fox nestled in her pocket, feeling its cool, chipped surface under her fingers. It was a talisman, a piece of the nursery's forgotten history, but it felt heavier than that—a weight of memories pressing against her heart.

“Clara,” he said, his voice softening, “if we don’t do something, we’ll be stuck here, just waiting for something to happen.” His eyes flickered back to the door, and she could see the tension etched in his features, the uncertainty that mirrored her own.

“Maybe waiting is the best we can do,” she replied, her voice barely more than a whisper. It was a sound she hadn’t heard clearly, but something in her gut twisted at it, a reminder of all they had lost.

“Do you hear that?” Jun asked, his expression shifting from concern to alarm. She shook her head, but the truth was, she felt it more than heard it—a distant echo of something that shouldn’t be there.
