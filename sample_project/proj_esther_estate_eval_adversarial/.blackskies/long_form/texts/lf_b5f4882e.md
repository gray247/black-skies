The dim glow of the lantern flickered again, casting nervous shadows that danced erratically along the corridor walls. Clara held it out before her, the weak light sputtering like her own resolve. “I swear I heard something,” she whispered, her voice barely breaking the thick air, which felt heavy with the scent of wet earth and mildew. Jun’s eyes were wide, his expression a mix of fear and disbelief, as if he could still hear the echoes of whatever had unnerved him moments before.

“Maybe it’s just the wind,” he suggested, though the tremor in his voice betrayed him. The chain rattled at the door behind them, a sharp clink that sent a shiver racing down Clara’s spine. She turned toward it, her breath hitching as she imagined someone—or something—testing the lock from the other side. “Did you see those tracks?” she pressed, glancing back at the muddy prints trailing away from the door, leading deeper into the passage. They were fresh, and they were leading away from them.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice lowering to a near whisper. “What if someone’s in here with us?”

She shook her head, trying to dismiss the creeping dread that clawed at her throat. “There’s no one here. We need to keep moving.” Clara’s gaze flitted to the parlor key tied to her wrist, a splash of red against the damp fabric of her coat, and the thought of it offered her a strange comfort—a reminder of her resolve. “We’ll find our way out.”

Jun took a shaky breath, the lantern swaying slightly in his hand as they pressed forward. The corridor expanded and contracted like something alive, the shadows seeming to breathe around them. Clara could almost feel them curl around her ankles, whispering secrets she was not meant to hear. “You’re sure about this? About what we’re doing?”
