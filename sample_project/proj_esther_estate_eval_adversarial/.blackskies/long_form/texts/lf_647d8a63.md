“I didn’t hear anything.” Clara’s denial felt thin, stretched across the chasm of uncertainty that lay between them. Clara inserted the red ribbon key, the metal cold and unyielding in her hand. It felt wrong, a sense of dissonance vibrating through her as she turned the lock, but she pushed the thought aside. They were here; they had to know.

The door didn’t budge. Instead, it fought back, a stubborn barrier against her curiosity. Clara pressed her shoulder against it, her breath hitching as the wood groaned in response, as if it were alive. “Help me,” she said, glancing back at Jun, whose expression had hardened, eyes narrowed in concentration.

He stepped forward, placing his hands on the door beside hers, warmth radiating off him, contrasting sharply with the chill of the air. “On three,” he whispered, and their voices melded together, a fragile alliance against the unknown. “One… two… three!”

They pushed together, the door shuddering but refusing to yield, a stubborn sentinel guarding whatever lay within. “It’s not going to work,” Jun gasped, frustration lacing his tone, and Clara felt a pang of despair at the finality of his words. The house sighed around them, a deep, resonant sound that seemed to echo their defeat.

“We can’t give up,” Clara insisted, the words spilling from her like a desperate breath. The lantern flickered one last time before dimming entirely, plunging them into a suffocating darkness. “Jun,” she said, her voice trembling. “What if there’s someone in there?”

He hesitated, the air thickening with unspoken fears. “Nora said no child has slept in the nursery for years,” he reminded her, but there was a tremor in his voice that betrayed his own uncertainty.
