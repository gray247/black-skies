She could feel Jun’s presence beside her, the warmth of his shoulder brushing against hers, but the weight of the silence wrapped around them felt suffocating, as if the very walls were holding their breath.

“What if it’s not locked?” Jun’s voice broke through her thoughts, unsure and hesitant, like the lantern’s failing light. “What if it’s just... empty?”

Clara swallowed hard, her mouth dry, a bitter taste of doubt lingering on her tongue. “Nora said it’s been locked for years. She wouldn’t lie about that.” Yet the memory of Nora’s flinching gaze when she had seen the ceramic fox nestled in Clara’s pocket nagged at her, a persistent whisper of uncertainty. She glanced down at the chipped glaze peeking out from the folds of her coat, its once-vibrant colors dulled, much like her own resolve.

“I don’t like this,” Jun muttered, his voice barely above a whisper, as if the house itself had ears, waiting to devour their secrets. He shifted his weight, and Clara could see the tension in his shoulders, the way he flexed his fingers around the lantern’s handle, as if it were a lifeline. “Maybe we should just go back.”

“Back?” Clara’s heart raced. The thought of retreat felt like a betrayal, a failure to confront whatever lay behind that door. “We can’t. Not now.” She turned the key in the lock, a tentative twist, and the mechanism protested, rusty and stubborn. Her breath caught as she felt the click, the door shuddering slightly in its frame.

“Clara...” Jun’s warning hung in the air, heavy with unspoken fears, but she pushed the door ajar, the old wood creaking in protest, echoing like a sigh through the stillness. A rush of stale air poured out, thick with the scent of dust and something more unsettling—something sweet and rotting that curled around her senses, making her stomach churn.

The lantern gasped, flickering out, plunging them into darkness.

“Damn it,” Jun hissed, fumbling blindly in the dark, the sound of his coat rustling echoing back to them. “I can’t see anything.”

“Just hold still.” Clara’s voice was steadier than she felt. She reached into her pocket for the fox, its familiar weight a comfort against the encroaching darkness. “I’ll find the matches.” The last remnants of light had slipped away, leaving her feeling vulnerable, as if the shadows were wrapping around her, encasing her in uncertainty.

The silence stretched, and she was acutely aware of every sound—the soft patter of water dripping from Jun’s coat, the distant creak of the house settling, and then, unmistakably, a faint cough, echoing from somewhere deep within the room. It was a child’s cough, fragile and lost, and it sent a chill down her spine.

“Did you hear that?” she asked, the words escaping before she could contain them.

Jun hesitated. “What? Clara, I—”

“It sounded like a child,” she pressed, her heart racing. “From inside the nursery.”

“Clara, there are no children here,” he said, his voice strained. “Nora said they haven’t been—”

“I don’t care what Nora said!” she snapped, the heat of frustration igniting her fear. “We need to go in.”
