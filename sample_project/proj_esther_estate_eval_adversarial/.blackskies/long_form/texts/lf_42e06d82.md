The lantern flickered again, a dying ember in Jun’s grasp, casting a fitful glow that barely penetrated the thick darkness of the corridor. Clara could feel the dampness of the stone walls seeping into her skin, an unwelcome reminder of the storm outside. She glanced at Jun, his brow creased with worry, and tried to suppress the rising tide of anxiety that threatened to swell within her. “We should keep moving,” she said, her voice sharper than intended, slicing through the silence.

Jun nodded, his eyes darting toward the chained door as though it might burst open at any moment. “What if it’s—” He hesitated, swallowing hard. “What if someone’s on the other side?”

“Then we’ll just have to find another way,” Clara replied, though her heartbeat quickened at the thought. She wrapped her fingers tighter around the parlor key tucked beneath the layers of her coat, the red ribbon pressing into her skin like a warning. The sensation was grounding, yet it did little to quell the chill creeping up her spine.

With each step, the sound of their footfalls echoed softly, swallowed by the oppressive darkness, and Clara’s gaze was drawn to the muddy prints marring the corridor floor. They were fresh, leading away from the door. “Jun,” she whispered, “look.”

He leaned closer, the lantern’s dim light illuminating the trail of mud, droplets clinging to the cracks in the stone as if something had rushed past them. Clara’s breath hitched; the path was leading toward the far end of the corridor. “Should we follow them?” Jun asked, his voice strained.

“Of course we should,” she replied, more sharply than she meant to. The tension in the air was palpable, as if the walls themselves were holding their breath. “We can’t just stand here.”
