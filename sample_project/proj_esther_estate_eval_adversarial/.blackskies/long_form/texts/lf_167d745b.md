The air in the corridor felt thicker, as if it had absorbed the dampness from the greenhouse and held it close. Clara’s heart thudded in her chest, a wild metronome against the silence that pressed in from all sides. Jun shuffled beside her, the flickering light of the cracked brass lantern casting erratic shadows on the walls, each flicker a reminder of the uncertainty that loomed ahead. Water dripped from his coat, pooling at their feet, mingling with the muddy prints that seemed to lead them deeper into a darkened labyrinth.

“Do you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice breaking the stillness like a twig snapping underfoot. His eyes darted to the chained door, tension coiling in his shoulders. She could see the fear etched in his features, the way his hands trembled as he adjusted his grip on the lantern.

Clara narrowed her focus, straining to catch any sound that might answer his question. The corridor was a tomb, the air scented with mildew and wet wood, yet beneath it all, there was a muffled noise, a faint cough that echoed off the walls. Her skin prickled as she turned towards the source, but the sound slipped away like smoke, leaving only an unsettling quiet.

“Nothing,” she replied too sharply, a brittle edge to her tone. She felt the ribbon on her wrist tug slightly as she gestured, the key nestled against her skin, a stubborn weight that reminded her of the urgency of their escape. “We should keep moving.”

Jun hesitated, glancing at the door as if expecting it to open, to reveal whatever lurked on the other side. The chain rattled softly, a whisper of movement that sent a shiver down Clara’s spine.
