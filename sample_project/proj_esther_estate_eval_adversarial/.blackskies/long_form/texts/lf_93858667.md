She could feel the erratic pulse of her heartbeat in her throat, a drum echoing against the silence that smothered them. The lantern in Jun’s hand sputtered, casting a flickering glow that danced with shadows, making the crumbling plaster seem alive, as if it might shift and breathe at any moment.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely more than a whisper, his eyes wide and glistening, the soaked fabric of his coat clinging to him like a second skin. Clara shook her head, though the echo of a child’s cough still hung in her mind, a ghostly whisper that crept in from the sealed upper floor. The thought made her stomach churn, the sensation slick and uncomfortable, rising to her throat where her pulse quickened.

“Just the wind,” she said, the words tumbling out sharper than intended, but she couldn’t afford to show him how deeply unsettled she felt. The lantern flickered again, its light dimming further, and she stepped forward, her feet squelching against the mud that seemed to gather at their heels, a trail leading deeper into the gloom. The prints ahead of them were unmistakably fresh, dark and muddy against the stone floor, as though someone else had slipped through just moments before.
