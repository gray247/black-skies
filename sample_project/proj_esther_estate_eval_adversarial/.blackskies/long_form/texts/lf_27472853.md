Clara's breath caught as she pressed her palm against the rough wood of the door, feeling the grain shift under her fingertips, its texture a stark contrast to the smoothness of the key tied to her wrist. The dim glow of the lantern flickered, casting shadows that twisted and contorted, illuminating the edges of her mounting dread. Each sputter of light seemed to tease at the darkness beyond, urging her to confront whatever lay hidden in the nursery. Jun shifted beside her, his soaked coat clinging to his frame, water droplets slipping down the collar like tears that refused to dry.

“Do you think someone’s up there?” he asked, his voice low and edged with a tension that mirrored her own. She turned to him, catching the uncertainty in his eyes, a reflection of her own swirling thoughts. They both felt it—the weight of the house, its secrets seeping through the walls, wrapping around them like a shroud.

“I don’t know,” Clara whispered, her voice barely a breath. The air felt thick, heavy with the scent of damp wood and something else, something stale, as if the room itself held its breath, waiting. She focused on the key, its red ribbon a vivid contrast against the pale skin of her wrist, a reminder of her purpose, yet the memory of tying it tighter slipped through her mind like sand through fingers. Why couldn’t she recall the moment?

The clatter of the chain above rattled her thoughts, each clink a reminder that they were not alone in this vast, echoing house. Clara stepped back instinctively, her heel catching on the edge of the threshold. She could almost hear a voice among the creaks and sighs of the settling structure, a soft, insistent cough that danced on the edge of her consciousness. Jun’s gaze darted to the ceiling, a fleeting look of fear passing over his features, and she wondered if he had heard what she had not.

“Maybe we should just go,” he suggested, his voice softer now, a plea laced with the uncertainty of youth. “We could find Nora—or anyone. This place…” He hesitated, swallowing hard as the lantern flickered again, dimming to a near-ghostly glow. “It feels wrong. Like it’s holding its breath.”

Clara’s fingers curled around the key, the cool metal grounding her, yet her heart raced with the pulse of unspoken fears. “If someone is up there,” she said, drawing the words from the depths of her resolve, “we can’t leave them.” The thought of abandoning whatever—or whoever—might be in the nursery felt like a betrayal, a thread of guilt weaving through her.

But as she stepped forward, the door loomed larger, its surface a patchwork of scars and knots, each imperfection a story, a memory trapped in wood. She could almost feel the house leaning in, pressing against her back, urging her to reconsider. The muddy prints they had stumbled upon felt alive, as though they were still shifting in the shadows, a testimony to the uncertainty that gnawed at her insides.

“What if it’s not a child?” Jun’s voice broke through her spiraling thoughts, a quiver of doubt threading through his words. “What if it’s—”

“Stop.” Clara turned sharply, the urgency in her voice surprising even herself. “We can’t think like that. We need to see.”

The lantern flickered one last time before surrendering to darkness, leaving them in a heavy, suffocating silence. Clara felt the weight of the moment settle around them, a palpable tension that twisted her stomach into knots. She could hear the soft rustle of fabric as Jun shifted beside her, the tension in his posture mirroring her own.
