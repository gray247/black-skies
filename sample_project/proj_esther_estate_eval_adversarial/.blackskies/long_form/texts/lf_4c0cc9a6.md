Clara walked beside him, her brow furrowed, and he could sense the tension thrumming between them.

"What if the prints lead somewhere we don’t want to go?" she murmured, glancing back toward the dining room where Nora had cornered them with her questions, her too-bright laughter echoing unsettlingly. "It’s just mud—probably from the storm," Jun replied, though his own voice lacked conviction. He felt the weight of Clara’s gaze, the way her eyes searched his, probing for something he couldn’t quite offer. He tried to shake off the feeling that the house, with its creaking beams and shifting shadows, was watching them too, its secrets layered like dust on the furniture.

“Jun,” she whispered, biting her lip, “Did you hear that?”

He nodded, but the sound of his own heart pounding drowned out the whispers from beyond the chain. “Could be the wind,” he said, though the words felt hollow. “Or… something else.” The lantern sputtered again, casting a weak light that danced across the door, illuminating the chipped edges of the nursery’s frame, the thick layer of dust that coated the handle.
