Clara stepped closer to the door, her heart thudding against her ribs as she reached out, fingertips brushing the cold metal of the chain. The rattling persisted, a low, whispered call that sent shivers spiraling down her arms, and she tried to ignore the way her breath quickened. “I—I think it’s solid,” she murmured, her voice barely rising above the echoes of the house settling around them. Jun’s lantern threw a weak glow, illuminating the dust motes swirling in the stagnant air, but it felt like a fragile barrier against the darkness that pressed in.

Jun shifted beside her, the dampness of his coat mixing with the cool air, and she caught the scent of wet earth and the faint tang of mildew. “We should check the nursery,” he said, his eyes scanning the shadows. The way he held the lantern, fingers wrapped tight around its cracked brass, made her acutely aware of the vulnerability they were wrapped in. “If there’s someone in here…” His words trailed off, a question left hanging between them, heavy with unspoken fears.

Clara turned her gaze back to the door, feeling the weight of the chained latch as if it were a living thing. “Do you think there could be?” she asked, fighting the urge to glance over her shoulder, to catch a glimpse of whatever might be lurking just out of sight. The cough from upstairs echoed in her mind, a child’s cough, but she had to remind herself that the nursery had been empty for years, or so they had been told. The chipped ceramic fox in her pocket felt heavy, its presence both comforting and foreboding, as if it held secrets she wasn’t yet ready to uncover.

“Maybe it’s just a trick of the house,” Jun suggested, though his voice wavered slightly, and Clara could see the uncertainty etched in the lines of his face. “Like it’s just playing with us.” He shifted his weight, glancing down the corridor before looking back at her, his expression turning earnest. “You heard that cough, didn’t you?”

Clara hesitated, her heart constricting. “No, I—” She caught herself, remembering the way she had cut him off earlier. “I mean, I didn’t hear anything.” But the truth felt slippery, like trying to grasp smoke, and she could feel the tension between them tightening. The house creaked above, an ominous sound that sent a tremor through her.
