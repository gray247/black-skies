Clara pressed her back against the damp wall of the service corridor, the faint light from Jun’s sputtering lantern casting long shadows that flickered like restless spirits across the peeling wallpaper. The air was thick with the scent of mildew and something more acrid, a hint of burnt earth that clung to the edges of her throat. She could feel the pulse of her heart quicken, each thud echoing in the stillness, almost drowning out the distant sound of water dripping somewhere nearby.

“Did you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice low and strained, the cracked brass lantern trembling in his grip. He pushed his soaked hair back from his forehead, revealing wide, dark eyes that seemed even more haunted by the dimness. Clara nodded, though her throat felt tight, the sound of a child’s cough still echoing faintly in her mind. It was so far away, yet it wrapped around her like a chill, tightening its hold.

With each shuffling step, Clara felt the weight of her pocket pressing against her thigh, the chipped ceramic fox nestled inside her coat like a secret she was not ready to share. She glanced at Jun, whose coat clung to him, dark and heavy, a remnant of their hurried escape from the greenhouse. The lantern flickered again, the light dimming further, and for a moment, she resented the way it illuminated the path ahead, revealing muddy footprints that led deeper into the corridor, a stark contrast to the clean slate of their footprints behind them.

“Clara,” Jun’s voice broke through her thoughts, sharp and urgent. “We need to go back, now.”

“No,” she snapped, the word escaping her lips before she could rein it in. The bite of her tone surprised her, but the fear swirling in the pit of her stomach demanded action, not retreat. “We can’t just turn back without knowing. What if—”
