The corridor seemed to narrow as Clara and Jun pressed on, the dim glow of the lantern sputtering like a heartbeat struggling to keep pace with their fear. Each step forward was a whisper of mud beneath their feet, squelching and sucking at their boots, as if the ground itself wanted to pull them into its dank embrace. Clara’s fingers brushed the cool metal of the parlor key, the red ribbon trailing off her wrist like a bloodied thread. She fought the urge to yank it free, to toss it away, but the thought of losing it felt like losing a lifeline in the encroaching darkness.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely above a murmur, fear lacing his words as he paused, the lantern’s light catching the glisten of his eyes. Clara felt the tension between them thickening, like the air before a storm. She shook her head, forcing a smile that didn’t quite reach her eyes. “It’s just the wind,” she said too quickly, her voice sharper than she intended. Inside, a tangle of doubt twisted her stomach—what could possibly make that sound?

They moved further, the chains rattling against the door behind them as though something on the other side was testing its bonds. Clara glanced back, the flickering light casting shadows that danced along the walls, twisting into grotesque shapes. “We should keep going,” she urged, her voice steadier than she felt. The echo of a child’s cough slipped through her mind uninvited, a chilling reminder of the sealed upper floor, but she pushed it away, focusing instead on the mud-slicked floor ahead of them.
