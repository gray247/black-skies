The lantern’s flickering light cast ghostly patterns, illuminating the sweat on his brow, and the way his hands trembled around the handle. “We should keep moving,” she said, her voice sharper than intended, slicing through the oppressive silence. The words felt brittle, as if they might shatter under scrutiny.

“Do you really think it’s—” Jun began, but she cut him off, the urgency bubbling up within her like a pot about to boil over. “No, I don’t think it’s safe, but standing here won’t help.” She gestured toward the chained door, its heavy metal links rattling softly as if something on the other side were testing their resolve. Clara could almost feel the tension in the air, thick and electric, like the moment before a storm breaks.

With each step deeper into the passage, her senses sharpened. The musty scent of mold mingled with the sharp tang of rain-soaked wood, and she could almost taste the dampness clinging to her lips. A low rumble of thunder echoed in the distance, a reminder of the tempest still raging outside, and for a fleeting moment, she wished to be anywhere but here, in this cramped, shadowy corridor.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice quivered, pulling her from her reverie. She shook her head, but the truth was, she had heard something—a faint cough, muffled and distant, echoing from the sealed upper floor.
