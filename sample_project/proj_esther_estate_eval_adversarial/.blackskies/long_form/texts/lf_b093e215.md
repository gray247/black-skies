“Of course I did,” she snapped, irritation flaring for a moment, but it extinguished just as quickly when she saw the strain on his face. This was not the time to unravel. “We need to focus.” She stepped closer to the nursery door, the faded paint peeling in strands, like old skin. “We’ve come this far.”

“Right,” he said, and there was a determination in his voice, but it was undercut by a wavering certainty. He lifted the lantern again, the light flickering weakly, and Clara felt the weight of the moment settle heavily on her shoulders. She reached for the key, its surface cold against her fingertips, and tried to shake off the sense of foreboding that clung to her.

The clock in the hallway began to strike, the sound echoing like a warning bell, counting down the seconds until something inevitable would happen. “Thirteen,” she muttered, a chill racing through her. “Why thirteen?”

“Maybe it’s just broken,” Jun offered, but even he sounded unconvinced. The uncertainty sank like a stone in the pit of her stomach, as if the very house was alive, shifting under their feet, aware of their every thought.
