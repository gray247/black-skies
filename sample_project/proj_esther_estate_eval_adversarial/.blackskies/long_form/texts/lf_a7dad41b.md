The air in the corridor was thick with the musty scent of damp wood and something else, something sweet and rotting that made Clara’s stomach twist. She clutched the lantern tighter, its flickering light barely cutting through the dark, but it was all they had. Jun stood a few paces ahead, the soaked fabric of his coat clinging to him like a second skin, the water pooling at his feet as he shifted nervously. His eyes darted toward the chained door, the sound of metal rattling mingling with the echoes of their own breaths.

“Clara,” he whispered, his voice barely rising above the sound of the rain that still drummed against the greenhouse roof, “do you hear that?”

She forced herself to listen, straining to catch the whisper of any sound beyond their own. The rain had become a relentless percussion, but it was the dread lurking behind it that made her heart race. “It’s just the storm,” she replied too quickly, the sharpness of her tone surprising even herself. The weight of the parlor key, tied to her wrist with that stubborn red ribbon, felt heavier, as if it were a chain anchoring her to this moment of uncertainty.

But Jun wasn’t convinced. “No, it’s not just the storm,” he insisted, eyes wide, reflecting the dim light of the lantern. “I thought I heard…” He trailed off, fear tightening the corners of his mouth.

Clara swallowed hard, trying to shake the image of a shadow lurking just beyond the weak glow. “We need to keep moving,” she said, stepping forward, her muddy boots squelching against the floor. She felt the ceramic fox in her pocket shift, a reminder of childhood innocence, but it was a hollow comfort now.
