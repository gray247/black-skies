Clara’s fingers tightened around the parlor key, the red ribbon digging into her wrist as she took a step closer to the nursery door. Jun shifted beside her, his soaked coat clinging to him like a second skin, the lantern’s flickering light casting erratic shadows that seemed to mock their hesitation. “We have to open it,” he urged, his voice barely above a whisper, a mixture of determination and dread threading through his words.

She nodded slowly, her breath hitching as the air thickened with an unshakable tension. The heavy door loomed before them, old wood splintered and darkened with age, as if it had been waiting for them to return. What lay beyond it felt like a weight pressing against her chest, the familiar pain of grief intermingled with an inexplicable anxiety. “You hear that?” Jun’s voice broke through her thoughts, a tremor of urgency there.

Clara strained her ears, but the house was quiet now, the distant sounds of settling wood and the gentle creak of the floorboards above them a mere backdrop to the silence. “I don’t hear anything,” she said slowly, the words feeling wrong in her mouth. But then, just beneath the surface of that silence, she thought she caught a faint echo—a cough, perhaps. But it faded quickly, leaving only the pulse of her own heartbeat.

Jun’s brow furrowed as he leaned closer to the door, the lantern’s light sputtering again, casting a pale glow that danced like fireflies. “We have to know,” he insisted, glancing back at her, his eyes pleading. It struck Clara then, the way his gaze flickered to the chipped ceramic fox peeking from her coat pocket. She felt a surge of heat rise to her cheeks, a rush of guilt for bringing it along. It was a relic of a past neither of them could escape, and she had almost forgotten it was there until now, until Jun’s eyes betrayed a hint of recognition.

“Maybe we should just go back,” she suggested, her voice wavering. The thought of retreat felt like a betrayal, yet the house seemed to pulse with a life of its own, its dark corners whispering secrets she was not ready to uncover.

“No,” Jun replied, stepping back from the door, the lantern casting a long shadow behind him. “We can’t just run away. What if—” He broke off, the unspoken fear hanging heavily between them. What if they weren’t alone?

The muted chain rattled again, more pronounced this time, reverberating through the corridor as if someone were testing it from the other side. Clara’s heart raced, a surge of adrenaline flooding her veins, and she glanced at Jun, who had gone pale. “Did you hear that?”

“I did,” he replied, his voice barely a whisper. “It’s coming from the service corridor.”

Clara’s breath quickened. “But it shouldn’t be. The chain is—”

“Let’s just open the door,” Jun interrupted, a note of urgency creeping into his voice. “We can’t stay here.”

With a shaky hand, Clara reached for the doorknob, cold and unyielding beneath her palm. The lantern flickered again, its last defiant gasp, and in that moment, she felt something shift in the air—a static charge, a promise of revelation. The murmur of the house seemed to swell around them, urging her forward, as if it hungered for the truth buried within these walls.
