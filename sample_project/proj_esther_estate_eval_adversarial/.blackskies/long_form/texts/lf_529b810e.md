Clara pressed on through the corridor, her breath shallow, feeling the weight of the air compressing around her like a vice. The lantern’s flickering light danced erratically, illuminating the peeling wallpaper that seemed to twist and warp in the shadows, each pattern morphing into specters of her unease. The sound of Jun’s damp footsteps echoed beside her, the squelch of his soaked shoes mixing with the distant drip of water from the greenhouse, a reminder of the tempest that had soaked them to the bone.

“Do you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled, barely more than a whisper, as if the walls themselves might be eavesdropping. Clara’s heart quickened as she strained to listen, her chest tightening. She wanted to tell him it was nothing, that the house was just settling, but the echo of a child’s cough lingered in her mind, a sound she couldn’t quite place. It had pierced the stillness, haunting and forlorn, but she hadn’t heard it—hadn’t wanted to hear it.

“Just the wind,” she said, forcing a steadiness into her tone, though the sharpness felt like a brittle facade. She caught a glimpse of Jun’s face, pale and drawn, shadows pooling under his eyes like bruises. The lantern sputtered again, casting a fleeting beam that caught a reflection of something in the corner—muddy prints trailing ahead of them, stark against the worn wooden floor, leading deeper into the passage.

“Clara…” His voice wavered, and she felt a surge of annoyance, a desperate need to reassure him slipping through her fingers like water. “What if someone’s in here? What if—”
