The lantern flickered in Jun's trembling hand, its dim light casting wobbly shadows that danced along the damp stone walls of the corridor. Clara felt the weight of silence pressing against her ears, a tightness that threatened to drown out the echo of their own heartbeats. The air was thick with the scent of earth and decay, the mustiness of old wood mingling with something more acrid, something that clawed at the back of her throat. She slid her fingers along the cool surface of the wall, her skin prickling with an awareness that something was not just hidden but lurking.

“Do you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, shaking like a brittle leaf. His coat, still soaked from the greenhouse rain, clung to him, the fabric heavy and slick. Clara jerked her head, trying to catch any sound that might confirm his fear. “It’s coming from upstairs,” he added, his gaze darting to the ceiling as if expecting a specter to drop through the floorboards.

Clara turned her eyes back to the chained door, the harsh metal glinting under the lantern’s weak glow. It rattled softly, a delicate yet insistent sound, almost as if someone were testing it from the other side. She swallowed hard, the knot in her stomach tightening as she thought of the locked rooms above them. “It’s probably just the wind,” she said too quickly, her voice sharp, cutting through the air like a knife. She tried to convince herself as much as him, but the tremor in her tone betrayed her.

As they moved deeper into the passage, muddy footprints appeared ahead, fresh and vivid against the cool stone.
