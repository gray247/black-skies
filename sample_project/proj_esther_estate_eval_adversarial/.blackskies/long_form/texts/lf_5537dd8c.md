The air in the corridor felt thick, almost viscous, as Clara and Jun stepped deeper into the dimness. The weak light from the cracked brass lantern flickered uncertainly, casting jittery shadows on the peeling walls. Clara glanced at Jun, whose coat was still damp from their hasty exit from the greenhouse, the fabric clinging uncomfortably to his skin. He adjusted the lantern, its dim glow making his features look gaunt, like a ghost emerging from the past.

“Did you hear that?” he whispered, voice low and taut. His eyes darted toward the far end of the corridor where the shadows pooled, stretching and receding like dark tides.

Clara swallowed hard, a lump lodged in her throat. “It’s just the wind,” she replied too quickly, the sharpness of her tone surprising even herself. The words felt brittle in the thick silence, as if they could shatter at any moment. Yet, even as she spoke, her heart raced, the sense of being watched prickling at the back of her neck. She clutched the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key still dangling from it, a reminder of the door they had just opened. It rustled against her skin, its weight both comforting and burdensome.

“More like a child,” Jun murmured, his gaze fixed on the door at the end of the passage, the chain rattling softly as though someone were testing it from the other side.
