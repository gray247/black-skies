Clara’s breath came in shallow bursts, the damp air of the corridor clinging to her skin like a second layer. She squinted into the flickering light of Jun's lantern, its weak beam barely cutting through the gloom. The muddy footprints, strange and unsettling, swirled ahead of them, as if beckoning her deeper into an unseen horror. Each step she took felt like wading through thick, heavy water.

“What if someone’s in there?” Jun’s voice trembled, breaking the silence that pressed against them. His eyes darted toward the chained door as if expecting it to swing open at any moment. Clara could feel the weight of his fear infecting her own resolve, stirring something dark within her. She pressed her lips together, trying to stifle the rising tide of panic.

“We have to keep going,” she said, her voice sharper than she intended, cutting through the tension like the crack of a whip. The words tasted bitter in her mouth, but she refused to show weakness. The red ribbon around her wrist felt tighter, and she could almost hear the whispers of the key hidden beneath the folds of her coat.

The chain rattled softly behind them, a sound that sent ice through her veins. Clara turned her head, eyes widening as she tried to gauge what lay just beyond the door. “Did you hear that?” she whispered, her heart pounding in her chest like a drum.

Jun nodded, his expression a mix of confusion and dread. “Maybe it’s just the wind?” His attempt at reassurance felt hollow, swallowed by the oppressive silence of the corridor. Clara could see the way his hands clenched around the lantern, knuckles turning white. The cracked brass seemed to reflect the flickering uncertainty in his eyes.
