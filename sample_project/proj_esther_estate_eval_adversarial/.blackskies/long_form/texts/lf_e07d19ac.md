The air in the corridor was thick with the scent of damp earth, an unsettling reminder of the rain that had soaked Jun’s coat through. He stood close to Clara, the cracked brass lantern between them flickering erratically, casting shadows that danced like restless spirits on the peeling wallpaper. With each sputter, Clara felt the weight of the room settle in around them, as if the walls were listening, holding their breath.

“Do you think anyone’s still in here?” Jun’s voice trembled, a soft echo against the silence. He shifted, mud squelching beneath his feet, and glanced back at the chained door. The chain rattled again, a faint clink that sent a shiver down Clara’s spine.

“I don’t know,” she replied too quickly, her heart hammering against her ribs. She tugged at the red ribbon around her wrist, the key feeling heavy against her skin. She had never tied it this tight—had she? The thought slipped from her like water through fingers. “We should—”

“Clara.” Jun’s voice cut through her racing thoughts. His eyes were wide, reflecting the lantern’s weak light as if he were searching for something she could not see. “There are footprints. They’re heading away from us.”

She followed his gaze, her breath hitching as she spotted the muddy prints leading deeper into the corridor, the soles of shoes pressed firmly into the ground. “Maybe it’s just someone who… went to check on the storm or something.” But even as she spoke, a knot twisted in her stomach. The rain had come down in sheets, but the footprints looked fresh, as if whoever had made them had just passed through, leaving their mark in the mud.
