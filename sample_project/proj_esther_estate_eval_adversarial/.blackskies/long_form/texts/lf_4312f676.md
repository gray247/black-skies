Clara could feel the weight of the parlor key against her wrist, the red ribbon biting into her skin, a constant reminder of its presence. As they moved deeper into the corridor, the flickering light from Jun’s brass lantern cast wavering shadows on the peeling wallpaper, a ghostly dance that only heightened the sense of unease swelling between them.

“Did you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice barely above a tremor. His coat clung to him, heavy with the remnants of the rain, the fabric darkened and slick. The lantern sputtered again, the glow dimming to a mere flicker, and Clara felt a rush of anxiety knotting her stomach. “Just the wind,” she replied too briskly, her own bravado feeling brittle, like glass poised to shatter. Muddy prints tracked across the floor ahead of them, trailing into the dark, and Clara instinctively tightened her grip on the key ribbon.
