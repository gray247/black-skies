The damp air clung to Clara’s skin like a second layer, heavy and suffocating, as she and Jun stepped cautiously into the service corridor. The dim light from the cracked brass lantern flickered, casting dancing shadows that seemed to stretch and warp along the narrow walls. Each step they took sent faint echoes cascading through the cramped space, a reminder of how utterly alone they were—even the house felt silent, as if it held its breath. Clara’s fingers traced the red ribbon around her wrist, the soft fabric a distracting comfort against the clammy chill that seeped into her bones.

Jun’s coat was still soaked from their hasty exit from the greenhouse, droplets clinging to the fabric like the remnants of some forgotten storm. He shivered, his breath visible in the cool air, and for a moment, Clara felt a swell of frustration. “You’ll catch cold,” she snapped, more harshly than she intended, her voice bouncing back from the walls.

“I’m fine,” he replied, though his voice trembled, and the lantern sputtered again, casting the corridor into a deeper twilight. The faint scent of mildew mixed with something else, something earthy and unsettling, as Clara’s eyes darted toward the far end of the passage.

“Look,” she whispered, pointing to the muddy footprints that marred the polished floor ahead of them. They led away from the chained door, fresh impressions in the dust, as if someone had come from beyond the threshold rather than retreated. “Someone else has been here.”

Jun’s gaze followed hers, and his expression twisted into one of fear. “What if they’re still here?” he murmured, tightening his grip on the lantern. It flickered again, the light dimming further, leaving only a weak glow that seemed to mock their predicament.
