Clara pressed her palm against the nursery door, the wood cool and rough beneath her fingers. The lantern flickered weakly in Jun’s grasp, casting elongated shadows that danced along the hallway walls, teasing the edges of her anxiety. She could feel the weight of the key on her wrist, the red ribbon taut against her skin, a tether to the world outside this suffocating space. Jun shifted beside her, the damp fabric of his coat clinging to his frame, a reminder of the rain-soaked greenhouse and the secrets it harbored.

“What do you think’s inside?” his voice was low, almost a whisper, but it carried an edge of urgency that cracked the silence surrounding them.

“I don’t know,” she replied, her throat tight. The cough she had not heard echoed in her mind, a ghostly reminder of what they were up against. “Nora said no one’s been in here for years.” The words felt like a lie, a futile reassurance. The memory of the muddy prints, their origin still a mystery, gnawed at her.

Jun glanced at the door, his gaze lingering on the paint-chipped edges. “Then why does it feel like we’re not alone?”

He was right. A chill coursed through her, the air thick with unspoken tension. The house creaked above them as if in agreement, settling into its own conflicted history. Clara tightened her grip around the key, the metal cool against her palm. “We have to see,” she insisted, a surge of defiance rising within her.

“Are you sure?”

His doubt was palpable, a thread of concern woven through his words. She could see the flicker of worry in his eyes, the way he shifted his weight, uncertain.

“Do you want to go back?” she countered, the sharpness of the question caught between challenge and vulnerability. She didn’t want to retreat, not now, not when they were so close to uncovering whatever lay behind this door. But his hesitation twisted something in her stomach.

“No,” he said quickly, almost too quickly, as if the idea alone sent a shiver down his spine. “I just—” He paused, the lantern dimming further, and for a moment, they were enveloped in uncertain darkness.

“Just what?”

“I heard a cough,” he admitted, his voice barely above a murmur.

The admission hung between them, heavy and charged. Clara’s breath caught in her throat. “But I didn’t hear it,” she blurted out, the words spilling forth before she could catch them. The truth sliced deeper than she intended. “What if it’s just the house playing tricks?”

“It didn’t sound like a trick,” he said, the weight of his certainty pressing against her. “It was real.”

The lantern sputtered, illuminating the edges of his face, revealing the tension etched in his brow. Clara turned back to the door, the key trembling against her wrist as she fought to steady herself. “We’ll find out,” she said, her own voice steadier than she felt. She lifted the key, testing the lock, its surface smooth and unyielding beneath her fingers.

As she fumbled with it, the air shifted, thickening around them. A sound echoed from above, a faint scraping, like nails on wood, and Clara froze, heart hammering against her ribs.

“Did you hear that?” she whispered, eyes wide, searching Jun’s face for answers he didn’t have.

He nodded slowly, the lantern’s light faltering again, as if the very battery knew the weight of their fear. “It sounds like it’s coming from the landing.”
