The lantern flickered again, its feeble glow casting long shadows that danced across the floor like specters. She could feel the pulse of the house beneath her fingers, thrumming with an energy that was both familiar and unsettling. The air was thick, heavy with dust and the distant scent of something damp—mold or rain, perhaps—clinging to the edges of her consciousness. Her heart raced, a wild drumbeat that drowned out the soft patter of rain against the windows, each drop a reminder of the storm raging outside.

“Clara,” Jun whispered, his voice low, almost reverent. He shifted beside her, his coat still slick from their earlier escapade, water pooling at his feet. “We can’t just stand here.” His gaze darted to the sealed nursery door, the chipped ceramic fox just barely visible in the pocket of her coat, its eyes gleaming like tiny lanterns in the dim light. The weight of it felt like a promise or a threat—she wasn’t sure which.

“I know,” she breathed, her voice barely above a whisper. The thought of turning away made her skin prickle. “But what if—”

“Let’s just see,” he urged, edging closer, the brass lantern casting a flickering halo around him. The light caught the worry etched into his features, the way his brows knitted together as he glanced back toward the corridor. “We’ve come this far.”

A sudden crack echoed above, sharp and jarring. Clara flinched, her fingers tightening around the key, the red ribbon rough against her skin. It felt like a tether, binding her to something she couldn’t quite grasp. “Did you hear that?” she asked, her voice trembling slightly.

Jun nodded, his eyes widening. “It sounded like…” He hesitated, swallowing hard. “Like a child.”

Her heart sank at the implication, a shiver racing down her spine, but she shook her head, pushing the thought away. “No, it’s just—”

“Just what? Just a house settling?” His voice rose, tinged with frustration. “Clara, we both know it’s more than that.”

She opened her mouth to argue but stopped, the weight of his gaze pinning her in place. He was right; something was wrong. The air crackled with it, a static charge that made her skin itch. The lantern sputtered again, its light dimming to a near-glow, and for a moment, Clara felt as if they were suspended in time, caught between the living and the lost.

“Look,” Jun said, pointing at the floor. Muddy prints trailed in a line toward the door, deep and fresh against the worn boards. The sight sent a chill through her—where had they come from? Had someone been here, waiting?

Without thinking, Clara stepped forward, drawn to the door as if it were a magnet, her breath hitching in her throat. She reached for the handle, her fingers trembling, the cool metal grounding her in the moment. “We can’t ignore this,” she whispered, half to herself, half to Jun, who remained a breath behind her, the warmth of his presence a stark contrast to the chill that swept through the hall.

“Clara—” he started again, but she turned to him, her eyes fierce.

“No, we have to open it. We need to know.”
