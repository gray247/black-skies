The lock resisted, but she twisted harder, and the door shuddered as if responding to some unseen force. A thin crack of light spilled through the widening gap, illuminating the dust motes that danced and swirled like lost whispers in the air. Jun, still hovering behind her, let out a breath that sent a chill through the stillness, mingling with the stale scent of the corridor—old carpets, damp walls, and a faint tang of something metallic she couldn’t place.

“Maybe it’s just stuck?” he murmured, his voice barely rising above the house's creaks and groans. But the quiver in his tone echoed her own uncertainty. What lay beyond the door? She paused, caught in the moment, the weight of the key heavy against her wrist, the red ribbon a stark reminder of her resolve.

“Let’s try again,” Clara said, her voice stronger than she felt. She turned the key once more, and this time, it clicked, the sound reverberating like a heartbeat. The door swung open, revealing the nursery in a shroud of shadow, the air thick with a musty smell that clawed at her throat.

“Just a room,” she whispered, though the tremor in her words betrayed her. The lantern flickered again, casting ghostly shapes that seemed to watch them from the corners. Jun moved cautiously beside her, the cracked brass lantern clutched like a lifeline, its sputtering light barely piercing the gloom. “We’re fine,” he reassured, though the tightness in his jaw suggested otherwise.

Stepping inside, Clara felt the weight of years pressing down on her. The dusty floorboards sighed beneath her feet, and she squinted into the dimness, searching for the source of the cough she thought she had imagined. The silence felt too alive, thick with the memory of laughter, the fading echoes of a child’s joy now turned hollow.

“Do you think it’s…?” Jun began, but the words hung in the air, unfinished, heavy with unspoken fears.

“No,” she cut him off, the instinct to protect—herself, him, whatever secrets the house held—surging through her. “Nora said no one’s been here for years.” But even as she spoke, a chill brushed against her skin, and she couldn’t shake the feeling that something was watching them, something just beyond the reach of the flickering light.

The walls felt too close, the air too thick. Clara dug her fingers into her coat pocket, feeling the cool ceramic of the fox charm wrapped in layers of fabric. It was a relic from another time, one that should have been forgotten like the nursery itself. Why had she brought it? It felt wrong, like an intrusion. The house whispered around her, its sighs echoing her unease.

“Do you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, his eyes darting toward the shadowed corners. Clara strained to listen, the silence alive with their breaths and the distant creaking of the house, but she heard nothing—nothing except for the soft, persistent thud of her own heart racing.

“It’s just the house,” she said, but doubt gnawed at the edges of her certainty. The chain on the corridor door rattled once more, a sharp sound that made her freeze, the air thickening with tension. Jun’s breath hitched, and she could feel the weight of his gaze on her, searching for reassurance she wasn’t sure she could provide.
