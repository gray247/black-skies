The dim light of the cracked brass lantern flickered, casting restless shadows on the walls of the narrow corridor. Clara Vale’s breath quickened as she exchanged a glance with Jun Park, the weight of silence pressing heavily against her chest. The faint drip of water echoed from somewhere above, mingling with the distant creaking of the house settling into its own bones. She could feel the dampness of Jun’s coat brushing against her arm, a reminder of the greenhouse's relentless rain, and the earthy scent of wet soil clung to both of them like a second skin.

“What if it’s not just mud?” Jun's voice was low, barely a whisper, yet it reverberated in the narrow space, amplifying the tension that had curled around them since they left the greenhouse. Clara clutched the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon like a lifeline against the encroaching darkness. She could still hear the latch of the greenhouse snapping open, the rush of cold air spilling out, and the way the vines had tangled around the key, as if trying to reclaim it.

“We need to find out what’s up there,” Clara replied, her own voice steadying as she leaned closer to the door that loomed before them, the chain rattling faintly in response to a breeze that shouldn’t exist in a house so old and still. She reached for the doorknob, her fingers trembling slightly as she tried to suppress the memory of the child’s cough echoing in her mind. It was a sound that had cut through the air like a knife, but she had forced herself to dismiss it, to focus instead on the pressing need to move forward, to discover what lay beyond the door.

Jun shifted nervously beside her, the lantern sputtering ominously, casting a sickly glow that barely illuminated the edges of the doorframe. “If Nora finds us—" he began, but Clara interrupted, her voice sharp, “Nora doesn’t know everything.” The words tasted bitter on her tongue. Nora Barrett had a way of twisting the truth, of smoothing over the cracks in their shared reality. The muddy prints leading away from the nursery were a testament to something hidden, something lurking just beyond their understanding.

The hallway clock began to strike thirteen, each chime reverberating through the air, sending a shiver down Clara's spine. “Did you hear that?” she asked, the urgency palpable in her voice. She glanced at Jun, searching for the flicker of fear that had been growing between them, but his eyes were fixed on the door, brow furrowed in concentration.

“What if we’re too late?” he said, his voice barely above a whisper, and Clara felt a chill that had nothing to do with the air around them. She turned her attention back to the door, the chain rattling in a way that felt almost like a heartbeat, as if something was testing its own boundaries from the other side. A quick inhale filled her lungs with the scent of dust and something else—a hint of something sweet, almost like the faintest wisp of perfume, a memory of laughter that seemed to laugh back at her from the gloom.

Jun nodded, though the uncertainty lingered in his eyes. “On three?”
