Clara bit her lip, her breath quickening as she stood before the sealed nursery door, the cool metal of the red ribbon key pressing into her wrist like a pulse of unease. The lantern’s sputtering light cast erratic shadows that danced along the door’s surface, where the wood seemed to absorb the darkness like a sponge. Jun shifted beside her, the damp fabric of his coat clinging uncomfortably to his skin, a reminder of the greenhouse rain that hadn’t yet dried. “We should just go,” he whispered, his voice a thread of tension, fraying at the edges.

With a deep breath, Clara reached for the doorknob, her fingers trembling as she traced the outline of the keyhole, the soft click of the mechanism echoing in her mind like a heartbeat. “What if the cough came from inside?” she asked, her voice barely above a whisper, as if speaking too loudly might draw whatever lingered beyond the door. She remembered Jun’s almost admission, the way he hesitated to share what he had heard, and she felt the weight of that silence settle between them.

“Or from the house,” Jun replied, glancing over his shoulder, his eyes wide and searching the dim corridor behind them. The lantern flickered again, a feeble pulse that sent the shadows lunging forward. “I don’t like this.”

Clara turned the key, wrenching it in the lock, but it was the wrong one—a dull thud reverberated through her chest, echoing the disappointment that bloomed within her. “We have to try something,” she insisted, forcing the stubborn hope from her throat despite the creeping dread. The key felt heavier in her hand, like an anchor pulling her down into the depths of uncertainty.

“Maybe we should check the other rooms first,” Jun suggested, his eyes darting to the darkened hall behind them, as if something unseen lurked just out of sight. “What if we’re not alone?”

A shiver coursed through Clara, a chill unmistakably different from the coolness of the air. She could almost hear the faint echo of a child’s cough, threading through the air like a lost lullaby, but she shook her head, desperate to dismiss the thought. “Nora said no one sleeps in there anymore. It’s been years.”

But as she spoke, the chain rattled again from the service corridor, a sound that seemed too deliberate, too insistent. Clara’s heart raced; she could feel it thumping against her ribs, a frenetic drumbeat urging her to act. “Did you hear that?” she asked, her voice sharper now, straining to hold the lingering fear at bay.

Jun nodded, his face paling as he stepped closer, the tarnished brass of the lantern barely illuminating their surroundings. “Someone’s testing it,” he murmured, eyes narrowing in concentration. The sound echoed again, a metallic whisper that seemed to mock their hesitation, sending prickles of anxiety racing down Clara’s spine.

“Let’s just open the door,” she said, the words spilling out before she could think better of them. The weight of the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket felt heavier, a reminder of the nursery’s secrets. “We can’t keep waiting for something to happen.”

“Clara—” Jun began, but she was already turning back to the door, determination flooding her veins like fire. She could feel the tension in the air, thick and electric, as if the house itself was holding its breath. Her fingers brushed against the doorknob, and for a moment, everything stilled—the lantern flickered once more, a gust of wind whispered through the cracks, and the world around her faded into a hushed anticipation.
