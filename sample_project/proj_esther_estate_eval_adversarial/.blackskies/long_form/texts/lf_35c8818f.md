Clara pressed her palm against the nursery door, feeling the pulse of the old wood beneath her fingers, as if it held a heartbeat of its own. The thirteenth chime echoed in her ears, a sound thick with dread and expectation. She leaned in closer, straining for any hint of the cough she had not heard but felt, an echo of a child’s voice wrapped in the fog of memory. The chill in the air clung to her skin, mingling with the dampness from Jun’s coat, which still dripped tiny puddles onto the floorboards.

“Did you hear it?” she asked, her voice a mere wisp against the oppressive silence, her breath hitching in her throat. The shadows cast by the sputtering lantern danced around them, flickering like trapped ghosts in the corners of the dim corridor. The ceramic fox nestled in her pocket felt heavier, as if it bore the weight of secrets long buried.

Jun was beside her, his brow furrowed, the lantern’s fading light catching the sharp angles of his face. “I didn’t hear anything, Clara,” he replied, his voice steady but laced with an unspoken tension. He shifted his weight, the crackling sound of his wet coat a reminder of their hasty exit from the greenhouse, and Clara couldn’t shake the feeling that something had followed them, something lurking just out of sight.

The chain rattled again from the service corridor door, a sound that sent a shiver down Clara’s spine. “Someone’s testing it,” she whispered, glancing at the door as if it might spring open at any moment to reveal whatever lay beyond. “What if—”

“Stop,” Jun interrupted, a sudden urgency igniting in his tone. “We need to focus on what’s in front of us. The nursery.” He shifted the lantern, its cracked brass catching the last glimmers of light, but it flickered like a dying ember, leaving them in a deepening gloom.

Clara nodded, her heart pounding as she pulled the key from the ribbon around her wrist, the red fabric warm against her skin. It felt like an anchor, yet she couldn’t remember how tightly she had tied it, or why it had become a lifeline in this moment. She glanced at Jun, whose eyes darted toward the door, and she could see a flicker of fear behind his resolve.

She took a step back, contemplating the key. “What if it doesn’t unlock anything? What if we’re meant to leave it sealed?” The thought slipped from her lips, a fragile thread woven through the heavy air, but the idea of leaving the door closed felt wrong, as if they were denying something vital.

“Clara,” Jun said gently, his voice wrapping around her like a steadying hand. “You felt the cough. We can’t ignore that. Whatever it is, it’s here.” He nodded toward the door, his gaze unwavering. “You have to try.”

Her resolve firmed, bolstered by the certainty in his voice. She approached the door once more, the wood cool under her palm, the key glinting dully in the lantern’s meager glow. Clara slid the key into the lock, hesitating for a heartbeat. The air thickened, and she could almost taste the dust of forgotten memories and the weight of expectation. She turned the key, and the lock clicked, a sound that reverberated through the hallway like the echo of a long-held breath.

“Clara, wait!” Jun’s voice sliced through the moment, a sudden panic rising as the door creaked open. The lantern flickered violently, casting erratic shadows that swirled around them as if warning them to turn back.
