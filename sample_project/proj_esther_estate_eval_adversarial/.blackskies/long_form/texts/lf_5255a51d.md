Clara pressed forward, her heart thudding against her ribs as if trying to break free. The dim light from Jun's lantern flickered, the weak glow barely revealing the scuffed floorboards beneath their feet, slick with moisture and mud. Each step felt like a plunge into a hidden world, where shadows twisted in the corners like secrets waiting to be uncovered. The air was thick with the musty scent of damp earth, the walls seeming to absorb every tremor of unease that hung in the silence. Jun’s coat dripped onto the floor, water pooling in the dark as remnants of their hurried exit from the greenhouse mingled with the dirt beneath them.

“Clara, we shouldn’t be here,” he murmured, his voice low and shaky, a tremor of urgency threading through the words. She caught the way his fingers clenched around the lantern, knuckles pale against the brass, and it sent a ripple of worry through her. His fear felt contagious, tugging at her own resolve, but she forced herself to respond with a steadiness that felt precarious at best.

“We have to find out what’s going on,” she retorted, her voice sharper than she meant it to be, the words slicing through the heavy air. They lingered between them, thick with unvoiced fears. The red ribbon around her wrist, holding the parlor key, felt both reassuring and constricting, as if it were tightening with every breath she took. “It’s just an old house, Jun. Nothing to be afraid of.”

But even as she spoke, a chill slithered down her spine. Ahead, the corridor twisted into darkness, shadows thickening as her gaze caught the muddy footprints stretching out before them, not leading back but deeper into the gloom. The realization sent a shiver through her, a prickling sensation that they were not alone in this oppressive space.
