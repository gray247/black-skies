Clara pressed against the damp wall of the corridor, the cold seeping through her coat like a whisper of the storm outside. The cracked brass lantern in Jun's grip flickered uncertainly, its weak battery struggling against the darkness that swirled in thick tendrils around them. She could feel the weight of the parlor key on her wrist, the red ribbon tied tightly, yet it seemed to pull at her skin, a reminder of something she couldn’t quite grasp.

"Did you hear that?" Jun's voice trembled, barely above a whisper, as his eyes darted to the shadows pooling in the corners. Clara felt her heart quicken, a frantic drumbeat resounding in the silence. She shook her head, her throat too tight to form the words. The air was thick with an earthy scent, damp and musty, mingling with the faint traces of something floral from the greenhouse, remnants of the rain that had soaked Jun's coat through. The fabric clung to him, dark and heavy, an echo of his unease.

Ahead, muddy footprints marred the polished wood of the corridor floor, leading toward the chained door at the far end. Clara's breath caught as she noticed the chain shifting slightly, as if someone—or something—on the other side was toying with it, testing its strength. “We should go back,” Jun insisted, his voice rising, panic threading through his words. “We shouldn’t be here.”

His fear radiated off him like heat from a fever, and Clara's stomach twisted uncomfortably. She needed to be strong, to reassure him, but the words stumbled in her throat. "We can't," she replied too sharply, her voice echoing in the stillness, the sound almost alien. “The others are counting on us.”
