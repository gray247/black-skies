Clara pressed her back against the damp, peeling wall of the corridor, her breath hitching in the thick, musty air that clung to her skin like a wet blanket. The weak glow of the lantern flickered beside her, casting long, trembling shadows that twisted and twisted again, almost as if they were reaching out, yearning for something—someone—lost in the dark. She could feel the pulse of anxiety thrumming in her chest, a frantic drumbeat that matched the distant, hollow thud of her pulse in her ears.

Jun stood a few paces ahead, his coat still heavy with rain, the fabric clinging to his arms like wet paper. The lantern’s sputtering light caught the sharp angles of his face, revealing the tension etched into his features. “Clara,” he whispered, glancing back with wide eyes, “what if someone is really there?”

She straightened, forcing her voice to stay steady, though the tremor beneath it betrayed her resolve. “No one’s here. It’s just the wind.” But even as she spoke, a chill crawled up her spine, and she couldn’t shake the sensation that the shadows were deeper than they ought to be, that they were watching her, waiting.

The door to the service corridor rattled again as if something on the other side was testing the chain, a metallic clink that sent a shiver through her. Clara instinctively tightened her grip on the lantern, the cold metal biting into her palm, the weight of the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket pressing against her thigh, a reminder of innocence lost. “We shouldn’t be here,” she murmured, though a part of her was drawn forward, her curiosity igniting against the fear that clutched at her throat.
