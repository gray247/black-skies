Clara pressed her back against the cool wood of the nursery door, the cracked brass lantern flickering weakly in Jun’s hands. Shadows danced around them, morphing the corners of the room into lurking shapes as if the house itself breathed, its sighs echoing through the walls. The faint smell of damp earth still clung to her clothes, mingling with the faint sweetness of something long gone; perhaps it was the dust of old memories. She let the delicate fabric of her coat slide against the smooth ceramic of the fox charm buried in her pocket, its coolness a reminder of the childish warmth she once felt when it was merely a trinket rather than a talisman of unease.

“Do you hear that?” Jun's voice was barely above a whisper, yet it was punctuated by the sharp crack of the lantern sputtering, a sound that felt too loud in the stillness. Clara turned to him, noting the way his brow furrowed, those dark eyes darting toward the shadows as if they could reveal the source of the cough he had almost mentioned. The weight of unspoken words hung between them, thick like the humidity that had soaked through his coat in the greenhouse. She wanted to reach out, to reassure him, but her heart thudded too heavily in her chest, caught in a rhythm of anxious uncertainty.

“Maybe it’s just the house settling,” she offered, her voice steadier than she felt. “You know how it is.” The walls groaned in response, a deep creaking that seemed to seep into her bones. Jun shifted, his gaze lingering on the door as if expecting it to crack open and reveal whatever lay hidden beyond. Behind them, the service corridor lay in shadow, the chain rattling softly, testing its bonds as if something were struggling against it from the other side. Clara's stomach twisted.

“Clara,” he began, and she could feel the weight of his hesitation. “I—” But the words caught in his throat, and she could see the flicker of doubt in his expression. She raised a hand, instinctively brushing the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key still dangling from it. An anchor, yet it felt more like a burden, a reminder of everything they had yet to face.

“Let’s just—” she said, the urgency in her voice startling even herself. “Let’s just open the door.” The idea of waiting felt unbearable, an invitation for the shadows to creep closer. Jun nodded, the lantern’s light dimming further as he stepped closer to the door. She felt the air thrum with anticipation, each second stretching taut like a string pulled too tight. Clara reached for the knob, her heart racing as her fingers brushed the cool metal, a fleeting image of the nursery's forgotten toys flashing through her mind—dusty dolls, a rocking horse, a blanket that smelled like childhood dreams.

With a swift turn, she pushed the door open, the creak of the hinges a protest against the stillness. The lantern flickered once more, illuminating the nursery’s interior: a room swallowed in shadows, the window painted shut, blocking the outside world completely. The air felt thicker here, almost cloying, as if the walls were holding their breath. Clara stepped inside, her footfalls silent against the worn floorboards, the sensation of something watching prickling at her skin.

“Maybe it’s just a draft,” Jun murmured, but even he sounded unconvinced. Clara could feel the weight of his presence behind her, an anchor in the chaos of her thoughts.
