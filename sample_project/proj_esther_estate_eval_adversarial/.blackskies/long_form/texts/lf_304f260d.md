The walls, once a vibrant hue, were now peeling and faded, revealing patches of old plaster that had long since surrendered to neglect. Shadows flickered across the surfaces as Jun held the cracked brass lantern aloft, its beam weak and wavering, illuminating the swirling dust motes that danced in the thin light. Clara’s heart raced, each beat a frantic reminder of the tension coiling in her chest, mirroring the rapid intake of breath from Jun beside her.

She tightened her grip on the lantern's handle, the cool metal sending a shiver up her arm. She glanced at Jun, whose coat still glistened with raindrops, heavy and clinging as if the storm had left an imprint of its fury on him. “Can we just go back?” he whispered, his voice trembling, as if he feared the very walls might hear him.

“No,” Clara replied, her voice sharper than she intended, a steel edge laced with urgency. The word lingered between them, thick with the weight of unspoken dread. A gnawing unease twisted in her stomach; the thought of retreating sent a chill down her spine. The greenhouse door had closed behind them like a tomb, locking away the secrets nestled in the shadows of that place. Her wrist felt the tug of the parlor key, the red ribbon binding it a stark contrast against her skin, as though it were a yoke anchoring her to this moment.

A soft rustling broke the heavy silence, and Clara’s breath hitched in her throat, a sudden sharp intake of air that felt like ice.
