Clara pressed on, the flickering light of the brass lantern barely fighting back the encroaching shadows that clung to the edges of the corridor. The air was thick with the scent of wet earth, mingled with the faint, bitter tang of something metallic. She could feel the cold seep deeper into her skin, tightening her muscles, as if the very walls were drawing inwards. “We have to keep moving,” she said, her voice sharper than she intended. It echoed unnaturally in the confined space, making her wince.

Jun stumbled slightly behind her, the sound of his wet coat slapping against his legs a reminder of the storm that had battered the greenhouse. “Clara, I swear I heard something—” His voice faltered, doubt creeping in like the shadows around them. The lantern sputtered again, casting long, jittery shapes that seemed to dance on the walls, and she could see the flicker of fear in his eyes.

“Maybe it’s just the wind,” she replied, but even as she spoke, a shudder ran down her spine. The chain on the door rattled softly, a chilling counterpoint to her words, and she hesitated, the pulse of her heart quickening. “We should go back,” Jun urged, glancing over his shoulder, his breath a rapid mist in the cool air. “It’s not safe here.”

Clara tightened her grip on the lantern, the metal warm against her palm, her thoughts racing. The parlor key tied to her wrist felt heavier, its presence a constant reminder of the path she had chosen. “We can’t go back. Not now,” she insisted, forcing the words out as though they could solidify her resolve. The key glinted dully, the red ribbon catching the lantern’s light for a brief moment.
