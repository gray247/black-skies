Clara pressed forward, the lantern’s sputtering light barely managing to carve out a path in the oppressive darkness. Each step echoed softly against the stone walls, a muted reminder of their presence in this hidden passage. The musty air clung to her skin, thick and damp, like a second layer of clothing, and she could feel her pulse quickening in time with the flickering glow. Beside her, Jun’s soaked coat clung to his frame, the fabric heavy with rainwater, and she caught a glimpse of his eyes—wide and anxious as he scanned the shadows, searching for something just beyond reach.

“Are you sure we should be here?” he murmured, voice barely above a whisper, his breath puffing out in quick, nervous clouds. Clara could hear the tremor beneath his bravado, the way he tried to mask his fear with a veneer of bravado that only deepened her own unease.

“I… I don’t know,” she replied, too sharp, the words spilling out before she could temper them. She felt the ribbon against her wrist, the parlor key a tangible weight, a tether to something familiar. “We have to find out what’s going on.” But even as she spoke, the doubt gnawed at her—why did they have to be the ones? A chill crept down her spine, and she shivered, a gooseflesh sensation that seemed to resonate in the air around them.
