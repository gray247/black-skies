The dim glow of the lantern flickered, casting weak shadows along the narrow corridor, where the air felt damp and heavy with the scent of wet earth. Clara clutched the parlor key, the red ribbon wrapped tightly around her wrist, its rough fabric grounding her amidst the chaos. She could feel the pulse of her heartbeat in her fingertips, a steady reminder of her resolve, even as her mind raced through the possibilities of what they might find ahead.

Jun, a step behind her, shifted from foot to foot, his soaked coat clinging to him like a second skin. The fabric squelched with each movement, and he frowned, glancing nervously down the corridor where the shadows pooled like dark water. “Clara, do you hear that?” he whispered, his voice barely above a breath. The tremor in it made her stomach twist.

She hesitated, straining to catch any sound that might betray their solitude. Just the faint drip of rainwater from the greenhouse roof, echoing like a metronome in the silence. “It’s nothing,” she replied too quickly, her words sharp against the stillness, though the knot in her stomach tightened at the thought of the unseen. She couldn’t shake the image of the muddy footprints leading away from them, the way they seemed to beckon deeper into the shadows.
