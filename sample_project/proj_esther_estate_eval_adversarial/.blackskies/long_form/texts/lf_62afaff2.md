The sincerity in his tone tugged at her, but the thought of turning away from the door now felt like a betrayal, a surrender to whatever had been watching them through the cracks of this house. She swallowed hard, the taste of dust and something metallic lingering at the back of her throat. “I can’t leave it like this,” she whispered, more to herself than to him. The key, a red ribbon tied tightly around her wrist, felt like a lifeline and a noose all at once.

The lantern sputtered again, the flame barely illuminating the cramped passageway, and she could see the edges of Jun’s coat glistening with rain, the fabric clinging to him like a second skin. He exhaled slowly, frustration buried beneath layers of concern. “What if it’s just a story, Clara? Something the house is playing at?”

But it wasn’t just a story. The faint cough she had chosen to ignore echoed in her mind, a distant whisper that gnawed at her resolve. Nothing about this house felt right. The muddy prints leading away from the door, the way the chain on the corridor door rattled as if something were testing it from the other side — it all hinted at a deeper, darker truth, one she felt she was skirting dangerously close to.

She turned back to the door, cupping the rough grain of the wood with both hands now, grounding herself against the palpable weight of her fears. “There’s something in there, Jun. I can’t just let it stay hidden.” The words came out stronger than she felt, a mask of bravery to hide the tremor beneath.

Jun shifted, his breath catching in his throat. “And what if it’s worse than we think?”

“What if it’s not?” she shot back, her voice sharp with a desperation that surprised even her. The thought of whatever lay behind the door — a child’s laughter, a memory, or an echo of grief — filled her with a mix of dread and resolve. She reached for the key, fingers brushing the ribbon, a rush of warmth flooding her palm.

The lantern flickered one last time before dying completely, leaving them cloaked in near darkness. Clara’s breath quickened, the absence of light amplifying the sounds around them; the house creaked and groaned, as if it were alive, responding to the tension in the air. She fumbled for the keyhole, her hand trembling as she inserted the key. The metal felt foreign, cold and unyielding, and she hesitated for a moment, the weight of every memory tied to this place pressing against her.

“Clara…” Jun’s voice was barely a whisper, a plea laced with the urgency of their surroundings.

With a deep breath, she twisted the key, the mechanism catching for a moment before clicking open. The door creaked ominously as she pushed it ajar, revealing a space cloaked in shadows, the air thick with dust and the faint scent of something sweetly decayed.
