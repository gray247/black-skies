Clara pressed her palm against the cool, grainy surface of the nursery door, her fingertips tracing the intricate woodwork as if it held the answers she sought, though the feeling was nothing but a lingering chill. The quiet was deceptive, thick with the weight of secrets, and she could hardly breathe, the air stale and damp around her. The lantern’s flicker turned the shadows into a restless sea, where each movement felt like a warning. Jun’s breath was shallow beside her, the sound barely breaking the silence, but it was enough to draw her attention.

“What if there’s someone in there?” he whispered, the tremor in his voice underscoring the tense stillness. The way his brow furrowed, the hesitation in his gaze, made her feel as if they stood on the precipice of something vast and unknowable. She glanced at him, the lantern light catching the droplets that clung to his coat, remnants of the greenhouse rain. “You heard the cough, didn’t you?” she pressed, feeling the need to bridge the growing chasm between them.

He hesitated, the lantern sputtering in his grip, a dying ember of light in their darkening world. “I—” he started, but his voice faltered. Clara’s heart raced; she could see the conflict play across his features, the way uncertainty seeped in from the edges, muddling his thoughts. She wanted to reach out, to assure him it was all right, but the words felt trapped in her throat, tangled like the ribbon around her wrist.

“Clara,” he began again, the name slipping from his lips like a confession. “It’s just an old house, right? We’re being ridiculous.”

But the tremor in his voice betrayed him. She couldn’t shake the feeling that the house was listening, its walls holding their breath, waiting for them to make the next move. What if the cough was a child’s, hidden in the shadows? What if it was something else—something that had no business being here? She closed her eyes for a moment, letting the dampness of the air seep deeper into her lungs, grounding her against the creeping unease.

The sound of chains rattling from above made her jump, the noise echoing down the corridor like a ghostly whisper. Clara turned to the door, heart thudding in her chest as she recalled how the chain had shifted earlier, a strange foreboding that had settled over them like fog. “That chain… it shouldn’t be there,” she murmured, her voice barely above a breath.

“Maybe it’s just the wind,” Jun suggested, though his eyes darted toward the ceiling, betraying his own doubts. They stood in tense silence, the only sound the distant, rhythmic ticking of the clock somewhere in the house, each tick a reminder of the time slipping away. Clara’s fingers brushed the cool ceramic of the fox charm in her pocket, and she could almost feel it pulse with a life of its own, but she shook her head, trying to dispel the thoughts weaving through her mind like dark threads.

“Let’s just try the key again,” she said finally, determination hardening her voice. The red ribbon seemed to tighten around her wrist as if it were a tether, binding her to the moment, to the choices that lay ahead. She fished the key from her pocket, its weight familiar yet foreign, and pressed it to the lock, hesitating. “One more time.”

Jun shifted, the lantern’s flicker casting long shadows across his face. “If it doesn’t work, we need to leave,” he urged, a mixture of fear and resolve in his eyes. “We can’t keep—”
