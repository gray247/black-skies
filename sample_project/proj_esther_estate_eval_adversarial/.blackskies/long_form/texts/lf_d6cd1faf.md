Clara pressed her palm against the nursery door, the rough wood warm under her fingers, a stark contrast to the icy air of the corridor. It felt alive, resonating with the vibrations of the house, as if every creak and sigh was a whispered secret waiting to be uncovered. The lantern in Jun’s grip flared then sputtered, the feeble light casting flickering shadows that leapt across the walls, revealing the peeling paint and timeworn grime that clung to the surfaces like memories refusing to fade. Each flicker felt like a taunting reminder that they were not alone in this place, the darkness thickening around them.

“Do you really think it’s locked?” Clara whispered, her breath catching slightly as she leaned in, straining to hear any sound from beyond the door. She felt Jun’s presence beside her, the damp fabric of his coat clinging to him, the earthy scent of rain-soaked soil mingling with the stale air that hung heavy with expectation.

“Should be,” Jun replied, his brow furrowing as he scrutinized the rusted lock, a stubborn relic of neglect. “But…” He trailed off, his gaze drifting past her, as if the walls were whispering secrets he dared not voice.

Clara’s pulse quickened. “But what?”

He hesitated, the lantern’s light flickering again, illuminating his features with an unsettling intensity. “It feels like something’s waiting,” he murmured, his voice low and reverent. “Like the house knows we’re here.”

A chill ran down Clara’s spine, and she inhaled deeply, trying to shake off the feeling of being watched. The air was thick with an unnameable tension, pressing against her chest, making it hard to breathe. “You heard it too, right?” she pressed, her fingers absently brushing the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the key dangling like a fragile promise—or perhaps a warning.

His gaze shifted to her pocket, where the chipped ceramic fox lay hidden beneath her coat, a token of their uncertain journey. “I thought… I thought I heard a cough,” he said finally, his voice barely above a whisper, as if speaking it aloud would summon whatever lingered in the shadows.

Clara’s stomach tightened, unease flaring within her. “What kind of cough?”

“Childish, maybe. Like a cold.” He shifted, gripping the lantern tighter as though it were a lifeline against their shared trepidation. “It came from up there.” He nodded toward the ceiling, beneath which the nursery lay sealed and silent, the painted window tightly shut against whatever lay beyond.

“Up there?” Her breath hitched, the sense of claustrophobia gnawing at her as she glanced up at the darkened expanse. “Nora said—”

“Nora’s not here,” Jun interrupted, his tone sharper than she anticipated. “And she flinched when she saw the fox.”

Clara swallowed hard, the memory of Nora’s fleeting fear flickering through her mind, an emotion masked by her usual indifference. “Maybe it’s just a coincidence,” she suggested, but even she could hear the uncertainty laced in her voice.

Jun shrugged, the damp fabric of his coat brushing against her arm, a reminder of the rain-drenched greenhouse that felt worlds away. “Things don’t just get moved without a reason, Clara. And that chain…” His gaze flicked back to the ceiling, tension etched into his features. “It shouldn’t be moving like that, should it?”

“No,” Clara admitted, her heart racing as she recalled the unsettling rattle she’d heard moments before, an echo of dread that seemed to swirl above them. “It’s like someone’s testing it from the other side.”
