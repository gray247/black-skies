Clara's heart thudded in her chest, a steady drumbeat of anxiety as she stared at the sealed nursery door, its surface warped and painted shut. The lantern flickered weakly, casting shadows that danced like specters across the walls. Jun stood beside her, his breath clouding in the cool air, the faint smell of rain still clinging to him like a shroud. She could see the tension in his jaw, a tight line that spoke of unspoken fears. “Clara,” he said, his voice barely above a murmur, “we should go back.”

“Back where?” she shot back, a flash of irritation sparking in her voice. “To the dining room? To Nora’s questions?” Her fingers clutched the ceramic fox wrapped in her coat pocket, the sharp edge digging into her skin, grounding her in the moment even as her thoughts spiraled. It felt like a tether to something solid amidst the chaotic whispers of the house.

He looked at her, his eyes dark and stormy, mirroring the tempest outside that had drenched them only moments ago. “The prints. They don’t make sense. We should—”

“Should what? Follow them?” Clara snapped, her voice rising. “They lead nowhere. Just more questions.” The air felt electric, charged with something unsaid. The clock above them ticked ominously, each second stretching out as if mocking their indecision.

Jun’s lips pressed into a thin line, and for a moment, silence enveloped them, broken only by the soft, persistent drumming of rain against the nursery window. Clara turned her gaze back to the door, shadows pooling at its base. She could almost feel the weight of the house pressing down, a living entity with breath and pulse, watching, waiting. “You heard the cough,” she said, the words slipping from her before she could stop them. “Why didn’t you say anything?”

“I—” He hesitated, the lantern sputtering again, the light dimming as if sharing in his uncertainty. “I thought you heard it too.”

“I didn’t,” she replied, her voice firmer now, though a flicker of doubt swirled in her gut. The idea of an intruder lurking in the darkness, or worse—a child lost to time—sent a chill racing down her spine. She could almost see it, an echo from the past, a playful laugh that echoed in the corners of her memory.

“Clara.” He took a step closer, the lantern swinging between them, illuminating their faces for a brief moment. “If there’s something in there—”

“Then we need to find out.” Her resolve solidified, a flicker of defiance igniting within her. She reached for the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon a stark contrast against her pale skin. But as she grasped it, a sense of unease washed over her. She couldn’t remember tying it tighter, the knot secure but frail, as if it could unravel at any moment.

“Wait,” Jun said, his voice urgent. “The chain. It shouldn’t be moving. It’s too far down.”

She could hear the faint clink above them, as if someone was testing the door from the other side. Clara’s breath hitched, the sound stealing the warmth from the air between them. “What if someone is trying to get in?” she whispered, her heart racing anew.

“Or out,” he replied, the gravity of his words hanging in the air like the damp scent of earth and rain. She felt the weight of his gaze, the fear woven into the fabric of his soaked coat, a silent plea for safety in this house that felt increasingly alive, increasingly malevolent.
