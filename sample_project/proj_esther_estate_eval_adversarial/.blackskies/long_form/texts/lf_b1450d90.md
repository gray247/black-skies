In the dim light of the narrow corridor, Clara felt the weight of the parlor key against her wrist, its red ribbon a bright, stubborn contrast to the gloom that seemed to seep from the very walls. The lantern in Jun's hand flickered weakly, casting long shadows that danced like memories half-remembered. Rainwater dripped from his coat, pooling at their feet, a small reminder of the greenhouse's relentless downpour.

“Do you think it’s locked?” Jun asked, his breath a thin plume of vapor in the cool air. He leaned closer, peering at the door to the nursery, its paint chipped and weary. Clara could hear the faintest rattle of the chain on the other side, a sound that threaded through her thoughts like a needle through fabric, stitching together worry and dread.

“I have to,” she whispered, the words escaping before she could pull them back. The lantern sputtered again, its light waning, and Clara felt a flutter of panic. She gripped the ribbon tighter, the fabric biting into her skin, grounding her in the moment. It was absurd, really, how something so simple could tether her to reality in the face of... whatever this was.

Jun shifted beside her, his brow furrowed. “We could go back. Find someone—”

“No,” Clara interrupted, her voice sharper than intended. The echo of her own authority startled her, and she quickly softened her tone. “The prints lead here. We have to see.” She glanced down the corridor, the gloom stretching endlessly, a mouth that might swallow them whole if they let it.

He nodded, though uncertainty lingered in his eyes, the kind of uncertainty that could turn to resolve or panic in an instant. “Alright. Just... be careful.”

With a deep breath, Clara stepped forward, the key hanging from her wrist feeling like a promise. She jiggled the lock, the metal cold under her touch, and the memory of the last time she’d turned a key in this house surfaced unbidden. That moment had been filled with laughter, sunlight slanting through the windows, the scent of fresh paint mingling with the heady perfume of roses from the garden. But now, the echoes of joy felt like ghosts, mocking her with their absence.

As she twisted the key, the door creaked open with a reluctant groan, revealing a darkened room swallowed by shadows. The air inside felt heavier, as if it had been holding its breath for years, and Clara stepped over the threshold, her heart racing in her chest. The lantern flickered again, its light barely illuminating the edges of the nursery's worn furniture, the dust motes swirling like tiny specters in the dimness.

“What if there’s nothing here?” Jun’s voice cracked slightly, betraying the edge of anxiety that lay underneath his calm exterior. He followed her inside, the door creaking ominously as it swung shut behind them.

“Then we’ll know,” Clara replied, though she felt the weight of her own words pressing down on her. The nursery chest, its wood bleached by time and neglect, stood against the wall, an unyielding sentinel. Clara approached it, curious yet wary, the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket feeling suddenly heavier, as if it had its own secrets to tell.

A cough echoed through the room, sharp and sudden, slicing through the silence. Clara froze, the sound so vivid it seemed to resonate against the walls. It was a child’s cough, small and strained, and the chill that raced down her spine was both familiar and terrifying. She glanced at Jun, but his eyes were wide, fixed on the corner of the room where the shadows pooled thickest.
