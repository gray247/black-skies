She brushed her fingers against the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon soft against her skin, but the knot felt uncomfortably tight, almost as if it were a tether binding her to the secrets of this house.

“Are you sure you want to do this?” Jun's voice was low, almost swallowed by the hush that enveloped them. His coat dripped water onto the floor, forming dark puddles that mingled with the muddy prints trailing into the nursery, leading them into a mystery that felt alive and breathing.

“I can’t leave it like this,” Clara replied, her voice a whisper that seemed to echo back at her from the corners of the dim corridor. She could feel the weight of Nora's gaze still lingering on her, the way the older woman had forced a laughter that never truly reached her eyes. The dining room had felt stifling, full of questions that hung in the air like the dust motes catching in the last rays of fading light. Clara had shielded herself from Nora's probing, but she could still sense the concern, the suspicion.

As she stepped closer to the door, her pulse quickened. The wood was worn, the handle cold beneath her palm. The lantern sputtered again, its light weakening, and Clara's breath caught in her throat. Something shifted behind her, a subtle sound like the creaking of old bones, but when she glanced back, Jun was staring at the door as if it were a portal, too.

“Do you hear that?” he asked, his brows knitted together. Clara didn’t respond, the weight of her silence heavy between them. She had heard it— the soft cough, like a whisper of a child lost in the folds of memory. But the air felt thick, and she didn’t want to invite the truth into the open, didn’t want to shatter the barrier of her denial.

The lantern flickered one last time before the light gave out completely, leaving them in a shroud of darkness. Clara's heart raced, the silence pressing in around them, and she fumbled for the key, her fingers trembling as she found the lock. It felt wrong, the way the key slid into place, and for a moment, she hesitated. What lay behind this door? The nursery had been sealed for years, a forgotten room in a house that bore witness to everything—love, loss, and the weight of unspoken words.

Jun shifted beside her, the soaked fabric of his coat brushing against her arm, grounding her. “Clara,” he said, his voice a low murmur, “we can turn back. We don’t have to—”

“No,” she interrupted, a sudden surge of defiance flaring within her. “We need to know.” And with that, she turned the key, the sound of the lock clicking echoing through the corridor, a sound both final and liberating.

The door creaked open, revealing a room steeped in shadows and dust, the air thick and stale, as if it had been holding its breath. Clara stepped inside, her heart pounding in rhythm with the silence.
