The lantern flickered again, its light barely illuminating the damp corridor, and Clara tightened her grip on the handle, the brass cold and slick against her palm. Jun’s eyes darted to the chained door, the metal glinting as if mocking their fear. “We shouldn’t be here,” he murmured, his breath hitching in the still air, a mix of anxiety and the lingering scent of wet earth that clung to the walls like a warning.

“Stop it,” Clara snapped, the words sharper than she intended. She felt the pulse of her heart quicken, a drumbeat of urgency pushing her forward. “There’s nothing here we can’t handle.” But even as she spoke, her gaze flicked nervously to the shadows that pooled in the corners, as if something might lunge out at any moment. The key around her wrist, tied with a frayed red ribbon, felt heavier, a reminder of the locked parlor, the secrets hidden behind its door.

Mud squelched beneath Jun’s boots, the sound all too loud in the oppressive silence. “Clara, look.” He pointed, and she followed his gaze to the fresh prints trailing down the corridor, dark and deep against the worn floorboards. They were unmistakably small, child-sized, and her stomach twisted at the thought. “But we—”

“Someone must have come through here,” she interjected, forcing a calmness she didn’t feel. “Maybe it’s just a prank.” The words tasted bitter in her mouth, a desperate attempt to dismiss the creeping dread that coiled around her stomach. She could feel Jun’s fear radiating off him, a tangible thing that clashed with her own resolve.
