Clara pressed her back against the jagged stone wall, listening intently as the chain rattled once more, a metallic clink that sent a shiver racing down her spine. The dim light from Jun’s lantern flickered weakly, casting erratic shadows that danced like specters down the corridor, illuminating the muddy footprints that led deeper into the darkness ahead. “It’s just the wind,” she said, though the tremor in her own voice betrayed her confidence. The scent of damp earth mingled with that cloying sweetness, thickening the air, wrapping around her like a tightening noose. What was it? She shook her head, dismissing the thought as she felt the cold metal key tied to her wrist press against her skin, a reminder of their frantic search.

Jun’s coat hung heavily on him, soaked through and clinging to his frame, the fabric darkened to a deep charcoal, and she could see how his shoulders slumped, the lantern’s sputtering light reflecting the unease etched in his features. “What if someone’s in there?” he whispered, glancing back toward the chained door, his eyes wide and glistening. Clara felt a pulse of frustration, a dark impatience flaring within her. “Then we’ll deal with it,” she replied sharply, her words harsher than intended. She didn’t want to consider the possibility, didn’t want to linger on the thought of whatever could be testing the chain from the other side. “We have to keep moving.”

She stepped forward, each footfall sinking into the damp ground, the squelch beneath her boots a reminder of the storm that had passed, yet the air felt heavy, as if the world outside still churned in turmoil.
