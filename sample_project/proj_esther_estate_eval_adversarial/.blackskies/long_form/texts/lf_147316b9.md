The lantern flickered again, a stutter of light that caught the edges of her anxiety, illuminating the chipped wood of the door and the cobwebs strung like silver threads in the corners. She could feel Jun's breath behind her, warm and tentative, mingling with the stale air that clung to the room like a shroud.

“Clara,” he murmured, the concern in his voice a soft echo of her own spiraling thoughts. “We don’t have to do this.”

But even as she turned her head slightly to meet his gaze, she could sense the determination in his stance, the way he shifted his weight, the soaked fabric of his coat clinging to him like a second skin. She couldn’t see his eyes well in the dim light, but she imagined them searching hers for a sign of resolve. “We came this far,” she replied, her voice steadier than she felt.

Just then, a low thud reverberated through the hallway, rattling the chain on the other side of the door. Clara’s heart jolted, and she froze, every instinct screaming at her to run. The nursery’s silence pressed in on her, heavy and expectant, as if the very walls were holding their breath. “Did you hear that?” she whispered, her voice barely breaking the tension.

Jun nodded, his brow furrowing. “It’s probably just the house settling,” he said, but his tone lacked conviction. The lantern flickered again, and Clara felt the warmth of its glow fade, the shadows deepening around them.

“Right,” she said, though doubt twisted in her stomach. The house had always settled like this, but today it felt different, charged with an energy she couldn’t grasp. As she turned back to the door, her fingers brushed against the cool surface, feeling the rough texture beneath her fingertips. The red ribbon tied around her wrist felt tight, a reminder of the key she’d found tangled in the wet vines, a key to a past she had nearly forgotten.

“Maybe we should—” Jun started, but Clara cut him off, her breath catching again. “Wait.” She could feel it then, a soft sound, like a child’s cough echoing from somewhere deep within the room, a sound that twisted her heart with grief and confusion. She turned to Jun, her eyes wide, the thought of a child in this place stirring something raw and uncomfortable. “Did you hear that?”

He hesitated, the flickering lantern illuminating the uncertainty in his expression. “I didn’t—”

“Jun,” she pressed, her voice sharp with urgency. “That cough. It’s real.”

He swallowed hard, the pulse in his throat visible under the dim light. “Clara, you know no child has been in this nursery for years.” The statement came out more like a question, a plea for clarity.

“Then what was it?” she shot back, the desperation in her voice rising like the tide.

Before he could answer, Clara grasped the doorknob, its surface cold and slick beneath her palm. She hesitated, the weight of the moment pressing down on her. She couldn’t shake the feeling that the house was watching, listening, waiting for her to make her choice.
