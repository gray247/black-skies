Jun stood behind her, the dim light of the lantern glinting off the water-beaded fabric of his coat, his breath a cloud of anxiety in the stillness.

Her heart skipped, a rush of adrenaline coursing through her as she glanced back at him, catching the flicker of fear in his eyes. “No,” she admitted, the word hanging between them like a weight. “It’s not.”

She pressed her palm against the door, feeling the coolness seep into her skin, a reminder of the world outside—damp and heavy with the aftermath of the rain. Memories of the greenhouse filled her mind, the way the latch had resisted their desperate attempts to get in, the parlor key tangled in those wet vines. The thought of it made her stomach twist. She couldn’t shake the feeling that something was off, that they were being watched, even in the dark recesses of their own home.

“Clara, we should—” Jun began, but she cut him off, the urgency in her chest flaring into a flame.

“No. Just wait.” She pressed closer, her ear straining against the wood, her breath shallow. It felt as if the house was holding its breath too, every creak and groan a protest against the silence that enveloped them. She could almost feel the pulse of the place under her skin, a heart that beat in time with her own anxious rhythm.

Then, a soft sound—a rustle, a whisper? It stirred just beyond the door, and her breath caught. “Did you hear that?” she asked, her voice trembling as she turned to Jun, who nodded, eyes wide.

“Maybe it’s a child,” he suggested, though his tone was laced with disbelief, as if he were trying to convince himself more than her. “But Nora—”

“—Nora flinched at the sight of the fox,” Clara interrupted, her heart racing at the thought of the chipped ceramic charm nestled in her pocket. She pulled it out, the surface cool against her fingers, and for a moment, it felt like a talisman against the dark uncertainty pressing in on them. “It’s like she knows something we don’t.”

Jun’s gaze darted between her and the door, the shadows deepening around them. “What if we’re wrong? What if we’re just chasing ghosts?”
