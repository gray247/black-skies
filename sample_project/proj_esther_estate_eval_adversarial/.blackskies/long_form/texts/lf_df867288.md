She could almost hear the heartbeat of the house beneath the silence, a muffled thrum that echoed the unease in her chest. Beside her, Jun adjusted the lantern, its sputtering flame flickering like a dying star. The shadows it cast seemed to stretch and swirl, pulling at her thoughts, tugging at the edges of her resolve.

“Should we knock?” Jun’s voice was a low murmur, tinged with something she couldn't quite place—fear, curiosity, or perhaps, a reluctant acceptance of the unknown. He shifted his weight, the damp coat clinging to him, a reminder of their hurried escape from the greenhouse. Clara could see the sheen of rain still glistening on the fabric, a stark contrast to the oppressive stillness of the house.

Clara swallowed hard, her eyes darting to the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon a stark slash against the pale skin of her forearm. She could feel the weight of it, the promise of access it represented, yet the memory of how it had come to be there was a gnarled knot in her mind. “It’s just a door,” she said, though her voice sounded hollow even to her ears. “What’s the worst that could happen?”

Jun chuckled softly, but the sound held no real mirth. “You’ve never been to this nursery, have you? You don’t know what’s behind it.” His gaze dropped to the floor, to the muddy prints they’d followed, now deepening the unease that settled between them like a thick fog. Clara's heart raced, a syncopation of dread and anticipation.

“I just…” She hesitated, the words tangled in her throat. “Nora said no one has been in here for years.” The memory of Nora's forced cheer washed over her, the way her eyes flickered, almost imperceptibly, at the sight of the ceramic fox tucked in her coat pocket. It was silly, a child’s trinket, yet it felt heavy with unspoken histories.

“Maybe it’s just dust and old toys,” Jun suggested, but the tremor in his voice betrayed him. “Or maybe it’s something worse.” His eyes darted to the corridor behind them, where the chain rattled softly, a ghost of a movement that sent a chill through Clara’s spine.

“Something worse?” she echoed, instinctively tightening her grip on the doorknob, its cold metal biting into her palm. The cough she had thought she’d imagined floated back to her, a sound that had no place in this house, echoing as if from a distant memory. Clara shook her head, trying to dispel the encroaching shadows of doubt. “You heard it too, didn’t you?”

Jun hesitated, his gaze momentarily distant, as if he were sorting through his own tangled memories. “I didn’t,” he finally said, but the way he looked at her—half in certainty, half in confusion—made her doubt his words.
