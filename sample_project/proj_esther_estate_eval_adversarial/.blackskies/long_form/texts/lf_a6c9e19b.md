The dim glow of the cracked brass lantern flickered, casting trembling shadows across the narrow corridor. Clara’s heart thudded in her chest, a steady drumbeat that drowned out the sound of her own breath. She tightened the red ribbon around her wrist, the parlor key cool against her skin, reminding her of the urgency that propelled them forward. The air was thick with the smell of damp earth and something more acrid, a hint of decay that made her stomach churn.

Jun stepped cautiously beside her, his coat still soaked from the rain, droplets pooling at his feet and mixing with the mud that seemed to cling to everything. “Do you think someone’s in here?” he whispered, his voice barely a breath, as if the very walls might eavesdrop.

“I don’t know,” Clara replied, too quickly, her tone sharp enough to slice through the tension. She stepped over a muddy print that had appeared before them, its edges blurred and fresh, as if someone had just passed through. “But we need to keep moving.”

The lantern sputtered again, the light dimming to a mere glow before sputtering back to life. Clara felt a flicker of panic in her stomach but pushed it down. They had come too far to turn back now. She glanced at Jun, whose eyes darted along the corridor, searching for something unseen. The fear etched into his features stirred a memory within her—a child’s cough echoing through the upper floor, ghostly and distant. She blinked, forcing the thought away.

The door at the end of the corridor loomed, its chain rattling softly as if responding to an unseen force. Clara paused, the rhythmic sound sending a shiver down her spine. “It’s just the wind,” she said, trying to sound convincing, though her voice wobbled.
