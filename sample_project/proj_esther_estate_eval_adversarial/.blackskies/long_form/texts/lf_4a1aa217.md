The lantern flickered in Jun's grasp, its light sputtering against the encroaching shadows of the narrow corridor. Clara felt the cold seep through her damp clothes, the fabric clinging to her skin like a shroud. Each breath was a rasp, echoing in the stillness that stretched out before them, the air thick and oppressive, as if the walls themselves were holding their breath. She could barely make out the muddy footprints that led deeper into the darkness, a stark contrast against the polished wood of the floor, and her pulse quickened at the thought of what—or who—had passed this way before them.

"Clara," Jun whispered, his voice barely a tremor above the silence, "do you see that?" His eyes darted nervously, the lantern's weak glow casting elongated shadows that danced like restless spirits. Clara’s gaze followed his, landing on the door at the end of the passage, its chain rattling softly as if someone was testing their resolve from the other side. She felt the urge to step back, to distance herself from the door's foreboding presence, but instead she tightened her grip on the lantern, feeling the weight of the cracked brass against her palm.

“Just the wind,” she replied too quickly, her voice sharper than she intended. She pulled at the red ribbon around her wrist, its smooth texture grounding her in the moment, but she couldn’t shake the feeling that it was somehow tighter than before, a constriction she couldn’t quite understand. The ribbon felt like a tether to something—protection or a curse, she couldn’t decide.

The corridor felt alive, the shadows creeping closer, the air thick with the scent of damp earth and something else—something sweetly rancid that clawed at her throat. “We should keep moving,” she urged, forcing her feet into motion.
