Clara pressed her palm against the rough wood of the door, feeling the grain shift under her fingertips as she fought the urge to bolt. The muffled clatter of the chain above sent a tremor through her, a warning bell resonating in her chest. She stole a glance at Jun, his features illuminated in the dim glow of the lantern, his brow furrowed in a determined frown that belied the fear lurking in his eyes. The weak light sputtered again, casting flickering shadows that danced like phantoms on the walls, each one a reminder of the secrets this house held.

“Do you think someone’s up there?” Jun’s voice was low, almost a breath, yet the question hung heavy between them, thick as the damp air. Clara swallowed hard, the taste of rain still lingering on her tongue, mingled with the musty scent of the corridor that felt too far removed from the world outside.

“I don’t know,” she replied, her voice steadier than she felt. The ribbon tied around her wrist tugged slightly as she gestured toward the staircase, the parlor key dangling just above her wrist like a promise—or a threat. “We need to check the nursery. Nora said—”

Jun interrupted, his gaze darting to the door as if expecting it to burst open. “Nora said no one has been in there for years. But that cough...”

His words trailed off, leaving a silence that seemed to stretch endlessly, filled only by the distant creaks of the house settling, as if it were shifting to accommodate their presence. Clara could feel the weight of his unsaid thoughts, a shared anxiety simmering beneath the surface. She knew he had heard the cough; she had seen it in his eyes, that flicker of recognition before she had cut him off.

“We can’t just stand here,” she said, her voice more insistent now. “The prints—”

“—they don’t make sense.” He finished for her, shaking his head. “It’s like they’re leading us somewhere.”

The muddy footprints marked the floor ahead, dark and wet against the faded wood, a trail that seemed to beckon them forward even as the air thickened with dread. Clara felt an urge to pull the chipped ceramic fox from her pocket, to cradle its familiar weight in her palm, a talisman against the uncanny. But she resisted, the memory of Nora’s reaction still sharp in her mind. What did it mean that the fox had found its way back to her?

Jun shifted, his damp coat brushing against her arm, and she could feel the chill seeping through to her skin. “Let’s just get this over with,” he said, his voice steadying as he moved toward the staircase, the lantern flickering again, a frail light against the encroaching shadows.

With each step they took, the air grew heavier, thick with the scent of must and age, as if the house itself were exhaling its secrets. Clara’s heart raced, pounding against her ribcage like a warning drum. The chain rattled again above them, a sound that felt deliberate, as if someone was testing the door from the other side, taunting them. She paused, a chill racing down her spine, and glanced at Jun, who had halted as well, the shared tension palpable between them.

“Did you hear that?” she whispered, her voice trembling slightly.

“I did.” His gaze was locked on the ceiling, where the sound had come from, his brow furrowed in concentration. “It’s like...it’s like someone’s waiting.”
