The air in the service corridor felt thick and clammy, like a damp quilt pressed against Clara’s skin. Every breath tasted of mildew and the faint tang of rust, a stark contrast to the crisp sweetness of the rain-soaked greenhouse they had just fled. Jun’s lantern flickered beside her, its weak beam barely illuminating the peeling wallpaper that seemed to cling to the walls as if seeking refuge from an unseen horror. Shadows danced in the corners, elongating with each sputter of the dying light, and Clara felt a prickle of unease crawl up her spine.

“Did you hear that?” Jun whispered, his voice tight, the crack in its usual calm laying bare his fear. He stood too close, his soaked coat brushing against her arm, the chill of the fabric mingling with the warmth of her body. She could smell the damp earth on him, a reminder of their frantic escape through the greenhouse and the creeping dread that lay ahead.

Clara’s eyes darted to the chained door that loomed at the end of the corridor, its presence heavy, almost sentient. It rattled slightly, the chain stirring like a serpent testing its bonds. “It’s just the wind,” she said, too quickly, her voice sharper than intended. She tightened her grip on the lantern, the brass cool against her palm, and felt the reassuring weight of the parlor key still tied to her wrist, the red ribbon digging into her skin in a way that both grounded and unnerved her.

But the longer they stood there, the more it felt like they were not alone. Muddy footprints snaked across the floor ahead of them, leading deeper into the shadowed passage, and Clara’s heart began to race. “We have to keep moving,” she urged, her voice barely above a whisper, a retreat into action that felt like a flimsy shield against the encroaching darkness.
