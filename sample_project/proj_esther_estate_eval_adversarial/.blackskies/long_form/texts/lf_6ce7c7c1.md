She could feel the weight of the parlor key against her wrist, the red ribbon a tether to something she couldn’t quite grasp, something that flickered like the lantern’s flame, barely holding on.

With a deep breath, she turned the knob, and the door resisted, as if it knew her intentions, a heavy silence pressing against her. Jun stepped closer, the lantern’s glow illuminating the determination etched into his features, the shadows deepening the concern in his eyes. “What if there’s someone in there?” he asked, though the hint of curiosity undermined his caution.

“Then we’ll deal with it,” she replied, more fiercely than she intended, a sudden surge of bravado pushing her forward. The door clicked as she pressed against it, a sound that reverberated through the silence, and a shudder ran through the walls, as if the house itself recoiled at the idea of being disturbed.

“Clara…” Jun’s voice was softer now, a plea that hung in the air, heavy with the weight of unspoken fears. “You heard the chain, right? It’s like someone’s testing it from the other side.”

“I know,” she said, eyes fixed on the door, the wood splintered and worn, a reminder of the lives that had passed through it, the secrets held within. “But what if it’s nothing? What if it’s just the house?”

The lantern flickered again, the light dimming to a mere whisper, and in that moment, Clara felt something shift in the atmosphere, a pulse in the air that charged her skin with prickling awareness. She could almost hear the echoes of laughter from years gone by, the distant thrum of children playing, a sound that clashed violently with the emptiness that surrounded her now.

“Maybe it’s time to go back,” Jun suggested, the words slipping out like a reluctant confession, and Clara felt a pang of disappointment at the thought. The fear that had been simmering beneath the surface rose up, a dark tide threatening to pull her under.

“No,” she said firmly, her resolve hardening like ice. “We’re here now. We owe it to ourselves to find out what’s in there.”

The door creaked as she pushed against it again, a slow, reluctant swing that revealed only darkness beyond.
