Clara pressed her palm against the nursery door, the wood cool and unyielding beneath her fingers. The faint echo of the clock's chime reverberated in her chest, the thirteenth stroke sending a ripple of dread through her. The lantern's dim light flickered, casting long shadows that writhed like specters across the peeling wallpaper. Jun stood beside her, his soaked coat clinging to him, droplets still beading on the fabric, glistening in the lantern's wan glow. He shifted from foot to foot, the sound of mud squelching beneath his shoes punctuating the heavy silence.

“This isn’t right,” he whispered, his breath fogging in the chill that seemed to seep from the very walls. “That cough… I swear I heard—”

“Don’t,” Clara interrupted, her voice sharper than intended. She could feel the weight of the ceramic fox in her pocket, a reminder of the nursery's past, of laughter and light that had long since faded. “It’s just the house settling. It always does that.”

But even as the words left her lips, doubt gnawed at her. Why had she felt the urge to glance behind her, to check for shadows lurking just out of sight? The chain rattled softly from the corridor, a sound that sent shivers down her spine. She caught Jun's eye, his gaze flickering toward the sound. “Did you hear that?” he asked, his voice barely above a whisper.

“Yes,” she admitted, swallowing hard. “But it could be… anything.” The excuse felt flimsy as it hung in the air, the tension thickening between them. They both knew that the house held secrets, some far darker than they dared to acknowledge.

Jun’s lantern sputtered again, the light dimming further, and Clara felt a knot of panic twist in her stomach. “We can’t stay out here forever,” she said, trying to sound more resolute than she felt. “We have to go in.”

“Are you sure that’s wise?” Jun replied, glancing back toward the shadows that stretched behind them. “What if—”

“Jun, please.” She gripped the key tied to the red ribbon around her wrist, its presence grounding her even as her heart raced. “We can’t just turn back now. We need to know what’s in there.”

With a reluctant nod, he stepped closer, the lantern illuminating the door's surface, revealing grooves that told of wear and neglect. Clara's heart thudded in her chest, matching the rhythm of the clock somewhere above. She took a breath, the air thick with the scent of damp wood and something else—something musty, forgotten.

“On three,” she said, her voice steadier than she felt. She could almost hear the whispers of the nursery's past, the laughter of children echoing through the years. “One… two…”

The door creaked open at her touch, revealing darkness beyond, a void that seemed to swallow the light whole. Clara stepped inside, her footfall muffled by the thick dust that had settled like a shroud. The lantern flickered, casting ghostly shapes that danced across the room's corners, where faded toys lay abandoned, the dust thick enough to blanket them in memories.

“Clara?” Jun’s voice quivered, barely audible, as if he feared the shadows might respond. “What if there’s something in here?”

“Then we’ll face it,” she replied, the determination in her voice bolstered by the need to protect the fragile threads of her past. She pushed further into the room, her fingers trailing along the wall, tracing the contours of faded murals that spoke of dreams long forgotten.
