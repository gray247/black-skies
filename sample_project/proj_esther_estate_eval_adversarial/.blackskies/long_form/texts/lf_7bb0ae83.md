Clara pressed her palm against the cool wood of the nursery door, the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket pressing against her thigh as if it were alive with urgent whispers. The lantern flickered low beside Jun, its weak glow casting elongated shadows that danced across the doorframe, momentarily illuminating the dust motes swirling in the stale air. She felt the weight of the key on her wrist, the red ribbon a lifeline she clutched tighter, though she couldn't remember tying it in the first place.

“Do you think we should knock?” Jun’s voice was barely a whisper, an echo of uncertainty that matched the shudder in her chest. The rain had soaked through his coat, the damp fabric clinging to him like the shadows that seemed to wrap around them in the dim corridor. He shifted his weight, his gaze darting to the darkened windows of the nursery. “I mean, what if someone’s inside?”

Clara closed her eyes for a heartbeat, listening—not to the steady rhythm of their breathing or the distant groans of the house settling around them, but to something deeper, something that felt like the very pulse of the walls. The cough she thought she’d imagined echoed in her mind, and she shook her head. “There hasn’t been a child here for years,” she said, a quiet resolve threading her voice, though doubt etched deeper lines on her brow.

“Still... what if—” Jun began, but she cut him off, the sharpness of her tone surprising even herself.

“Then we’ll find out,” she insisted, her fingers trembling as she fumbled with the key, the ribbon slipping slightly over her wrist, a reminder of the last time she felt secure. “We can’t leave this locked up forever.” The key slid into the lock with a dull click, but the door resisted, the wood creaking almost in protest.

“Clara, wait,” he said, his hand reaching for her elbow, but she pushed the door, the weight of it forcing her forward. The lantern flickered again, casting a momentary glow across the room inside, revealing faded wallpaper and a dust-laden crib that seemed to cradle only silence.

Inside, the air was thick, heavy with the scent of old wood and something faintly sweet, a remnant of childhood laughter that had long since faded. Clara stepped over the threshold, her heart pounding in her ears, drowning out the creaks of the house. “Look,” she pointed to the far corner where the nursery chest sat, its surface unmarred by the years, untouched by time. “It’s been moved.”

Jun followed her gaze, his brow furrowing. “But it shouldn’t have been. It was always next to the window.” There was a tremor in his voice, a fear that lodged itself in the back of her throat.

“Someone’s been here,” Clara whispered, the words tasting bitter on her tongue. She stepped further in, drawn toward the chest, the dim light barely revealing its intricacies. She could feel the weight of the house pressing down, the walls closing in, a suffocating reminder of all that had been lost.

“Clara,” Jun called, his voice strained, pulling her back to the present, but it felt like a distant echo. She turned, the lantern sputtering out a last gasp of light, and for a moment, they were cloaked in darkness, the shadows layering thick like memories they couldn’t quite grasp.
