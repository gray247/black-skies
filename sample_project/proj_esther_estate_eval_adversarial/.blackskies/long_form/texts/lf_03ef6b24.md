Jun’s breath fogged in the frigid air as he squeezed the cracked brass lantern, its flickering glow barely pushing back the shadows. "Clara, did you really hear something?" he asked, voice taut with uncertainty. Clara's heart thudded, a heavy echo in her chest that matched the oppressive silence of the corridor. She shook her head, but the memory of that cough lingered, jagged and insistent, clinging to the edges of her mind like the dampness in the air.

The walls seemed to shift, the wood creaking as if the house were alive, a body breathing around them. Clara’s fingers brushed the smooth surface of the parlor key, its weight both a comfort and a curse. She tightened the red ribbon around her wrist, a reflexive gesture, feeling the fibers dig into her skin. It was an anchor, a lifeline tethered to sanity in a place that threatened to unravel it. “We should keep moving,” she finally said, her voice steadier than she felt.

They moved deeper into the corridor, the floorboards groaning underfoot, each step a reminder of the burden they carried. With every dim flicker of the lantern, Clara caught glimpses of the dusty remnants of a life once vibrant—faded floral wallpaper peeling at the edges, the distant echo of laughter caught in the cobwebs. But laughter was long gone, replaced by the chill of isolation that seeped into her bones.

The chain rattled behind them, a metallic whisper that made Clara’s stomach twist. “Did you feel that?” Jun stopped, eyes wide, scanning their surroundings as if the very walls might divulge their secrets. She wanted to reach for him, to bridge the gap between fear and comfort, but the distance felt too vast, too precarious.

“Just the house settling,” she said, forcing a laugh that felt brittle in her throat. The sound echoed back, mocking. Yet, the uncertainty in Jun’s gaze mirrored her own, a reflection of the weight of the unspoken between them. She glanced at the nursery door ahead, its surface worn and painted shut, an impenetrable barrier that housed whatever memories it guarded.

“Do you think it’s locked?” he asked, stepping closer, the lantern casting a dance of shadows against the door. Clara could feel the pull of the key on her wrist, a silent invitation, but her mind raced with images of the last time they’d tried to unlock something that should have remained sealed. The taste of regret lingered on her tongue.

“Let’s just try it,” she said, voice barely above a whisper. Her hand trembled as she approached the door, the wood cool against her palm. She could almost hear the heartbeat of the house, a rhythm that matched her own increasing pulse. “If we’re going to find out what’s up there…”

“Clara, wait.” Jun’s voice broke through her thoughts, urgency lacing his words. “What if we shouldn’t?”

But the thought of turning back sent a chill racing down her spine. The muddy prints had led them here, and the echo of that cough still haunted her. “We have to know,” she insisted, the resolve hardening in her chest as she inserted the key into the lock. It fit snugly; she could feel the promise of revelation thrumming in the air.

As she twisted the key, the lock clicked, a sound so loud in the stillness that it felt like a crack of thunder. The door shuddered but did not yield. Clara pressed against it, and the wood groaned, resisting her touch. A fleeting thought brushed her mind—what if it was meant to stay closed?
