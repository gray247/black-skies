The dim light of the cracked brass lantern flickered as Jun Park moved deeper into the narrow confines of the servants' passage. The air was thick with the scent of damp plaster and something sharper, a hint of decay that clung to the walls like an unwelcome memory. Clara trailed a step behind, her heart thudding in time with the soft patter of rain against the ceiling above. She could still feel the weight of the parlor key around her wrist, the red ribbon digging into her skin, a constant reminder of the choices that had brought them here.

“Are you sure we should be doing this?” Jun's voice wavered, a thread of panic weaving through his words. He turned the lantern, its beam sweeping across the corridor, illuminating the peeling wallpaper that seemed to peel away from the walls in desperation. Each shadow seemed to twist into a shape, a fleeting specter that made the hairs on the back of Clara's neck stand on end.

“It’s just a door,” Clara snapped, the sharpness of her tone surprising even herself. She swallowed hard, forcing her voice to steady. “We need to see what's behind it.” The sound of her own voice echoed back at her, hollow and unsettling. She took a breath, trying to calm the storm of thoughts swirling in her mind. The child’s cough she had not heard resonated in her memory, a ghostly reminder of something she couldn’t quite grasp.

As they moved further along the corridor, muddy footprints appeared ahead of them—fresh, as if someone had just passed through. Clara's stomach tightened at the sight. “Look,” she whispered, pointing ahead. “Someone’s been here.”
