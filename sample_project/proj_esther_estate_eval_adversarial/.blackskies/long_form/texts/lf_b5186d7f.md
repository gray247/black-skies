The lantern flickered, its feeble glow casting long, quivering shadows across the nursery door, which groaned as if the house itself were holding its breath. Clara’s heart thudded against her ribcage, a frantic beat that mirrored the storm raging outside. A damp chill seeped through her clothes, clinging to her skin like the unease that filled the air. Jun held the cracked brass lantern in his trembling hands, its flame sputtering and casting shadows that twisted and shifted, taunting her with shapes just beyond her comprehension.

“Are you sure we should be here?” Jun whispered, his voice barely above the whisper of the wind outside, as if the walls might overhear. Water dripped from his coat, pooling on the floorboards and darkening the old wood, which creaked under the weight of their uncertainty. Clara didn’t answer right away; her fingers traced the outline of the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon digging into her skin, a painful reminder of the chaos swirling in her mind.

“I—I don’t know,” she finally replied, her voice quavering, each word laced with the weight of her doubt. Her gaze flitted to the door, splintered and imposing, then back to Jun, whose furrowed brow betrayed his worry. “Nora said the nursery hasn’t been used in years. But—”

“Clara,” he interrupted, his eyes locked on the door as if it might reveal its secrets at any moment. “That cough… it sounded like a child.”

Her breath caught, the echo of the sound wrapping around her like a shroud. She could almost hear it again, soft yet persistent, tugging at something deep within her. But the nursery had been abandoned, hadn’t it? Yet here they stood, clutching the remnants of a past that felt both distant and achingly close.

“Maybe it was just the wind,” Clara said, though her voice wavered. She felt the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket, its surface rough against her fingers, a relic from a childhood she could barely recall, its paint worn away like the memories of laughter that once filled these halls. “We shouldn’t linger here.”

“Then let’s open it,” Jun urged, stepping closer, his eyes glinting with a mixture of fear and determination. Clara noticed the tension in his shoulders, the way his breath quickened, matching her own rising anxiety. With a surge of resolve, she stepped forward, her fingers brushing against the cool, weathered wood of the door, her heart pounding in time with the distant ticking of the clock on the landing.

She withdrew the key from the ribbon, its metal cool and oddly foreign in her palm, and pressed it against the lock. It felt wrong, an unsettling heaviness in her grip; the door loomed before her like a guardian of buried secrets. She hesitated, the key hovering just above the lock, and for a heartbeat, the world shrank to the sound of rain outside, its soft patter mingling with the hurried thrum of her pulse.

“Clara,” Jun’s voice broke through her reverie, urgent and raw. “We need to know what’s in there. If there’s a child…” His words hung between them, heavy and pregnant with implications, like the musty scent of dust that permeated the air.

With a shaky breath, Clara turned the key. The lock clicked, a sharp sound that shattered the stillness, and her heart raced as the door creaked open, unveiling an abyss that seemed to swallow the light whole. For a fleeting moment, she stood frozen at the threshold, caught between the known and the unknown, the thrill of what lay ahead tinged with an overwhelming sense of dread.
