Clara’s grip tightened around the lantern as they moved deeper into the corridor, the weak beam of light flickering like a heartbeat against the walls. The shadows seemed to stretch and retreat, whispering secrets in the silence that enveloped them. Each step echoed, an unsettling reminder of their isolation in this damp, forgotten place. She could feel the cool metal of the parlor key pressing against her wrist, a persistent reminder of her fraying resolve.

“Clara, we shouldn’t—” Jun hesitated, his voice trembling as he turned to peer back at the door they had just come through. The chain rattled softly, a ghostly echo as if something was testing the lock from the other side. “What if someone’s...?”

“Stop it,” she snapped, the edge in her tone sharp enough to cut through the murk. The words tumbled out before she could catch them, leaving an uncomfortable silence in their wake. Clara hated how quickly fear could unravel her composure, yet the atmosphere pressed in on her, thick and suffocating. She forced herself to breathe, to focus. “We need to find out what’s going on.”

Jun swallowed hard, his coat still dripping with the remnants of their escape from the greenhouse, water pooling at his feet, mingling with the mud that had somehow found its way inside. “I just... I don’t like this. Those prints—” He gestured ahead, where the muddy footprints trailed into the darkness, leading them deeper into uncertainty. “They’re fresh.”

“I know,” she said, feeling her heart race. The prints were alarming, but more unsettling was the void left by their absence—no one had followed them, yet someone else had been here, and recently. “But we can’t turn back now. We have to see what’s at the end of this corridor.”
