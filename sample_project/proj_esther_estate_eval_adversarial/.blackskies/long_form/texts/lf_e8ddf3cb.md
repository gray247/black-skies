Clara stepped cautiously into the corridor, the cold air licking at her exposed skin, a reminder of the rain-soaked greenhouse they had just escaped. Jun followed closely, his coat still dripping, water pooling at his feet as the lantern's feeble glow flickered and dimmed, painting ghostly shadows across the walls. Each step felt heavier as if the very floor was pulling her down, urging her to turn back. The muddy footprints ahead twisted into the darkness, a path that felt both inviting and menacing, a whispered invitation that sent shivers racing up her spine.

“Did you see that?” Jun’s voice trembled, a sharp edge to it that sliced through the thick silence. His eyes darted toward the door at the end of the corridor, where the chain rattled with an unsettling rhythm, as if someone was testing it from the other side. Clara wished she could dismiss his fear, but the knot in her stomach tightened, a familiar dread unfurling within her.

“No.” She answered too quickly, the sharpness of her tone surprising even herself. “It’s just the wind.”

“Wind doesn’t make footprints.” He stepped closer, squinting into the dimness. Clara could see the glint of fear in his eyes, mirrored in the way his hands trembled around the lantern. The brass was warm against his palm, but it felt like a relic of comfort in a world gone awry.

She tugged at the red ribbon around her wrist, feeling the key's weight against her skin, a tether to something solid amidst the uncertainty. It had been tied tightly moments before, yet now it felt looser, the fabric slipping through her fingers like a memory she couldn’t quite grasp. “We should keep moving,” Clara urged, her voice steadier than she felt.
