The air grew heavier, clinging to her skin with a clammy embrace, making every breath feel labored. “We shouldn’t be here,” she whispered, half to herself, half to Jun, whose worry was palpable, manifesting in the way his fingers twitched around the lantern.

“Maybe we should turn back,” he suggested, his voice barely above a murmur, but Clara could see the spark of her own fear reflected in his eyes. She hated that he was frightened; she hated that she was frightened. “It’s just a corridor,” she shot back, sharper than intended, the words tasting like ash on her tongue. She tugged at the red ribbon around her wrist, feeling the parlor key's reassuring weight but unable to remember when it had grown so tight. It was a tether, a reminder of the world outside this oppressive gloom.

The chain rattled again from the opposite side of the door, a cold jangle that echoed through the corridor and froze her in place.
