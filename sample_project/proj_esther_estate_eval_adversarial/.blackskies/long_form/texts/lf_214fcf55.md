Clara pressed deeper into the corridor, the weak beam of the lantern flickering as if it were caught in a struggle of its own. Shadows danced on the stone walls, twisting and sprawling like tendrils of her own anxiety. She could feel the dampness in the air clinging to her skin, a clammy reminder that they were far from safe. With every step, the echo of her heart drummed in her ears, competing against the incessant drip of water somewhere in the depths of the passage. Jun lagged behind, his coat still drenched from the greenhouse, the fabric clinging to him like a heavy shroud.

“Did you hear that?” he whispered, his voice barely rising above the muted sounds of the house, a tremor betraying the facade of calm he tried to maintain. Clara paused, straining to listen, but all she could hear was the relentless whispering of the wind outside, mingling with the soft, unsettling sounds that seemed to rise from the very walls around them. She shook her head, forcing a smile that felt brittle on her lips, though inside she felt the familiar pulse of dread unfurling like a dark flower.

“It’s just the wind, Jun. It’s always making strange noises in the old parts of the house.” The words slipped out too fast, sharp and brittle, and she immediately regretted her tone. She turned, catching the flash of fear in his eyes, and felt a pang of guilt.
