The lantern flickered in Jun’s grasp, its light sputtering like a weak heartbeat against the suffocating darkness of the corridor. Clara glanced at him, her heart racing, not from the chill in the air but from the uneasy stillness that enveloped them. The damp fabric of his coat clung to his skin, the scent of wet earth mingling with the acrid tang of oil from the lantern. She could see it in his eyes—the fear had settled in there, deep and raw.

“Are you sure we should be here?” Jun asked, his voice barely above a whisper, as if the shadows could hear him. The words hung between them, a thread stretched taut, threatening to snap. Clara felt the weight of the parlor key against her wrist, the red ribbon soft and strange, an odd comfort tethered to uncertainty.

“Of course,” she replied too quickly, too sharply. The defiance in her voice masked the tremor in her hands. She tightened her grip on the chipped ceramic fox nestled in her pocket, its edges rough against her palm. “We can’t just stand here.”

The corridor stretched ahead, lined with walls that seemed to lean in, shrouded in a suffocating darkness. The flickering lantern cast erratic shadows, twisting the shapes into grotesque forms that danced around them. Each step they took echoed, a hollow sound swallowed by the oppressive quiet. The air was thick, heavy with something unspeakable, as if the walls themselves were exhaling secrets.

Jun paused, the light dimming again, and Clara felt the tension in his shoulders. “Clara, did you hear that?” he asked, a tremor lacing his voice.
