The lantern flickered, its dying light casting tremulous shadows that seemed to pulse with each shallow breath she took. She could feel the dampness from the air seep into the fabric of her clothes, the chill creeping under her skin as if the house had reached out to caress her with a cold, clammy hand.

“Should we?” Jun’s voice broke the quiet, low and urgent, but it felt like part of the house’s echo, a whisper from the walls that knew more than they dared to imagine. He shifted his weight, the soaked fabric of his coat rustling softly, and she caught a glimpse of his eyes, dark pools reflecting the lantern’s waning glow. It was as if he were waiting for her decision, though the weight of it pressed down on her like the thick fog that seemed to wrap around the world outside.

“It’s just a locked door,” she murmured, though her voice trembled. The words felt hollow, a flimsy shield against the tight knot of fear that twisted in her stomach. Yet, as she glanced at the key tied around her wrist, the red ribbon stark against her pale skin, a flicker of resolve ignited within her. She had come this far; she couldn’t turn back now.

“Nothing’s just a locked door in this house,” Jun replied, his brow furrowing as he tilted the lantern closer, illuminating the chipped edges of the doorframe. A sense of foreboding hung thick in the air, mingling with the lingering scent of lavender and damp wood. The house felt alive, breathing with her, and she swore she could hear the faintest echo of a child’s cough, soft and distant, curling around her thoughts like a wisp of smoke.

Clara pressed her palm against the door, fingers brushing against the cold, peeling surface. “There’s something in there,” she said, more to herself than to Jun, as her mind raced with possibilities. The nursery—once a place of laughter and innocence—now felt like a tomb, a repository of all the things they had lost. She could almost see the shadow of a little girl, skipping across the floor in a sundress, hair bouncing with each step. But that girl was gone, and what remained was a hollow ache that echoed through the bones of the house.

“Maybe we should wait for Nora,” Jun suggested, his tone cautious, but Clara felt the urgency in his voice, a desperate wish to avoid whatever lay behind the door. But as the chain rattled faintly from the corridor, her resolve hardened. The sounds were unsettling, like whispers of secrets buried too deep to remain hidden.

“No,” she said, her voice firmer now. “We can’t wait. We need to know.” With a swift motion, she unwrapped the key from her wrist, the red ribbon sliding like silk through her fingers. The moment felt charged, a pulse of electricity thrumming between them as she placed the key into the lock. It fit snugly, a perfect match for the tension in the air, and with a turn, she felt the mechanism give way.

The door creaked open, revealing a nursery steeped in shadows, the air inside stale and thick with the scent of dust. The dim light from the lantern barely penetrated the darkness, illuminating a room frozen in time, with faded toys scattered like memories left behind. Clara stepped inside, her breath catching in her throat as the floorboards groaned beneath her weight, a reminder that she was not alone in this stillness.
