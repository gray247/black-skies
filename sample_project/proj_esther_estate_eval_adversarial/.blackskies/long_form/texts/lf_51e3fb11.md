The air in the sealed nursery felt thick and stagnant, as if the walls themselves had absorbed years of silence. Clara stood before the door, her heart thrumming against her ribcage like a trapped bird. The cracked brass lantern flickered weakly in Jun's grasp, casting long shadows that stretched and quivered across the peeling wallpaper, a faded garden where flowers had long since wilted. She could feel the weight of the ceramic fox in her coat pocket, a strange comfort and an unsettling reminder of all that had been forgotten—or perhaps never truly known.

“Do you think it’s locked?” Jun’s voice came low, edged with uncertainty, his soaked coat clinging to him like a second skin. A bead of water slipped from the hem and pooled on the floor, mingling with the dust, a small testament to their frantic escape from the greenhouse.

Clara nodded, though her throat felt tight. “Nora said no one’s been in here for years,” she whispered, her eyes scanning the dim-lit corridor. The chain on the door rattled above them, the sound a brittle echo that seemed to resonate with a pulse of its own. Something—or someone—was testing it from the other side.

Jun shifted his weight, the lantern sputtering again, the light waning further as if in response to their apprehension. “But shouldn’t the chain be two floors down?” He frowned, his brow furrowing deeper as he cast a glance upward, where the shadows pooled thicker, darker. Clara felt the urge to step back, to retreat from the oppressive presence of the nursery door, but something held her there, a magnetic pull that was both fearsome and intoxicating.

“Maybe it’s just the house settling,” she offered, though her voice shook, betraying her own doubt. The old bones of the structure creaked and sighed around them, but the unnerving sensation of being watched lingered, more palpable than any draft. She tightened her grip on the red ribbon that bound the key to her wrist, the frayed edges brushing against her skin like an anxious whisper.

“No child has slept in there for ages,” Jun said, his tone dropping as if the very mention of it might summon something from the shadows. He glanced at her, and for a moment, Clara saw the reflection of her own worry mirrored in his dark eyes. It was a look she recognized too well, a tapestry woven from their shared history of secrets and lies.

“Still…” Her voice trailed off, swallowed by the heavy air, and she cast her gaze back toward the door, her heart racing with the need to know. What lay beyond? Memories, perhaps, or echoes of laughter turned to whispers? She pressed her palm against the wood, feeling its coolness seep into her skin, and closed her eyes, imagining the room beyond—the dust motes dancing in shafts of light, the faded wallpaper telling stories of children who had once roamed free.

Jun shifted beside her, his breath hitching as he hesitated. “If there’s really someone coughing in there…” His words hung between them, a fragile thread of possibility that sent a shiver down her spine. She could not shake the notion that the house had secrets, dark corners that twisted into something sinister, but she couldn’t bring herself to voice that fear, not yet.

“Let’s just see.” The words slipped from her mouth before she could think better of them, fueled by a reckless courage that felt foreign. She fished the key from her wrist, the red ribbon trailing like a lifeline, and glanced back at Jun, who looked both relieved and apprehensive, as if he were about to step into a dream from which he might not awaken.

“Okay, but if anything—”

“I know,” she interrupted, the urgency mounting as she inserted the key into the lock.
