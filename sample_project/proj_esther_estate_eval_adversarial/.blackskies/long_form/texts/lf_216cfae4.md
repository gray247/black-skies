“I don’t know,” she replied, her voice trembling against the stillness of the house. The thought of knocking felt like a betrayal, as if she might summon something not meant to be disturbed. Was it merely the oppressive silence that made her heart race, or was there something lurking behind that door, something she dared not confront? The chain rattled faintly above them, an echo of a presence unseen, and she felt the urge to retreat.

But the key, tied around her wrist, pulled her back. It was a promise she had made to herself, a whisper of resolve that urged her forward. She glanced at Jun, who was watching her, uncertainty etched in the furrow of his brow. “We can’t just stand here,” she said, more to herself than to him, the words spilling out like a defiance against the thickening tension. “We need to see what’s inside.”

With a deep breath, Clara fished the key from her wrist, its smooth surface cool against her palm. She hesitated for a moment, the weight of the decision pressing down on her. The house seemed to exhale, a shift in the air that sent a shiver down her spine. She inserted the key into the lock, its teeth catching and grinding against the mechanism. The sound was jarring, a discordant note in the otherwise quiet corridor.

“Wait,” Jun said, his hand reaching out to still her. “What if—”

“Jun,” she interrupted, a sudden surge of determination coursing through her. “We can’t keep waiting for something to happen. We’re here now.” She turned the key, and the lock clicked, the sound resonating in the silence like a thunderclap. The nursery was cloaked in an unsettling stillness, the air thick with the scent of dust and something more vague, something that felt like loss. Clara stepped inside, her heart pounding in her chest, every instinct screaming at her to turn back. The sunlight filtering through the painted-shut window cast a dim light that barely illuminated the edges of the room, bringing life to the outlines of the forgotten toys and the old rocking chair that stood sentinel in the corner.

“Do you hear that?” Jun’s voice cut through the stillness, laced with urgency. Clara strained to listen, her own breathing muffled in her ears. There it was—a faint cough, like a child’s, echoing softly from somewhere deep within the nursery’s shadows. It sent a chill racing down her spine, igniting a flare of fear that twisted in her gut.
