“Maybe it’s not locked,” he murmured, but the uncertainty in his voice made Clara’s stomach twist. “Let’s just try,” she said, trying to exude a confidence she didn’t feel. Her heart drummed in her chest, a wild rhythm that matched the sharp thumping of the clock overhead, each strike a reminder that time was slipping away. Or perhaps it was some other measure of time, one that didn’t adhere to the clock’s hands, one that lurked in the shadows and crevices of the house, waiting for them to falter.

“Clara…” Jun’s voice fell into an uneasy silence, and she could see the hesitation etched on his brow, the way his gaze flickered to the door and then back to her, as if daring to voice the fears that loomed between them. She took a breath, feeling the damp air fill her lungs, thick and heavy like the weight of unspoken words.

“Just… hold the lantern steady, okay?” she said, her voice steadier than she felt. The shadows danced with every flicker of light, and she could almost hear them whispering, secrets swirling around them like smoke. She turned the key again, and this time, there was a soft click—an almost inaudible sigh escaping the door’s worn hinges.

The door creaked open, revealing a narrow, dark space beyond, the air stale and cold, as if it hadn’t been touched in years. Clara stepped inside, her pulse quickening, every instinct screaming at her to retreat, but the curiosity—the need to know what lay within—pulled her forward. Jun followed closely, the lantern casting long, wavering shadows against the walls, illuminating dust motes that danced in the air like tiny stars caught in a cosmic web.

“What if something’s in here?” he whispered, glancing over his shoulder, his voice taut with caution.
