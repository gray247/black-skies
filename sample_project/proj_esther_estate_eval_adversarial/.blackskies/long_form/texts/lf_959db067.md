Clara pressed her palm harder against the nursery door, feeling the rough grain of the wood bite into her skin, a visceral reminder of the barrier between her and whatever lay beyond. The cold seeped through her fingertips, sending a shiver racing up her arm, as if the door itself were alive, a creature reluctant to reveal its secrets. She glanced back at Jun, whose face was a mixture of concern and trepidation, the dim light from the sputtering lantern casting shadows that danced across his features.

“Clara,” he murmured, his voice low and urgent, “we don’t have to do this.” The warmth of his breath brushed against her, mingling with the stale air that clung to the corridor, thick like the weight of their unspoken fears. But she couldn’t turn away. Not now.

With a deep breath, she turned her attention back to the door, her heart thrumming in her chest like a wild thing. The latch was old, rusted, and she could almost hear it whispering tales of the past, of laughter and tears, of children who once played and perhaps still lingered in the shadows. She reached for the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon soft against her skin, and for a fleeting moment, she hesitated. Hadn’t Nora said no child had slept in this nursery for years? And yet, the memory of that cough—sharp and hollow—echoed in her mind, a haunting presence that refused to fade.

“Do you think we should—” Jun started, but Clara cut him off, a sudden surge of defiance filling her. The lantern flickered again, its light sputtering as if it too feared the darkness that awaited them.

“I have to know,” she said, her voice stronger than she felt. “There’s something in here. Something…” She trailed off, uncertain how to articulate the sensation clawing at her insides, the blend of grief and curiosity that twisted in her gut.

Jun shifted behind her, the sound of his wet coat rustling like leaves against a biting wind. “The prints,” he said, his tone edged with urgency. “They didn’t just appear out of nowhere. What if someone—”

“Stop.” Clara shot him a look, a mixture of desperation and anger. “What if it’s just… just the house?” The words hung in the air, heavy and fraught. She could feel the weight of the world pressing down on them, the house settling and groaning around them, as if it were alive and aware of their presence.

Just then, a faint sound echoed from above, a cough that rolled like thunder through the silence, wrapping around her heart like a vice. Jun’s eyes went wide, and she could see the flicker of fear in his gaze, though he remained silent. The lantern dimmed again, the light dwindling to a mere whisper of flame, but Clara felt the heat of her resolve grow.

Resolutely, she placed the key in the lock, the metal cool against her palm. It slid in smoothly, and she twisted it, a sharp click reverberating in the air. The door didn’t budge. “Why isn’t it opening?” she whispered, her voice barely breaking the stillness.

“Try again,” Jun urged, stepping closer, his breath warm against her ear. The lantern flickered ominously, and the shadows seemed to shift, forming shapes that danced just beyond her periphery. She turned the key again, and again, her fingers trembling as the lock held firm, as if it were a guardian of secrets she wasn’t meant to uncover.
