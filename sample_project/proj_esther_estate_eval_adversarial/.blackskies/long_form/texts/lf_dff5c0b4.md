Clara pulled back from the door, her heart thudding like a drum against her ribcage, the faint thump echoing in her ears, punctuating the unsettling silence that hung in the air. The wood felt cold and unyielding beneath her palm as she turned to Jun, his brow knitted in thought, droplets from his wet coat pooling on the floorboards beneath their feet. The lantern flickered weakly, its light barely pushing through the dim corridor, shadows stretching and curling like fingers along the walls, hinting at secrets lurking just beyond sight.

“What if there’s someone in there?” Jun’s voice was low, laced with an edge of urgency that sent a shiver crawling up Clara’s spine. She caught his gaze, searching for reassurance but finding only uncertainty reflected back at her.

“Then we should—” she began, but the words snagged in her throat, tangled with the memory of that cough she had not heard, the sound that had drawn Jun’s attention but eluded her own senses. It felt as if the house itself was breathing, inhaling softly, yet Clara could not shake the feeling that something was watching them, waiting just beyond that door.

The lantern sputtered again, its weak glow dimming further, casting their faces in a ghostly light. Clara glanced at the red ribbon tied tightly around her wrist, the parlor key dangling from it like a lifeline. She could hardly remember the last time she had adjusted the knot, yet it felt secure, almost as if it were protecting her from whatever lay ahead. “We have to try the key,” she said, her voice steadier than she felt.

Jun hesitated, his eyes darting to the door’s brass handle, then back to her. “What if it’s locked for a reason?” His fingers grazed the lantern, a nervous habit that spoke of deeper fears than the darkness surrounding them.

“Or what if it’s not?” Clara countered, her own resolve hardening. “We need to know.” The words hung in the air, charged with a mixture of dread and determination. Jun took a breath, the damp scent of rain still clinging to him. Clara could see the internal battle waging behind his eyes—the desire to forge ahead clashing with the instinct to retreat.

Finally, he nodded, though his expression remained guarded, as if bracing for a storm. Clara stepped closer to the door, her fingers trembling slightly as she reached for the key. The metal felt cool and foreign as she inserted it into the lock, her heart racing with each click.

Before she could turn it, the sound of chains rattling echoed from above, a sharp contrast to the stillness that enveloped them. Clara froze, her breath hitching in her throat. “Did you hear that?” she whispered, her voice barely above a breath.

Jun’s eyes widened, and he nodded slowly, the fear in his gaze mirroring her own. “It’s coming from the service corridor,” he said, glancing over his shoulder as if expecting something to leap out at them from the shadows. The sound faded, leaving only the low hum of uncertainty in its wake, the house around them settling like a living creature.

Clara turned back to the door, her hand growing clammy around the key. “I have to know. We have to know.” The mantra rolled through her mind, pushing her forward. She twisted the key again, the lock clicking softly, a sound that seemed both a promise and a warning.
