Clara’s fingers trembled as she raised the parlor key to the nursery door, its cold metal biting into her palm. The air was thick with the musty scent of aged wood and something else—something she couldn't quite place, a lingering hint of childhood fears. The lantern flickered again, sputtering like a tired old man, casting long, wavering shadows that danced over the peeling wallpaper.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice low and strained, “maybe we should just go back.” He shifted beside her, his coat still soaked from the rain, the damp fabric pressing against his skin like a second layer of anxiety. Clara could see the droplets glisten on his cheeks, reflecting the dim light as they pooled at his feet, merging with the muddy prints that seemed to lead nowhere and everywhere at once.

“No,” she replied, her voice barely above a whisper, thick with dread. She forced herself to look up at him, searching his eyes for reassurance, but found only the same flicker of uncertainty that mirrored her own. The tension between them thickened, a silent acknowledgment of the fear that coiled in their chests, constricting like a serpent.

The lantern dimmed further, the light now barely a whisper, and Clara turned her attention back to the door. It loomed before them, the wood dark and unyielding, the knob cold under her touch. She hesitated, her heart pounding in rhythm with the distant echo of the clock striking thirteen overhead.

“Do you hear it?” Jun asked suddenly, his voice dropping even lower, as if the very walls might eavesdrop. Clara frowned, straining to listen, but all she could hear was the oppressive silence and the faint sound of her own breathing. The cough—could it have been a figment of her imagination? Her mind raced, flitting between possibilities, but she couldn’t shake the feeling that they were not alone.

“I don’t hear anything,” she replied, her words laced with a tension that belied her growing unease. The chain on the corridor door rattled softly, a sound like a whispering child, and Clara felt a shiver race down her spine. She glanced up at Jun, who was staring at the door with wide eyes, his expression shifting from concern to something darker, a shadow of dread crossing his features.

“Clara,” he said, his voice a bare breath, “what if there’s someone in there?”

A flicker of panic surged within her, but she clenched her jaw, forcing herself to breathe. “Then we’ll find out,” she said, resolute, though her heart thudded erratically against her ribs. The red ribbon binding the key around her wrist felt both like a lifeline and a noose, tightening with every moment of uncertainty.

With a sudden burst of courage, she turned the key in the lock. It fit, surprisingly smooth against the rusted metal, but as she twisted it, the door didn’t budge. A weight pressed against it from the inside, as if something—or someone—was holding it shut. Clara’s breath hitched, a wild instinct warning her to retreat, but she stood firm, muscles taut with the expectation of confrontation.

“Clara,” Jun repeated, his voice fraying, “we should—”

“Just give me a moment,” she interrupted, her voice sharper than she intended. She felt a flicker of guilt for cutting him off, but the weight of the moment bore down on her, and the memory of their conversation with Nora echoed in her mind. “No child has slept in the nursery for years,” she recalled, the way Nora’s eyes had flickered when she spoke, a small crack in her practiced composure.
