The air in the service corridor was thick with dampness, each breath a reminder of the rain-soaked greenhouse they had just escaped. Clara pressed her back against the cool, peeling wall, the chipped ceramic fox in her pocket pressing against her thigh like a weight she couldn’t quite bear. She could feel the weight of the parlor key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon a stark contrast against her cold skin, a tether to something she had no desire to confront just yet.

Jun shifted beside her, the lantern flickering in his grip, its weak light casting shadows that danced like restless spirits on the wall. “Do you think anyone’s up there?” he whispered, his voice barely breaking through the silence. His coat clung to him, still drenched from the downpour, and she could see the way his shoulders hunched, as if the weight of the world pressed down upon him.

“I don’t know,” Clara snapped, her tone sharper than intended. The last thing she needed was to feed his fear, but the chain rattled again, a metallic clink that reverberated through the narrow passage. Her heart raced, each pulse a drum echoing in her ears. “We should just—”

“Just what?” Jun interrupted, his eyes wide, reflecting the lantern’s sputtering glow. “You heard it too. Something’s behind that door.” He pointed toward the heavy wooden barrier, the chain quivering as if it were alive, testing the strength of its bonds.
