Clara took a shallow breath, the damp air clinging to her lungs like a shroud. The narrow corridor felt both confining and endless, shadows draping over the cracked plaster walls, the flickering lantern casting fragmented light that barely reached the far end. Jun’s grip on the brass handle was tight, his knuckles white against the dark fabric of his soaked coat. “Do you hear that?” he whispered, his voice cracking like the brittle light above them.

She shook her head, though a tiny part of her, the part that still believed in the ghosts of childhood, felt a shiver race down her spine. “What do you mean?” The words came out sharper than she intended, echoing in the confined space, and she rubbed the smooth ceramic of the fox in her pocket, as if it could offer her some sort of comfort or protection.

“It sounded like… a cough. From upstairs.” Jun’s eyes darted toward the ceiling, where dust motes danced in the wavering light. Clara could feel the weight of his gaze pressing down on her, searching for reassurance she didn’t have. She felt the parlor key tugging at her wrist, the ribbon a reminder of a door she wasn’t ready to open, a threshold she had no desire to cross.

“I don’t hear anything,” she replied, forcing a calmness into her tone, though her heart raced against the quiet. The chain on the door rattled again, a low metallic echo that sent her pulse racing. The sound was too deliberate, too alive, as if someone were testing their hold on the other side. “We should keep moving.”

Jun nodded, though uncertainty shadowed his features. He stepped forward, the lantern sputtering dimly, and the light caught a series of muddy footprints leading away from them, fresh and stark against the cold stone. Clara’s stomach twisted. Where were they going?
