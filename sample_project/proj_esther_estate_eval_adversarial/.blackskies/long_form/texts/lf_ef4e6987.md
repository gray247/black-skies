Jun’s breath quickened beside her, the faint scent of wet earth and mildew clinging to his soaked coat. “We have to keep moving,” he urged, his voice barely above a whisper, but edged with urgency.

A loud clatter rattled the door behind them, the sound of the chain shifting as if someone was testing it from the other side. Clara's stomach twisted, and she instinctively tightened her grip on the parlor key, the red ribbon digging into her wrist. “What if it’s someone... someone who shouldn’t be there?” she stammered, glancing over her shoulder, half-expecting to see a face peering through the gloom.

Jun shook his head, his dark eyes wide and glistening with a sheen of fear. “We can’t stay here, Clara. We need to find another way out.” His voice cracked slightly, and she noticed the tremor in his hands as he adjusted the lantern again, the light sputtering, threatening to fade entirely.

Clara’s mind raced, torn between the instinct to flee and a stubborn attachment to the creaking old house that had been her refuge for so long. “But what if—”

A soft cough echoed from above, a sound so faint it could have been a figment of her imagination, yet it sent a shiver racing down her spine.
