Jun shifted beside her, the lantern sputtering again, its flickering light casting a ghostly glow that seemed to twist the shadows into shapes that danced just beyond her vision. “I can’t stand this,” he muttered, his voice barely rising above the low hum of anxiety that filled the corridor. The quaver in his tone made Clara's stomach tighten. She wanted to reassure him, to say something that would wipe the fear from his eyes, but the weight of the air seemed to steal her words.

“Just a few more steps,” she said instead, the words coming out sharper than she intended, but she couldn’t help it. The tension was like a tightrope strung between them, and she was too close to falling. The key tied around her wrist felt heavier now, the smooth metal cool against her clammy skin, a reminder of the strange warmth of the greenhouse where they had first found it tangled in the wet vines. “We’re almost through.”

As she moved deeper into the passage, she caught a glimpse of something on the floor—a trail of muddy prints leading away from them. Her breath caught in her throat. “Jun,” she whispered, pointing, her voice trembling with a mix of dread and curiosity. “Look.” The prints were fresh, trailing toward the chained door at the end of the corridor. It was as if someone had just passed, their presence still lingering heavy in the air.
