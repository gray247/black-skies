Shadows danced along the damp stone walls, elongated and grotesque, twisting like the fears knotting Clara's stomach. Each step sent a shiver of dread skittering down her spine, the icy fingers of the unknown curling around her heart.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice trembled, cutting through the oppressive silence. He glanced back, his soaked coat clinging to him like a second skin, the dim light catching the glint of panic in his eyes.

Clara shook her head, but her heart raced—a low, persistent echo that drowned out her certainty. “It’s just the wind,” she said too quickly, her tone sharper than intended. The words felt brittle, cracking under the weight of their shared unease. She couldn’t let him see how the shadows seemed to whisper secrets, how the air thickened with foreboding.

They pressed on, the lantern sputtering again, casting a flickering glow that barely illuminated the path before them. Muddy prints marred the stone beneath their feet, fresh and deep, trailing toward the far end of the corridor.
