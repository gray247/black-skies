Jun, hovering close behind her, shifted his weight, the damp fabric of his coat clinging to him like an unwelcome shroud. The lantern flickered again, a sputtering heartbeat in the enveloping darkness, casting shadows that danced with an unsettling life of their own.

“Maybe it’s just stuck?” he suggested, though the knot in his voice betrayed his uncertainty. Clara could feel the warmth of his breath against her neck, mingling with the cool air that crept in from the cracks of the old house, thick with the musty scent of forgotten memories.

The key turned, but the door didn’t budge. Clara pressed her shoulder against the wood, leaning in with a forceful push that sent a shiver through the frame. “It should open,” she muttered, her heart thrumming erratically, each pulse echoing the tension that coiled between them. She glanced back at Jun; his brow was furrowed, the lantern casting a hesitant glow over his features. The way his eyes darted toward the dim corners of the corridor made her stomach twist.

“Maybe we should just—” he began, but she cut him off, the desperation to know pushing her forward. “No, we need to try again.” She pulled the key out, the movement feeling heavy in her hand, as if the door itself were resisting their efforts.

“Clara…” His voice trailed off, and the weight of the silence pressed down on her. The house settled, a low creaking sound that seemed to rise from the very bones of the structure, and she could swear she felt it—a pulse, a breath, a waiting. She glanced toward the dining room, where Nora’s forced cheer still hung in the air like stale perfume, mingling with the impending storm that rumbled outside, promising more rain and chaos.

“Do you hear that?” Jun asked suddenly, his voice dropping as if he feared to disturb whatever lay beyond the door. Clara strained her ears, the quiet enveloping them like a shroud, but in the distance, she thought she heard it—a soft cough, muffled and fragile, slipping through the cracks like a lost whisper. Her heart leapt, a mix of dread and something else—hope?

“Someone’s in there,” she breathed, her voice barely above a whisper. But then doubt crept in, like the shadows that darted behind the peeling wallpaper. “Or maybe it’s just… the house.”

Jun's hand brushed against her arm, grounding her in the moment. “You’re sure it wasn’t just… a memory?” His eyes searched hers, the flicker of the lantern illuminating the uncertainty etched across his face.

Clara's throat tightened. “Nora said no child has been here for years.” But the words felt hollow, echoing against the reality of the ceramic fox in her pocket, the chipped glaze worn smooth by unknown hands. She could almost hear the laughter again, thin and ghostlike, threading through the air. “But what if...?”

Before she could finish, the chain on the service corridor door rattled once more, a sharp, metallic sound that jolted them both. They exchanged a glance, a moment of silent understanding passing between them, the air thick with unspoken fears.
