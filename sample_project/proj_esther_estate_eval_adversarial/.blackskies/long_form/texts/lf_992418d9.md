Clara’s breath hitched as a metallic rattle echoed through the narrow corridor, the sound of chains being tested from the other side. The lantern sputtered, its flickering light casting eerie shadows that danced along the crumbling walls, illuminating the worry etched on Jun's face. He stood slightly hunched, his soaked coat clinging to him like a second skin, beads of water glistening in the dim glow before they plopped onto the floor, joining the muddy prints that trailed ahead.

“Did you hear that?” he whispered, his voice barely rising above the hum of the dripping water. Clara nodded, her heart racing, but the sound of a child’s cough still echoed in her mind, haunting and distant. She brushed a damp strand of hair from her forehead, her fingers brushing against the cool fabric of her coat pocket where the chipped ceramic fox lay nestled. It felt oddly comforting, its familiar weight grounding her in the unsettling atmosphere.

“Maybe it’s just the wind,” she said too quickly, the sharpness of her tone betraying the unease swelling within her. She took a step forward, the floorboards creaking beneath her weight, as if they too were reluctant to venture further into the darkness. Jun hesitated, his eyes darting toward the chained door, the flickering lantern barely illuminating the links that glinted like teeth waiting to snap shut.

“Clara,” he said, his voice trembling, “we shouldn’t be here. We should go back.” The plea hung heavy between them, the air thick with unspoken fears. Clara could sense the tremor in his hands as he gripped the lantern tighter, the light waning again, and she felt a sudden surge of frustration. Why was he so afraid? They had the key, didn’t they? She glanced at the ribbon around her wrist, bright against her damp skin, a tether to hope she couldn’t quite grasp.
