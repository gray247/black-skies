Clara’s heart thudded in her chest as she pressed deeper into the corridor, the dim light of the lantern casting jittery shadows that flitted like restless spirits across the walls. The air felt thick, pregnant with the scent of decay, and she could almost taste the mildew that clung to the corners of her mouth. “What is it?” she asked, her voice sharper than she intended, as if to pierce through the heavy silence surrounding them. Jun remained still, his eyes darting towards the chained door, the faint clink of metal echoing through the stillness.

“Did you hear that?” he repeated, his tone dropping, laced with an urgency that made her skin prickle. The lantern sputtered again, its light flickering ominously, and Clara felt a chill travel down her spine as she searched the dim corners for whatever unseen threat lurked beyond the darkness. She tightened her grip on the parlor key, the ribbon digging into her wrist, a tang of warmth mixing with the cold dampness that seeped into her bones.

“No,” she said too quickly, forcing a laugh that felt brittle in her throat. “It’s just the wind.” But even as she spoke, she felt the doubt creeping in, gnawing at the edges of her certainty. She wished the lantern would hold its light for just a moment longer, but it dimmed again, leaving them with only a weak glow that barely illuminated the swirling dust motes in the air.

Jun took a step closer to her, his coat still dripping, the fabric darkened by rain and fear. “It sounded like a cough,” he murmured, his voice trembling slightly. “From upstairs.”
