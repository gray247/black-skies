Clara swallowed hard, her throat constricted with a mix of dread and the musty air that pressed in around them. She could feel the weight of the parlor key against her wrist, the red ribbon cutting into her skin, a tether to something that felt both vital and ominous. Jun shifted beside her, his soaked coat clinging to his frame, droplets from the greenhouse rain still pooling at his feet. The lantern flickered again, a stuttering gasp of light that barely illuminated the nursery door's oppressive presence.

“Maybe we should just go back,” she whispered, her voice trembling as she glanced over her shoulder, half-expecting to see shadows shifting in the hallway behind them. The chain on the service corridor door rattled faintly, a sound too deliberate to be ignored. It was as if someone on the other side was testing their resolve, the faint clink echoing like a warning through the stillness.

“Clara.” Jun’s voice was low, steady. He stepped closer, his breath warm against her ear, wrapping around her like a protective barrier. “We can’t turn back now. Not after…everything.” His gaze darted to the door, then back to her, concern flickering in the dim light. “We need to know what’s in there.”

She nodded, though her heart raced as if trying to escape her chest. The house sighed around them, the old wood creaking in response to their indecision. A cough echoed softly from the upper floor, curling through her mind like tendrils of smoke, and Clara pressed her lips together, feeling the chill it left behind. Jun met her eyes, and for a brief moment, she saw his own uncertainty mirrored there.

“Did you hear that?” he asked, a frown deepening the lines on his forehead.

“No,” she lied, though the tremor in her voice betrayed her. She couldn’t admit what she feared—couldn’t let the thought take root. A child’s cough, a sound that belonged to memories long buried, but now clawed its way back to the surface, threatening to unravel everything she thought she knew.

With a deep breath, Clara reached for the key, the ribbon loosening slightly as she twisted it in her fingers. “I’ll do it,” she said, forcing herself to step forward, the chill of the air wrapping around her like a shroud. The door loomed before her, cracked and unwelcoming, as if it had been waiting for them to arrive, to push against its barrier of silence.

As she inserted the key into the lock, her fingers trembled, the metal cool against her skin. The lantern flickered once more, casting long shadows that danced across the nursery door, merging with the peeling paint that flaked away like time itself. A soft thud echoed from within, a heartbeat of sound that made her pause.

“What was that?” Jun asked, his voice barely above a whisper now.

“I don’t know,” Clara replied, the truth tasting bitter on her tongue. She twisted the key, the lock clicking open with a reluctant groan. It felt wrong, like pulling at a scab that should have been left alone. The door creaked as she pushed it ajar, a protest that sent shivers down her spine.

Inside, the nursery revealed itself, dim and cluttered, a forgotten space draped in shadows that seemed to breathe. Dust motes danced in the weak light, swirling like lost memories, and the faint scent of mildew clawed at her nostrils. The chest, once a vibrant hue, lay shrouded in cobwebs, its edges soft with the passage of time.
