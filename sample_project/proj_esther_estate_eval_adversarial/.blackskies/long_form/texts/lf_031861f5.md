Clara pressed forward, the dampness of the corridor seeping deeper into her skin as the lantern’s flickering light sputtered, casting a trembling glow over the faded wallpaper. Each step echoed against the walls, a rhythmic whisper that felt too loud in the oppressive silence. She could sense Jun’s uncertainty beside her, an electric tension crackling in the air. His coat, still heavy with greenhouse rain, clung to him like a second skin, and she caught the faint scent of wet wool mingling with the earthy fragrance of the corridor.

“Clara,” he murmured, his voice low and strained. “What if they’re still inside?” The words hung between them, heavy with dread. She could see the pulse at his throat, a frantic drumbeat that matched her own racing heart.

“They can’t be,” she replied too quickly, the sharpness of her tone a fragile mask over her own rising panic. The sound of the chain rattling on the service corridor door echoed in her mind, a reminder that they weren't alone in this suffocating space. She glanced back at the door, its shadow looming larger in the dim light, as if something—or someone—was testing its strength from the other side.

With a quick motion, she tugged at the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key dangling like a pendulum, its weight a reminder of the choices that had led them here. She felt the fabric bite into her skin; she couldn’t remember tying it tighter, but the thought slipped away as quickly as it came. “We just need to find another way out,” she insisted, trying to convince herself as much as him. Muddy footprints ahead beckoned, stark against the worn floorboards, leading deeper into the darkness.
