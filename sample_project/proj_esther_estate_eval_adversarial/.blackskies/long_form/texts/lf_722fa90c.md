Clara pulled the damp fabric of her coat tighter around her, the chill seeping through the layers, a constant reminder of the storm still thrumming outside. The weak light from Jun’s lantern flickered erratically, casting shadows that clawed at the corners of the narrow corridor. Each step she took seemed to echo, the sound reverberating off the peeling wallpaper that hung like a faded memory of a more vibrant life. The musty scent of wet earth mixed with the sharper tang of metal, as if the very walls were absorbing the remnants of the storm.

“Did you hear that?” Jun’s voice was barely above a whisper, trembling in the humid air. His eyes darted toward the chained door at the end of the passage, the chain rattling softly as if something—someone—was testing it from the other side. Clara felt her heart quicken, a staccato rhythm that matched the rising tension in the air. She shook her head, forcing a casualness she didn’t feel. “It’s just the wind,” she said too quickly, her voice sharper than intended, slicing through the thick silence.

But Jun’s brow furrowed, his gaze still locked on that door. “I don’t think so,” he murmured, his voice low, filled with a dread that seeped into Clara’s bones. She didn’t want to look. The thought of what lay beyond made her stomach twist. Instead, she focused on the ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key dangling like a forgotten promise. She couldn’t remember tying it tighter, yet it felt oddly reassuring, its presence a tether to something solid in this spiraling uncertainty.
