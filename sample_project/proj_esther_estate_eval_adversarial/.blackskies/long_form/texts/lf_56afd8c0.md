Clara held her breath, the damp chill of the service corridor wrapping around her like an unwelcome shroud. She could feel the weight of the parlor key, its ribbon tight against her wrist, a tether to something both familiar and distant. Jun's lantern sputtered again, the light flickering like a candle in the wind, and the shadows twisted into grotesque shapes against the cracked walls. “What do you think it is?” he asked, his voice a fragile thread in the enveloping dark, eyes wide and glistening with fear.

“I don’t know,” Clara replied too quickly, the words spilling out sharper than she intended. She shifted her weight, her back pressing against the cold, clammy surface of the wall, wishing it could dissolve her unease. The muddy footprints ahead of them were stark against the worn floorboards, each step echoing with an ominous promise. The further they went, the more Clara felt a sense of urgency clawing at her. The air grew thick, heavy with the scent of mildew and something darker, as if the corridor itself was holding its breath.
