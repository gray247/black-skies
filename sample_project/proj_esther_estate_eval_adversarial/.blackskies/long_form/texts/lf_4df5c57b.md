The dim light of the cracked brass lantern flickered, casting elongated shadows along the damp walls of the servants' passage. Jun Park squeezed the lantern tighter, his fingers trembling as they brushed against the cool metal, the weak battery sputtering like a breath held too long. Clara Vale's heart thudded in her chest, a frantic rhythm that echoed through the musty corridor. She felt the weight of the key around her wrist, its rough edges digging into her skin, a reminder of the door that had once felt so far away. The ribbon, now a damp red against the chill of the air, seemed to pulse with a life of its own.

She glanced at Jun, whose damp coat clung to his shoulders, darkened by water and fear. “We have to keep moving,” she said, her voice sharper than she intended, cutting through the silence like a knife. He nodded, though his eyes darted toward the chained door at the far end, where the faintest sound of rattling came again, a ghostly echo that sent prickles of unease skittering down her spine.

“Did you hear that?” he whispered, barely audible, as if speaking too loudly would summon whatever lay beyond. Clara turned her gaze toward the door, the chain still shifting as if someone was testing it from the other side. She swallowed hard, the taste of damp earth lingering in her mouth, mingling with the tang of adrenaline. “It’s nothing. Just the wind,” she lied, though the words felt hollow even to her.

They pressed on, the lantern casting weak light over the muddy prints that marred the corridor floor, leading away from them instead of back towards the greenhouse. Clara’s stomach twisted. Each step felt like a descent deeper into a dream she couldn't wake from. The air was thick with the scent of mildew and something sweeter, too sweet, like rotting fruit.
