Clara pressed her palm against the cold, painted wood of the nursery door, feeling the rough texture beneath her fingers. The lantern flickered, casting shadows that danced like restless spirits across the floor, and she could almost convince herself that the muffled cough she thought she heard earlier had been a trick of the wind. Jun shifted beside her, his coat still heavy with rain, the damp fabric clinging uncomfortably to his shoulders. He raised the lantern, its weak beam barely illuminating the faded patterns of the wallpaper, where flowers seemed to wilt under the weight of neglect.

“Are you sure about this?” he asked, his voice low, almost lost in the creaks and sighs of the old house. He glanced at the door, the chain rattling softly as if it had a life of its own. Clara caught the tremor in his voice and felt a knot tighten in her stomach.

“Do you want to go back?” she replied, the challenge in her tone masking her own uncertainty. A moment of silence passed, thick like the air before a storm, punctuated only by the faint sound of water dripping somewhere in the distance.

Jun hesitated, his gaze flicking toward the staircase, where the darkened hallway seemed to stretch on forever. “No,” he finally said, determination creeping back into his voice. “We need to see what’s in there.”

Clara nodded, though doubt gnawed at her insides. She found herself tracing the edge of the red ribbon that held the parlor key, the fabric still damp from their earlier escapade. How had it gotten so tangled in her thoughts, binding her to this moment? She felt a rush of warmth as she thought of the ceramic fox tucked away in her pocket, a fragile reminder of childhood, of a time when the world hadn’t felt so heavy.

“Here goes nothing,” she whispered, and with a swift motion, she turned the key in the lock, the sound of metal against metal echoing in the stillness. The door resisted for an instant, and her heart raced at the thought of it being stuck, of being denied entry once more. But then it yielded with a reluctant creak, revealing a narrow sliver of darkness beyond.

She stepped inside, the air thick with dust and a pungent, stale scent that made her choke back a reflexive cough. The room opened up before her like a long-forgotten memory, the corners shrouded in shadows, and the remnants of a child’s world long since abandoned. A small wooden chest lay askew against the wall, its lid slightly ajar, daring her to uncover its secrets. Clara felt a chill run down her spine as she stepped further in, Jun following close behind, the lantern’s sputtering light barely managing to chase away the darkness.

“What if…” Jun began, but he stopped, his eyes darting around the room as if afraid of what might emerge from the shadows. “What if someone is still here?”

“Someone?” Clara’s voice came out a whisper, thick with fear. The thought of an intruder sent a shiver through her, though deep down, a darker part of her wondered if the cough she had dismissed was something far more sinister.

The chest drew her in, and she knelt beside it, the floorboards creaking under her weight. She pulled the lid open slowly, revealing a jumble of old toys, their colors faded and muted. A small doll with one eye missing stared back at her, its mouth sewn shut in a permanent, silent scream. Clara recoiled for a moment before reaching in, her fingers brushing against the remnants of childhood dreams, each item a ghost of what had once been.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice a thread of urgency. “We need to go. Now.”
