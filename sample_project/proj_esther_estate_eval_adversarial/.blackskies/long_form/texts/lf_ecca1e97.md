Clara pressed her ear against the nursery door, each breath a shallow whisper, mingling with the stale air that seemed to seep through the cracks. The cool wood felt eerily warm under her touch, thrumming softly as if it were a living thing, cradling the weight of secrets long buried. The lantern in Jun’s hands sputtered, its glow a feeble flicker that barely chased away the encroaching shadows, casting distorted shapes that danced along the walls. She could almost hear the echoes of laughter and cries that once filled this space, but now only silence reigned, thick and suffocating.

“Clara,” Jun murmured, his voice barely above a breath, “do you think we should really do this?” His brow furrowed, water still clinging to the fabric of his coat, droplets rolling down like tears from a forgotten past. She turned to meet his gaze, the worry in his eyes mirroring the unease coiling in her own stomach.

“I don’t know,” she admitted, the words tasting bitter on her tongue. “But we can’t just leave it like this, can we?” The memory of the muddy prints had imprinted itself in her mind, leading them to this very moment, a path they had not chosen but felt compelled to follow. A chill slid down her spine, and she shifted her weight, the chipped ceramic fox nestled in her pocket pressing against her—a reminder of something fragile that should have been left behind.

The house around them seemed to inhale, the wood creaking and settling, a low groan that echoed through the narrow corridor. Another sound cut through the silence, a faint cough, muffled yet clear enough to send a jolt of adrenaline racing through her veins. Clara’s heart thudded, an erratic drumbeat that drowned out the quiet. Jun’s eyes widened, and for a moment, they shared a silent understanding, a recognition of the tension that hung between them, thick as fog.

“Did you hear that?” Jun asked, his voice trembling slightly, betraying his usual steadiness.

“I—I didn’t hear anything,” Clara replied, the words slipping out before she could stop them. She felt the weight of his gaze, the way he seemed to study her, as if searching for the truth beneath her veneer of calm. “Maybe it was just the house,” she added hurriedly, a flimsy excuse that felt inadequate even as she said it.

Jun hesitated, his lips parting as if to say more, but the lantern flickered violently, and a shadow seemed to dart just beyond their periphery. “Clara,” he said, urgency creeping into his tone. “We need to make a decision.”

The door loomed before them, a boundary to be crossed, holding within it the echoes of what had been and the uncertainty of what lay ahead. Clara tightened the ribbon around her wrist, the key biting into her skin, a tether to the past and a reminder of the choices she still had to make. The key felt heavier now, a burden as much as a solution.

“Let’s try it,” she whispered, more to herself than to him, her hand shaking as she reached for the doorknob. The wood was cool and smooth against her palm, and for a fleeting moment, she hesitated, her mind racing with visions of what they might find—or what might find them.

Jun moved closer, his breath warm against her neck, and she could smell the damp earth clinging to him, a reminder of the storm that had swept through the greenhouse. “On three?” he suggested, though it sounded more like a question than a statement. She nodded, gathering her resolve, feeling the weight of the lantern’s dim light and the quiet certainty that they were no longer alone.
