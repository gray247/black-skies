The lantern flickered again, sputtering weakly as Clara pressed her ear against the cold wood of the nursery door. The dampness from the greenhouse still clung to her skin, a chill that sank deeper than her coat could fend off. She closed her eyes for a moment, the world around her fading into the suffocating silence of the landing. Jun shifted beside her, his weight pressing against the wall, the cracked brass lantern casting erratic shadows that danced like restless spirits.

“Clara,” he murmured, his voice low, “we should—”

“Wait.” She cut him off, the urgency in her tone sharper than she intended. The air was thick, almost tangible, and it felt as if the house were holding its breath, waiting for something to break the stillness.

Her fingers brushed the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key swinging gently as she moved. The memory of finding it tangled in the wet vines flooded her mind, the image of the key glistening with rainwater a stark contrast to the grim uncertainty that had followed. With a sharp intake of breath, Clara turned her attention back to the door. It loomed large, a barrier to whatever lay beyond.

“What are we waiting for?” Jun’s voice cracked, his eyes darting toward the darkened corridor that stretched away behind them, as if the house were whispering secrets he couldn’t bear to hear. The lantern flickered again, its light dimming further, and an uneasy knot formed in Clara’s stomach.

“Just a second,” she whispered, her words barely escaping her lips as she leaned closer to the door. The wood felt cool against her forehead, and she could almost imagine the pulse of the house behind it, a heartbeat reverberating through the walls.

A sudden sound made her jerk back—was that a cough? It echoed softly, a child’s cough, not distant but close enough to send a tremor through her. Jun’s eyes widened, and she could see the flicker of fear in his gaze. “Did you hear that?” he asked, his voice barely a whisper.

Clara nodded slowly, the realization knotting tighter in her chest. “It sounded like…” She hesitated, her mind racing through the implications. Nora had said no child had slept in the nursery for years, yet here it was, a small voice calling out from the shadows.

“Maybe we should go,” Jun suggested, glancing nervously at the chained door that rattled softly as if something on the other side was testing its strength.

“No,” Clara replied, her determination solidifying. “We have to see what’s in there.” She reached for the key, the red ribbon slipping through her fingers as she fumbled with it, her heart pounding against her ribcage. The lantern sputtered again, the light dimming to a mere flicker, and she felt a rush of despair at the thought of what lay beyond the door. It felt like a threshold to something she was both drawn to and terrified of.

“Clara—” Jun’s voice was laced with an urgency that sent a chill down her spine. “What if—”

“I need to know.” She cut him off again, the words sharp and desperate. The key slipped into the lock, the metal cold against her fingertips, and she turned it slowly, feeling the resistance of the mechanism, the weight of years of silence pressing against her.

The lock clicked, and she paused, breathless, before pushing the door open. It creaked on its hinges, the sound echoing through the stillness, and Clara stepped into the room beyond, her heart racing with a mixture of dread and curiosity.
