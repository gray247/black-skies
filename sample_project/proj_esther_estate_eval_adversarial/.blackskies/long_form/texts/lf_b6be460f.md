The air in the nursery felt thick, as if the room itself were holding its breath. Clara stood before the door, the cracked brass lantern in Jun’s hand sputtering like an old man wheezing for breath. The shadows cast by its fading light danced across the walls, flickering against the peeling wallpaper that bore the ghosts of brighter days. She glanced at the key tied to her wrist, the red ribbon digging into her skin, a reminder of something both precious and burdensome.

“What now?” Jun’s voice was a low murmur, the weight of their journey pressing down on him like the damp coat clinging to his shoulders. He shifted, the lantern’s light bobbing as if it were unsure of its footing, and Clara could see the uncertainty in his eyes, mirroring her own.

“We can’t just stand here,” she said, though her own heart felt like a stone lodged deep in her chest. Her fingers brushed against the ceramic fox in her pocket, its chipped surface cool against her palm, a silent companion in this unsettling moment. “We have to open it.”

“Open what?” he asked, glancing over his shoulder, as if expecting someone—or something—to emerge from the shadows. “This door? Or the one upstairs?”

Clara let out a shaky breath. The memory of the child’s cough echoed in her mind, a sound that had no business being here, and yet it lingered, taunting her. “This one,” she decided, the word feeling foreign on her tongue. She reached for the lock, her fingers trembling as she fumbled with the key, its shape still unfamiliar despite her earlier attempts.

“Are you sure that’s the right key?” Jun shifted his weight, his eyes darting toward the door, the chain rattling softly as if responding to their presence.

She hesitated, the doubt gnawing at her resolve. “Nora said—”

“Nora isn’t here,” he interrupted, his voice sharper than she'd expected, and she felt the sting of his frustration. “We are. Right now, it’s just us and this house.”

Clara's gaze flickered to his soaked coat, water droplets clinging to the fabric like memories refusing to fade. “We can’t ignore it,” she said, more to herself than to him, as the clock overhead began to tick, each sound amplifying the silence threatening to swallow them whole. “If there’s a child… if someone’s up there…”

“We’ll check the nursery first.” Jun’s voice softened, but the urgency remained. He stepped closer, the lantern casting a warm glow that felt fragile, as if the light itself might shatter under the weight of unspoken fears.

Clara bit her lip, her heart racing as she turned the key in the lock. The mechanism clicked, a sound both satisfying and ominous, and she pushed the door open. The hinges creaked, like an old man stretching after a long slumber, and a musty scent wafted out, mingling with the faintest hint of lavender that had long since wilted.

Inside, the nursery lay cloaked in shadows, the furniture draped in dust-covered sheets. Sunlight had long abandoned this room, leaving only the dim promise of what it once was.
