The lantern flickered again, its weak glow barely piercing the thickening shadows that pressed against them in the cramped corridor. Jun’s breath hitched as he willed it to stay lit, the sputtering light casting nervous shapes that seemed to creep along the walls. Clara’s heart thudded in time with the rattling chain at the door behind them, a sound that twisted in her gut like a serpent, coiling tighter with each jangle. “We shouldn’t be here,” she blurted, her voice sharper than intended, slicing through the oppressive silence.

Jun’s eyes darted around, searching for something, anything, in the damp gloom. “Clara, did you hear it?” he asked, voice trembling, as if just saying it would summon the sound again. She shook her head, frustration bubbling beneath her skin. How could he be so frightened? “It’s just the wind,” she insisted, but the words felt hollow, like they were meant to soothe him more than herself. A child’s cough echoed in her mind, but she shoved it away, focusing instead on the muddy footprints that marred the floor ahead—imprints that led deeper into the shadowed corridor instead of back toward the greenhouse.

Jun took a step forward, the floor creaking underfoot, and she followed, each footfall heavy with reluctance. The air thickened, pressing against her like a damp blanket as a musty odor clawed at her senses. She clenched her fingers around the chipped ceramic fox nestled in her coat pocket, its surface cool against her palm, a fragile anchor in the turmoil of the moment. “Do you think it’s safe?” Jun’s voice broke through her thoughts, a whisper wrapped in uncertainty.
