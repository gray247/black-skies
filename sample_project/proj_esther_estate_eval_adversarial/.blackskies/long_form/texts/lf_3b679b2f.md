Clara’s breath quickened as she leaned closer to the flickering lantern, its dying glow casting long shadows that skittered across the walls like wisps of smoke. The wallpaper, with its faded floral design, seemed to pulsate, and she had to blink hard to convince herself it wasn’t alive. Jun shifted beside her, his soaked coat clinging to him like a second skin, and she could see the unease etched across his face, his eyes wide, reflecting the dim light as if searching for something—anything—in the gloom.

“Do you hear that?” he repeated, his voice taut, straining against the silence that enveloped them. Clara strained to listen, her heart drumming in her ears, but all she could detect was the distant drip of water, a rhythmic reminder of the rain that had soaked them and turned the path behind them into a muddy mess. She shook her head, a quick gesture she hoped would dispel the tension thickening the air. “No, I don’t hear anything.”

But even as she spoke, she felt a chill that had nothing to do with the dampness in the corridor. Her fingers brushed the red ribbon tied around her wrist, the parlor key hanging from it a weight she couldn’t shake.
