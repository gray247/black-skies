The air felt colder here, as though the walls themselves exhaled a chill that crept beneath her skin. She glanced sideways at Jun, his expression taut, the flicker of fear in his eyes mirroring the unease gnawing at her gut. “We should keep moving,” she urged, her voice sharper than intended, cutting through the silence with a brittle edge.

“Did you hear that?” he whispered, his voice barely above a breath, eyes darting to the far end of the corridor where the darkness seemed to pulse, a living thing. Clara hesitated, straining to listen. There it was again, a soft cough, the sound of a child. It echoed off the walls, a faint, desperate plea that sent her heart racing. She shook her head, trying to dispel the growing dread coiling tightly around her thoughts. “It’s just the wind,” she insisted, though a sliver of doubt crept in. How could she explain the chill that settled in her bones or the way the shadows seemed to stretch toward her, reaching out with unseen fingers?

Jun’s brow furrowed, his soaked coat clinging to him like a shroud. “No, Clara, it’s not just the wind.
