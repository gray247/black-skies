The air in the corridor was thick with the scent of damp earth and something faintly metallic, a reminder of lingering storms outside. Clara pressed forward, her heart quickening with each step, the frayed hem of her dress brushing against the cool stone floor. The lantern's flickering light cast long shadows that danced along the walls, and she clutched the cracked brass handle tighter, willing it to stay lit. Jun shuffled behind her, his coat still dripping from the greenhouse rain, each droplet echoing against the silence like a muffled heartbeat.

“Clara,” he whispered, his voice barely breaking the stillness, “Do you really think it’s safe in here?”

She shot him a glance, the urgency in his eyes mirroring the apprehension bubbling beneath her skin. “We have to keep moving. There's no time to waste.” Her tone was sharper than she intended, but the darkness pressed in on her, urging her to push through. The red ribbon tied around her wrist felt constricting, the parlor key a heavy reminder of what they had risked to retrieve it.

As they rounded a corner, the faint clinking of metal met her ears. Clara froze, the sound creeping along her spine like an unwelcome chill. “What was that?” Jun’s voice trembled, his breath warm against the chilled air.

“Probably just the chain,” she replied, though her own voice wavered, a thin veil over her rising dread. The door to the service corridor rattled again, a persistent clanging that seemed to mock their presence, as if something on the other side was testing its bonds, yearning for escape.

“Maybe we should go back,” he urged, his eyes darting down the hallway, searching for an exit that felt increasingly elusive.

“No.” The word escaped her lips before she could consider it, propelled by a mix of fear and defiance.
