Clara's breath came in shallow bursts, the damp air of the corridor wrapping around her like a shroud, heavy with mildew and the faint, lingering scent of wet earth. The lantern flickered in Jun's hand, its sputtering light casting erratic shadows that danced along the peeling wallpaper, revealing half-formed shapes that twisted and recoiled at their approach. She could feel his fear like a palpable force, a weight pressing down on her chest, but she forced a steady tone as she whispered, “We need to keep moving.”

Jun nodded, his coat still drenched from the downpour in the greenhouse. Water dripped from the hem, pooling on the wooden floorboards beneath their feet. “The chain—it's moving,” he said, his voice strained, eyes darting toward the door that separated them from whatever lay beyond. Clara could hear it too, a soft scraping sound, like fingernails against metal, persistent and unnerving. She clenched her fingers around the parlor key, the red ribbon digging into her wrist, a strangely comforting sensation amidst the chaos.

“Maybe it’s just the wind,” she replied too quickly, her words cutting through the silence with a sharpness that surprised her. She could feel the pulse of her heart in her throat, each beat a reminder of the fear she refused to fully acknowledge. The muddy prints on the floor, fresh and unmistakable, led deeper into the corridor. They were not her own, and she could not shake the feeling that they belonged to something much more ominous.

“Clara,” Jun said, his voice trembling slightly, “we can’t just stand here.” He stepped forward, lantern raised, and the light revealed more of the corridor—a narrow passageway lined with old, dust-covered furniture draped in white sheets, like ghosts waiting for a long-forgotten party.
